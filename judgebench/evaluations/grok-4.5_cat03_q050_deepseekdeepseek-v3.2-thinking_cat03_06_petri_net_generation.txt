The submitted code fails at a fundamental level of Petri-net correctness and therefore cannot realize the required accepting Petri net.

**Critical structural defects (render the net unusable)**  
- Transitions `t_DV` and `t_FC` each possess two distinct input places (`p_after_CR`/`p_before_DV` and `p_after_DV`/`p_before_FC` respectively). In the token-game semantics of a Petri net a transition is enabled only when *every* input place carries a token. Consequently the main path is immediately deadlocked: the initial token never enables `t_DV`. The same dual-input construction also destroys the intended loops; rework tokens cannot re-enable the activities either.  
- The intended exclusive-choice / loop pattern (single shared input place fed by both the normal predecessor and the rework transition) is never realized. The extra “before” places are therefore not merely superfluous; they invert the required XOR-join into an AND-join.

**Secondary modelling errors**  
- After Claim Assessment the silent transition that bypasses Approval is acceptable in principle, yet the subsequent place is named `p_after_AP` even when the silent path is taken—an unnecessary source of confusion.  
- The opening activity “Insurance Claim (C)” mentioned in the scenario is absent; the net begins directly at Claim Registration. While this could be regarded as a minor omission, it already violates a literal reading of the process description.  
- No guard or data annotation distinguishes the two post-CA branches; the silent transition is therefore an undocumented assumption rather than a faithful encoding of the stated “if-threshold” rule.

**Minor technical / stylistic issues**  
- Superfluous print statements and an inconsistent mixture of place-naming conventions reduce readability.  
- The final marking is correctly a singleton on the sink, and the pm4py API calls themselves are syntactically valid; these are the only redeeming features.

Because a Petri net that cannot fire even the happy-path sequence is, by definition, not an accepting Petri net for the given process, the solution scores only 2.0. Even a single dual-input error of this kind would have been sufficient to drop the grade below 5; the presence of two independent instances of the same error, together with the secondary modelling gaps, justifies the still lower mark.
