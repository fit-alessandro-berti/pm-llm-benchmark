### Normative baseline

A standard **successful-hire** path is roughly:

**Post_Job_Ad  Screen_Candidates  Conduct_Interviews  Make_Hiring_Decision  Onboard_Employee  Add_to_Payroll  Close_Case**

Two important principles follow from that:

- **The hiring decision should come after candidate evaluation**, especially after screening and interviewing.
- **If the employee is onboarded, payroll should not be optional.**
- **Close_Case should be the last meaningful step.**

(If one wanted to model rejection, that would normally be a branch after the decision, before onboarding/payroll.)

---

## Model 1 analysis

### What it gets right
- `Post_Job_Ad` before `Screen_Candidates`
- `Onboard_Employee  Add_to_Payroll  Close_Case`
- All core activities are present

### Main anomaly
The key problem is that **`Conduct_Interviews` is not ordered before `Make_Hiring_Decision`**.

From the partial order:

- `Screen  Decide`
- `Screen  Interview`

but there is **no `Interview  Decide`**.

So the model allows:
- deciding **before** interviewing, or
- deciding **in parallel with** interviewing.

### Consequence
This is worse than just a small sequencing issue: because `Interview` is not constrained before the rest of the chain, the model even allows traces like:

**Post, Screen, Decide, Onboard, Payroll, Close, Interview**

So the case can effectively be closed before the interview happens.

### Severity
This is a **serious anomaly**, because it undermines the logic of candidate evaluation.  
Still, the downstream HR administration is at least structurally sensible: once the decision is made, onboarding, payroll, and closure happen in a coherent order.

---

## Model 2 analysis

### What it gets right
- It correctly enforces **`Interview  Decide`**

### Anomalies

#### 1. Screening is disconnected from the decision path
The model has:
- `Post  Screen`
- `Post  Interview`
- `Interview  Decide`

But there is **no relation between `Screen` and `Interview` or `Decide`**.

This means:
- interviews can happen **before screening**
- the hiring decision can be made **without waiting for screening**
- screening can even occur **after onboarding or after case closure**

For example, a valid visible trace is:

**Post, Interview, Decide, Onboard, Close, Screen**  
(if payroll is skipped)

That is a very strong deviation from normative hiring logic.

#### 2. Onboarding is modeled as a loop
`loop_onboarding = LOOP(Onboard, skip)`

This means onboarding happens **at least once**, but may be repeated arbitrarily many times:
- `Onboard`
- `Onboard, Onboard`
- `Onboard, Onboard, Onboard`
- etc.

Repeated onboarding is generally not a normal Hire-to-Retire behavior.

#### 3. Payroll is optional
`xor_payroll = XOR(Payroll, skip)`

So after onboarding, the process may:
- add the employee to payroll, or
- skip payroll entirely and go straight to closing the case.

That is a **fundamental process violation** for a successful hire.  
If the employee is onboarded, omitting payroll is not just bad practice; it breaks the basic logic of employment administration.

### Severity
Model 2 contains **multiple severe anomalies**:
- screening may become irrelevant or happen too late,
- onboarding may repeat unnecessarily,
- payroll can be skipped,
- the case can close without payroll completion.

---

## Which model is closer to a normative Hire-to-Retire process?

### **Model 1 is closer to the normative process.**

### Why?
Because its anomaly is mainly a **single major ordering defect**:
- the interview is not enforced before the hiring decision.

By contrast, **Model 2** introduces several deeper problems:
- screening is detached from decision-making,
- screening may happen after closure,
- onboarding can repeat,
- payroll is optional even after onboarding.

So although Model 2 fixes one issue (`Interview  Decide`), it does so while creating **more severe violations of process correctness and integrity**.

---

## Final judgment

- **Model 1:** closer to standard Hire-to-Retire, but still flawed because it allows hiring decisions and even closure before interviews.
- **Model 2:** less normative overall, because it breaks both recruitment logic and post-hire administrative integrity.

If I had to rank them:

1. **Model 1** — better / closer to normative  
2. **Model 2** — worse / more anomalous
