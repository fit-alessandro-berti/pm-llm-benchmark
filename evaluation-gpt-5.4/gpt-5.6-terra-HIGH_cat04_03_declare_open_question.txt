### Major problems
1. **`responded_existence` is misinterpreted**
   - The answer says `responded_existence(D, E)` means:
     > If D occurs, E must occur; and if E occurs, D must exist somewhere.
   - That is incorrect under standard DECLARE semantics. `responded existence(A,B)` is one-way: if `A` occurs, `B` must occur somewhere. The converse does **not** follow.
   - This is a nontrivial formal error.

2. **`altprecedence` is interpreted in the wrong direction**
   - Given the answer’s own stated assumption — “dictionary key is the source activity and `target` is the related activity” — `altprecedence(H, G)` should be read in the standard way as: every `G` must be preceded by `H` in the alternating sense.
   - The answer instead says:
     > Each customer notification must have a preceding fund transfer.
   - That reverses the constraint.

3. **`chainprecedence` is also interpreted in the wrong direction**
   - Under standard semantics, `chainprecedence(F, B)` means every `B` is immediately preceded by `F`.
   - The answer reads it as:
     > Every contract authorization must be immediately preceded by a preliminary credit check.
   - Again, this reverses the template.

4. **One infeasibility proof is therefore formally unsound**
   - The second contradiction argument relies on the reversed reading of `chainprecedence`.
   - The model may still be infeasible, but that particular proof is not correct as written.

- Some practical interpretations are speculative in ways that are fine for business discussion but not rigorous for formal model analysis.
