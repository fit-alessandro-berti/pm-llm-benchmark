## Why it is not a top score
The main issue is that the prompt specifically asks for **“targeted, open-ended questions.”** This answer only partially satisfies that.

### 1. Too many questions are not truly open-ended
A significant number are framed as:
- “Who approves...?”
- “Can X happen before Y?”
- “Is there...?”
- “What happens if...?”
- “Under what circumstances...?”

These are useful, but many are **closed or semi-closed** rather than fully open-ended. They often invite short factual answers instead of richer explanation.

Examples:
- “Is there a management agreement with the landlord, and when must it be signed?”
- “Can these reviews run in parallel, or must they follow a particular sequence?”
- “Is reinspection always required after remediation...?”
- “Are activation and publication to external listing channels simultaneous or separate steps?”

These are valid clarifications, but they are not consistently “open-ended” in the strongest sense.

### 2. Some questions are overly granular or drift into policy-detail interrogation
The answer occasionally starts sounding like a compliance checklist or operating manual review rather than an interview aimed at improving conceptual understanding.

Examples:
- “How are adverse decisions communicated, reviewed, and retained?”
- “Which findings block activation, lease signing, or move-in?”
- “What conditions must be satisfied before keys or access credentials are released?”

These are not wrong, but some feel **too operationally fine-grained** relative to the stated goal.

### 3. There is some redundancy
Several questions overlap materially:
- activation criteria / readiness checklist / final authority / system failures
- approvals across documentation, pricing, lease, exceptions
- repeated variants of “who decides / who approves / who owns”

A tighter set of questions would likely be more effective in a real interview.

### 4. The list is arguably too long for practical interviewing
Ninety questions is exhaustive, but **not well-calibrated for an interview setting** unless explicitly presented as a question bank. The prompt asks for “a series of questions,” and while this qualifies, it lacks prioritization. A better answer might group and sequence a smaller set of highest-value questions first, with follow-ups.

### 5. A few questions introduce assumptions not established in the process
Some questions presume process components not clearly stated:
- management agreement
- move-in inspection
- adverse decisions retention
- executive approval
- capacity limits as formal rules

These are reasonable hypotheses to test, but under strict grading, they slightly weaken the precision of the questioning.

## Bottom line
This is a **very good discovery-question set** and far better than an average response. However, it falls short of a top-tier score because:
- it is **not consistently open-ended**,
- it is **somewhat repetitive**,
- it is **overly long and operationally granular** in places,
- and it could be **better prioritized and more interview-friendly**.

A nearly flawless answer would preserve this coverage while using fewer, more open-ended, more synthesized questions.
