This is a solid prompt in general, but under a hyper-strict reading it falls well short of being nearly flawless.

Main strengths:
- It is clear, structured, and usable.
- It correctly restates the schema and expected process flow.
- It explicitly asks for anomaly detection, root-cause hypotheses, and executable PostgreSQL queries.
- It encourages analysis beyond the toy sample.

Why the score is not higher:
1. **It violates the “without giving any hints” requirement.**  
   The answer explicitly steers the target LLM toward:
   - “control-flow, temporal, resource, and data perspectives”
   - “process inefficiencies, compliance risks, and undesirable behaviors”
   - possible causes like “system errors, policy violations, human error, lack of training, intentional circumvention, high workload”  
   These are helpful, but they are still hints/guidance. The original requirement was specifically to avoid that.

2. **It truncates the example data.**  
   The original prompt includes four cases, with important anomaly patterns in 1003 and 1004. This version only shows 1001 and 1002, then says “... [more cases exist in the full database]”. That is a loss of fidelity to the provided scenario.

3. **It changes the task framing slightly.**  
   The original emphasis is on “identify anomalies and undesirable behaviors in the given event log” and then hypothesize why. This answer reframes it as an audit for “process inefficiencies, compliance risks, and undesirable behaviors,” which is reasonable but not perfectly faithful.

4. **It is somewhat over-engineered.**  
   “You are a Senior Process Mining and Forensic Analyst expert in Order-to-Cash processes” is fine, but it adds stylistic scaffolding rather than improving alignment with the core requirement.

5. **It does not maximize neutrality.**  
   A better version would ask the model to analyze the event log and propose SQL, without pre-categorizing anomaly types or root-cause buckets.

So: good practical prompt, but not a precise fulfillment of the “no hints/guidance” constraint. Under strict grading, that prevents a high score.
