## 1. Some technical statements are inaccurate or sloppy
This matters a lot under strict grading.

- **Makespan definition is flawed.**  
  The answer says:
  > “Makespan for a planning horizon is obtained by summing or taking the maximum completion times of all jobs released in that window.”

  That is incorrect or at least badly phrased. Makespan is not “summing or taking the maximum completion times.” It should be something like:
  \[
  \max(C_j) - \min(r_j)
  \]
  over the relevant job set/horizon. This is a notable scheduling error.

- **Utilization calculation is oversimplified.**  
  It defines idle time as gaps between consecutive tasks, but in a real shop floor this must distinguish:
  - off-shift time
  - planned maintenance
  - breakdown downtime
  - blocked time
  - starved time
  - setup time  
  The answer partially fixes this later, but the metric definition is still too casual for a senior-level response.

- **Routing probabilities from the discovered process map** are not ideal for a high-mix, low-volume job shop where routings are often order-specific, not probabilistic in a stable flow-shop sense. A better answer would discuss:
  - family-conditioned routings
  - actual sampled routings by product class
