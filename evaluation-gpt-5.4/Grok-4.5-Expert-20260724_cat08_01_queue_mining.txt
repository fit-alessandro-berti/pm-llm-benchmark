## Why it does **not** deserve a very high score

### 1. It equates all inter-activity gaps with “pure queueing time”
This is one of the biggest conceptual weaknesses.

The answer defines waiting time as:

> COMPLETE of activity A_i to START of immediately succeeding activity A_{i+1}

That is a common operational approximation, but calling it **“pure waiting/queueing time”** is too strong and somewhat inaccurate in healthcare. That gap may include:
- medically necessary waiting
- patient movement time
- patient-caused delays
- documentation lag
- batching logic
- intentional clinical holds
- asynchronous result turnaround

A stronger answer would explicitly distinguish:
- **inter-activity elapsed time**
- **true queue time**
- **transport/coordination delay**
- **clinical delay**
- **patient delay**

This matters because the question is specifically about **queue mining**, not just elapsed-time decomposition.

---

### 2. The handling of zero or negative waits is sloppy
The answer says:

> “Filter out negative or zero waits (data-quality artifacts)…”

That is too blunt and partly wrong.
- **Zero waits are not artifacts** by default. They can be perfectly valid immediate handoffs.
- **Negative waits** may indicate clock issues, logging granularity problems, or overlapping/concurrent activities rather than simple bad data.

Under strict grading, this is a meaningful methodological flaw.
