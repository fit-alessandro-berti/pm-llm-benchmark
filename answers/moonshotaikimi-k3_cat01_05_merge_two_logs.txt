## Conflict Resolution Rules Applied

1. **Timestamps:** A is the primary timeline (per context), so `ts_A` is the sort key and canonical time; `ts_B` is preserved alongside. For B-only events, `ts_B` becomes the primary timestamp.
2. **Event naming:** A's convention is canonical. Normalization map: `OrderReceived  Order Received`, `OrderValidation  Order Validated`, `PaymentCheck  Payment Processed`, `Shipping  Item Shipped`. `Quality Check` retained as-is (B-only).
3. **Attributes:** Union of both schemas. B-only attributes (`user_id`, `resource_id`, `notes`) are null for A-only events. A `source` field (`A`, `B`, `A+B`) records provenance.
4. **order_id:** Identical across all records (`Order#1234`) — no conflict.

## Caveats

- The skew direction is inconsistent (B is earlier for events 1–2, later for events 3 and 5), suggesting **clock drift plus processing delay** rather than a fixed ingestion lag — this justifies a *symmetric* ±2 s window.
- Boundary cases at exactly 2 s (events 1 and 5) were treated as matches under an inclusive reading of the tolerance; a strict `< 2 s` reading would separate them.
- With a single order, matching is easy; in a multi-order log, one-to-one assignment constraints and per-order sequence alignment would be needed to avoid cross-order mismatches.
