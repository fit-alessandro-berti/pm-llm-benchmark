Based on standard **Procure-to-Pay (P2P)** best practices, the correct control flow is generally:

1. **Create Purchase Requisition (PR)**  2. **Approve PR**  3. **Issue Purchase Order (PO)**  4. **Receive Goods**  5. **Receive Vendor Invoice**  6. **Match Invoice to PO/Receipt** (three-way match)  7. **Release Payment**  8. **Close Case**

Key control principles include: **approval before commitment** (PO), **receipt of goods/services before (or concurrent with) payment verification**, and **invoice matching BEFORE payment release**.

Below is the case-by-case analysis.

---

### Case 1001 — Standard / Normal Flow
| Step | Timestamp | Activity |
|------|-----------|----------|
| 1 | Feb 1 08:00 | Create PR |
| 2 | Feb 1 08:15 | Approve PR |
| 3 | Feb 1 09:00 | Issue PO |
| 4 | Feb 2 10:00 | Receive Goods |
| 5 | Feb 2 11:30 | Vendor Invoice Received |
| 6 | Feb 5 09:00 | Match Invoice to PO |
| 7 | Feb 6 10:00 | Release Payment |
| 8 | Feb 7 15:00 | Close Case |

**Assessment:** This case follows the ideal sequence. The PR is approved before the PO is issued; goods are received before the invoice and well before payment; the invoice is matched to the PO/receipt before payment is released; and payment occurs only after verification. The 3-day gap between invoice receipt and matching is administratively acceptable. **No anomalies detected.**

---

### Case 1002 — Severe Control Breakdowns (Multiple Critical Anomalies)
**Recorded Sequence:**
1. Create PR (Feb 1 08:30)
2. **Issue PO (Feb 1 08:40)** — *No approval recorded yet*
3. Vendor Invoice Received (Feb 3 07:00)
4. **Release Payment (Feb 3 07:15)** — *Before goods, before match, and before approval*
5. **Approve Purchase Requisition (Feb 3 08:00)** — *After PO, invoice, and payment!*
6. Receive Goods (Feb 4 09:00) — *After payment*
7. Match Invoice to PO (Feb 5 08:00) — *After payment*
8. Close Case (Feb 6 16:00)

**Anomalies & Problems:**

| Violation | Why It Is Problematic |
|-----------|----------------------|
| **PO issued before PR approval** | The requisition was never authorized before the company committed to spend with a vendor. This bypasses budget/authority controls and enables unauthorized purchasing. |
| **Payment released before goods received, invoice matched, and PR approved** | This is the most critical failure. The company paid the vendor (Feb 3) before confirming: (a) that the goods were actually delivered (received Feb 4), (b) that the invoice was valid and matched to the PO/receipt (matched Feb 5), and (c) that the original request was even approved (approved Feb 3, *after* payment). |
| **Approval occurs after spending and payment** | The approval is rendered meaningless—it's a retroactive rubber stamp rather than a preventive control. |
| **Goods received after payment** | Paying before delivery means the company bears 100% of the risk if the vendor never delivers or delivers non-conforming goods. There is no leverage to enforce delivery. |
| **Matching occurs after payment** | The three-way match (PO, receipt, invoice) is designed to prevent overpayment or payment for wrong/duplicate invoices. Doing it after payment eliminates its protective value—any discrepancy discovered is already a realized financial loss. |

**Business Risk:** High risk of **fraud, duplicate/overpayment, maverick spending, and unrecoverable cash outflows** with no verification that value was received.
