## Which log exhibits bias?

**Group B exhibits clear procedural bias.** Candidates affiliated with the **LocalBusinessLeadersClub** receive an explicit **+5 cultural-fit adjustment**, while Group A candidates receive no comparable opportunity for an adjustment.

## How the bias manifests

- **Unequal scoring rule:**  
  U001’s cultural-fit score rises from 60 to 65, and U003’s rises from 58 to 63 solely because of association membership—not demonstrated job-related performance.

- **Potentially outcome-determinative advantage:**  
  Both boosted candidates, U001 and U003, are hired, while U002, the only unboosted Group B candidate, is not hired. U003 is especially notable because comparatively modest raw scores are supplemented by the association bonus.

- **Proxy discrimination risk:**  
  In these logs, Group A candidates are non-local and have no association membership, whereas the favored association appears only among local, unprotected candidates. Thus, association membership may function as a proxy for group status, locality, socioeconomic access, or professional networks.

- **Inconsistent treatment of comparable candidates:**  
  A raw cultural-fit score of 60 remains 60 for P002 and U002, but becomes 65 for U001 because of club membership. Candidates are therefore not evaluated under a uniform standard.

## Fairness implications

The raw hiring rates happen to be equal:

- Group A: 2 of 3 hired = **66.7%**
- Group B: 2 of 3 hired = **66.7%**

However, equal aggregate outcomes do **not** establish fairness. They mask the unequal process used to reach those outcomes. Group B members receive preferential treatment based on social affiliation, creating an advantage unavailable to the protected group. This undermines procedural fairness, equal opportunity, and merit-based selection and may perpetuate exclusion from influential networks.

The appropriate response would be to remove the association bonus unless it is demonstrably job-related and legally justified, use a consistent scoring rule for all candidates, and audit decisions using both raw and adjusted scores. Because the sample is very small, it cannot establish the size of any broader disparate impact, but the explicit scoring adjustment is direct evidence of a biased hiring mechanism.
