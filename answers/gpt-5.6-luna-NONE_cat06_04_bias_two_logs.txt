Group B’s log exhibits the clearest evidence of bias, specifically preferential treatment based on `LocalResident` and `CommunityGroup`.

### Evidence of bias

- All Group B applicants are `LocalResident = TRUE`, while all Group A applicants are `LocalResident = FALSE`.
- Applicants belonging to **Highland Civic Darts Club** receive a **+10 Community Boost**:
  - U001: 720  730, approved
  - U003: 695  705, approved
- Group A applicants receive no comparable adjustment; their scores remain unchanged.
- The adjustment appears to affect outcomes materially. U003, with an original score of 695, is approved after the boost raises the score to 705. Meanwhile, Group A’s applicants with scores of 710 and 720 receive different outcomes, including a rejection at 710.

### How the bias manifests

The decision process grants an advantage to applicants associated with the local community group. Because membership produces a positive score adjustment, similarly situated applicants can receive different final scores and decisions based on community affiliation rather than the underlying preliminary score alone.

The pattern is:

- **Local resident/community-group member:** possible +10 boost and approval.
- **Non-local/non-member:** no boost, with approval depending entirely on the original score.

Although the sample is small and does not establish causation by itself, it shows a systematic disparity in the scoring mechanism. The `ScoreAdjustment` field makes the treatment difference explicit and provides a pathway by which `LocalResident` and `CommunityGroup` status can influence final decisions.

Strictly speaking, the logs demonstrate **potential disparate treatment** against Group A or preferential treatment for Group B. Whether this constitutes unlawful discrimination would require additional information about the legitimate purpose of the community boost, decision thresholds, and a larger set of comparable cases.
