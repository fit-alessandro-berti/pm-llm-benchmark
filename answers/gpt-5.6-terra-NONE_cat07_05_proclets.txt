The visualization illustrates a healthcare/laboratory workflow modeled with **two process (proclet) classes** that communicate through message channels.

### (a) Two proclet classes connected through channels
On the left is the **Lab visit** process:

- A doctor initiates the visit.
- The lab assistant performs tasks such as:
  - **Order entry**
  - **Take blood sample**
  - **Judge results**
  - If necessary, **do tests again**
  - **Send report**
- The process includes a decision point, “Everything fine,” which determines whether the workflow finishes or repeats testing.

On the right is the **Lab test** process:

- A chemical analyst:
  - **Performs test**
  - **Makes report**
  - Either **finishes the lab test** or, if results require it,
  - **Performs the test again**
  - **Provides an updated report**

The two processes exchange information through two dashed communication channels:

- **Order system**: sends lab-test orders from the lab visit to the lab-test process.
- **HIS** (Hospital Information System): transfers test results and updated results back to the lab visit.

The labels such as `1..1` and `1..*` indicate the cardinality of message relationships—for example, one lab visit can require one or more lab tests.

### (b) Class diagram
The upper-right diagram abstracts the workflow into two classes:

- **Lab visit**
- **Lab test**

A lab visit **requires** one or more lab tests (`1..*`), while each lab test belongs to one lab visit (`1..1`).

### (c) Example performance/event record
The bottom table gives an example of a logged communication event:

- **Time:** 11:00
- **Channel:** Order system
- **Sender:** Lab visit – John
- **Receiver:** Lab test – HGB John
- **Action:** Create
- **Content:** “Can you perform a HGB test for John?”
- **Scope:** Private
- **Direction:** OUT

Overall, the visualization demonstrates how a patient’s lab visit coordinates with one or more lab tests through asynchronous message exchanges.
