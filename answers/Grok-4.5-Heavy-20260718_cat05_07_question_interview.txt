**Targeted open-ended questions to clarify the property onboarding process:**

1. Could you walk us through the key roles (property coordinator, assigned property manager, leasing agent if separate, digital marketing team, compliance officer, financial analyst, IT, and others), the main handoffs between them, ownership of decisions, and overall accountability for the timeline at each major stage?

2. Beyond the roles already described, are there other internal or external stakeholders who commonly become involved, and under what circumstances? Who else typically interacts directly with the landlord, and how are those interactions coordinated?

3. When assigning a property manager, how are the factors of current workload, geographic specialization, experience with similar property types, and track record with unique landlord requirements balanced or prioritized when they conflict? Is the process formal/structured or primarily judgment-based?

4. What is the typical end-to-end timeline for onboarding a standard property? Which steps commonly run in parallel versus strictly sequentially, and what are the critical dependencies or approval gates—especially around inspection clearance, marketing preparation, and activation?

5. At what precise point is the property considered “activated” and available for applications, and what must be fully complete before that milestone?

6. In cases with multiple qualified tenant applicants, what criteria or decision framework (beyond standard credit, employment, and reference checks) guides prioritization, negotiation of lease terms or promotions, and final selection? How much influence does the landlord typically have at that stage?

7. How are inspection findings handled when issues are identified? Who decides on required repairs, who coordinates any contractors, and what happens if the landlord is slow, unwilling, or unable to remediate promptly—does this lead to pause, decline, or other paths?

8. How does the process adapt for exceptions such as properties requiring special insurance, unique local regulatory requirements across cities, or landlords who insist on non-standard or custom lease clauses? Who identifies these early, how are they escalated, and who has authority to approve deviations or decline the property?

9. What happens if key documentation remains incomplete after requests, the landlord becomes unresponsive or withdraws, or expectations cannot be aligned during the introductory meeting? Are there formal or informal off-ramps, and how are delays tracked and escalated?

10. Can you describe when the quality checks and audits by the compliance officer, financial analyst, and IT department occur relative to activation, tenant selection, and lease finalization? What specifically do they verify, and what happens if concerns are raised after the property is activated or a lease is signed?

11. Who has the authority to pause, significantly delay, or reject a property mid-process, and what typically triggers those go/no-go decisions?

12. What formally marks the completion of the onboarding process, and how is the transition or handoff to ongoing property management and tenant relations handled? Are there meaningful variations in the process depending on property type (e.g., multi-unit, luxury, student housing, furnished) or across different cities/regions?
