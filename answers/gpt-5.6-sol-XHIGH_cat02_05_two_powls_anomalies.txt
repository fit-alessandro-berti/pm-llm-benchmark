## Normative baseline

For the successful-hire path, the expected dependencies are approximately:

`Post Job Ad  Screen Candidates  Conduct Interviews  Make Hiring Decision  Onboard Employee  Add to Payroll  Close Case`

The important point in a partial order is that the order of nodes in the `nodes=[...]` list has no execution meaning. Only precedence edges impose ordering. Unrelated nodes may execute in either order or concurrently.

## Model 1

The model establishes:

- `Post  Screen`
- `Screen  Interview`
- `Screen  Decide  Onboard  Payroll  Close`

### Correct aspects

- Posting precedes screening.
- Screening precedes both interviews and the decision.
- The decision precedes onboarding.
- Onboarding, payroll, and closure are correctly ordered.
- Every activity is mandatory and occurs exactly once.

### Anomaly

There is no precedence relation between `Interview` and `Decide`. Consequently, the model permits:

- The hiring decision before interviews.
- Interviews concurrent with the decision.
- Interviews after onboarding or payroll.
- Even `Close_Case` before `Conduct_Interviews`.

For example, this is a valid topological execution:

`Post, Screen, Decide, Onboard, Payroll, Close, Interview`

This undermines the role of interviews as input to the hiring decision and also means that `Close_Case` is not necessarily the last labeled activity.

Nevertheless, a normative execution is also permitted:

`Post, Screen, Interview, Decide, Onboard, Payroll, Close`

Model 1 can be repaired simply by adding:

```python
model1.order.add_edge(Interview, Decide)
```

Through transitivity, this also forces interviews before onboarding, payroll, and closure.

## Model 2

The main ordered chain is:

`Post  Interview  Decide  Onboarding Loop  Optional Payroll  Close`

Screening is only constrained by `Post  Screen`.

### Correct aspects

- Interviews necessarily precede the hiring decision.
- The decision precedes onboarding.
- If payroll is performed, it occurs after onboarding and before closure.
- Onboarding occurs at least once.

### Anomalies

#### 1. Screening is disconnected from the rest of the hiring chain

There is no `Screen  Interview` or `Screen  Decide` dependency. Screening can therefore occur:

- Concurrently with interviews.
- After interviews.
- After the hiring decision.
- After onboarding or payroll.
- Even after `Close_Case`.

For example, the model permits:

`Post, Interview, Decide, Onboard, Payroll, Close, Screen`

Thus the candidate can effectively be hired and the case marked closed before screening occurs.

#### 2. Onboarding may be repeated arbitrarily

With:

```python
LOOP(Onboard, skip)
```

the visible behavior is one or more executions of `Onboard_Employee`:

`Onboard`, `Onboard, Onboard`, and so on.

Some onboarding rework may be realistic, but an unrestricted loop with only a silent redo step gives no business reason, correction activity, or completion condition. It can also imply duplicated accounts, access rights, records, or onboarding tasks.

#### 3. Payroll can be omitted

The XOR:

```python
XOR(Payroll, skip)
```

makes `Add_to_Payroll` optional. The process can therefore onboard an employee and close the case without adding that employee to payroll.

For an actual employee, this is a fundamental process-integrity problem, potentially resulting in an employee who cannot be paid. A skip might be valid for a contractor or rejected candidate, but the model contains no such labeled branch—and onboarding has already occurred—so the omission is not justified by the modeled context.

## Comparison

| Requirement | Model 1 | Model 2 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Screening before interviews | Guaranteed | Not guaranteed |
| Interviews before decision | Not guaranteed | Guaranteed |
| Onboarding exactly once | Yes | No, may repeat |
| Payroll mandatory | Yes | No |
| Close necessarily after all other activities | No, interview may follow | No, screening may follow |

Both models admit the normative trace, but a good process model should also avoid admitting clearly invalid traces.

**Model 1 is closer to the normative Hire-to-Retire sequence.** Its anomaly is serious because a decision can be made before interviews, but it preserves the completeness and cardinality of the successful-hire path: all essential activities occur exactly once, onboarding follows the decision, and payroll is mandatory before closure. It essentially needs one missing precedence relation.

Model 2 correctly orders interviews before the decision, but it has several additional structural problems: screening can happen too late, onboarding can repeat indefinitely, and payroll can be skipped entirely. In particular, closing an onboarded employee’s case without payroll more fundamentally compromises the integrity of the employment process.

If unsuccessful candidates are also intended to be represented, both models would additionally need an explicit choice after the hiring decision, such as an acceptance branch leading to onboarding and payroll and a rejection branch leading to closure.
