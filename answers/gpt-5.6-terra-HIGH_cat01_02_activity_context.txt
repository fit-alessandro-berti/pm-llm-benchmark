## 1. Recurring activities and contextual interpretations

The activities that recur within individual cases are:

| Case | Original activity | Occurrences | Contextual distinction |
|---|---|---:|---|
| C001 | Verify Documents | 2 | First occurrence is an initial check that finds missing documentation; second is a re-verification after the customer uploads the requested ID scan. |
| C002 | Call Customer | 2 | First call is to clarify an account discrepancy; second is a follow-up confirmation after an account adjustment. |
| C003 | Verify Documents | 2 | First occurrence is an initial in-person ID check; second is a re-verification after back-office account corrections. |

Although `Call Customer` occurs only once in C001 and C003, its purpose also differs across cases. Renaming those calls makes the log more meaningful: one requests missing documents, while the other asks the customer to return to the branch.

## 4. Justification for the naming choices

- **Initial vs. repeat verification:**  
  `Verify Documents` was split into initial verification and re-verification activities. The later verification events are clearly triggered by an intervening corrective action:
  - C001: customer uploads a missing ID scan.
  - C003: back office corrects missing account information.

- **Different purposes of customer calls:**  
  `Call Customer` was renamed according to why the call occurred:
  - C001: request missing documentation.
  - C002 first call: clarify an account discrepancy.
  - C002 second call: confirm that the adjustment was completed.
  - C003: request that the customer return to the branch.

- **Account adjustments made more explicit:**  
  `Adjust Account` is contextually refined because C002 concerns adjusting details after discrepancy clarification, whereas C003 concerns correcting missing information.

- **Initiation channel retained:**  
  Request initiation is labeled differently for online and branch-originated cases because the resource and context indicate distinct intake channels.

These revised labels preserve the original events while making repetitions, rework loops, follow-up interactions, and channel differences explicit for process analysis.
