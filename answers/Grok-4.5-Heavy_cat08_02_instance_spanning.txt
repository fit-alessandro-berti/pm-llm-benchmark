**1. Identifying Instance-Spanning Constraints and Their Impact**

Process mining on the multi-attribute event log (Case ID, Activity, Timestamp with START/COMPLETE, Resource, Order Type, Destination Region, Requires Cold Packing, Hazardous Material) begins with standard discovery and performance analysis, then extends to inter-case techniques because the constraints couple different order instances.

Key techniques include:
- Resource occupancy timeline reconstruction and queue mining.
- Object-centric process mining (OCPM / OCEL) treating stations, batches, and regions as interacting objects alongside order cases.
- Temporal concurrency / interval-overlap analysis.
- Log enrichment with derived attributes (e.g., concurrent count at ready time, resource busy flag, batch progress).
- Multi-perspective and multi-dimensional analysis (process cubes sliced by combinations of attributes).

**Shared Cold-Packing stations (limited capacity, e.g., 5):**  
Filter cold-required cases. Reconstruct occupancy of stations whose Resource IDs indicate cold capability. For each such order compute ready-for-packing time (prior COMPLETE) versus actual Packing START. Detect saturation periods (exactly 5 concurrent cold packs).  
Metrics: average / median / 95th-percentile cold-packing waiting time; percentage of cold orders waiting above a threshold; cold-station utilization and peak concurrency; contribution of cold-wait to overall cycle time and SLA misses (via performance annotation or regression); cold-station throughput (orders per hour). High utilization periods correlated with elevated end-to-end times quantify impact.

**Shipping batching:**  
Group by Destination Region (and, when present, explicit Batch ID such as “Batch B1”). Measure the interval from Quality Check COMPLETE (or Packing COMPLETE) to Shipping Label Generation COMPLETE. Cluster label-generation timestamps that co-occur.  
Metrics: average / distribution of batch waiting time overall and by region; batch-size distribution and its correlation with waiting time for early-finishing orders; percentage of orders whose only significant delay is batch-induced; contribution of batch wait to missed delivery targets; holding / idle time of completed orders.

**Priority (Express) handling:**  
Detect preemption and queue-jumping by examining resource timelines: an Express order acquiring a resource while a Standard order is active or queued, or abrupt switches in resource assignment. Compare sojourn times of Standard activities in the presence versus absence of concurrent Express demand.  
Metrics: frequency of interruptions / preemptions; additional delay inflicted on Standard orders (difference in waiting or activity duration); average priority-induced delay; Express versus Standard cycle-time differential attributable to resource contention; percentage of Express orders that seize contested resources.

**Hazardous-material concurrency limit (≤ 10 simultaneous Packing or Quality Check):**  
Extract all intervals [START, COMPLETE] for Hazardous = TRUE activities in Packing or Quality Check. Apply a sweep-line or interval-tree algorithm to produce a time series of concurrent active hazmat orders.  
Metrics: observed maximum concurrency; percentage of time spent at or near the limit (e.g., 8–10); number and duration of any violations or near-limit episodes; waiting time of hazmat orders that become ready while the counter is already high (even if physical stations are free); throughput reduction relative to an unconstrained baseline; correlation of high-concurrency periods with broader backlog growth.

**Differentiating within-instance versus between-instance waiting:**  
For every inter-activity gap (prior COMPLETE → next START) enrich the log by querying the reconstructed resource state at the exact ready timestamp:  
- If a suitable resource is free yet the case still waits → primarily within-instance (case-specific factors, setup, data readiness, staffing gaps outside the constrained pool).  
- If all suitable resources are occupied by other cases → between-instance contention.  
- Batch waiting is between-instance when the case is ready and resources free but other region mates are incomplete.  
- Priority-induced delay and hazmat-limit waiting are likewise between-instance.  

Further statistical decomposition (regression, attribution models, or techniques from the waiting-time cause literature) quantifies the relative contribution of each. Visualizations such as dotted charts colored by wait type, occupancy heat-maps, and process maps with arcs annotated by percentage between-instance wait make the distinction actionable. This analysis ranks the constraints by impact (e.g., cold-packing contention may dominate peak-season delays) and supplies the baseline metrics against which later improvements are measured.

**2. Analyzing Constraint Interactions**

Instance-spanning constraints rarely operate in isolation; their couplings amplify delays and create trade-offs that isolated analysis misses. Multi-attribute filtering, OCPM, and correlation of the constraint-specific metrics above reveal the interactions.

- Priority × Cold-Packing: An Express cold order can preempt or jump the queue at the five stations, inflating waiting time for Standard cold orders. The delayed Standard orders may then miss their shipping batches, cascading the effect. Quantified by comparing Standard cold-wait distributions in high-Express versus low-Express periods and by measuring excess delay attributable to priority.

- Batching × Hazardous limits: Multiple hazmat orders destined for the same region force the batch to wait while the global concurrency counter (≤ 10) serializes their Packing/Quality Check. This stretches batch completion time or risks temporary over-limit pressure. Detected by region-grouped concurrency counts and correlation of batch wait with the number of hazmat members.

- Priority × Batching: Express orders desynchronize readiness times within a region or force early release of incomplete batches (losing route efficiency) or, conversely, are themselves delayed if forced to wait for the batch. Measured by variance of ready-for-shipping times within regions and frequency of express-triggered batch releases.

- Cold-Packing × Hazardous (and higher-order combinations): Orders that are both cold and hazmat consume scarce stations while also counting against the concurrency limit. An Express cold-hazmat order receives highest priority and can starve other combinations. Peak-season coincidence of high volumes across attributes multiplies contention at every shared point.

- Broader amplification: Priority interruptions create context-switch overhead; batch holding occupies space and creates downstream pressure; any resource saturation raises the probability that the hazmat counter or cold stations become binding.

Understanding these interactions is essential because optimizing one constraint in isolation often degrades others. Aggressive Express prioritization may improve Express KPIs while lengthening Standard cycle times and batch delays, ultimately harming overall on-time delivery. Conversely, rigid batching can exacerbate hazmat concurrency pressure. Holistic multi-objective analysis and joint simulation of the coupled system are therefore required; siloed fixes produce unintended side-effects. Process cubes, correlation matrices of the constraint-specific wait metrics, and OCPM views that surface object interactions (stations ↔ batches ↔ orders) supply the necessary visibility.

**3. Developing Constraint-Aware Optimization Strategies**

Three complementary, data-driven strategies address the constraints and their interactions. All are parameterized from the process-mining results of Section 1 (contention patterns, demand distributions by attribute and time, historical wait contributions).

**Strategy 1 – Predictive, priority-aware dynamic allocation of cold stations and hazmat slots**  
Primarily targets shared cold-packing, priority handling, and hazmat limits (and their interactions).  
A real-time layer continuously tracks cold-station occupancy and the hazmat concurrency counter. Waiting orders receive a dynamic score combining priority class, predicted remaining sojourn (from historical duration models), downstream batch impact, and SLA urgency. Express orders receive a high base score; Standard orders receive aging to prevent starvation. One or two cold stations may be preferentially reserved for Express during predicted high-demand windows; hazmat packing/Quality Check slots are allocated via a token/semaphore system capped at 10. Soft rather than hard preemption is preferred when a Standard packing step is nearly complete.  
Data leverage: predictive models trained on the event log (time-of-day, day-of-week, open-order mix by cold/hazmat/region/Express); historical contention periods and preemption frequencies set thresholds. OCPM quantifies past interaction severity.  
Expected outcomes: lower cold-packing waits (especially for Express), reduced disruption to Standard orders through smarter rather than pure preemptive prioritization, strict hazmat compliance with higher packing throughput, and smoother feeding of orders into downstream batches. Between-instance contention measured in Section 1 declines measurably.

**Strategy 2 – Adaptive, constraint-aware batching with dynamic triggers and priority overrides**  
Primarily targets shipping batching and its interactions with priority, hazmat, and (indirectly) cold-packing.  
Static batch rules are replaced by dynamic release logic per region: release when size reaches an optimized N, or the oldest ready order has waited a maximum T minutes, or an Express order is ready (allowing Express-only or forced-include smaller batches), or forecasted future arrivals are low. When forming a batch the current and projected hazmat concurrency is checked; hazmat-heavy batches may be split or deprioritized. Express orders can use dedicated expedited shipping lanes that bypass full batching when volume justifies. Region- and time-of-day-specific N/T parameters are tuned offline.  
Data leverage: historical batch-wait distributions by region, correlation of batch size with both route efficiency and order delay cost, peak patterns, and frequency of Express-forced releases. Optimal N and T are obtained by cost-minimizing what-if analysis or simulation on the log.  
Expected outcomes: substantial reduction in post-completion batch waiting (especially in low-volume windows or for Express), better balance between routing efficiency and speed, lower risk of hazmat pile-ups delaying entire regions, and reduced cycle-time variance. The interaction whereby upstream resource delays are amplified by batch holding is mitigated.

**Strategy 3 – Intelligent multi-objective scheduling, capacity buffering, and limited process redesign**  
Addresses all four constraints by reducing the frequency and severity of interdependencies.  
A centralized scheduler (integrated with the WMS) sequences orders into picking and packing using a multi-objective function that considers current cold-station availability, remaining hazmat slots, priority, destination-batch potential, and predicted total sojourn. Aging and constraint-programming techniques prevent starvation. Capacity analysis of the log (utilization heat-maps) justifies adding one or two flexible cold stations or convertible stations if peak utilization is chronically high. Minor redesign options include soft preemption, temporary staging areas with better visibility, or a dedicated Express cold path if volume warrants; for non-hazmat orders a light Quality Check may be moved earlier to free concurrent slots.  
Data leverage: full bottleneck and resource-performance analysis, multi-dimensional discovery of frequent interacting patterns (high cold + high hazmat + many Express), and offline simulation of alternative capacities and policies on the three-month log.  
Expected outcomes: fewer reactive interruptions, higher sustainable throughput under the regulatory and resource limits, lower end-to-end times through proactive avoidance of contention, and greater resilience in peak seasons. Between-instance waits decline across all measured categories while compliance is preserved.

The three strategies are complementary; they can be introduced incrementally and are explicitly designed so that improvements in one dimension do not inadvertently worsen the interactions identified in Section 2.

**4. Simulation and Validation**

Before live deployment, discrete-event simulation (DES) models—implemented in tools such as SimPy, Arena, AnyLogic, or process-mining-aware simulators—are calibrated directly from the event log. Inter-arrival processes (by Order Type, cold/hazmat flags, region, and time-of-day/season), activity duration distributions (conditioned on resource and order attributes), and current policy parameters are fitted statistically.  

The model explicitly encodes the instance-spanning constraints:
- Cold stations as a multi-server queue of capacity 5 with priority disciplines.
- A global semaphore / token pool of size 10 for hazmat Packing and Quality Check activities (orders wait when the counter is exhausted even if physical stations are free).
- Per-region batch collectors that accumulate ready orders and release according to configurable size/time/Express rules.
- Priority queues supporting both preemptive and soft-preemption logic, plus aging.

Baseline “as-is” runs reproduce the observed metrics of Section 1 (within statistical tolerance). Each proposed strategy—and selected combinations—is then encoded as alternative control logic. Scenarios include normal load, peak-season multipliers derived from the log, and stress tests. Key performance indicators tracked under the enforced constraints include: cycle-time distributions (overall and by order type), throughput, on-time delivery rate, resource utilizations, queue-length time series, decomposition of waiting time into within- versus between-instance components, batch-efficiency metrics, and hazmat-compliance rate (must remain 100 %).  

Sensitivity analysis and multi-objective Pareto examination reveal trade-offs (e.g., Express gains versus Standard degradation). Historical log replay under the new policies provides an additional validation check. Only parameterizations that improve the target KPIs while respecting all hard constraints (hazmat ≤ 10, physical station capacities) advance to pilot implementation. This ensures the strategies are robust to the very interdependencies that pure case-centric analysis would miss.

**5. Monitoring Post-Implementation**

Continuous process-mining dashboards, fed by the live event stream, track the same metrics defined in Section 1 together with leading indicators of constraint health:
- Cold-station occupancy and queue-length time series; waiting-time distributions for cold orders, segmented by Express/Standard.
- Batch-waiting histograms and average batch size by region; percentage of orders whose dominant delay is batch-induced.
- Preemption / priority-induced delay counts and the resulting extra wait imposed on Standard orders.
- Real-time concurrent hazmat gauge (with color-coded thresholds approaching 10) and any near-limit or violation alerts.
- Overall and segmented cycle times, throughput, on-time delivery, and the percentage of total waiting time that remains between-instance.
- Resource utilization heat-maps and residual bottleneck scores.

Alerts trigger when cold queues exceed calibrated thresholds, the hazmat counter approaches the limit, batch waits lengthen, or between-instance wait share rises. Periodic re-discovery and concept-drift detection (comparing recent logs with the original three-month baseline) verify that the process has not shifted into new pathological patterns. Side-by-side comparison of pre- and post-implementation metric distributions quantifies realized gains and surfaces any unintended interaction effects early, enabling rapid policy retuning.  

Collectively these monitoring views confirm that the instance-spanning constraints are being managed more effectively—shorter contention queues, faster yet still efficient batch formation, preserved regulatory compliance, and a measurable shift of waiting time from between-instance to residual within-instance causes—while overall fulfillment performance improves.
