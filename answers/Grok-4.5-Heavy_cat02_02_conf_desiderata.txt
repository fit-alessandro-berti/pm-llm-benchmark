We reached full consensus on the evaluation of the event log against the given normative rules and desiderata.

### 1. Cases Violating the Normative (Strict) Rules

**Only Case 2 violates the normative rules.** It contains multiple serious, explicit breaches:

- **Order violation**: Risk Assessment occurs at 08:20, *before* Credit Check at 09:00. This directly breaks the required sequence “Credit Check must occur before Risk Assessment.”
- **Missing required activity**: There is no “Inform Applicant” activity after the Loan Decision. The process description states that the applicant *must* be informed of the outcome following the decision, and the mandated sequence places Inform Applicant after Loan Decision (and before the approval/rejection branch).
- **Forbidden action on rejection path**: After a Loan Decision (Rejected), the case performs Finalize Documentation at 10:00 and then Close Case. This is explicitly disallowed: “If the loan is rejected, the case should be closed without finalizing documentation” and “Performing Finalize Documentation after a rejection” is listed among the things that are not allowed.

**Case 1** is fully compliant with the strict normative sequence and decision logic:  
Application Received → Credit Check → Risk Assessment → Loan Decision (Approved) → Inform Applicant → Finalize Documentation → Close Case.

**Case 3** is also fully compliant with the strict normative sequence and decision logic:  
Application Received → Credit Check → Risk Assessment → Loan Decision (Approved) → Inform Applicant → Finalize Documentation → Close Case.  
All ordering constraints and the approval-branch requirement (Finalize before Close) are respected.

### 2. Cases Failing to Meet the Desiderata (Recommended but Not Strict)

These are suboptimal practices, **not** serious violations.

- **Case 1**:  
  – Timings are excellent (Credit Check ~30 min after application; Inform Applicant ~2 h after decision).  
  – Missing recommended Follow-up Call before Close Case.  
  → Minor deviation only.

- **Case 2**:  
  – Credit Check timing is short, but the process is already broken by normative violations, so timing assessment is secondary.  
  – Missing recommended Follow-up Call.  
  – (Inform Applicant is absent, so the “prompt information” desideratum cannot be evaluated.)

- **Case 3**: Clear desiderata shortfalls:  
  – Credit Check performed ~49 hours after Application Received (exceeds the preferred ≤24-hour window).  
  – Inform Applicant performed ~51 hours after Loan Decision (exceeds the preferred ≤48-hour window).  
  – Missing recommended Follow-up Call.  
  → These represent suboptimal behavior (delays and omission of a recommended step) but do **not** constitute serious process violations.

### 3. Possible Interpretations / Justifications

- **Case 1**: Ideal execution of the core process. The absence of a Follow-up Call may simply reflect that it is optional and was judged unnecessary for a straightforward, rapid approval, or it may be a minor operational omission.

- **Case 2**: Difficult to justify under the given constraints. The reversed order (Risk before Credit), complete omission of the mandatory Inform Applicant step, and performance of Finalize Documentation on a rejected case point to process errors, staff mistakes, possible system/configuration issues that allowed out-of-order execution, or logging incompleteness. These are clear non-conformances rather than defensible variations.

- **Case 3**: The timing delays are more readily interpretable. Application was received on Friday 12 January 2024 at 09:00; Credit Check occurred on the following Monday (14 January) at 10:00. The intervening weekend naturally accounts for a large portion of the ~49-hour gap. The subsequent ~51-hour delay in informing the applicant could stem from workload, case complexity, or resource constraints. The missing Follow-up Call is again only a recommended step. Importantly, the core normative sequence and decision logic remained intact, so process integrity on the hard rules was preserved.

**Summary**  
- Normative (must-follow) violations: **Case 2 only**.  
- Desiderata (nice-to-have) shortfalls: primarily **Case 3** (timings + no follow-up), plus missing Follow-up Call in Cases 1 and 2.  
- Case 1 is the closest to ideal practice; Case 3 shows acceptable core conformance with room for improvement on recommended timings and the optional follow-up; Case 2 exhibits clear non-compliance that should be investigated.
