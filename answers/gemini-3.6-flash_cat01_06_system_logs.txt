### Logic & Explanation

#### 1. Case Identification Strategy (Object-Centric Grouping)
In desktop activity logs, raw actions often interleave multiple ongoing tasks. To construct meaningful cases suitable for process analysis, an **Artifact/Entity-Centric** approach was applied:
- **`CASE_DOC1`**: Tracks the full lifecycle of drafting, referencing, saving, and closing `Document1.docx`. Notice how the user paused this case to check emails, review PDFs, and update budget figures, then returned to finish `Document1.docx`.
- **`CASE_EMAIL_ANNUAL_MEETING`**: Groups reading, replying to, and sending a specific email handling cycle.
- **`CASE_REPORT_DRAFT`**: Tracks the document review and annotation sequence in Adobe Acrobat (`Report_Draft.pdf`).
- **`CASE_BUDGET_2024`**: Represents the financial update process within Microsoft Excel (`Budget_2024.xlsx`).
- **`CASE_QUARTERLY_REPORT`**: Tracks the initial opening at `08:59:50` and subsequent editing/closing sequence for `Quarterly_Report.docx`.

#### 2. Activity Naming & Abstraction Logic
Low-level user interface actions (`FOCUS`, `TYPING`, `SCROLL`, `CLICK`) were abstracted into high-level business process activities:
- **`TYPING`**  Translated to domain-specific edit actions such as **`Edit Document Content`**, **`Compose Email Reply`**, or **`Edit Spreadsheet Data`** depending on application context.
- **`SCROLL`**  Expressed as **`Review Email Content`** or **`Review PDF Content`**, as scrolling represents reading/consumption.
- **`FOCUS` / `SWITCH`**  Context switches that initiate or resume work were standardized to **`Open Document`**, **`Resume Document Editing`**, or **`Switch Application Context`**.
- **`SAVE` / `CLOSE` / `CLICK Send`**  Directly mapped to explicit lifecycle milestones: **`Save Document`**, **`Close Document`**, and **`Send Email`**.

#### 3. Suitability for Process Mining Tools
By mapping the raw data into standard attributes (`Case ID`, `Timestamp`, `Activity Name`), this log can immediately be ingested by tools like **Celonis, Disco, or ProM** to analyze:
- Process variants (e.g., direct completion vs. context switching).
- Throughput time per document lifecycle.
- User attention fragmentation and multitasking behavior.
