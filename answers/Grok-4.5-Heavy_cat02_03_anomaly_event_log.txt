**Standard Procure-to-Pay (P2P) sequence and key controls**

A typical, controlled P2P process follows this logical order:

1. Create Purchase Requisition  
2. Approve Purchase Requisition  
3. Issue Purchase Order  
4. Receive Goods  
5. Vendor Invoice Received  
6. Match Invoice to PO (ideally a three-way match: PO + Goods Receipt + Invoice)  
7. Release Payment  
8. Close Case  

Critical internal controls include:  
- No Purchase Order without prior requisition approval (authorization).  
- No goods receipt without a formal PO.  
- No payment without a matched invoice *and* confirmation of goods receipt (prevents paying for undelivered or incorrect goods).  
- Matching occurs *before* payment release.

**Case 1001**  
Sequence: Create PR → Approve PR → Issue PO → Receive Goods → Vendor Invoice Received → Match Invoice to PO → Release Payment → Close Case.  

This follows the standard flow exactly. No anomalies.

**Case 1002**  
Sequence: Create PR → Issue PO → Vendor Invoice Received → Release Payment → Approve PR → Receive Goods → Match Invoice to PO → Close Case.  

Anomalies:  
- Purchase Order issued immediately after creating the requisition and *before* any approval.  
- Invoice received and payment released *before* the requisition was approved, *before* goods were received, and *before* any matching.  
- Approval only occurs after payment has already been made.  

Why problematic: This completely bypasses authorization and verification controls. The organization committed to a purchase and paid without proper approval, without confirming goods were received, and without matching. It creates high risk of unauthorized spending, fraud, paying for non-existent or incorrect goods, and a broken audit trail. The later matching step is largely meaningless once money has already left the organization.

**Case 1003**  
Sequence: Create PR → Approve PR → Issue PO → Release Payment → Vendor Invoice Received → Receive Goods → Match Invoice to PO → Close Case.  

Anomaly:  
- Payment is released right after the PO and *before* the invoice is received and *before* goods are received. Matching occurs last.  

Why problematic: Payment occurs without an invoice and without proof of delivery. This violates the fundamental control that payment should only be authorized after three-way matching. Risks include paying for undelivered goods, incorrect amounts, or fictitious invoices, with limited ability to recover funds after the fact.

**Case 1004**  
Sequence: Create PR → Approve PR → Receive Goods → Issue PO → Vendor Invoice Received → Match Invoice to PO → Release Payment → Close Case.  

Anomaly:  
- Goods are received *before* a Purchase Order is issued.  

Why problematic: Receiving goods without a formal PO means there is no prior authorization or agreed terms against which to receive. This breaks procurement controls, complicates inventory and matching processes, creates a weak audit trail, and is consistent with maverick (unauthorized) buying. Subsequent matching and payment controls are harder to enforce properly.

**Summary**  
Cases 1002, 1003, and 1004 all violate core P2P controls around authorization, segregation of duties, and verification before payment. Case 1002 is the most severe (multiple control failures culminating in payment without approval or receipt). These sequence violations create financial risk, increase the chance of fraud or error, and undermine the purpose of the process controls.
