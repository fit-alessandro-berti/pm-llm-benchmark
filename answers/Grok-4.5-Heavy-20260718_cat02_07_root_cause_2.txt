**Cases with significantly longer durations**

Exact case lead times (Submit Claim → Close Claim):

- **2001** (Low, Region A): **1.50 hours**
- **2002** (Medium, Region B): **25.92 hours** (~26 h)
- **2003** (High, Region A): **48.33 hours** (~48 h)
- **2004** (Low, Region B): **1.42 hours**

The clear performance outliers are **2005**, **2003**, and (to a lesser extent) **2002**. The two Low-complexity cases complete in under 1.5 hours; the Medium and High cases take 17–50× longer.

**Root-cause analysis (Resource, Region, Complexity)**

**Primary driver: Complexity level (via repeated “Request Additional Documents” activities)**

| Complexity | Cases     | # of Request Additional Documents | Avg. duration |
|------------|-----------|------------------------------------|---------------|
| Low        | 2001, 2004| 0                                  | ~1.46 h      |
| Medium     | 2002      | 1                                  | ~26 h        |
| High       | 2003, 2005| 2–3                                | ~62.7 h      |

High-complexity claims generate multiple sequential “Request Additional Documents” events. Each iteration introduces substantial waiting time (commonly 19–29 hours between requests or from the last request to Approve Claim). These cumulative waits dominate overall case duration. Medium complexity shows the same pattern at lower intensity (one request). Low-complexity claims never enter this loop and finish the same day.

**Secondary observations (limited by n=5)**

- **Region**: Both Region A and Region B produce fast Low cases and slow High cases. Region B’s High case (2005) is the longest, but the difference is largely explained by the higher number of document-request cycles rather than region alone.
- **Resource**:
  - Adjuster_Mike (Region A High) and Adjuster_Lisa (Region B Medium + High) perform the iterative document requests.
  - Manager_Bill approves both High-complexity cases; Manager_Ann handles the Low- and Medium-complexity cases (which reach approval faster).
  - CSR and Finance resources show no material impact on overall lead time (Pay Claim and Close Claim remain quick once approval occurs).

With only five cases, individual resource or pure regional effects cannot be isolated with confidence; they appear secondary to the complexity-driven request loops.

**Explanations**

Complex claims inherently require more verification and supporting evidence. The observed process is reactive and sequential: the initial evaluation frequently proves incomplete, triggering repeated document requests. Each request adds external waiting time (customer response) plus internal follow-up time. The absence of strong upfront completeness checks or specialized handling for High-complexity claims allows these loops to accumulate into multi-day delays. Resource assignment patterns (certain adjusters and Manager_Bill on the High path) may amplify or simply reflect the extra work, but the data volume is too small to attribute causality strongly to specific people.

**Suggested mitigations**

1. **Front-end prevention (highest leverage)**  
   Strengthen claim-intake forms, required-document checklists, and guidance specifically for Medium/High complexity so that the first evaluation is more complete and fewer (or zero) additional-document cycles occur.

2. **Process design for complex claims**  
   - Create a dedicated or prioritized path for High-complexity claims (specialized adjusters, parallel document collection, or expedited queues).  
   - Introduce SLAs and automated reminders/follow-ups on outstanding document requests.  
   - Consider AI-assisted completeness checks at the Evaluate Claim step.

3. **Resource & monitoring actions**  
   - Review the High-complexity approval path (Manager_Bill) and adjuster follow-up practices.  
   - Track number of document-request cycles and inter-request wait times as key performance indicators.  
   - Gather more event-log data (or customer-response timestamps) to confirm secondary Region/Resource effects and measure improvement.

**Bottom line**  
The dominant root cause of long case durations is **claim complexity driving iterative “Request Additional Documents” loops** that introduce multi-hour to multi-day waits. Region and specific resources show secondary or inconclusive associations given the small sample. Focusing improvement effort on better initial information capture and specialized handling for High-complexity claims offers the clearest path to shorter lead times.
