* **Rationale:** These events represent the initial, logical phase of the manufacturing process where the raw material is acquired, identified, positioned, and physically prepared for the primary operation. They are temporally close and transition the product from a raw state to a state ready for assembly. 

**2. Welding Assembly**
* **Included Low-Level Events:** Pick up welding tool, Weld corner A, Weld corner B.
* **Rationale:** These events form the core physical assembly or modification of the product. The operator retrieves the necessary tool and performs the structural joining of the material. This is a distinct phase of active manufacturing transformation.

**3. In-Process Quality Inspection**
* **Included Low-Level Events:** Measure weld integrity.
* **Rationale:** Immediately following the welding operation, an automated quality check is performed. Grouping this separately highlights the quality gate in the process, ensuring that the assembly meets structural standards before any further processing (like coating) is applied. 

**4. Surface Finishing**
* **Included Low-Level Events:** Apply protective coating, Dry coating.
* **Rationale:** These steps logically follow each other as a distinct post-assembly phase aimed at protecting the newly assembled product. Applying the coating and then drying it via a heating unit are tightly coupled events that complete the physical manufacturing of the item.

**5. Final Quality Assurance**

* **Rationale:** This is the final gate before the product completes its lifecycle in this log. It is a distinct manual review phase performed by an operator to ensure the overall product—both the weld and the coating—meets final visual and quality standards.
