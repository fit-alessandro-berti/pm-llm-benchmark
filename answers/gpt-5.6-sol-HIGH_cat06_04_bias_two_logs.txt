## Finding

**Group B’s log exhibits the clearest biased treatment mechanism**, and the resulting system-level bias favors Group B while potentially disadvantaging protected Group A.

### How the bias manifests

- Every Group A applicant is marked `LocalResident = FALSE`, has no `CommunityGroup`, and receives **no positive score adjustment**.
- Group B applicants are all local residents. Those belonging to the **Highland Civic Darts Club** receive a **+10 “Community Boost.”**
- This boost is not based on the recorded preliminary score and is unavailable in the observed Group A cases.

| Case | Base score | Community status | Adjustment | Decision |
|---|---:|---|---:|---|
| P001 | 720 | None | 0 | Approved |
| P002 | 710 | None | 0 | Rejected |
| P003 | 740 | None | 0 | Approved |
| U001 | 720 | Darts Club | +10 | Approved |
| U002 | 710 | None | 0 | Rejected |
| U003 | 695 | Darts Club | +10 | Approved |

The most concerning comparison is **U003**: it starts with the lowest score, 695, receives the club boost, and is approved at an adjusted score of 705, while both P002 and U002 are rejected at 710. This suggests that club membership may affect the outcome beyond—or in addition to—the numerical adjustment.

### Role of the attributes

- **LocalResident:** There is no explicit local-resident adjustment shown for U002, so residency alone is not proven to cause favorable treatment. However, all Group B applicants are local and all Group A applicants are nonlocal, making residency completely correlated with group membership in this dataset.
- **CommunityGroup:** Club membership directly triggers the +10 adjustment and is found only in Group B. It therefore acts as a potentially preferential criterion or proxy.
- **ScoreAdjustment:** Positive adjustments occur only for Group B club members. Group A applicants have no comparable way to receive a boost, producing unequal scoring opportunities.

### Outcome and limitation

The raw approval rates are identical—**2 of 3 in each group**—so this small sample does not show aggregate disparate impact. Nevertheless, it shows **procedural or treatment bias**: Group B club members receive a benefit unavailable to Group A, and both club members are approved.

Because the groups differ simultaneously in protected status, residency, and community membership, and because there are only six cases, the logs cannot establish causality conclusively. A larger matched analysis controlling for base score and other legitimate risk factors would be needed. Still, the community boost is a clear fairness concern unless it has a valid, consistently available, non-proxy justification.
