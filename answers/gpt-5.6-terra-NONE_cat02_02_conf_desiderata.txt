## 1. Normative-rule violations

### Case 1 — Normatively compliant
Case 1 follows the required path exactly:

1. Application Received  
2. Credit Check  
3. Risk Assessment  
4. Loan Decision: Approved  
5. Inform Applicant  
6. Finalize Documentation  
7. Close Case  

Because the decision is **approved**, documentation is finalized before the case is closed. No mandatory step is skipped or reordered.

---

### Case 2 — Normatively non-compliant

Case 2 contains multiple hard-rule violations:

1. **Risk Assessment occurs before Credit Check**
   - Recorded order:
     - Application Received: 08:00
     - Risk Assessment: 08:20
     - Credit Check: 09:00
   - This violates the mandatory ordering rule that **Credit Check must precede Risk Assessment**.

2. **Loan Decision is made without a validly ordered prior Risk Assessment**
   - Although both activities appear in the log, the risk assessment was performed before the credit check.
   - Therefore, the decision is based on a sequence that is already invalid under the stated process model.

3. **Documentation is finalized after a rejection**
   - Loan Decision: **Rejected** at 09:30.
   - Finalize Documentation occurs at 10:00.
   - For rejected applications, documentation must **not** be finalized; the case should proceed to closure after informing the applicant.

4. **Inform Applicant is missing**
   - The normative sequence requires the applicant to be informed after the loan decision.
   - The log goes from a rejected decision directly to documentation finalization and closure, with no “Inform Applicant” activity recorded.
   - Unless that event occurred but was omitted from the log, this is a further mandatory-sequence violation.

**Conclusion:** Case 2 is a clear normative violation and should be treated as non-compliant.

---

### Case 3 — Normatively compliant
Case 3 follows the mandatory activity order:

1. Application Received  
2. Credit Check  
3. Risk Assessment  
4. Loan Decision: Approved  
5. Inform Applicant  
6. Finalize Documentation  
7. Close Case  

The approved-case branch is correctly followed: documentation is finalized before the case is closed. Therefore, despite delays and the missing follow-up call, Case 3 does **not** violate the strict normative rules.

## 2. Desiderata failures

These are recommendations rather than hard compliance obligations. They indicate suboptimal performance, but not severe process violations.

### Case 1 — No observed desiderata failures
- Credit Check occurs 30 minutes after application receipt, well within the suggested 24-hour window.
- Inform Applicant occurs two hours after the decision, well within the suggested 48-hour window.
- No Follow-up Call is recorded. If the follow-up call is expected as a recommended practice, this is a minor desiderata deviation; however, it is not a normative violation.

---

### Case 2 — Timing cannot be meaningfully assessed; follow-up is absent
- Credit Check is performed within 24 hours of receipt, so the timing recommendation is satisfied.
- There is no recorded “Inform Applicant” event, so the recommendation to inform the applicant within 48 hours cannot be confirmed.
  - More importantly, the missing notification is a **normative** issue, not merely a desiderata issue.
- No Follow-up Call is recorded, which is a minor recommended-practice deviation only.

Because Case 2 is already normatively invalid, its best-practice performance is secondary to correcting the mandatory process failures.

---

### Case 3 — Desiderata failures, but no severe compliance breach

1. **Late Credit Check**
   - Application received: 2024-01-12 09:00  
   - Credit Check: 2024-01-14 10:00  
   - Elapsed time: approximately 49 hours.
   - This exceeds the recommended 24-hour window.

2. **Late applicant notification**
   - Loan Decision: 2024-01-14 12:00  
   - Inform Applicant: 2024-01-16 15:00  
   - Elapsed time: approximately 51 hours.
   - This exceeds the recommended 48-hour window.

3. **No Follow-up Call**
   - The log explicitly notes that no follow-up call occurred.
   - Since the call is encouraged but not required, this is a minor desiderata deviation.

**Conclusion:** Case 3 is structurally compliant but operationally suboptimal because of delayed handling and lack of follow-up.

## 3. Possible interpretations or justifications

### Case 1
Case 1 represents the intended process behavior. The rapid credit check and prompt notification suggest efficient handling. If no follow-up call occurred, it may simply reflect that the team considered the approved application straightforward or that the applicant did not require further contact.

### Case 2
Possible explanations may exist, but they would not make the recorded sequence compliant without additional evidence:

- The risk assessment at 08:20 may have been a preliminary screening rather than the formal mandatory risk assessment. If so, it should be recorded under a different activity label.
- The credit check could have been performed earlier but recorded late. This would be a data-quality or event-logging issue that should be investigated.
- The documentation-finalization event may have been misclassified—for example, it may refer to archiving rejection materials rather than finalizing approval documentation. If that is the case, activity definitions should be clarified.
- “Inform Applicant” may have occurred but not been captured in the log. If confirmed, the event log should be corrected.

Even with such explanations, the organization should ensure that formal system records clearly distinguish preliminary work, mandatory checks, rejection administration, and applicant notification.

### Case 3
The delays may be understandable without violating the mandatory model:

- The credit check may have been delayed because of a weekend, public holiday, unavailable external credit bureau, incomplete applicant information, or manual review requirements.
- The delayed notification may have resulted from internal approval queues, attempts to contact the applicant, or communication-channel issues.
- A follow-up call may have been omitted because the applicant was already informed through another sufficient channel, such as email or a customer portal.

These explanations can justify why the recommendations were not met, but the case should still be viewed as an opportunity to improve responsiveness and customer communication.
