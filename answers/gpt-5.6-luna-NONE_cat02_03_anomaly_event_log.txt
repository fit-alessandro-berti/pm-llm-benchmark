### Expected P2P sequence

A typical controlled P2P flow is:

**Create requisition  Approve requisition  Issue purchase order  Receive goods  Receive invoice  Match invoice to PO/receipt  Release payment  Close case**

Some organizations may allow limited variations, but approval and invoice matching are generally important controls before payment.

### Case 1001 — Normal sequence

Case 1001 follows the expected sequence:

- Requisition created and approved
- Purchase order issued
- Goods received
- Invoice received
- Invoice matched
- Payment released
- Case closed

No obvious anomaly is present.

### Case 1002 — Several control and sequencing anomalies

The sequence is:

**Create requisition  Issue PO  Invoice received  Payment released  Requisition approved  Goods received  Invoice matched  Close**

Potential issues:

1. **Purchase order issued before requisition approval**  
   The PO is created at 08:40, while approval occurs later at 08:00 on the following day. This bypasses or weakens the authorization control. An unauthorized purchase could be committed before management approval.

2. **Payment released before invoice matching**  
   Payment occurs at 07:15 on February 3, but matching does not occur until February 5. This violates the usual three-way-match principle involving the PO, goods receipt, and invoice. It increases the risk of paying incorrect, duplicate, inflated, or fraudulent invoices.

3. **Payment released before goods receipt**  
   Goods are not received until February 4, after payment has already been made. Unless advance payment was explicitly authorized by contract, this creates a risk of paying for goods that were never delivered or were delivered incompletely.

4. **Invoice received before goods receipt**  
   This is not necessarily wrong—vendors often invoice before delivery—but it should normally trigger a payment hold until receipt and matching are completed.

### Case 1003 — Payment and invoice matching occur in the wrong order

The sequence is:

**Create requisition  Approve requisition  Issue PO  Payment released  Invoice received  Goods received  Invoice matched  Close**

The primary anomaly is that **payment is released before the invoice is received and before goods are received or matched**.

This is a significant control failure. There is no apparent invoice or receipt evidence supporting the payment at the time it is made. It could indicate:

- An unauthorized advance payment
- Payment based on an incomplete or incorrect record
- A risk of fraud or duplicate payment
- Failure to enforce invoice and goods-receipt controls

If advance payment is legitimate, it should be explicitly documented, contractually authorized, and subject to separate approval. The event log provides no such indication.

### Case 1004 — Goods received before the purchase order

The sequence is:

**Create requisition  Approve requisition  Receive goods  Issue PO  Invoice received  Match  Payment  Close**

The anomaly is that **goods are received before the purchase order is issued**.

This is a “receipt before PO” or potentially a “maverick purchasing” pattern. It may mean:

- The supplier was instructed to deliver without an approved PO
- The PO was entered retrospectively
- The receipt was recorded incorrectly
- The organization committed to a purchase before formal authorization

This weakens procurement controls and can make it difficult to verify whether the delivery was properly authorized, priced, and sourced. Although payment occurs after matching in this case, the late PO creation remains a governance and audit concern.

### Summary

- **Case 1001:** Standard and well-controlled.
- **Case 1002:** Anomalous; PO and payment occur before required approvals, receipt, and matching.
- **Case 1003:** Highly anomalous; payment occurs before invoice receipt, goods receipt, and invoice matching.
- **Case 1004:** Anomalous; goods are received before the PO is issued, suggesting a retrospective or unauthorized purchase.

The most serious risks are premature payment, bypassing approval, failure to perform a three-way match, and commitments or deliveries occurring without a valid purchase order.
