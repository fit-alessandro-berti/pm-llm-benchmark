## 1. Normative-rule violations

### Case 1 — Normatively compliant
Case 1 follows the required path exactly:

1. Application Received  
2. Credit Check  
3. Risk Assessment  
4. Loan Decision (Approved)  
5. Inform Applicant  
6. Finalize Documentation

### Case 2 — Normative violations
Case 2 violates several mandatory rules:

1. **Risk Assessment occurred before Credit Check**
   - The required order is:
     `Application Received  Credit Check  Risk Assessment`
   - In this case, Risk Assessment occurred at 08:20, while Credit Check occurred later at 09:00.
   - This is an ordering violation, even though the credit check was eventually performed.

2. **Applicant was not informed after the decision**
   - A Loan Decision (Rejected) was made at 09:30.
   - There is no recorded **Inform Applicant** activity before closing the case.
   - This violates the requirement that the applicant must be informed after a loan decision.

3. **Documentation was finalized despite rejection**
   - The decision was “Rejected.”
   - For rejected applications, the process must close the case **without** Finalize Documentation.
   - Finalizing documentation at 10:00 is therefore explicitly prohibited.

4. **Case was closed despite incomplete/incorrect decision handling**
   - Although closing a rejected case is appropriate in principle, it was done after an impermissible documentation-finalization step and without a recorded applicant-notification step.
   - Therefore, the closure does not satisfy the full normative process.

## 3. Possible interpretations or justifications

### Case 1
- This is largely an exemplary process execution.
- If no Follow-up Call occurred, a possible explanation is that the applicant interaction during **Inform Applicant** was considered sufficient in practice. However, unless that interaction included a distinct follow-up purpose, it would still not satisfy the recommended Follow-up Call practice.

### Case 2
Possible explanations may exist, but none make the observed sequence compliant as recorded:

- The early “Risk Assessment” could have been a preliminary screening mistakenly labeled as a formal risk assessment.
- The Credit Check and Risk Assessment timestamps could be recorded incorrectly or entered late.
- The Finalize Documentation event may represent an administrative error, such as preparing rejection records rather than finalizing approval documentation.
- Applicant notification may have occurred but not been logged.

Even with these possible explanations, the recorded event history violates hard constraints. A compliant correction would require proper event classification, complete logging, no documentation finalization for rejected cases, and recorded applicant notification before closure.

### Case 3
The delays may be understandable operationally:

- The delayed Credit Check could be due to weekends, system outages, missing applicant information, external credit-bureau delays, or a backlog.
- The delayed notification could result from manual review, attempts to contact the applicant, staffing delays, or waiting for documents.
- The lack of a Follow-up Call may be reasonable if the applicant was unreachable or if no further clarification was needed.

Nevertheless, these are service-quality concerns. They do not invalidate the process path, but they indicate opportunities to improve responsiveness and applicant communication.

---

## Overall conclusion

- **Case 1:** Normatively compliant; potentially misses the recommended Follow-up Call.
- **Case 2:** Normatively noncompliant due to incorrect Credit Check/Risk Assessment ordering, missing applicant notification, and documentation finalization after rejection. It may also miss the recommended follow-up.
- **Case 3:** Normatively compliant, but suboptimal with respect to recommended timing targets and the Follow-up Call.
