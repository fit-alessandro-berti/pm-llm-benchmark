**Process Mining Analysis of the Manufacturing Event Log**

The team (Grok, Benjamin, Harper, and Lucas) independently analyzed the 15-event sensor log and reached strong consensus on the phases. The data shows a clear, short manufacturing cycle on Machine M1 that begins and ends in an idle state. Distinct multi-sensor signatures allow reliable inference of high-level activities even without explicit labels.

### Labeled Activities and Event Groupings

**1. Idle / Standby**  
**Events:** 1–3 and 13–15  

**Sensor signature:** Temperature ≈ 20–21 °C (ambient), pressure = 1 bar, vibration = 0 Hz, material flow = 0, tool position = 0 mm, energy ≈ 0.40–0.50 kWh.  

**Rationale:** All sensors are at baseline. The machine is powered but inactive. The tool resets to position 0 at the end of the cycle, confirming the return to a ready/waiting state between process cycles.

**2. Cutting Metal**  
**Events:** 4–6  

**Sensor signature:** Temperature rises 50 → 58 °C, pressure 1–1.5 bar, vibration high and rising (30 → 38 Hz), material flow steady at 5 units/s, tool position advances 5 → 10 mm, energy rises 2.00 → 3.00 kWh.  

**Rationale:** The combination of progressive tool movement into the workpiece, continuous material feed, elevated vibration from tool–material interaction, and frictional heat generation is the classic signature of a cutting or machining operation.

**3. Assembling Parts**  
**Events:** 7–8  

**Sensor signature:** Temperature drops/stabilizes at 40 °C, pressure increases to 2 bar, vibration falls to 10 Hz, material flow reduces to 2 units/s, tool position held constant at 15 mm, energy steady at 2.00 kWh.  

**Rationale:** The tool is now stationary (holding position), vibration and material flow decrease markedly, and pressure rises. These changes indicate a controlled joining or fitting step—pressing or clamping components together—rather than aggressive material removal.

**4. Welding**  
**Event:** 9 (with Event 10 as immediate post-weld cooling/transition)  

**Sensor signature (Event 9):** Temperature spikes to 80 °C, pressure remains 2 bar, vibration peaks at 50 Hz, material flow drops to 0, tool position held at 15 mm, energy peaks at 5.00 kWh.  
**Event 10:** Rapid temperature drop to 30 °C, vibration ≈ 1 Hz, flow = 0, tool still at 15 mm, energy falls to 1.00 kWh.  

**Rationale:** The extreme, simultaneous spike in temperature, vibration, and energy consumption with zero material flow is characteristic of a high-energy joining process such as welding (arc, resistance, or friction). Event 10 shows the expected rapid thermal decay while the tool remains in the welding position—clear post-process cooling/transition.

**5. Quality Inspection**  
**Events:** 11–12  

**Sensor signature:** Temperature near ambient (25 °C), pressure = 1 bar, low vibration (5 Hz), slight material flow (1 unit/s), tool repositions to 20 mm, energy ≈ 1.50–1.60 kWh.  

**Rationale:** After the high-energy welding step, the sensors show only mild, precise activity: a small tool movement, low vibration, and minimal flow. This pattern matches gentle probing, scanning, or measurement rather than production or bulk handling. (Packaging is less plausible here because the activity is light, occurs immediately after welding, and precedes a full reset to idle.)

### Overall Inferred Process Sequence
```
Idle / Standby → Cutting Metal → Assembling Parts → Welding → Cooling/Transition → Quality Inspection → Idle / Standby
```

This sequence forms a coherent single-part manufacturing cycle. The sensor transitions are physically consistent with the expected physics of each step (friction heat and vibration during cutting, pressure application during assembly, high thermal/electrical input during welding, and low-energy precision movement during inspection).

The analysis demonstrates a classic process-mining outcome: meaningful activity labels and a process model successfully recovered from purely numerical, unlabeled sensor data.
