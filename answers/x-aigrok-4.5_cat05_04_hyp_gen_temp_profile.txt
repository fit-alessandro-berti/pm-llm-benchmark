**Anomalies in the Temporal Profile Model**

The following pairs show clear irregularities relative to a typical multi-day insurance claim handling flow (Receive  Assign  Evaluate  Approve  Notify  Close):

- **('R', 'P')** – Average  25 hours with an extremely tight standard deviation of only 1 hour. This implies almost artificial scheduling rigidity for an activity pair that normally depends on variable evaluation effort and should show far more natural spread.
- **('P', 'N')** – Average delay of 7 days (STDEV 2 days). Notification after approval is expected to be near-immediate; a week-scale lag indicates systematic queuing or intentional withholding.
- **('A', 'C')** – Average of only 2 hours between assignment and closure. Closing a claim so rapidly after assignment is inconsistent with required intermediate evaluation and approval steps.
- **('E', 'N')** – Average of 5 minutes between evaluation and customer notification. This transition is unrealistically short and suggests either automation that skips human review or missing intermediate approval events.
- **('E', 'C')** – Average 1 hour with high relative variance also hints that some claims are being closed immediately after evaluation without a proper approval gate.

Collectively these timings do not form a coherent, stable process; they contain both “too-fast” shortcuts and “too-slow” bottlenecks that are unlikely to arise under normal operating conditions.

**Hypotheses for the Observed Anomalies**

1. **Automated or scripted approval / notification paths**  
   The ultra-low variance of RP and the five-minute EN gap are consistent with batch jobs or bots that stamp “Approve” and “Notify” events at fixed offsets rather than recording genuine human actions.

2. **Premature closure (shortcut / batch-close) logic**  
   The 2-hour AC average suggests that a subset of claims is being closed by an automatic rule (or by an adjuster who simply marks the case closed) without performing evaluation or approval, possibly for cost or KPI reasons.

3. **Notification backlog or deliberate delay**  
   The multi-day PN gap with large variance points to a capacity or policy problem: notifications are queued, held for audit, or batched weekly, producing long and uneven delays.

4. **Resource or region-specific bottlenecks**  
   The inconsistent spreads (especially RP versus PN) may correlate with particular adjusters, claim types (home vs. auto), or geographic regions that lack capacity or that apply informal work-arounds.

5. **Data-quality / logging artefacts**  
   Missing intermediate event rows, clock-synchronisation issues, or bulk inserts can produce both artificially short and artificially long intervals.

**Verification Queries (PostgreSQL)**

```sql
-- 1. Flag claims where RP interval is outside the “normal” range
--    (using the model’s mean ± 2 as a simple threshold)
WITH rp AS (
  SELECT
    r.claim_id,
    EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (p.timestamp - r.timestamp)) AS secs_rp
  FROM claim_events r
  JOIN claim_events p
    ON r.claim_id = p.claim_id
   AND r.activity = 'R'
   AND p.activity = 'P'
   AND p.timestamp > r.timestamp
)
SELECT claim_id, secs_rp / 3600.0 AS hours_rp
FROM rp
WHERE secs_rp < 90000 - 2*3600   -- too fast
   OR secs_rp > 90000 + 2*3600;  -- too slow

-- 2. Locate claims closed almost immediately after assignment
WITH ac AS (
  SELECT
    a.claim_id,
    EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (c.timestamp - a.timestamp)) AS secs_ac
  FROM claim_events a
  JOIN claim_events c
    ON a.claim_id = c.claim_id
   AND a.activity = 'A'
   AND c.activity = 'C'
   AND c.timestamp > a.timestamp
)
SELECT claim_id, secs_ac / 3600.0 AS hours_ac
FROM ac
WHERE secs_ac < 7200 + 2*3600;   -- within ~4 h of the 2 h mean

-- 3. Identify extreme approval-to-notification lags and join to adjuster / claim context
WITH pn AS (
  SELECT
    p.claim_id,
    EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (n.timestamp - p.timestamp)) AS secs_pn,
    c.claim_type,
    c.customer_id,
    e.resource AS approve_resource
  FROM claim_events p
  JOIN claim_events n
    ON p.claim_id = n.claim_id
   AND p.activity = 'P'
   AND n.activity = 'N'
   AND n.timestamp > p.timestamp
  JOIN claims c ON c.claim_id = p.claim_id
  LEFT JOIN claim_events e
    ON e.claim_id = p.claim_id AND e.activity = 'P'
)
SELECT
  claim_id,
  claim_type,
  customer_id,
  approve_resource,
  secs_pn / 86400.0 AS days_pn
FROM pn
WHERE secs_pn > 604800 + 2*172800   -- > ~11 days
ORDER BY days_pn DESC;

-- 4. Correlation check: average interval by adjuster specialisation / region
SELECT
  adj.specialization,
  adj.region,
  AVG(EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (e2.timestamp - e1.timestamp))) AS avg_secs,
  STDDEV_SAMP(EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (e2.timestamp - e1.timestamp))) AS std_secs,
  COUNT(*) AS n
FROM claim_events e1
JOIN claim_events e2
  ON e1.claim_id = e2.claim_id
 AND e1.activity = 'R'
 AND e2.activity = 'P'
 AND e2.timestamp > e1.timestamp
JOIN claims cl ON cl.claim_id = e1.claim_id
LEFT JOIN adjusters adj ON adj.adjuster_id::text = e1.resource  -- or appropriate join key
GROUP BY adj.specialization, adj.region
HAVING COUNT(*) > 5
ORDER BY avg_secs;

-- 5. Detect claims that skip Evaluate entirely yet still close quickly
SELECT a.claim_id
FROM claim_events a
JOIN claim_events c
  ON a.claim_id = c.claim_id
 AND a.activity = 'A'
 AND c.activity = 'C'
 AND c.timestamp - a.timestamp < INTERVAL '4 hours'
WHERE NOT EXISTS (
  SELECT 1 FROM claim_events e
  WHERE e.claim_id = a.claim_id AND e.activity = 'E'
);
```

These queries surface concrete claim_ids, resources, claim types and regions that drive the anomalous averages, allowing targeted investigation of process shortcuts, automated bot behaviour, or capacity problems.
