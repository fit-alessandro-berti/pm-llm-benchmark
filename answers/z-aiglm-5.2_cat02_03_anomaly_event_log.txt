Based on standard Procure-to-Pay (P2P) best practices, the ideal sequence of activities is generally:
1. Create Purchase Requisition (PR)
2. Approve Purchase Requisition
3. Issue Purchase Order (PO)
4. Receive Goods
5. Vendor Invoice Received
6. Match Invoice to PO (Three-way match: PO, Receipt, Invoice)
7. Release Payment
8. Close Case

Using this standard sequence as a baseline, here is the analysis of the event log, identifying anomalies and explaining why they are problematic.

### Case 1001: Normal
**Analysis:** The activities follow the standard, logical P2P sequence. A requisition is created, approved, and converted into a PO. Goods are received, an invoice is received and matched to the PO, payment is released, and the case is closed. 
**Anomalies:** None.

---

### Case 1002: Severe Sequence Anomalies
**Analysis:** The timestamps indicate that activities are highly out of order.
- **Issue Purchase Order** occurs (08:40) before the **Approve Purchase Requisition** activity (08:00 two days later).
- **Release Payment** occurs (07:15) before **Receive Goods** (09:00 the next day) and before **Match Invoice to PO** (08:00 two days later). 
- In fact, payment is released before the requisition is even approved.

**Why this is problematic:**
- **Internal Control Failure:** Issuing a PO before a PR is approved completely bypasses authorization controls. 
- **Financial Risk:** Releasing payment before receiving goods or matching the invoice to a PO means the company is paying blindly. If the vendor fails to deliver or delivers incorrect/damaged goods, the company has no leverage.
- **Compliance/Audit Risk:** This workflow would likely fail an internal audit, as it violates the basic segregation of duties and sequential controls designed to prevent fraud and unauthorized spending.

---

### Case 1003: Payment Before Receipt or Invoice
**Analysis:** 
- **Release Payment** (02-08) occurs before **Vendor Invoice Received** (02-09), **Receive Goods** (02-10), and **Match Invoice to PO** (02-11).

**Why this is problematic:**
- **Paying Without an Invoice:** Releasing a payment before an invoice is received is highly unusual and suggests the company is estimating or paying upfront without formal billing documentation.
- **Paying Before Delivery:** Paying before goods are received puts the buyer at significant risk of non-delivery. 
- While prepayments *do* exist in business for specific suppliers or custom orders, in a standard P2P process, a three-way match (verifying that the invoice matches the PO and the goods receipt) is required before funds are disbursed to prevent overpayment or paying for undelivered items.

---

### Case 1004: Goods Received Before Purchase Order
**Analysis:** 
- **Receive Goods** (02-06 09:00) occurs before **Issue Purchase Order** (02-06 09:15).

**Why this is problematic:**
- **Unplanned Receipts:** Receiving goods without a valid PO in the system makes it difficult to track purchasing commitments and manage inventory. 
- **Invoice Matching Issues:** If goods are received before a PO exists, a subsequent three-way match becomes impossible or requires manual workarounds. The system will not be able to automatically tie the receipt to a PO because it didn't exist at the time of delivery.
- **Unauthorized Procurement:** It suggests that a vendor was engaged and delivered goods without a formal, system-generated purchase order, which is a breach of standard procurement controls. 

### Summary
Cases 1002, 1003, and 1004 demonstrate significant deviations from standard P2P controls. The most severe is **Case 1002**, which combines unauthorized PO issuance with early payment. **Case 1003** exhibits blind payment, and **Case 1004** exhibits receipt of goods without a contractual PO in place. These anomalies expose the organization to financial loss, fraud, and compliance violations.
