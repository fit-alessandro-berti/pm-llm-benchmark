## Proposed high-level process model

The low-level events can be aggregated into five high-level activities:

1. **Material Identification and Preparation**
2. **Welding Operation**
3. **Weld Quality Verification**
4. **Protective Coating and Curing**
5. **Final Visual Inspection**

### 1. Low-level-to-high-level mapping

| High-level activity | Included low-level events | Grouping rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Material Identification and Preparation** | Retrieve raw metal sheet; Scan barcode; Place metal sheet on table; Align metal sheet; Preheat metal sheet | These events prepare and identify the same workpiece before fabrication. Although several resources are involved, all actions serve the common objective of making the material ready for welding. The barcode scan provides traceability, while placement, alignment, and preheating establish the required processing conditions. |
| **Welding Operation** | Pick up welding tool; Weld corner A; Weld corner B | Tool acquisition is setup for the immediately following welding actions. The events are performed by the same operator, occur consecutively, and collectively represent the fabrication or joining operation. |
| **Weld Quality Verification** | Measure weld integrity | This is an in-process quality gate performed by a dedicated quality sensor. It is separated from welding because its purpose is verification rather than production, and its result may determine whether rework is required. |
| **Protective Coating and Curing** | Apply protective coating; Dry coating | Applying and drying the coating are two parts of one surface-treatment stage. Drying or curing completes the coating operation and makes the protective layer usable. |
| **Final Visual Inspection** | Visual check | This is the final quality check after fabrication and coating. It is performed by an inspection resource and produces an explicit acceptance result. |

## Why quality checks are kept separate

Although **Measure weld integrity** occurs shortly after welding, it should remain a separate high-level event because:

- It has a different business purpose: inspection rather than transformation.
- It uses a specialized quality resource.
- It produces a quality measurement that could lead to a pass, failure, or rework path.

Similarly, the final visual check should not be combined with coating because it independently verifies the finished product after curing.

## Suggested aggregation rules

The following rules can be applied to the full event log:

```text
Retrieve raw material
+ Scan barcode
+ Place material
+ Align material
+ Preheat material
 Material Identification and Preparation

Acquire welding tool
+ Weld component/corner/joint events
 Welding Operation

Measure or test weld integrity
 Weld Quality Verification

Apply protective coating
+ Dry/cure coating
 Protective Coating and Curing

Visual check after surface treatment
 Final Visual Inspection
```

### Processing logic

1. Partition events by `CaseID`.
2. Sort each case by `Timestamp`.
3. Assign each low-level activity to a semantic high-level category.
4. Merge consecutive events assigned to the same category.
5. Set:
   - `StartTime` to the first child event timestamp.
   - `EndTime` to the last child event timestamp.
   - `Resources` to the distinct resources involved.
   - `AggregatedDetails` to relevant identifiers, settings, measurements, and outcomes.
6. Preserve the original child-event references for traceability.

Semantic purpose should take priority over temporal proximity. For example, weld measurement is close to welding in time but represents a separate quality gate.

## Expected high-level process sequence

```text
Material Identification and Preparation
        
Welding Operation
        
Weld Quality Verification
        
Protective Coating and Curing
        
Final Visual Inspection
```

If a weld verification or final inspection fails, the model should preserve the deviation—for example, by creating a **Weld Rework** or **Coating Rework** activity followed by another occurrence of the relevant inspection step.
