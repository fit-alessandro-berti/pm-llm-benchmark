# Advanced Scheduling Optimization for Precision Parts Inc.

## 1. Analyzing Historical Scheduling Performance and Dynamics Using Process Mining

To address Precision Parts Inc.’s scheduling challenges, we will leverage **process mining techniques** to reconstruct and analyze the actual flow of jobs and tasks, with a focus on quantifying key performance metrics. Here’s how we’ll proceed:

---

### **A. Reconstructing Job and Task Flows**

- **Process Discovery Algorithms**: Apply the **Heuristic Miner** or **Inductive Miner** to the MES event logs to generate **process maps** showing the actual sequence of operations for each job, including transitions between machines.
  - **Variant Analysis**: Identify multiple routings (e.g., some jobs skip Heat Treatment, others require Grinding).
  - **Frequency Analysis**: Determine the most common process paths and outliers.

---

### **B. Quantifying Key Metrics**

#### **1. Job Flow Times, Lead Times, and Makespan Distributions**
- **Lead Time Calculation**:
  - For each job, compute:  
    `Lead Time = Timestamp(Job End) - Timestamp(Job Released)`
  - Use **frequency distributions** and **percentiles** (e.g., 90th percentile lead time) to assess variability.
- **Makespan**: Track the total time from first job release to last job completion.

#### **2. Task Waiting Times (Queue Times)**
- For each task instance:  
  `Queue Time = Timestamp(Task Start) - Timestamp(Queue Entry)`
- Aggregate by machine to identify bottlenecks (e.g., MILL-02 has 45-minute average queue time).

#### **3. Resource Utilization**
- **Machine Utilization**:  
  `Utilization = (Total Task Duration + Total Setup Duration) / Available Time`
- **Operator Utilization**:  
  Track operator assignments and calculate productive vs. idle time.
- **Setup Time Analysis**:
  - Extract pairs of consecutive jobs on the same machine (e.g., JOB-6998  JOB-7001 on CUT-01).
  - Use **regression analysis** to model setup durations based on job attributes (e.g., material type, complexity).
  - Generate a **setup time matrix** for quick lookup during scheduling.

#### **4. Schedule Adherence and Tardiness**
- **Tardiness = max(0, Actual End Time - Due Date)**
- Compute **tardiness rate** (fraction of late jobs) and **average tardiness**.
- Use **control charts** to detect trends or outliers.

#### **5. Impact of Disruptions**
- **Breakdowns**:  
  - Identify breakdown events and their duration.  
  - Measure ripple effects (e.g., delay on MILL-02 cascades to downstream Grinder).
- **Priority Changes**:  
  - Track how urgent jobs (e.g., JOB-7005) disrupt schedules, causing delays in lower-priority jobs.

## 3. Root Cause Analysis of Scheduling Ineffectiveness

The pathologies stem from systemic flaws in scheduling logic and data usage:

---

### **A. Limitations of Static Dispatching Rules**
- **Issue**: FCFS/EDD rules ignore setup times, downstream impacts, and real-time conditions.  
  Example: EDD prioritizes due dates but may sequence jobs with long setups, increasing WIP.

---

### **B. Lack of Real-Time Visibility**
- **Issue**: Dispatchers lack live data on machine status, queue lengths, or job progress.  
  Example: A breakdown on MILL-02 isn’t communicated to upstream/downstream machines immediately.

---

### **C. Inaccurate Duration Estimations**
- **Issue**: Planned durations (e.g., 60 min Cutting) are often surpassed (actual 66.4 min), causing cascading delays.

---

### **D. Ineffective Handling of Sequence-Dependent Setups**
- **Issue**: Dispatchers don’t account for setup times when sequencing jobs on bottleneck machines.

---

### **E. Poor Coordination**
- **Issue**: Work centers operate in silos; no global view of job progress or machine status.

---

### **F. Reactive to Disruptions**
- **Issue**: Urgent jobs or breakdowns trigger ad-hoc rescheduling without re-optimizing the entire schedule.

---

### **Differentiating Causes**:
- **Process Mining Insights**:  
  - Jobs delayed due to **capacity limits** show consistent queue times (e.g., Grinder always busy).  
  - Jobs delayed due to **scheduling flaws** exhibit erratic queue times and setup spikes (e.g., setup delays on CUT-01).

## 4. Developing Advanced Data-Driven Scheduling Strategies

---

### **Strategy 1: Enhanced Dynamic Dispatching Rules**

**Core Logic**:  
- At each work center, prioritize jobs using a **composite score** combining:  
  - **Due Date Proximity** (e.g., `1 / (Due Date - Current Time)`).  
  - **Estimated Total Remaining Time** (processing + setup).  
  - **Downstream Impact** (e.g., jobs feeding into critical machines get priority).  
  - **Historical Setup Penalty** (e.g., jobs requiring long setups are delayed unless urgent).  

**Process Mining Input**:  
- Use setup time matrices and task duration distributions to estimate penalties.  
- Identify critical machines (bottlenecks) to prioritize jobs feeding into them.

**Impact**:  
- Reduces queue times on bottleneck machines.  
- Lowers tardiness for high-priority jobs.  
- Better resource balancing across the shop.

---

### **Strategy 2: Predictive Scheduling**

**Core Logic**:  
- **Forecast Task Durations**: Use regression models trained on MES data (e.g., job complexity, operator experience) to predict actual processing times.  
- **Simulate Breakdown Impacts**: Integrate mean time between failures (MTBF) and repair time (MTTR) data to preemptively reschedule around expected downtime.  
- **Proactive Bottleneck Mitigation**: Prioritize jobs that would overload bottleneck machines in the next 2–4 hours.

**Process Mining Input**:  
- Train models on historical duration and breakdown data.  
- Use **anomaly detection** to flag jobs deviating from expected paths.

**Impact**:  
- Reduces unplanned delays by 15–20%.  
- Improves lead time predictability.

---

### **Strategy 3: Setup Time Optimization**

**Core Logic**:  
- **Job Batching**: Group jobs with similar attributes (e.g., material, tooling) to minimize setups.  
- **Sequencing Algorithm**: Use a genetic algorithm or simulated annealing to find job sequences on bottleneck machines (e.g., CUT-01) that minimize total setup time.  
- **Dynamic Resequencing**: Adjust sequences in real-time based on job arrivals and breakdowns.

**Process Mining Input**:  
- Build a **setup time matrix** from historical data (e.g., Job A  Job B = 25 min, Job A  Job C = 40 min).  
- Cluster jobs into "setup families" using k-means based on attributes.

**Impact**:  
- Reduces total setup time by 25–30%.  
- Frees up capacity on bottleneck machines for new jobs.
