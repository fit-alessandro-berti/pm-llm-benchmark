**Trace accepted by the model but invalid in the domain:**  
`Receive_Order` → `Check_Inventory` → `Ship_Order` → `Package_Items` → `Authorize_Payment` → `Close_Order`

(The interleaving of the three activities inside the parallel node can be any permutation; the one above is simply a concrete example.)

**Why the model accepts it**  
The process tree is structured as:  
`SEQUENCE(Receive_Order, Check_Inventory, XOR(PARALLEL(Authorize_Payment, Package_Items, Ship_Order), SEQUENCE(Cancel_Order, Close_Order)), Close_Order)`.  

After the two obligatory prefix activities the XOR chooses the successful branch, which is a parallel (concurrent) node. A parallel node generates every possible interleaving of its children. Consequently any ordering of Authorize_Payment / Package_Items / Ship_Order is a legal continuation, after which the final Close_Order is executed.

**Why the trace is domain-invalid**  
In a real Order-to-Cash process the three activities are not freely interchangeable:  
- items must be packaged before they can be shipped,  
- payment authorisation normally precedes (or at least does not follow) shipment.  

Shipping an order that has not yet been packaged (and possibly has not even been paid for) is a clear violation of the business constraints, even though the under-specified process tree treats the three activities as concurrent and therefore permits the sequence.  

(The same process tree also permits other undesirable behaviours, e.g. the double occurrence of Close_Order on the cancellation path, but the interleaving shown above is the most direct illustration of a missing ordering constraint.)
