## Conclusion

**Group B exhibits bias** because candidates affiliated with the **LocalBusinessLeadersClub** receive an explicit **+5 cultural-fit adjustment**, while otherwise comparable candidates in Group A receive no equivalent adjustment.

## How the bias manifests

- **Group A:**  
  All candidates have `ScoreAdjustment = 0`, regardless of background or affiliation. Their cultural-fit scores remain unchanged.

- **Group B:**  
  - **U001:** Cultural fit increases from 60 to 65 because of the association.
  - **U003:** Cultural fit increases from 58 to 63 because of the association.
  - **U002:** Receives no adjustment because they have no association.

The adjustment is applied during the cultural-fit stage and carried through the interview and hiring-decision stages. Thus, affiliation can affect the candidate’s apparent qualifications even though it is not a demonstrated job-related skill.

## Evidence in the outcomes

The data does not provide enough cases or a stated decision threshold to prove that the adjustment alone caused each hiring decision. Nevertheless, the pattern is concerning:

- **U003** was hired with raw scores of 75 skill, 65 personality, and 58 cultural fit, after the association boost raised cultural fit to 63.
- **P002** was not hired with higher scores of 78 skill, 75 personality, and 60 cultural fit, although the candidates may have differed in other unrecorded interview factors.
- **U001** was hired despite having a lower skill score than P001, while receiving a higher adjusted cultural-fit score than their raw score would justify.

These comparisons suggest that association membership may improve a candidate’s evaluation and potentially influence hiring decisions.

## Fairness and equity implications

This is a form of **preferential treatment** or **disparate treatment** based on a professional affiliation rather than a consistently applied, job-related criterion. It may:

1. **Disadvantage Group A candidates**, who receive no comparable opportunity for a score increase.
2. **Reward social or professional network access**, which may be more available to local residents or economically advantaged candidates.
3. Use **“cultural fit” as a vehicle for subjectivity**, making favoritism harder to detect.
4. Create a potential proxy effect because association membership is correlated with `LocalResident = TRUE`.
5. Undermine equal opportunity and confidence in the hiring process, even if the final hiring rates in this small sample happen to be similar.

The hiring rates are two of three candidates hired in each group, so the sample does not show a clear aggregate disparity in outcomes. However, equal hiring rates do not eliminate bias in the **evaluation process**. A process can be biased even when the observed final outcomes are balanced, especially when one group receives an unexplained scoring advantage.

## Recommended remedy

The association boost should be removed unless membership has a clearly documented, validated, and job-related relationship to performance. All candidates should be evaluated using the same criteria, with objective scoring rules, audit trails, and regular checks for disparities by group, residency, and other potentially sensitive or proxy attributes.
