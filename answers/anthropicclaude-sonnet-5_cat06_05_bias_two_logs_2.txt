# Bias Analysis: Group A vs. Group B Hiring Logs

## Which Log Exhibits Bias?

**Group B (Unprotected Group) exhibits clear bias.** The `ScoreAdjustment` field reveals a systematic "+5 Association Boost" applied to candidates affiliated with the **LocalBusinessLeadersClub**, directly inflating their `CulturalFit` scores before the hiring decision is made.

## How the Bias Manifests

### 1. **Unequal Treatment Mechanism**
The bias is embedded in the *process itself*, not just the outcome:

| Case | Association | Raw CulturalFit | Adjustment | Final CulturalFit |
|------|-------------|-----------------|------------|-------------------|
| U001 | LocalBusinessLeadersClub | 60 | +5 | 65 |
| U002 | None | 60 | 0 | 60 |
| U003 | LocalBusinessLeadersClub | 58 | +5 | 63 |

Compare this to **Group A**, where no candidate receives any adjustment — all `ScoreAdjustment` values are 0, regardless of qualifications.

### 2. **Proxy Discrimination via "Professional Association"**
`LocalBusinessLeadersClub` membership functions as a **proxy variable**. It is correlated with:
- `LocalResident = TRUE` (all Group B cases are local residents)
- Likely socioeconomic/network privilege (access to exclusive local business networks)

This means the boost isn't neutral — it structurally favors an already-advantaged subgroup, even though "association membership" appears to be a legitimate business criterion on its surface.

### 3. **Impact on Outcomes — A Counterfactual Test**
Look at **U003** specifically:
- Skill: 75, Personality: 65, Raw CulturalFit: 58  **Hired**
- Without the +5 boost, CulturalFit would be 58 — comparable to or worse than **P002** (CulturalFit 60), who was **Not Hired** in Group A.

This suggests the artificial boost may be the *deciding factor* that pushed a borderline candidate into the "Hired" outcome — an outcome that would not have occurred under Group A's neutral scoring rules.

### 4. **Comparable Candidate, Different Rule Sets**
| Candidate | Skill | Personality | Raw CulturalFit | Adjustment | Outcome |
|-----------|-------|--------------|------------------|------------|---------|
| P002 (Group A) | 78 | 75 | 60 | 0 | Not Hired |
| U002 (Group B, no association) | 78 | 70 | 60 | 0 | Not Hired |
| U003 (Group B, **with** association) | 75 | 65 | 58 | **+5** | **Hired** |

U003 has *lower* raw scores across the board than both P002 and U002, yet is hired — solely because of the association-based adjustment. This is a strong signal of **process-level bias**, not just statistical disparity in outcomes.

## Fairness & Equity Implications

1. **Violates Equal Treatment Principle**: Two candidates with identical underlying qualifications receive different final scores based on an attribute (club membership) unrelated to job performance.

2. **Indirect/Proxy Discrimination**: Even if "association membership" isn't a protected class itself, if membership correlates with race, class, gender, or local social networks (which "LocalBusinessLeadersClub" strongly implies), this constitutes **indirect discrimination** — a well-recognized concept in employment law (e.g., disparate impact doctrine).

3. **Reduces Merit-Based Selection**: The core hiring signal (skill, personality, genuine cultural fit) is corrupted by an exogenous adjustment, undermining the validity and defensibility of hiring decisions.

4. **Audit Trail Transparency Risk**: While this log is fortunately well-instrumented (the adjustment is logged), in many real systems such boosts might be applied silently within a scoring algorithm, making bias far harder to detect. This case demonstrates the *value* of transparent process mining/event logs for auditing.

5. **Systemic/Network Effects**: Membership-based boosts often reproduce existing social capital advantages — those already "in the network" get an easier path in, compounding historical inequities rather than correcting them.

## Conclusion

**Group B is the biased log.** The bias is not merely statistical (i.e., different hire rates) but **procedural** — a specific, traceable process step (`ScoreAdjustment: +5 Association Boost`) that systematically privileges a subgroup based on an attribute serving as a proxy for local/insider status. Group A, by contrast, applies identical evaluation criteria uniformly, with all `ScoreAdjustment` values at 0, representing a fairer, merit-based process. This case exemplifies how **process mining** can expose hidden bias mechanisms that a simple outcome-based fairness metric (e.g., hire rate comparison) might miss or fail to explain.
