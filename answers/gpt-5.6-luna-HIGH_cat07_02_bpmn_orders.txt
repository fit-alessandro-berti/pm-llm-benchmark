The visualization is a BPMN-style order-to-delivery process divided into four swimlanes: **Sales, Production, Procurement, and Warehouse**. Solid arrows show the sequence within a department, while dashed arrows represent messages exchanged between departments.

### Process flow

1. **Sales**
   - The process starts on **Monday morning**.
   - Sales checks whether the order is complete.
   - It then checks whether the customer exists in the database.
   - If the customer is not found, the customer is added to the database; otherwise, the process continues directly.
   - Sales sends the order details to both **Production** and **Procurement**.

2. **Production**
   - Production receives the order details.
   - A parallel gateway initiates production planning and the material-status coordination.
   - Production plans the work and then manufactures the product.
   - When manufacturing is complete, a **Product complete** message is sent to the Warehouse.

3. **Procurement**
   - Procurement receives the order details and enters a **Material Compensation Subprocess**.
   - It orders the required materials.
   - If necessary, a compensation action can be triggered to **return materials**.
   - Procurement checks the materials:
     - If the materials are acceptable, it communicates that they are **not faulty** to Production.
     - If a problem occurs, the process can issue an **order cancellation**.

4. **Warehouse**
   - The Warehouse receives the product-complete notification.
   - It sends the product onward.
   - Once the product is sent, it notifies Sales.

5. **Completion in Sales**
   - Sales receives the **Product sent** message.
   - It drafts the invoice, after which the process ends.

The diagram also includes a customer database linked to the customer-checking subprocess and uses gateways to model customer-existence decisions, parallel activities, material exceptions, and possible order cancellation.
