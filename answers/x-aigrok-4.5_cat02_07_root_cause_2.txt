Durations are measured from first event (Submit Claim) to last (Close Claim). The primary direction of delay is waiting after one or more “Request Additional Documents” steps (often multi-hour or overnight) plus longer intervals before Approve/Pay on the long cases.

### Root-cause analysis by attributes
1. **Complexity (strongest correlate)**  
   - Low-complexity cases (2001 Region A, 2004 Region B) follow a clean linear path (Submit  Evaluate  Approve  Pay  Close) with no requests for documents and finish same-day.  
   - Medium (2002 Region B) has exactly one Request Additional Documents, introducing roughly 1 day of wait before Approve.  
   - High-complexity cases require repeated requests: 2003 has two successive Request Additional Documents; 2005 has three. Each request adds a multi-hour/overnight lag, compounding total duration. High-complexity claims are the only ones that stretch into multi-day calendars.  

   Explanation/suggestion: High-complexity claims either require intrinsically more evidence or are being handled with incomplete initial packages / poor early identification of needed documents, producing iterative loops. Mitigation ideas:  
   - Up-front complexity triage + exhaustive checklists or specialist adjusters for “High” cases so that all documents are requested in one shot.  
   - Customer self-service portal or timed reminders to shorten external wait times.  
   - SLA/KPI differentiation by complexity floor so high cases receive appropriate resource allocation rather than being treated identically to low cases.

2. **Resource**  
   - Adjusters: Mike (Region A) generates two document-request loops on the high case; Lisa (Region B) generates three. Both are also the evaluators, so skill or process gaps around high-complexity evidence gathering concentrate here. Low/medium cases show far fewer (or zero) loops with the same resources.  
   - Approvers: Manager_Ann handles the fast low- and medium-duration cases with of short turn-around. Manager_Bill handles both long high-complexity cases and appears after the repeated document loops (approximately 19–23 h later).  
   - Finance/CSRs (Alan, Carl, Jane, Paul, Mary) execute Pay/Close promptly once reached and do not appear to be the primary delay drivers.  

   Explanation/suggestion: Certain resources either lack expertise/capacity for complex claims or create Tempo bottlenecks (Bill’s slower approval window after document loops). Mitigations:  
   - Route high-complexity claims to (or co-train) designated specialist adjusters/managers; audit why multiple sequential document requests occur with Mike/Lisa.  
   - Load-balance or priority-queue Manager_Bill’s high-complexity approvals; consider multi-skill Managers or parallel review when complexity = High.  
   - Performance dashboards that slice cycle time by Resource × Complexity to surface skill or workload issues quickly.

3. **Region**  
   - Region does not dominate overall: both A and B contain fast low-complexity cases and slow high-complexity cases.  
   - Within high complexity only, the Region-B case (2005) experiences one extra request-and-wait cycle and therefore runs longer than the Region-A high case (2003). Medium complexity also appears only in B in this sample.  

   Explanation/suggestion: Any regional difference is secondary and likely mediated by the local resources (Lisa vs Mike, Bill approvals) or volume of high-complexity work rather than an intrinsic regional process difference. Mitigations:  
   - Compare average requests-per-case and resource utilization across regions on a larger log; if B systematically shows more loops, standardize the high-complexity evaluation script/training used in A.  
   - Cross-region knowledge transfer or temporary staffing support for high-complexity spikes.

### Summary of performance diagnosis and recommended actions
The dominant root cause of long lead times is **high claim complexity that triggers iterative “Request Additional Documents” loops**, magnified by the specific adjusters and the Manager_Bill approval path. Region is weak secondary; straightforward low-complexity work finishes rapidly under any combination of resources tested.  

Priority mitigations:  
- Complexity-aware triage + single-shot document gathering (checklists/specialists/portals).  
- Resource skill-matching and load-balancing focused on high-complexity + Manager_Bill.  
- Process mining / ongoing monitoring of “# of document requests by Complexity × Resource” and waiting-time heat-maps so that the same loops do not reappear.  

These changes target the observed multi-day paths while leaving the already-efficient low-complexity path untouched.
