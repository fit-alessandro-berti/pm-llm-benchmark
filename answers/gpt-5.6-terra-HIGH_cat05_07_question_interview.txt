### Process Scope and Entry Criteria
1. What event formally starts the property onboarding process, and who is responsible for confirming that it has begun?
2. Are there property types, locations, or landlord situations that the company does not accept for management?
3. What information does the regional office collect during the initial landlord inquiry before assigning a coordinator?
4. How do you distinguish between a preliminary inquiry and a confirmed onboarding engagement?
5. What agreements, fees, or commitments must be in place before the company invests resources in onboarding the property?

### Documentation Review and Verification
6. Which documents are mandatory for every property, and which documents depend on the city, property type, or intended tenant market?
7. How does the coordinator determine whether a deed, compliance certificate, or inspection report is current and valid?
8. Who has the authority to approve documentation when it is unclear, incomplete, or inconsistent?
9. What happens if a landlord cannot provide a required document or disputes the need for it?
10. Are there deadlines for landlords to provide missing or updated documents, and what happens if those deadlines are missed?
11. How are document versions, approvals, and expiration dates tracked throughout the lifecycle of the property?
12. At what point is the property considered sufficiently verified to proceed to manager assignment and inspections?

### Property Data Collection and System Records
13. What specific property attributes must be recorded in the central database before the property can move forward?
14. Who validates the accuracy of property details such as unit count, square footage, amenities, and expected pricing?
15. How are discrepancies handled when the landlord-provided information differs from inspection findings or market data?
16. Which teams use the property data after it is entered, and what information is most important to each of them?
17. What changes to property information require reapproval or rework later in the process?

### Property Manager Assignment
18. Who makes the final decision about which property manager is assigned to a new property?
19. How are workload, geographic expertise, property type experience, and landlord preferences weighed against each other?
20. What happens when no available property manager has the ideal experience or capacity for a particular property?
21. Can a landlord request a specific manager, and how is that request evaluated?
22. Under what circumstances might a property manager be reassigned during onboarding or after activation?
23. What responsibilities remain with the coordinator after the property manager has been assigned?

### Landlord Introduction and Expectation Setting
24. What topics must be covered during the introductory meeting between the landlord and property manager?
25. How are special landlord requirements, such as custom lease clauses, pet policies, furnished units, or maintenance preferences, documented and approved?
26. Who has authority to agree to exceptions from standard company policies during these discussions?
27. How are disagreements between the landlord and the property manager resolved?
28. What information from the introductory meeting is shared with leasing, marketing, compliance, maintenance, and finance teams?

### Inspection, Photography, and Property Readiness
29. Can photography and inspection occur in parallel, or must one be completed before the other?
30. Who schedules the photographer and inspector, and how are scheduling conflicts with landlords or tenants handled?
31. What standards does the certified inspector use to determine whether a property is safe and habitable?
32. How are inspection findings categorized by severity, urgency, cost, or impact on the onboarding timeline?
33. Who decides whether repairs are the landlord’s responsibility, the company’s responsibility, or the tenant’s responsibility?
34. What is the process when repairs require outside contractors, permits, or additional inspections?
35. Can marketing preparation begin before all inspection issues are resolved, and if so, what restrictions apply?
36. What conditions must be met before the property is considered “inspection passed” and ready for listing?

### Pricing and Marketing Preparation
37. Who determines the final rental price, and how is the landlord’s expected price balanced against market data and profitability targets?
38. What inputs are used by the pricing team to establish the recommended rental range?
39. What happens when the landlord refuses the recommended price or requests a price outside the company’s strategy?
40. Who reviews and approves listing descriptions, photographs, brochures, and advertised amenities before publication?
41. How are special features, restrictions, or risks—such as insurance requirements, accessibility limitations, or parking rules—communicated in marketing materials?
42. What approval is required before the property can be activated in public-facing rental portals?

### Activation and Tenant Screening
43. What does “activation” mean operationally, and which systems or teams must confirm readiness before activation occurs?
44. Is there a final checklist before prospective tenants can view or apply for the property?
45. Who owns tenant screening: the property manager, leasing agent, a centralized screening team, or some combination?
46. What screening criteria are applied consistently to all applicants, and what discretion is allowed for individual cases?
47. How are credit, employment, rental history, and landlord references evaluated when information is incomplete or conflicting?
48. How do you ensure that screening and applicant prioritization comply with fair housing requirements?
49. When multiple qualified applicants are interested, what rules govern negotiations, promotions, and selection decisions?
50. Who can approve lease concessions, promotional discounts, or nonstandard lease lengths?
51. How are applicants informed of decisions, and how are rejected or withdrawn applications handled?

### Lease Finalization and Move-In
52. Who prepares the lease agreement, and who verifies that it reflects the approved property terms and landlord requirements?
53. What types of custom lease clauses are permitted, and who reviews them for legal and compliance risks?
54. What conditions must be met before a lease can be signed and a move-in date confirmed?
55. How are security deposits collected, recorded, and verified before occupancy begins?
56. What happens if a selected tenant withdraws, fails to pay the deposit, or cannot move in as planned?
57. At what point does ownership of the relationship transition from onboarding or leasing staff to ongoing property operations?

### Compliance, Financial, and IT Reviews
58. Which quality checks occur before activation, which occur before lease execution, and which occur after move-in?
59. What specifically does the compliance officer review, and what findings would prevent lease execution?
60. How does the financial analyst determine whether the rent and lease terms meet profitability and pricing requirements?
61. What does IT validate during onboarding, and how are data integration errors identified and corrected?
62. Can any of the audit or review steps occur in parallel, and which ones are strict gating points?
63. Who has the authority to approve an exception when compliance, finance, operations, and landlord preferences conflict?

### Exceptions, Escalations, and Process Controls
64. What are the most common exceptions that delay or complicate onboarding?
65. How are unusual cases—such as special insurance requirements, unpermitted work, custom lease clauses, or nonstandard property types—identified and escalated?
66. Is there a formal exception-approval process, and what information is required to make an approval decision?
67. Who is accountable for resolving blockers when several teams or external parties are involved?
68. How are risks communicated to landlords when their requested terms may delay activation or reduce tenant interest?
69. What happens if a property remains inactive for an extended period due to unresolved repairs, missing documents, or pricing disagreements?
70. Under what circumstances would the company stop onboarding a property after the process has already started?

### Timing, Communication, and Performance
71. What is the typical end-to-end onboarding timeline, and how does it vary by property type or market?
72. Which steps most commonly cause delays, and how are those delays monitored?
73. What service-level expectations exist for document review, manager assignment, inspections, listing creation, and tenant screening?
74. Which communications must be formally recorded, and where are they recorded when discussions occur through email, phone calls, or meetings?
75. How do regional offices, property managers, marketing, compliance, finance, IT, and external vendors stay aligned on the current onboarding status?
76. What dashboards, milestones, or reports are used to track progress and identify stalled properties?
77. How is process success measured—for example, time to activation, occupancy speed, compliance outcomes, landlord satisfaction, or profitability?
78. What lessons from completed onboardings are fed back into the process to improve future assignments, pricing, documentation checks, or exception handling?
