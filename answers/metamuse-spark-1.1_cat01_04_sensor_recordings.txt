`Idle [1-3] -> Cutting [4-6] -> Assembling [7-8] -> Welding [9] -> Quality Inspection [10] -> Packaging [11-12] -> Idle [13-15]`

### Rationale - Why these labels?

**1. Idle / Standby [1-3 and 13-15]**
This is the null state. Temperature is ambient ~20°C, pressure is at atmospheric minimum 1 bar, vibration is 0, material flow is 0, tool is at home position 0mm, and energy is <0.5 kWh - just enough for controller and sensors. Events 1-3 are startup/warm-up, and 13-15 are shutdown/return-to-home. This repetition confirms it's a cyclic manufacturing process.

**2. Cutting Metal [4-6]**
The classic signature of subtractive machining:
*   **Vibration 30-38 Hz** goes from 0 to high - tool in contact with metal.
*   **Material Flow 5 units/s** is at its highest - raw stock is being fed.
*   **Tool Position is progressive** 5mm -> 7mm -> 10mm - the cutting tool is traversing.
*   **Temperature 50-58°C rises steadily** due to friction from cutting.
*   **Energy 2.0-3.0 kWh rises** as cutting load increases.

**3. Assembling Parts [7-8]**
Distinct transition from cutting:
*   **Temperature drops** to 40°C - friction stops.
*   **Vibration drops sharply** to 10Hz - no more cutting, just low-level handling.
*   **Pressure increases to 2 bar** - indicates pneumatic clamping / press-fitting to hold parts together.
*   **Tool Position locks at 15mm** - no traversing, holding at an assembly station.
*   **Flow drops to 2** - minimal material, as parts are already cut.
*   **Stable readings for two consecutive events** indicates a controlled, steady operation.

**4. Welding [9]**
A short, intensive energy burst:
*   **Temperature SPIKE to 80°C**, **Vibration SPIKE to 50Hz**, **Energy SPIKE to 5.0 kWh** - all sensors hit their MAXIMUM in this single event. This is the only phase that can justify that.
*   **Material Flow = 0** - welding doesn't feed new bulk material, it fuses existing parts.
*   **Tool Position stays at 15mm** - welding occurs at the same assembly location. This is typical for a multi-purpose station.
This isolated spike is characteristic of welding, not a sustained process.

**5. Quality Inspection / Cooling [10]**
Immediately after welding, we must cool and check:
*   **Temperature plummets** from 80°C to 30°C - active cooling / natural cool-down.
*   **Vibration 1Hz, Energy 1.0kWh, Pressure 1 bar** all drop to near-idle - no mechanical work is being done.
*   **Flow 0 and Tool Position static** indicates a dwell time. The machine is not acting, but the part is still present at 15mm. In process mining this "non-productive" dwell between high-energy and handling phases almost always represents inspection, either visual by sensor or human.

**6. Packaging / Material Handling [11-12]**
Final handling phase:
*   **Tool moves to 20mm** - a new location, logically the end-of-line packaging chute / conveyor.
*   **Low, gentle values:** Vib 5Hz, Energy 1.5kWh, Temp 25°C - low-impact, non-destructive movement.
*   **Flow 1 units/s resumes** at a low rate - final product moving out, not raw material moving in.

This segmentation was done by looking for change-points in correlated attributes, grouping events with similar vector distance. This is exactly how unsupervised process mining algorithms like trace clustering would infer activities when no labels are present.
