In this POWL model the `StrictPartialOrder`:

```
R -> xor_credit -> loop_approve_invoice -> P -> O
```

looks normative at top level: `Receive_Order -> [Credit check] -> [Approve/Invoice] -> Receive_Payment -> Close_Order`.

The anomalies are hidden inside the two `OperatorPOWL` nodes.

### 1. Anomaly 1: `xor_credit = XOR(C, skip)`

```python
xor_credit = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[C, skip])
```

This is a classic workaround pattern: a mandatory control step put in a choice with a `SilentTransition`.

**Allowed behaviour:** `<..., Receive_Order, Approve_Order, ...>` without any `Check_Credit`

**Why this breaks O2C expectations:**
In the normative Order-to-Cash, `Check_Credit` is mandatory for risk management. It must happen after `Receive_Order` and before `Approve_Order`. Making it skippable means:

*  **Financial risk & compliance violation:** Orders from customers with bad credit history / exceeded credit limit can be approved. Leads to bad debt, DSO increase.
*  **Conformance problem:** In process mining this model has low precision. It will accept as fitting traces that the business would consider non-compliant. You can no longer detect this workaround by conformance checking, because the model itself legalizes it.
*  **Root cause:** This often indicates employees bypassing the check under time pressure, system unavailability of the credit rating service, or VIP customer handling without documented override.

### 2. Anomaly 2: `loop_approve_invoice = LOOP(children=[A, I])`

In `pm4py` / process tree semantics, `LOOP(do, redo)` executes as:

```
do (redo do)*
```

So here `do = Approve_Order`, `redo = Issue_Invoice` -> language: `A (I A)*`

That is `A`, `A I A`, `A I A I A`, etc.

This deviates from O2C in three ways:

**a) Invoice becomes optional and never last in the loop:**
The loop must end on `A`. A valid trace is:

> `R, C, A, P, O` - Receive Payment without ever Issuing an Invoice

> `R, C, A, I, A, P, O` - Invoice issued *before* the final approval

Both are illegal in accounting: you cannot recognize revenue / collect payment without an invoice, and you cannot invoice before final approval.

**b) Repeated approvals and invoices:**
The loop allows `A I A I A ...` - multiple invoices for the same order and multiple approvals. 

**c) Inverted dependency:**
Normative expectation is a strict sequence `Approve_Order -> Issue_Invoice` executed exactly once. Invoice is the consequence of approval, not the trigger for re-approval. The current model suggests a rework loop where an invoice forces a new approval.

**Why this matters:**

*  **Inefficiency / Rework signal:** Repeated `A-I` indicates the approval criteria are unclear, approval is rejected after invoicing, or the system allows creating invoices in error and requires re-approval to correct them.
*  **Customer experience & data quality:** A customer receiving 2-3 invoices for one order. Audit nightmare - which invoice is valid?
*  **Fraud & control risk:** Pattern `I -> A` allows invoicing first, approving later - a classic segregation of duties violation. An employee could issue an inflated invoice and then approve it himself in the second iteration to legitimize it.
*  **Payment without invoice:** `P` after a loop ending on `A` means closing books without legal document. This breaks SOX / tax compliance.

### What the normative model should be

```python
root = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[R, C, A, I, P, O])
root.order.add_edge(R, C)
root.order.add_edge(C, A)
root.order.add_edge(A, I)
root.order.add_edge(I, P)
root.order.add_edge(P, O)
```

No `XOR` with `skip`, no `LOOP`. Every order: Receive -> Check mandatory -> Approve exactly once -> Invoice exactly once -> Pay -> Close.

The discovered model with `XOR(C, skip)` and `LOOP(A, I)` does not describe the process as it *should* be, it describes the process as it *is* - including workarounds that have become normalized.
