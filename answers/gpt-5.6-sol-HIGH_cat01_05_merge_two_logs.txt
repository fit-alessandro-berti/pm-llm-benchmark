## Matching approach

- Matched events on the same `order_id`, compatible event semantics, and an **inclusive ±2-second tolerance**.
- For merged records, the **System A timestamp is the primary timestamp**, since Log A is the primary timeline.
- Both original timestamps and event names are retained for traceability.
- Events outside the tolerance were left separate unless there was enough evidence to identify them confidently as the same occurrence.

## Matching decisions

- **Order Received / OrderReceived:** Merged. The names are formatting variations of the same activity, and the timestamps differ by exactly 2 seconds.
- **Order Validated / OrderValidation:** Merged. The names are semantically equivalent, and the timestamps differ by 1 second.
- **Payment Processed / PaymentCheck:** Left separate. Although payment-related, the timestamps differ by 5 seconds, outside the selected tolerance, and “check” may represent a different step from successful processing. The note about a gateway delay also supports treating them as potentially distinct.
- **Quality Check:** Included as a System B-only event because no corresponding event exists in Log A.
- **Item Shipped / Shipping:** Merged. The names describe the same shipping occurrence, and the timestamps differ by exactly 2 seconds.
- **Item Delivered:** Included as a System A-only event because Log B has no delivery event.

Using a strict “less than 2 seconds” rule rather than the adopted inclusive ±2-second tolerance would leave the received and shipping pairs separate. Here, semantic equivalence and the known timestamp offsets make those matches sufficiently confident.
