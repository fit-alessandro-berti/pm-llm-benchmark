**This is a Performance Spectrum visualization from process mining**, applied to the well-known Road Traffic Fine Management Process event log.

### What a Performance Spectrum shows
It plots **every individual case** (here, every traffic fine) flowing between consecutive process activities over calendar time.  
- Horizontal lines mark the activities.  
- Each sloping or vertical line represents one case moving from one activity to the next.  
- The slope (or verticality) of a line encodes the duration of that segment for that case.  
- Color typically encodes performance class (duration quartile): grayer = faster, pink/redder = slower.

### Reading this specific plot

**Activities (top to bottom)**  
1. **Send Fine**  
2. **Insert Fine Notification**  
3. **Send for Credit Collection**

**Time axis**  
≈ 25 Jan 2000 → 24 Apr 2013 (the full span of the public Road Traffic Fine log).

**Upper segment (Send Fine → Insert Fine Notification)**  
- Extremely dense, almost solid block of **vertical gray lines**.  
- Interpretation: for the vast majority of cases these two activities occur with near-zero waiting time (essentially back-to-back). Processing is continuous and high-volume across the entire 13-year period, with no visible batching or seasonal gaps.

**Lower segment (Insert Fine Notification → Send for Credit Collection)**  
- Classic “batch-on-end” pattern: lines fan out from a range of start times and **converge to discrete points** on the bottom timeline, forming the characteristic inverted triangular / conical shapes.  
- Interpretation: fines receive their notification continuously over time, but are later grouped and sent for credit collection in batches on specific dates (typical of administrative/legal processes that run periodic collection runs).  
- The redder lines (especially the longer-sloped ones) highlight the slower performance class — cases that waited significantly longer before being batched for collection.

### Overall process insight
The early part of the process (issuing the fine and inserting the notification) is highly efficient and continuous.  
The later transition to credit collection shows clear batching behavior and much higher, more variable waiting times — a common pattern when unpaid fines are periodically handed over to a collection agency.
