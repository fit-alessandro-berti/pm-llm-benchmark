### 2. RootCause Analysis (Evidence from the Log)

#### A. The dominant bottleneck: an ~19hour overnight gap between “Investigate Issue” and “Resolve Ticket”
Look at the items that finish in the afternoon:

| Case | Investigate finished | Resolve started | Wait |
|------|----------------------|-----------------|------|
| 102 | 14:00 Mar1 | 09:00 Mar2 | **19h** |
| 104 | 13:00 Mar1 | 08:00 Mar2 | **19h** |
| 105 | 14:00 Mar2 | 09:00 Mar3 | **19h** |

By contrast, the fast cases investigated in the **morning** (08:45–09:00) resolved within **30–60minutes**.

**Implication:** The process appears to have a “nextday resolution” rule or batch. Once investigation is completed after ~12:00, the ticket sits idle until the next morning’s shift (~08:00–09:00). This single delay accounts for **~75% of the total cycle time** in cases 102 and 104, and **~40%** in 105.

#### B. Escalation to Level2 creates additional multihour (or multiday) waits
* **Case 102:** Escalated at 11:30 Mar1  Level2 investigation at 14:00 Mar1 (**2.5h**), then the overnight 19h wait.
* **Case 105:** Escalated at 10:00 Mar1  Level2 investigation not until 14:00 Mar2 (**28h**!), then another 19h overnight wait before resolution.

**Implication:** Escalation is highly correlated with long cycles, but the **severity depends on Level2 response time**. Case 105 shows a critical specialist backlog—almost a full business day passes before the escalated ticket is even investigated.

#### C. Preinvestigation / assignment delays compound the problem
* **Case 102:** 2.5h from “Assign to Level1” to escalation; another 2.5h from escalation to investigation.
* **Case 104:** 3.5h from assignment (09:30) to investigation (13:00) — the longest preinvestigation delay in the log.
* **Case 105:** Fast initial assignment (10min), but after escalation the ticket languishes.

**Implication:** Level1 agents (or the triage queue) are not always available to start work immediately. Case 104 has no escalation yet is just as long as 102 because of this frontend delay plus the overnight resolution gap.

#### D. Triage is a minor but noticeable delay for the slowest case
* **Case 104:** 40min from “Receive” to “Triage” (vs. 5–25min for others).
* **Case 102:** 25min (slightly elevated).

This is not the primary driver, but it pushes the start of work later, exacerbating afternoon cutoff effects.

### 3. How These Factors Increase Cycle Time & What to Do

| Factor | Impact on Cycle Time | Recommendation |
|--------|----------------------|----------------|
| **Afternoon investigation  nextmorning resolution (~19h)** | Adds a fixed ~19h “dead time” to every ticket that isn’t solved by midday. | **Eliminate batch resolution.** Allow agents to close/resolve continuously, or introduce an evening/late shift to handle afternoon completions. | **Escalation waits (2.5h  28h)** | Case 105 is the worst because Level2 takes a full day. | **Establish escalation SLAs** (e.g., Level2 must begin investigation within 2h of escalation). | **Assignment  start delays (15min  3.5h)** | Pushes investigation into the afternoon “cutoff” window. | **Skillbased / loadbalanced routing:** Assign tickets to available agents immediately rather than leaving them in an unassigned queue. Monitor “Assign  Investigate” as a leading KPI. |
| **Overnight/shiftbased process** | All long cases resolve at 08:00–09:00 the next day, indicating a rigid schedule. | **Overlapping shifts or 24h coverage** for Level1/Level2 can turn 19h waits into ~1–2h waits for tickets that arrive early in the day. |

### Key Insight
The **19hour overnight gap between investigation and resolution** is the single biggest structural bottleneck. It turns a 2hour support task into a 24hour (or 49hour) ordeal. Escalation and assignment delays are critical **amplifiers**—they push the investigation timestamp past the daily “resolution window,” forcing the ticket to wait for the next morning’s batch.

**Quickwin actions:**
1. **Monitor the “Investigate  Resolve” interval**; if it consistently jumps to ~19h in the afternoon, the issue is shift/batch scheduling, not agent skill.
2. **Cap escalation response time** at 2h (case 105’s 28h is an outlier that needs immediate specialist staffing intervention).
3. **Fasttrack morning cases** (as 101 and 103 show, they finish in ~1–2h) by ensuring highpriority or complex tickets are assigned and started by 09:00, avoiding the afternoon delay entirely.
