**Group B (Unprotected Group) exhibits bias.** Group A applies a uniform process with no score adjustments. Group B systematically applies a +5 CulturalFit “Association Boost” exclusively to candidates affiliated with the LocalBusinessLeadersClub.

### How the bias manifests
- **Preferential adjustment only for one affiliation**: In Group B, members of LocalBusinessLeadersClub (U001 and U003) receive an explicit +5 to CulturalFit (and the adjusted value is carried forward into later stages and the final decision). Non-members (U002) and every candidate in Group A receive 0. The logs record this as “+5 (Association Boost)” and show the inflated CulturalFit values (e.g., 60 → 65 for U001; 58 → 63 for U003).
- **Outcome distortion**:  
  - U001 (Skill 80, Personality 72, base CulturalFit 60 → 65) is Hired.  
  - U003 (Skill 75, Personality 65, base CulturalFit 58 → 63) is Hired.  
  - Comparable or stronger non-boosted profiles are rejected: P002 (Skill 78, Personality 75, CulturalFit 60) and U002 (Skill 78, Personality 70, CulturalFit 60) are both Not Hired.  
  The boost therefore changes ranking and final decisions in favor of club members.
- **Correlation with other attributes**: All Group B cases have LocalResident = TRUE; the club membership (and thus the boost) is available only to that group. Group A cases are uniformly LocalResident = FALSE and receive no equivalent pathway.
- **Process transparency**: The adjustment is applied at the CulturalFitCheck stage by the HR Analyst and then propagates, making the bias both visible in the event log and consequential for the HiringDecision.

### Implications for fairness and equity
- **Disparate treatment**: Candidates are evaluated under different rules solely because of membership in a particular professional association (and the correlated local-resident status). This violates formal equality of opportunity—identical merit signals (skill, personality, base cultural-fit scores) produce different outcomes depending on group membership.
- **Equity impact**: Protected-group candidates (Group A) have no access to the boost mechanism. Even when their raw scores equal or exceed those of boosted candidates, they cannot obtain the same adjusted CulturalFit. Over repeated hiring cycles this systematically advantages the unprotected group and disadvantages the protected group.
- **Downstream effects**: Hiring decisions, resource allocation (Interviewer, Hiring Manager time), and organizational composition become skewed toward club members. Perceived or actual unfairness can erode trust, invite legal or regulatory scrutiny (e.g., disparate-treatment claims), and reduce the quality of the talent pool by overweighting affiliation over pure assessed competence.
- **Process integrity**: Because the adjustment is logged and applied after objective assessments (SkillTest, PersonalityAssessment), it undermines the claim that the process is merit-based. Removing or neutralizing the association boost (or making any legitimate, job-related criteria equally available and transparently applied to all candidates) would restore consistency between the two groups.

In short, the bias is structural and explicit in Group B’s scoring rule; Group A’s log shows the neutral baseline against which that bias is visible.
