---

### **Case 2:  SEVERE VIOLATIONS (Two distinct rule breaches)**

**Violation A — Ordering Violation:**
- **Issue:** Risk Assessment (08:20) occurred *before* Credit Check (09:00).
- **Rule broken:** "Credit Check must occur before Risk Assessment."
- **Impact:** This is a control-flow violation — the risk evaluation may have been performed without complete credit information, potentially compromising the integrity of the decision.

**Violation B — Contradictory Action Violation:**
- **Issue:** Finalize Documentation (10:00) occurred *after* Loan Decision (Rejected) (09:30).
- **Rule broken:** "If loan is rejected, do not finalize documentation before closing the case" / "Finalize Documentation must not occur after a rejection."
- **Impact:** This is a serious logical contradiction — documentation finalization implies approval-related paperwork, which is inconsistent with a rejected outcome. This could indicate a data entry error, a system bug, or an actual process breakdown (e.g., staff finalizing the wrong case).

**Additional observation:** Case 2 is also missing an explicit "Inform Applicant" activity between the Loan Decision and Close Case — though this wasn't flagged in the log annotations, it is required by the sequence and its absence should be noted as a potential third violation (skipped activity).

### **Case 3:  COMPLIANT (Normatively)**

## 3. Interpretations & Justifications

### **Case 1:**
No interpretation needed — textbook execution. This case can serve as a benchmark/reference model for conformance checking.

### **Case 2:**
- **Possible explanation for ordering violation:** This could result from **parallel processing by different systems/departments** (e.g., a risk team pulling preliminary data before the credit bureau report was formally logged), or a **logging/timestamp error** where the credit check was performed in a backend system before being recorded.
- **Possible explanation for Finalize Documentation after rejection:** This might indicate:
  - A **system automation error** (e.g., a workflow trigger fired for the wrong outcome branch).
  - **Human error** — staff mistakenly performing an approval-track action on a rejected case.
  - A **data quality issue** in the event log (e.g., mislabeled Case ID or copy-paste error from another case).
- **Recommendation:** This case should be flagged for **manual audit** given the compliance risk — finalizing documentation on a rejected loan could have legal/financial implications (e.g., accidentally issuing loan documents to a rejected applicant).

### **Case 3:**
- **Possible explanation for delays:** The gap between Application Received and Credit Check (Jan 12 to Jan 14) suggests this may have spanned a **weekend or public holiday**, causing processing delays common in real-world banking operations.
- **Possible explanation for delayed applicant notification:** The 3-hour overshoot beyond the 48-hour window is marginal and could be due to **applicant unavailability** (e.g., call not answered, rescheduled communication) rather than process failure.
- **Missing Follow-up Call:** This may reflect a **resource constraint** (e.g., high case volume) or a **risk-based triage** where low-risk approved cases skip the follow-up step. Since it's a desideratum, this is acceptable but could be revisited for **process improvement** (e.g., automating follow-up reminders).
