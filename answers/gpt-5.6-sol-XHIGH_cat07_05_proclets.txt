The figure presents a process-modeling example in three parts:

1. **Workflow interaction (a):**  
   Two process classes—**Lab visit** and **Lab test**—are shown as linked workflows.
   - In **Lab visit**, a doctor enters an order, a lab assistant takes a blood sample, receives and judges the results, and either accepts them (“Everything is fine”) or requests additional tests. Updated results can be received before the final report is sent.
   - In **Lab test**, a chemical analyst performs the test, creates a report, and either finishes the test or repeats it and provides an updated report.
   - The workflows communicate through two dashed channel areas: **Order system**, which transfers the initial test order, and **HIS**, which transfers results, repeat-test requests, and updated reports. Small labels at channel endpoints indicate communication multiplicities.
   - Circular symbols mark starts, decisions/merges, and completion; nearby person icons identify the responsible roles.

2. **Class diagram (b):**  
   A **Lab visit** “requires” **Lab test** instances. Each lab test belongs to exactly one visit (`1..1`), while a visit requires one or more tests (`1..*`).

3. **Performative example (c):**  
   A table gives a sample message sent at **11:00** through the **Order system**. The sender is a lab-visit instance for John, the receiver is an HGB lab-test instance, and the action is **Create** with the content:  
   *“Can you perform a HGB test for John?”*  
   The message is marked **Private** and directed **OUT**.
