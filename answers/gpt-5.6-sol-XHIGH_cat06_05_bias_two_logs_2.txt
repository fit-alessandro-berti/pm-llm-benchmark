## Finding

**Group B exhibits bias.** Its process adds **+5 to cultural-fit scores** for members of the *LocalBusinessLeadersClub*, while Group A candidates receive no comparable opportunity for adjustment.

## How the bias manifests

- **Unequal scoring rule:** Club members U001 and U003 receive a five-point boost unrelated to their demonstrated skill or personality scores.
- **Group-correlated advantage:** All Group A candidates are non-local and have no association membership, while the candidates receiving the boost are in the unprotected/local group. Thus, association membership can function as a **proxy for group status, locality, social network, or socioeconomic access**.
- **Potential effect on decisions:**  
  - U003’s cultural-fit score rises from 58 to 63 and the candidate is hired despite relatively low underlying scores: Skill 75, Personality 65, raw Cultural Fit 58.
  - P002 receives no adjustment and is not hired with stronger or comparable underlying scores: Skill 78, Personality 75, Cultural Fit 60.
  
  The logs do not reveal the exact decision formula, so they do not prove that the boost alone caused U003’s hiring. Nevertheless, they show that candidates are evaluated under different rules based on club affiliation.

## Why outcome rates do not eliminate the concern

Both groups happen to have the same observed hiring rate:

- Group A: 2 of 3 hired
- Group B: 2 of 3 hired

However, equal aggregate selection rates do not establish fairness, especially with only three cases per group. Within Group B:

- Club members: 2 of 2 hired
- Nonmembers: 0 of 1 hired

The more important issue is **procedural and counterfactual fairness**: an otherwise identical candidate could receive a different score solely because of club membership.

## Fairness and equity implications

This policy may:

- Favor candidates with access to influential local networks.
- Disadvantage protected or non-local candidates who lack access to the club.
- Reinforce existing social and socioeconomic inequalities.
- Turn a subjective “cultural fit” measure into a mechanism for affinity or homophily bias.
- Create indirect or proxy discrimination even if protected status is not explicitly used.

A fairer process would remove the automatic association boost, use validated job-related criteria, apply identical scoring rules to all candidates, and audit both raw and adjusted scores for group disparities. Thus, **Group B’s association-based adjustment is the biased feature, even though the small sample’s overall hiring rates appear equal.**
