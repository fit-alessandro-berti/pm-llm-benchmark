The visualization is a **BPMN-style cross-functional process diagram** showing how a customer order moves through four organizational areas:

1. **Sales**
2. **Production**
3. **Procurement**
4. **Warehouse**

### Process flow

- The process begins in **Sales** on Monday morning.
- Sales checks the order for completeness and verifies whether the customer already exists in the database.
  - If not, the customer is added.
  - The database is updated.
- Sales then sends the order details to **Production** and waits for the product to be sent before drafting an invoice.

### Production

- Production receives the order details.
- Work is split into parallel activities:
  - Production planning and manufacturing
  - A material-related path
- If materials are faulty or unavailable, the process interacts with **Procurement**.
- Once manufacturing is complete, a “Product complete” message is sent to the warehouse.

### Procurement

- Procurement receives the order details and runs a **Material Compensation Subprocess**:
  - Materials are ordered.
  - Materials may be returned if necessary.
- Procurement checks the materials.
  - Successful material processing sends a notification onward.
  - A failure path can trigger **Order cancellation**.

### Warehouse

- The warehouse receives the “Product complete” message.
- It sends the product.
- A “Product sent” message is returned to Sales.

### Completion

- After receiving confirmation that the product was sent, Sales drafts the invoice and ends the process.

The diagram uses **message flows** between departments, **exclusive gateways** for decisions, **parallel gateways** for simultaneous work, **message events** for communications, and a **database data store** for customer information.
