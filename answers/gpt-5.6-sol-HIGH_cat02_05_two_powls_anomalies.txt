## Normative reference

For a successful-hire case, the main causal sequence would normally be:

`Post_Job_Ad  Screen_Candidates  Conduct_Interviews  Make_Hiring_Decision  Onboard_Employee  Add_to_Payroll  Close_Case`

The key point is not necessarily that every activity must be totally sequential, but that:

- screening precedes interviewing,
- both screening and interviewing inform the hiring decision,
- onboarding occurs only after a positive decision,
- a hired employee is added to payroll,
- the case is closed only after all required work is complete.

## Model 1

The defined relations include:

- `Post  Screen`
- `Screen  Interview`
- `Screen  Decide`
- `Decide  Onboard  Payroll  Close`

### Main anomaly: interview and decision are unordered

There is no edge from `Interview` to `Decide`. Consequently, they may execute concurrently or in either order.

For example, the model permits:

`Post  Screen  Decide  Onboard  Payroll  Close  Interview`

Thus, the hiring decision can be made before interviews are completed, and the interview can even occur after onboarding, payroll registration, or case closure.

This is a significant business-logic defect because interviews are normally an input to the hiring decision. It also means `Close_Case` is not guaranteed to be the final visible activity.

### Positive aspects

Apart from that missing dependency:

- all expected activities are mandatory,
- each activity occurs once,
- screening is correctly before interviewing,
- onboarding, payroll, and closure are correctly ordered.

A straightforward correction would be to add:

```python
model1.order.add_edge(Interview, Decide)
```

This would induce the expected successful-hire chain.

---

## Model 2

The main chain is:

`Post  Interview  Decide  onboarding loop  optional payroll  Close`

while `Screen` is constrained only by `Post  Screen`.

### 1. Screening is not ordered before interviewing or decision-making

`Screen` and `Interview` are unordered after `Post`. Therefore:

- interviewing can occur before screening,
- the hiring decision can occur before screening is complete,
- screening can even occur after onboarding or case closure.

For example, the following behavior is permitted:

`Post  Interview  Decide  Onboard  Close  Screen`

This violates the purpose of screening as an eligibility and suitability gate.

### 2. Onboarding can be repeated arbitrarily

For:

```python
LOOP(Onboard, skip)
```

the loop body `Onboard` executes at least once, and the silent redo branch allows it to execute repeatedly. Visible behavior can therefore include:

`Onboard  Onboard  Onboard  ...`

Repeating the entire onboarding activity without a visible rework or correction step is generally undesirable. It could lead to duplicate employee records, accounts, documents, training assignments, or benefit enrollments. If onboarding rework is intended, it should normally be represented explicitly rather than as an unrestricted silent repetition.

### 3. Payroll is optional

The XOR:

```python
XOR(Payroll, skip)
```

allows the process to proceed to `Close_Case` without executing `Add_to_Payroll`.

For a successfully hired and onboarded employee, this is a severe integrity problem: the person may start employment without being registered for payment. The skip is also not a proper representation of a rejected candidate, because onboarding has already occurred mandatorily.

### 4. Closure is not globally final

Although `Close` follows the payroll XOR, it does not depend on `Screen`. Hence the case can be closed while screening is still outstanding.

---

## Shared modeling issue

If these models are intended to cover both accepted and rejected candidates, both lack an explicit outcome choice after `Make_Hiring_Decision`. A fuller model would resemble:

- positive decision: `Onboard  Payroll  Close`
- negative decision: rejection notification or direct closure

If the models represent only successful-hire cases, this omission is less important. Model 2's optional payroll still does not correctly represent rejection because onboarding is mandatory before that choice.

## Conclusion

**Model 1 is closer to the normative Hire-to-Retire process.**

Its missing `Interview  Decide` dependency is serious because it permits hiring before interviews are complete. Nevertheless, the rest of its process is coherent: every expected activity occurs once, and onboarding, payroll, and closure follow the proper order.

Model 2 has several independent deviations:

1. screening can occur after interviewing or the decision,
2. onboarding can repeat arbitrarily,
3. payroll can be skipped,
4. closure can happen before screening.

In particular, allowing an onboarded employee to bypass payroll is a fundamental process-integrity failure. Therefore, Model 1 requires one central precedence correction, while Model 2 requires multiple structural and control-flow changes.
