## 2. Identifying Problem Cases

**Clear outliers:** Cases **2003** and **2005** (High complexity) show dramatically longer durations (48–77 hours) compared to Low complexity cases (2001, 2004: ~1.5 hours). Case 2002 (Medium) falls in between (~26 hours).

**Key pattern:** Duration scales strongly with **Complexity**:
- Low  ~1.5 hrs
- Medium  ~26 hrs
- High  48–77 hrs

## 3. Root Cause Analysis

### A. Complexity Driving "Request Additional Documents" Loops
- Low-complexity cases (2001, 2004): **zero** document requests  straight-through processing.
- Medium case (2002): **1** document request  adds ~1 day.
- High cases (2003, 2005): **2–3** document requests  adds 2–4 days.

**Root cause:** High-complexity claims require more back-and-forth for documentation, and each request cycle introduces significant idle time (waiting on customer/adjuster availability), not just processing time. In Case 2005, three sequential requests (each ~1 day apart) account for the majority of the 77-hour lead time.

### B. Resource Correlation
- **Adjuster_Lisa** handles both Case 2002 (Medium, 1 request) and Case 2005 (High, 3 requests) — she appears linked to cases needing more documentation, but this correlates with complexity assignment rather than being an independent driver (she also handles the fast Low case 2004).
- **Manager_Bill** approves both high-complexity cases (2003, 2005), each with a long delay between last event and approval (13–19 hours), suggesting **approval is a bottleneck** for high-complexity claims, possibly due to more thorough review or lower availability.
- **Manager_Ann** approves Low/Medium cases almost immediately after evaluation/documentation — no bottleneck observed.

**Root cause:** Manager_Bill's approval step consistently introduces long wait times for high-complexity claims — could be due to limited availability, escalation requirements, or manual review overhead for high-risk claims.

### C. Region
- Region A: one fast (2001-Low) and one slow (2003-High) case.
- Region B: one fast (2004-Low), one medium (2002), one very slow (2005-High).
- Region does not show a consistent independent effect — durations are explained by complexity, not region. Region is likely a confounding, not causal, factor here.

## 4. Summary of Root Causes

| Factor | Impact | Evidence |
|--------|--------|----------|
| **Complexity (High)** | Primary driver | Triggers multiple "Request Additional Documents" cycles, each adding ~1 day of waiting |
| **Approval step (Manager_Bill)** | Secondary bottleneck | 13–19 hour gaps before approval in high-complexity cases |
| **Resource (Adjuster_Lisa)** | Correlated, not causal | Handles more document-heavy cases, but this reflects complexity routing, not inefficiency |
| **Region** | No significant independent effect | Slow and fast cases exist in both regions |

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Streamline documentation requests for high-complexity claims:**
   - Request all necessary documents in a single consolidated request rather than iterative rounds.
   - Set SLAs/reminders to reduce customer response lag between requests.

2. **Address the approval bottleneck:**
   - Investigate Manager_Bill's workload/availability for high-complexity approvals.
   - Consider parallel or delegated approval authority for complex claims to avoid single-point delays.

3. **Segment process by complexity:**
   - Create a differentiated workflow (e.g., "fast track" for Low, structured checklist-driven track for High) so document/approval needs are anticipated upfront rather than discovered iteratively.

4. **Monitor resource-complexity assignment:**
   - Track whether certain adjusters are systematically assigned harder cases, and ensure they have adequate support/tools to avoid becoming inadvertent bottlenecks.

5. **Set duration-based alerts:**
   - Flag any case exceeding an expected threshold (e.g., >24 hours) after the second document request for managerial follow-up.

**Conclusion:** The dominant root cause of extended case durations is **claim complexity**, which drives repeated documentation cycles and elongates the timeline before approval. The **approval step under Manager_Bill** compounds this delay for high-complexity claims specifically. Region shows no independent effect, and resource effects appear to be a byproduct of complexity-based case routing rather than a standalone inefficiency.
