A typical P2P “happy path” is:

1. Create Purchase Requisition  
2. Approve Purchase Requisition  
3. Issue Purchase Order  
4. Receive Goods/Services  
5. Receive Vendor Invoice  
6. Match Invoice to PO (often including goods receipt: three-way match)  
7. Release Payment  
8. Close Case  

Some variation can be legitimate—for example, an invoice may arrive before goods—but key controls should remain: requisitions should be approved before a PO is issued, and payment should normally follow invoice validation/matching and, usually, receipt of goods/services.

## Case 1001 — Normal / broadly compliant

Sequence:

- Requisition created and approved
- PO issued
- Goods received
- Invoice received
- Invoice matched to PO
- Payment released
- Case closed

This follows the expected sequence and demonstrates standard control points. No obvious anomaly is present.

## Case 1002 — Multiple serious control violations

Sequence:

1. Create Purchase Requisition  
2. **Issue Purchase Order**  
3. Vendor Invoice Received  
4. **Release Payment**  
5. **Approve Purchase Requisition**  
6. Receive Goods  
7. Match Invoice to PO  
8. Close Case  

### Anomaly 1: Purchase order issued before requisition approval
The PO is issued at 08:40, but the requisition is approved only on February 3 at 08:00.

**Why problematic:**
- It bypasses the approval control intended to ensure the purchase is authorized.
- It may indicate unauthorized spending, weak delegation controls, emergency buying without documentation, or an attempt to circumvent budget approval.
- In standard practice, approval should generally precede commitment to a vendor through a PO.

### Anomaly 2: Payment released before approval, receipt, and invoice match
Payment is released on February 3 at 07:15, before:
- Requisition approval (08:00),
- Goods receipt (February 4),
- Invoice-to-PO matching (February 5).

**Why problematic:**
- Payment occurs without evidence that the purchase was authorized.
- Payment occurs before confirmation that goods or services were received.
- Payment occurs before invoice validation against the PO and receipt record.
- This creates risk of paying for undelivered goods, duplicate or fraudulent invoices, inaccurate amounts, or payments to an incorrect vendor.

This is the strongest anomaly in the log.

## Case 1003 — Payment made before invoice receipt, goods receipt, and matching

Sequence:

1. Create Purchase Requisition  
2. Approve Purchase Requisition  
3. Issue Purchase Order  
4. **Release Payment**  
5. Vendor Invoice Received  
6. Receive Goods  
7. Match Invoice to PO  
8. Close Case  

### Anomaly: Payment precedes core validation events
Payment is released on February 8, but:
- The invoice is received on February 9,
- Goods are received on February 10,
- The invoice is matched on February 11.

**Why problematic:**
- A payment normally requires an invoice or another formally approved payment basis.
- Payment before goods receipt exposes the organization to non-delivery risk.
- Payment before matching means there is no documented confirmation that the invoice, PO, and goods receipt agree on price, quantity, and terms.
- Although prepayments or deposits can be legitimate in certain contracts, they should be explicitly authorized and handled through a controlled prepayment process. Nothing in the event log indicates such an exception.

The requisition and PO portion is normal, but the payment sequence is anomalous.

---

## Case 1004 — Goods received before the purchase order is issued

Sequence:

1. Create Purchase Requisition  
2. Approve Purchase Requisition  
3. **Receive Goods**  
4. **Issue Purchase Order**  
5. Vendor Invoice Received  
6. Match Invoice to PO  
7. Release Payment  
8. Close Case  

### Anomaly: Goods receipt occurs before PO issuance
Goods are received at 09:00 on February 6, while the PO is issued at 09:15 on the same day.

**Why problematic:**
- Normally, the PO is the formal authorization sent to the supplier before goods are ordered and delivered.
- Receiving goods before issuing a PO may indicate a “after-the-fact PO,” sometimes called a confirming PO. This can occur when employees order directly from suppliers and create the PO later merely to regularize the transaction.
- It weakens procurement controls, including supplier selection, pricing validation, budget controls, and authorization requirements.
- It may also impair the integrity of the three-way matching process because the PO did not exist when the supply commitment was made.

The remaining sequence—invoice, match, payment, closure—is in the expected order. The main issue is the late PO.

## Summary of anomalies

| Case ID | Unusual Sequence | Main Risk |
|---|---|---|
| 1001 | No material anomaly | Standard P2P flow |
| 1002 | PO issued before requisition approval; payment made before approval, goods receipt, and invoice matching | Unauthorized purchases, fraud, payment for undelivered or invalid invoices |
| 1003 | Payment made before invoice receipt, goods receipt, and matching | Unvalidated prepayment, non-delivery risk, invoice fraud or error |
| 1004 | Goods received before PO issuance | Retrospective/confirming PO, bypassed procurement and authorization controls |

Overall, Cases **1002, 1003, and 1004** show deviations from standard P2P controls, with **1002** being the most concerning because it combines several control failures in one case.
