**Comprehensive Process Mining Approach to Optimize Resource Assignment at TechSolve Solutions**

As Process Improvement Consultants specializing in ITSM and process mining, we recommend a structured, data-driven methodology leveraging the rich event log (Case ID, Activity, Timestamp with START/COMPLETE semantics, Resource/Agent ID, Agent Tier, Agent Skills, Ticket Priority, Ticket Category, Required Skill, Notes, etc.). The analysis focuses on the resource perspective to diagnose assignment inefficiencies driving SLA breaches (especially P2/P3), reassignments, skill mismatches, and uneven workloads, then prescribe targeted improvements. The approach uses established process mining techniques: multi-perspective process discovery, performance analysis, social network analysis (SNA)/handover-of-work networks, organizational/role mining, variant analysis, decision mining, and resource utilization metrics. Tools such as Celonis, Disco, ProM (with Organizational Mining plugins), or equivalent platforms support these analyses.

### 1. Analyzing Resource Behavior and Assignment Patterns

The event log enables a detailed as-is analysis of individual agents, tiers (L1/L2/L3), and skill utilization, contrasting actual behavior against the intended logic (round-robin within tiers plus manual escalation).

**Key metrics for agents and tiers:**
- **Workload distribution**: Cases handled, total active work time (precise durations derived from Work * Start to End using Timestamp Type START/COMPLETE), concurrent open tickets, and utilization rate (busy time relative to available time/shifts). Visualize via dotted charts (y-axis resources sorted by load, x-axis time, colored by Priority or Required Skill) to reveal overload clusters versus idle periods. Quantify imbalance with variance or Gini coefficient of cases/time across agents within the same tier.
- **Activity processing times**: Mean, median, and percentile (P75/P90) handling times for Work L1/L2/L3 activities, segmented by Agent ID, Tier, Priority, Category, and Required Skill. Normalize comparisons for agents with overlapping skills.
- **First-call resolution (FCR) rate for L1**: Percentage of cases that complete without Escalate L2/L3 activities after L1 work. Break down by Category and Required Skill to identify high- versus low-success areas.
- **Frequency of handling specific ticket types/skills**: Per agent, volume and proportion of tickets by Required Skill versus documented Agent Skills. Track escalation and reassignment rates per agent (especially L1) and average number of handovers/reassignments per case.
- **Throughput and contribution to cycle time**: Cases resolved per agent/period and post-assignment waiting times (Assign to Work Start).

**Process mining techniques revealing actual patterns:**
- Multi-perspective process discovery and conformance checking produce resource-annotated models. Replay the log against an idealized hierarchical model (Ticket Created → Assign L1 → Work L1 → Resolve or Escalate L2 → …) to measure deviations.
- Resource interaction analysis and SNA on handovers: Construct a handover-of-work network (nodes = agents or aggregated by Tier/Skills; weighted edges = frequency of direct succession, e.g., after Work End/Escalate/Reassign → next Assign). Metrics include degree centrality (busy agents), betweenness (agents acting as frequent “fixers”), and density of intra-tier versus inter-tier edges. This exposes actual flows—frequent L1→L2 escalations, within-L2 reassignments for skill changes (as in the INC-1001 example), ping-pong patterns, or self-assignments (common on phone-channel tickets)—versus the intended clean tier progression and limited reassignments.
- Role discovery / organizational mining: Cluster agents by activity profiles, skill sets, and behavioral similarity. This may surface informal roles (e.g., some L2 agents functioning as generalists or de-facto specialists) that diverge from documented tiers.
- Trace variant analysis focused on resource sequences and attribute projections (filter by Priority/Category/Required Skill) highlights dominant inefficient paths involving multiple Assign/Reassign steps.

**Actual versus intended assignment logic**:
The intended mix of round-robin (fairness within tier) and manual escalation produces, in practice, skill-ignorant initial assignments, dispatcher delays, self-assignments, and frequent mid-process reassignments when the initial agent’s skills do not match (or when Required Skill is updated). SNA quantifies the deviation (elevated within-tier and cross-skill handovers); performance analysis shows the resulting waiting-time inflation.

**Skill utilization analysis**:
For every Work activity compute a skill-match score (binary intersection or |Agent Skills ∩ {Required Skill}|). Aggregate time spent by specialists (holders of scarce skills such as Networking-Firewall, Database-SQL, Security-IAM) on matching high-skill tickets versus lower-complexity or mismatched work. Compare demand (frequency of each Required Skill) against supply (agents possessing the skill and their capacity). This reveals whether specialists are systematically under-utilized on matching work or overloaded, and whether L1/L2 agents are handling tickets outside their documented capabilities. Attribute filters, process cubes, and resource performance dashboards make these patterns transparent.

Collectively these analyses establish a quantitative baseline of current resource behavior and the gap relative to intended logic.

### 2. Identifying Resource-Related Bottlenecks and Issues

Building directly on the metrics and networks above, targeted filtering, performance analysis, and cohort comparison isolate concrete problems and quantify their impact.

- **Skill-availability bottlenecks**: Filter cases by Required Skill and measure waiting time from Assign to Work Start (or queue time at specialist pools). Dotted charts and performance spectra colored by skill expose queues for scarce capabilities. Compare average/median waits and throughput against overall baselines and Priority-specific SLA thresholds. Illustrative quantification approach: if Networking-Firewall tickets show markedly higher post-assignment waits and a disproportionate share of P2 breaches, cross-reference with the count of agents possessing that skill versus demand volume.
- **Delays from frequent or unnecessary reassignments/escalations**: Count Reassign and Escalate activities per case; compute the percentage of cases with ≥1 reassignment and the time delta between successive Assignments (or from Reassign to next Work Start). SNA highlights cyclic or high-frequency handovers (including intra-tier versus inter-tier and self-assignment patterns). Quantification: average delay introduced per reassignment (context-switch + re-queue time); relative increase in cycle time and SLA-breach rate for cases with multiple handovers.
- **Impact of incorrect initial assignments**: Derive a skill-mismatch indicator at each Assign L1/L2 event (Agent Skills versus Required Skill at that moment). Measure subsequent time-to-reassign or immediate escalation rate, especially short Work L1 durations followed by Escalate. Self-assignments (phone channel) versus dispatcher assignments can be contrasted. Quantification: percentage of escalations/reassignments occurring within a short window of assignment that correlate strongly with mismatch; wasted effort hours.
- **Overloaded versus underutilized agents/teams**: Aggregate cases, active time, and concurrency per Agent ID/Tier/Skills; apply statistical measures of dispersion. Flag outliers in processing-time distributions (normalized by Category/Priority/Required Skill) and utilization extremes. Dotted charts sorted by resource make imbalances visually immediate. Quantification: share of specialized volume concentrated on a small subset of agents; utilization ranges (e.g., some specialists >80–85 % while peers remain under-loaded).
- **Correlation with SLA breaches**: Derive or use SLA-breach flags (case duration versus Priority thresholds). Segment compliant versus breached cohorts and compare distributions of reassignment count, skill-mismatch indicators, waiting times, escalation paths, and specific agents involved. Process cubes (Priority × Category × skill-match × #handovers) and simple statistical or tree-based importance ranking quantify the contribution of resource factors. Illustrative method: proportion of P2/P3 breaches that involve at least one skill-mismatched assignment or reassignment; relative risk elevation for pure round-robin-style assignments versus skill-matched ones.

Derived attributes (skill_match score, cumulative_handovers, time_to_reassign, proxy workload at assignment) enrich the log and make the above analyses precise and prioritizable. Bottleneck analysis focused on high-wait resource-dependent activities, combined with SNA centrality metrics, surfaces the highest-impact issues first.

### 3. Root Cause Analysis for Assignment Inefficiencies

The observed bottlenecks stem from several interconnected root causes that can be validated and prioritized with the event log.

1. **Deficiencies in current assignment rules**: Round-robin within tiers optimizes volume fairness but ignores skill fit, real-time workload, and ticket complexity/priority. Manual escalation decisions lack consistent criteria. Evidence appears as high post-assignment Reassign rates when Agent Skills do not intersect Required Skill, and as long waits after Assign even when other matching agents had capacity (detectable via concurrent-activity or calendar analysis).

2. **Inaccurate or incomplete agent skill profiles**: Profiles may be outdated, binary rather than proficiency-scaled, or missing experiential nuance. Organizational mining that reveals behavioral clusters diverging from documented skills, together with success-rate cross-tabs (short processing time + no further reassign versus listed skills), quantifies profile quality problems.

3. **Poor initial ticket categorization or skill-requirement identification**: Incomplete or evolving Required Skill / Category fields (illustrated by the mid-case skill change in INC-1001) cause downstream mismatches. High frequency of Required Skill updates and correlation of initial Category/Channel with subsequent reassignments provide direct evidence; text mining on Notes (when available) further illuminates the gap.

4. **Lack of real-time visibility into agent workload and availability**: Dispatchers and L1 agents operate without current queue-length, concurrency, or skill-availability views. Dotted charts and utilization metrics by time-of-day expose resulting overload of some agents while others remain idle, especially during skill-specific peaks.

5. **Insufficient training or empowerment of L1 agents**: Low FCR for particular Categories/Required Skills and high volumes of short-duration Work L1 followed by Escalate indicate premature escalation. Specialists consequently absorb lower-complexity work.

**Variant analysis and decision mining** sharpen the diagnosis. Discover process variants and partition them into “efficient” (few/no reassignments, short cycle time, SLA met, resolved at the lowest appropriate tier) versus “inefficient” (multiple Reassign/Escalate, long waits, breaches). Compare attribute distributions (initial Agent Skills versus Required Skill, Priority, Category, Channel, time of day, number of handovers). This isolates how much performance variance is attributable to assignment quality. At decision points (Escalate after Work L1 End, Reassign, Assign Lx) apply decision mining (decision trees or rule induction) using case and resource attributes as predictors. The discovered rules (e.g., escalate when Required Skill not in Agent Skills and Priority = P2) can be compared with intended policy, revealing suboptimal logic such as time-based rather than skill-gap-based escalation. Additional techniques—correlation of reassignment count with cycle time/SLA outcome, and SNA metrics identifying frequent “fixer” agents—complete the picture. Root causes compound; addressing them requires simultaneous improvements to data quality (profiles, ticket enrichment) and assignment logic.

### 4. Developing Data-Driven Resource Assignment Strategies

Three concrete, complementary strategies directly attack the diagnosed issues. Each is grounded in the process-mining insights above and can be implemented in a modern ITSM platform (or adjacent routing engine) with continuous feedback from the event log.

**Strategy 1: Multi-factor skill-based routing with workload balancing**  
Addresses skill mismatches, uneven workloads, specialist under-/over-utilization, and reassignments caused by rigid round-robin.  
Leverages skill-utilization rates, handover-network mismatch frequencies, workload-distribution variance, and waiting-time statistics by skill.  
Data required: maintained Agent Skills matrix (ideally with proficiency levels), real-time agent status (availability, current open cases or estimated remaining work, shifts), ticket Required Skill (enriched at creation), Priority, and historical average handling times per skill/agent.  
Implementation: compute a composite score for each eligible agent, e.g.,  
Score = (skill_match_score × w1) + ((1 − normalized_current_load) × w2) + priority_affinity_factor.  
Assign to the highest-scoring available agent in the appropriate tier (or a flexible skill pool). Start with rules derived from decision mining and refine weights via simulation.  
Expected benefits: substantial reduction in reassignments (target 40–60 %), shorter waiting times for specialized tickets, improved P2/P3 SLA compliance, higher proportion of specialist time spent on matching work, and more balanced utilization.

**Strategy 2: Predictive ticket classification and intelligent initial assignment**  
Addresses poor initial categorization/skill identification and incorrect first assignments that trigger early escalations and reassignments.  
Leverages variant analysis (features distinguishing efficient versus multi-reassignment paths) and decision-mining predictors of required skill changes or successful first assignment.  
Data required: historical cases with creation attributes (Category, Priority, Channel), any free-text Notes/description for NLP features, final Required Skill / resolution path, and outcome labels (resolved without reassignment, SLA met, tier of resolution).  
Implementation: at Ticket Created, a classifier (or rules extracted by decision mining) predicts the most probable Required Skill(s), complexity tier, and recommended agent pool. Route automatically when confidence is high; surface a ranked suggestion to the dispatcher/L1 otherwise. Case-similarity search can further recommend “agents who successfully handled similar tickets.”  
Expected benefits: higher first-assignment accuracy, fewer mid-process skill updates and reassignments, reduced cycle time, and better targeting of L1 effort.

**Strategy 3: Data-driven escalation criteria and L1 enablement**  
Addresses excessive escalations, specialists handling low-complexity work, and low FCR.  
Leverages FCR rates by Category/Required Skill, short-handling-time escalations, successful L1 resolution variants, and discovered decision rules.  
Data required: historical L1-resolved versus escalated cases with attributes and step sequences; knowledge-article usage if available; continuous outcome feedback.  
Implementation: for each new ticket compute similarity to past successful L1 variants and surface guided diagnostic steps or a confidence score for L1 handling. Revise escalation policy to “escalate only if skill gap is material *and* similarity to successful L1 cases is low, *or* a dynamic time threshold is exceeded.” Pair with targeted training/playbooks extracted from high-FCR variants and auto-suggested knowledge. Continuously retrain thresholds from fresh process-mining results.  
Expected benefits: measurable FCR uplift (illustratively 15–25 % on target categories), reduced L2/L3 load on routine work, faster overall resolution, improved higher-tier skill utilization, and higher L1 capability and satisfaction.

These strategies are synergistic and can be rolled out in phases (visibility and basic skill-aware routing first, then predictive enrichment and L1 enablement). A fourth complementary capability—dynamic capacity reallocation (temporarily flexing agents across skill pools based on demand forecasts derived from historical arrival patterns)—can be added once the core routing engine is stable.

### 5. Simulation, Implementation, and Monitoring

**Business-process simulation** evaluates strategies before live deployment. Construct a discrete-event simulation model from the mined process model (control-flow plus resource perspective), parameterized by log-derived distributions: arrival rates by Priority/Category/Required Skill, processing-time distributions conditioned on skill-match quality and agent, resource pools defined by Tier and Skills with calendars/availability, and the current versus proposed assignment logic. Tools that natively support process-model simulation (e.g., Celonis Process Simulation) or general packages (AnyLogic, Arena) populated with mined statistics are suitable.  

Run controlled what-if experiments: baseline (round-robin + manual) versus each strategy (and combinations), varying parameters such as skill-match strictness, workload thresholds, predictive accuracy, and L1 success probability. Primary output KPIs include average/median cycle time, SLA compliance by Priority, average reassignments/handovers per case, resource-utilization variance (or Gini), percentage of specialist time on matching work, and waiting-time contribution. Sensitivity analysis quantifies robustness; the results supply expected impact ranges and risk bounds that inform prioritization and change-management messaging.

**Implementation outline**:  
- Enrich and govern master data (skill profiles with proficiency, ticket Required Skill at creation).  
- Integrate the chosen routing logic into the ITSM platform (real-time agent status feed, scoring engine, or ML service).  
- Pilot on a bounded scope (selected Categories or Priorities), measure against the simulation baseline, then expand.  
- Conduct change management (dispatcher training, L1 enablement, transparent communication of new fairness criteria).  
- Establish a continuous feedback loop: new event data continuously refresh the process-mining models and retrain predictive components.

**Post-implementation monitoring with process-mining dashboards** ensures sustained performance and detects drift. Stream the live event log into the process-mining platform and maintain the following resource-centric KPIs and views:

- Skill-match rate at initial assignment (% cases where Agent Skills intersect Required Skill).  
- Reassignment rate and average number of handovers per case (overall and by Priority).  
- FCR rate (L1 and end-to-end).  
- Workload balance (utilization variance/Gini, maximum concurrent cases).  
- Waiting time after assignment, segmented by skill and Priority.  
- SLA compliance by Priority, with attribution to assignment-quality indicators.  
- Specialist utilization on matching versus non-matching work.  
- Escalation rates (and coded reasons where available).

Supporting process views: live resource-annotated process model, periodically refreshed handover SNA, dotted charts for load visibility, performance spectrum for emerging bottlenecks, variant explorer focused on recent multi-reassignment cases, and skill demand-versus-supply heatmaps. Operational alerts trigger on rising mismatch or reassignment rates or on skill-specific queue growth.  

Review cadence: weekly operational dashboards for dispatchers and team leads; monthly deeper variant and decision-mining reviews for continuous refinement of scoring weights, prediction models, and escalation thresholds. This closed-loop use of process mining turns the original diagnostic capability into an ongoing management system for resource assignment excellence.

By systematically applying these process-mining techniques to the event log, TechSolve can replace opaque, inefficient assignment practices with transparent, data-driven logic that measurably reduces SLA breaches, eliminates unnecessary reassignments, balances workload, and deploys specialist skills where they create the greatest value.
