# Bias Analysis: Community/Geographic Scoring Adjustments

## 1. Explicit Bias Mechanism: The "Community" Adjustment

The most visible bias source is the **+10 point ScoreAdjustment** applied specifically to members of "Highland Civic Darts Club" (C001, C004). This is a **non-financial, affinity-based adjustment** that has nothing to do with creditworthiness, income, repayment history, or risk — yet it directly alters the PreliminaryScore and flows through to the final decision.

**Key evidence — C004 is the smoking gun case:**
- C004 has a *raw* score of **690** — the lowest in the entire log
- With the +10 community bonus, it's adjusted to **700** and ultimately **Approved**
- Meanwhile, C003 has a raw score of **715** (25 points higher than C004's raw score) and is **Rejected**

This means an applicant with objectively weaker underlying credit metrics was approved *solely* because of club membership, while an applicant with stronger fundamentals was denied. If C004 did not belong to the Highland Civic Darts Club, their unadjusted score (690) would likely have resulted in rejection too — the community affiliation appears to be the deciding factor, not creditworthiness.

## 2. The LocalResident Attribute — A Potential Hidden Proxy

Notice the correlation in the data:
- All **TRUE** LocalResident cases (C001, C002, C004)  **Approved**
- Both **FALSE** LocalResident cases (C003, C005)  Mixed (C003 Rejected, C005 Approved)

While C005 (FALSE, 740) was approved on merit, C003 (FALSE, 715) was rejected despite a *higher* raw score than C004 (690, TRUE, Approved). This suggests **LocalResident status may correlate with — or serve as a proxy for — geographic/residency-based discrimination**, even if it's not a direct scoring input. Geographic residency requirements can function as an indirect proxy for socioeconomic status, race, or historical redlining patterns, which raises fair-lending concerns (e.g., ECOA, Fair Housing Act implications in a U.S. context).

## 3. Structural Fairness Problems

| Issue | Impact |
|---|---|
| **Non-merit-based bonus** | The "Community" adjustment rewards social/organizational affiliation rather than financial risk indicators |
| **Arbitrary group selection** | Only one specific community group (Highland Civic Darts Club) appears to qualify — why this group and not others? This is an **unexplained eligibility criterion** that could mask preferential treatment (e.g., insider connections, local politics) |
| **Reviewer inconsistency** | Different reviewers (#2, #3, #4, #5, #7) handle cases with no visible standardized rubric — introducing potential inter-reviewer bias alongside the algorithmic bias |
| **Cascading bias** | Because ManualReview inherits the already-adjusted score, human reviewers may unconsciously anchor to the boosted number rather than re-evaluating raw creditworthiness |

## 4. Equity Implications

**Who is disadvantaged?**
- Applicants who are **not part of favored local organizations** (regardless of actual financial risk) are structurally denied a 10-point boost that could be the difference between approval and rejection
- **Non-local or newly-relocated applicants** may be systematically excluded from community bonuses simply because they haven't had time/opportunity to join local civic groups
- This creates a **de facto "insider advantage"** — people embedded in specific local social networks get preferential underwriting outcomes unrelated to ability to repay

**Disparate impact risk:**
If membership in "Highland Civic Darts Club" correlates with any protected characteristic (e.g., age, national origin, socioeconomic homogeneity of the club, long-term residency), this adjustment could produce **disparate impact discrimination** — even without explicit intent — because it systematically advantages a narrow, non-diverse social group.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Remove or heavily scrutinize the community affiliation bonus** — audit whether "Highland Civic Darts Club" membership correlates with protected classes
2. **Decouple LocalResident status from decisioning** unless it has a documented, defensible, non-discriminatory risk correlation
3. **Conduct disparate impact testing**: compare approval rates by community-group membership status, controlling for raw PreliminaryScore
4. **Require adjustment justification logging**: any non-standard score adjustment should require documented rationale tied to actual credit risk factors
5. **Re-run decisions using only raw PreliminaryScore** to see how many outcomes flip — this would quantify the bias's real-world impact (as shown starkly by the C003/C004 comparison)

---

**Bottom line:** The event log shows a clear, quantifiable instance where a community-based bonus overrode raw creditworthiness rankings, producing an outcome (C004 approved with 690 raw score) that contradicts a rejection of a higher-scoring applicant (C003, 715, no community bonus). This is direct evidence that affiliation — not risk — is influencing final decisions for at least a subset of cases.
