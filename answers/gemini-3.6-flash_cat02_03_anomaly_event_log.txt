Based on standard business practices and internal control frameworks (such as SOX compliance and three-way matching principles) for the **Procure-to-Pay (P2P)** process, here is a detailed analysis of the event log.

### **Overview of Findings**
* **Case 1001:** **Standard Process** (Compliant baseline).
* **Case 1002:** **Critical Anomaly** (Bypassed approvals, payment before goods receipt/matching, retroactive approval).
* **Case 1003:** **Major Anomaly** (Premature payment before invoice receipt, goods delivery, or matching).
* **Case 1004:** **Moderate Anomaly** (Maverick buying / After-the-fact Purchase Order).

---

### **Detailed Analysis by Case**

#### **1. Case 1001 — Standard / Compliant Process**
* **Sequence:** `Create PR` $\rightarrow$ `Approve PR` $\rightarrow$ `Issue PO` $\rightarrow$ `Receive Goods` $\rightarrow$ `Vendor Invoice Received` $\rightarrow$ `Match Invoice to PO` $\rightarrow$ `Release Payment` $\rightarrow$ `Close Case`
* **Assessment:** **No anomalies.** This case represents the textbook baseline P2P flow. Internal controls are maintained at every stage: approval precedes PO issuance, goods and invoices are received prior to matching, and payment is released only after successful verification.

---

#### **2. Case 1002 — Critical Control Failures & Out-of-Sequence Payment**
* **Anomalies Identified:**
  1. **PO Issued Before PR Approval:** The Purchase Order was issued on `2024-02-01 08:40`, but the Requisition was not approved until `2024-02-03 08:00`.
  2. **Payment Released Before Receipt & Matching:** Payment was released (`2024-02-03 07:15`) prior to PR Approval, Goods Receipt, or Invoice Matching.
  3. **Retroactive Approval & Late Matching:** Matching (`2024-02-05 08:00`) and Goods Receipt (`2024-02-04 09:00`) occurred *after* funds had already left the company.
* **Why It Is Problematic:**
  * **Financial Risk & Loss:** Paying a vendor before receiving and inspecting goods exposes the organization to paying for damaged, incorrect, or non-existent items, with limited leverage to recover funds.
  * **Internal Control Failure:** Releasing payment prior to invoice matching defeats the purpose of internal controls (3-way match). 
  * **Fraud & Unauthorized Spend Risk:** Issuing POs and disbursing cash before PR approval bypasses budget authorization controls, increasing the risk of fraud or unapproved spending.

---

#### **3. Case 1003 — Premature Payment / Bypassed Three-Way Matching**
* **Anomalies Identified:**
  1. **Payment Released Prior to Invoice and Goods Receipt:** Payment was released on `2024-02-08 11:00`, while the Vendor Invoice was received on `2024-02-09`, Goods were received on `2024-02-10`, and Invoice Matching occurred on `2024-02-11`.
* **Why It Is Problematic:**
  * **Violation of Three-Way Matching:** The basic prerequisite for accounts payable (AP) disbursement is performing a three-way match (matching the PO, Goods Receipt, and Vendor Invoice) *before* releasing funds.
  * **Working Capital & Dispute Risks:** Paying upfront without an agreed-upon prepayment contract harms cash flow optimization (working capital) and removes buyer leverage if the vendor delivers short or defective shipments.

---

#### **4. Case 1004 — Maverick Buying / After-the-Fact PO Creation**
* **Anomalies Identified:**
  1. **Goods Received Before PO Issuance:** Goods were received on `2024-02-06 09:00`, but the Purchase Order was issued 15 minutes later (`2024-02-06 09:15`).
* **Why It Is Problematic:**
  * **"Maverick Spending":** Placing orders with vendors informally before a legal contract/PO is established prevents procurement teams from negotiating optimal pricing, payment terms, or legal protections.
  * **Liability & Audit Risk:** Receiving goods without an active PO creates potential legal disputes regarding pricing, commitments, and liability for damaged goods in transit.
