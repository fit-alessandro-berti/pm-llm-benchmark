**1. Identifying Instance-Spanning Constraints and Their Impact**

Instance-spanning constraints couple distinct cases (orders) through limited shared resources, synchronization (batching), preemption, or global cardinalities. Process mining on the provided event log (with START/COMPLETE timestamps, resource IDs, Order Type, Destination Region, Requires Cold Packing, Hazardous flags) enables formal discovery and quantification via filtering/projection, performance annotation, multi-perspective analysis, and queue reconstruction. Techniques include process discovery (e.g., inductive miner or heuristic miner on activity sequences) for the control-flow backbone; performance analysis with lifecycle transitions; organizational mining/resource utilization views; temporal alignment to reconstruct state occupancy; attribute-based slicing (filter cases by cold-packing=TRUE, Hazardous=TRUE, Order Type=Express, or by Destination Region); and waiting-time breakdowns that contrast sojourn, processing, and waiting times.

- **Shared Cold-Packing stations**: Filter cases where Requires Cold Packing=TRUE. Group packing events by resource (e.g., Station C*) and reconstruct occupancy intervals from START/COMPLETE pairs. Detect contention whenever a case’s Packing START is delayed after its Item Picking COMPLETE while cold stations are fully occupied (all 5 busy). Quantify via: (a) mean/median/percentile waiting time in cold-packing queue; (b) utilization and blocking probability of the limited pool; (c) fraction of total cycle time attributable to cold-resource waits. Impact is measured as excess delay relative to standard stations and its correlation with peak-period throughput loss.

- **Shipping Batches**: Group cases by Destination Region. For each case, measure the lag between Quality Check COMPLETE and Shipping Label Generation. Identify co-batch members as those whose QC COMPLETE timestamps fall inside a common window preceding a shared Label-Gen event (or System (Batch Bx) annotation). Metrics: (a) batch formation delay (time from first ready order until trigger); (b) individualized waiting time for batch completion; (c) batch size vs. delay trade-off and impact on end-to-end time and SLA misses by region.

- **Priority Handling (Express)**: Identify Express cases and examine resource hand-over or interruption events. Detect preemption by locating a Standard case whose Packing/QC interval is interrupted (resource marked busy on another case mid-interval or abrupt COMPLETE then re-START after Express arrival). Metrics: (a) frequency and duration of preemptions; (b) added waiting/resume-setup time imposed on Standard orders; (c) “express-induced delay tax” on non-express cycle times and landings of that delay on overall SLA.

- **Hazardous Material Limits**: Reconstruct the global concurrent count of Hazardous=TRUE cases whose Packing or Quality Check intervals overlap (using START/COMPLETE). Flag any interval where the cardinality exceeds 10. Metrics: (a) occupancy time series and max concurrency; (b) frequency/duration of limit-induced queueing (cases ready but held below the threshold); (c) throughput reduction (effective packing/QC capacity throttled) and the contribution of this constraint to facility-wide waiting.

**Differentiating within-instance vs between-instance waiting**. Within-instance waiting/delay is pure activity duration (COMPLETE – START of the same case’s activity) or sequence-internal idle time independent of other cases. Between-instance waiting is the residual time from a case becoming enablement-ready (previous activity COMPLETE and all intra-case guards satisfied) until its own activity START, after subtracting pure service time. Attribution proceeds as follows:

- Reconstruct the global state at each timestamp: set of active resources, queue of enabled cases per activity/resource pool, concurrent hazmat count, open batch buffers per region.
- For every waiting interval compute explanatory labels: “cold-station busy by OrderX”, “batch incomplete for Region Y (awaiting N more)”, “preempted/suspended for Express OrderZ”, “hazmat concurrency at N10”.
- Aggregate statistics separately (e.g., “cold-contention wait time”, “batch synchronization wait”) and contrast with pure processing time distributions. Multi-perspective filtering (only cold or only hazmat cases) and concurrent-instance visualizations further isolate the between-case component. This distinction is essential: classical single-case process mining conflates the two, under-estimating the leverage of multi-instance policy changes.

**2. Analyzing Constraint Interactions**

The constraints interact and can amplify one another:

- An Express order that also Requires Cold Packing seizes a scarce cold station and can preempt a Standard cold case already in progress, lengthening the cold queue for many subsequent Standard cases while simultaneously advancing only the high-priority case. Predictions of cold demand therefore become highly volatile under mix shifts toward express.
- Batching by Destination interacts with the hazmat limit. Multiple Hazardous orders destined for the same popular region may all become ready near-simultaneously; the batching rule holds them until the batch fills, creating bursts that then push concurrent hazmat count toward or beyond the regulatory ceiling of 10 and force throttling of packing/QC starts.
- Cold-packing contention slows readiness for shipping labels for cold-order batches; if an Express cold order further disrupts the schedule, the entire regional batch may wait longer, magnifying the synchronization cost for hundreds of co-batched cases.
- When hazmat concurrency is already near limit, any priority-driven reordering that inserts extra Express hazmat work can starve Standard hybrids (hazmat+cold) and lengthen overall inventory/dwell time, increasing the chance of batch decomposition or expiry for perishables.

Understanding interactions is crucial because local improvements frequently produce negative externalities elsewhere (e.g., aggressive express preemption collapses standard SLA; larger fixed batches explode hazmat peaks). Optimization must therefore treat the constraints as a coupled multi-objective system rather than independent bottleneck reductions; metrics must be joint (cycle-time trade-off surface under joint utilization of cold stations + concurrent hazmat 10, etc.).

**3. Developing Constraint-Aware Optimization Strategies**

Three concrete, interdependent strategies that exploit the mined data:

**Strategy A – Predictive dynamic allocation and soft preemption for cold-packing stations**  
- Primary constraints addressed: Shared Cold-Packing + Priority Handling.  
- Changes: Maintain a real-time occupancy forecast of the 5 cold stations using arrival-rate predictors trained on historical filtered logs (cold cases, time-of-day, day-of-week, express ratio). Implement a policy that reserves 1–2 cold stations for Express when predicted Express arrival intensity is high, and otherwise allows Standard use with a soft-preemption rule (finish the Standard unit currently in progress unless the Express is within a perishable time window). Temporarily reassign under-utilized standard stations to cold work when physical constraints allow.  
- Data leverage: Process-mining performance analysis yields service-time and inter-arrival distributions; predictive process monitoring models (or simple regression/LSTM on case attributes) supply short-horizon demand. Queue-state reconstruction quantifies historical contention cost.  
- Expected outcomes: Substantially reduced cold-contention waits, lower preemption tax on Standards during normal load, higher overall cold throughput while protecting Express lead times; measurable via drop in cold-specific waiting percentile and reduced cumulative delay caused by Expresses.

**Strategy B – Dynamic, multi-criteria batch formation with deadline awareness**  
- Primary constraints addressed: Batching + interactions with priority/hazmat.  
- Changes: Replace fixed regional batches with a dynamic rule: trigger Label Generation when either (i) a maximum wait timeout (mined from historical delay distributions) is reached for the earliest ready order in the region, (ii) a minimum efficient batch size is attained, or (iii) an Express case or a near-limit hazmat case is present (force small/urgent batch). For regions with high hazmat density, further cap simultaneous batch membership so that packing/QC of the batch never violates the global limit.  
- Data leverage: Historical co-occurrence of destinations and hazmat flags, empirical distributions of QC COMPLETE-to-Label Gen lags conditioned on batch size, and predictive models of future arrivals per region derived from the event log.  
- Expected outcomes: Dramatic reduction of pure batch-synchronization waits (especially for early-ready orders), fewer artificial peaks that violate hazmat concurrency, controlled impact of Expresses that no longer wait for full batches; improved region-level cycle-time variance and higher SLA compliance without sacrificing load-density economies.

**Strategy C – Constraint-aware scheduling and capacity buffering for hazmat + hybrid orders**  
- Primary constraints: Hazardous Material Limit + its interactions with cold and priority.  
- Changes: Introduce a centralized multi-resource dispatcher (or enhanced WMS rule set) that (1) maintains a global hazmat concurrency token bucket of capacity 10 for Packing/QC and refuses new START if it would violate it, (2) prioritizes within the queue by a score that multiplies Order Type urgency, remaining cold-station slack, age, and perishability, and (3) creates temporary “holding” buffers or slight capacity uplift (extra QC staff or staggered packing windows) during peak seasons identified from the three-month log. Optionally add a limited number of dual-use cold+hazmat stations or pre-allocate hazmat-friendly packing slots. Decouple Quality Check slightly by allowing non-destructive samples of hazardous items to be checked off-line, reducing concurrent occupancy.  
- Data leverage: Exact reconstruction of past concurrency time-series from the log, discovery of correlated peaks between hazmat, cold, and express, and bottleneck analysis quantifying lost capacity.  
- Expected outcomes: Near-zero regulatory violations, smoother throughput under the hard 10-order ceiling, reduced knock-on queueing for non-hazmat cold and express traffic, higher overall system capacity during peaks; verified by lower max/mean concurrent occupancy variance, higher packing/QC utilization under constraint, and reduced secondary waits.

These three interact: Strategy A’s cold forecasts feed Strategy B’s express-force batches and Strategy C’s scoring function; thus they should be piloted jointly.

**4. Simulation and Validation**

Before live rollout, construct a discrete-event, multi-case simulation model that is calibrated to the event log. Replayer-style techniques (token-based or partial-order alignment) supply control-flow probabilities, activity duration distributions (conditional on attributes), and resource assignment frequencies. Explicitly encode:

- Finite cold-station pool (capacity 5) with preemption logic and exclusive occupation intervals;  
- Regional batch collectors that accumulate ready cases and fire on the (to-be-tested) dynamic trigger conditions;  
- Express priority rules (interrupt polynomials) including resume costs;  
- Global hazmat semaphore (counter 10) that gates Packing and QC STARTs;  
- Presence of shared standard stations and pickers as background contention.

Inject stochastic arrivals matching empirical inter-arrival and mix distributions (cold %, express %, hazmat %, regional flows). Run baseline (as-is) versus each of the three strategies (and combinations) under multiple peak-load scenarios extrapolated from the three-month log. Primary KPIs: end-to-end cycle time (mean, 95th, SLA hit rate), throughput (orders/hour), resource utilizations, cold-queue lengths, batch-wait percentages, hazmat max-concurrency violation counts, and the decomposed between-instance wait components. Sensitivity analysis on key parameters (cold capacity, max batch wait, hazmat concurrency soft/hard limit) reveals robustness. Statistical comparison (confidence intervals, paired runs) quantifies expected gains and risk of secondary effects (e.g., starvation of Standard cold orders). Only strategies whose simulated net improvement is stable and constraint-compliant proceed to limited A/B pilots.

**5. Monitoring Post-Implementation**

Deploy real-time and offline process-mining dashboards fed by the live event stream (or near-real-time extracts):

- **Waiting-time deconstruction panel**: stacked time series / histograms of total sojourn time broken down into pure processing, within-instance idle, cold-contention wait, batch-synchronization wait, preemption/resume wait, and hazmat-gating wait; sliceable by Order Type, region, day period.  
- **Resource contention & queue metrics**: live and historical occupancy of each cold station and of the whole pool, queue length/TTS for cold packing, preemption frequency and mean preemption cost; alarms when cold utilization >85% sustained.  
- **Batch health**: mean/percentiles of batch formation delay and size per region; fraction of batches triggered by timeout vs. size vs. express/hazmat urgency; residual unused batch capacity.  
- **Hazmat compliance & flow**: continuous concurrent-counter time series with hard ceiling line at 10; violation count (should be zero) and near-miss frequency (time living at 8–10); separate throughput of hazmat vs. non-hazmat streams.  
- **Global & interaction KPIs**: cycle-time SLA attainment by priority tier, overall facility throughput, correlation heat-maps between cold queue spikes and express volume or between batch delays and hazmat peaks.  
- **Constraint management effectiveness indices**: (a) reduction in between-instance wait ratio relative to baseline, (b) cold-station blocked time, (c) express-induced extra delay on Standards, (d) fraction of batches held solely by missing members, (e) hazmat capacity utilization under limit.  

Dashboards refresh on a rolling window (last 1 h / 24 h / week) and support drill-down to individual cases/resources. Periodic process-mining re-discovery (or conformance checks against the target policy) detects drift or unforeseen interaction patterns. Automated alerts fire when any between-instance wait component exceeds its post-implementation target or when hazmat concurrency approaches the regulatory ceiling, enabling rapid policy-parameter re-tuning. Collectively these ensure the instance-spanning constraints remain visible, measurable, and actively managed rather than becoming latent bottlenecks again.
