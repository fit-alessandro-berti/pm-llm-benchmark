**Comprehensive Process Mining Approach for Speedy Parcels Last-Mile Delivery Optimization**

As process mining consultants specializing in logistics and transportation, we outline a structured, data-driven methodology to analyze Speedy Parcels’ multi-source event data (GPS trackers, driver scanners, dispatch system, and maintenance logs). The approach discovers the actual operational process, quantifies performance and bottlenecks, validates root causes of delays and cost overruns, and delivers concrete optimization strategies while respecting operational constraints. All analyses treat the primary case notion as **Vehicle-Day** (e.g., V12-20241205), capturing the end-to-end shift from Start Shift to End Shift, with package-level views used for delivery-specific insights. Multi-perspective process mining (control-flow, time, data/location/speed, and resource/driver-vehicle) is applied throughout.

### 1. Process Discovery and Conformance Checking

**Data Preprocessing and Integration**  
The heterogeneous sources must be unified into a standard event log (Case ID, Activity, Timestamp + attributes such as Vehicle ID, Driver ID, Package ID, Location, Speed, Notes).

- **Case definition**: Primary case = Vehicle-Day. This captures the full operational cycle. Hierarchical or object-centric process mining (OCPM) views can additionally treat packages (and optionally drivers/vehicles) as interacting objects, which is particularly useful for analyzing multi-package stops and re-delivery loops.
- **Activity derivation and harmonization**:
  - Scanner events supply core discrete milestones: Start Shift, Route Assigned, Arrive Customer, Delivery Success, Delivery Failed (Attempted), Depart Customer, End Shift.
  - GPS trajectories are segmented into derived activities using speed, duration, and map-matching thresholds: Traveling (speed above threshold), Low Speed/Congestion, Idle/Stopped (speed ≈ 0 for sustained periods), Unscheduled Stop. Stops near known customer or depot coordinates are linked to scanner events.
  - Dispatch data enriches the log with planned sequences, time windows, package assignments, and capacities (used primarily for conformance and performance baselines rather than discovery).
  - Maintenance logs inject Start/End Maintenance or Unscheduled Repair events that interrupt the main process.
- **Timestamp and location alignment**: All timestamps are normalized to a common precision and timezone. Fuzzy spatial matching (e.g., 50–100 m radius) correlates GPS points with scanner events and planned addresses. Derived attributes are calculated (inter-stop distances via coordinate sequences or speed×time, dwell times, time-of-day, cumulative mileage).
- **Cleaning**: Noise filtering of GPS outliers, removal of incomplete cases, inference of missing scanner events from prolonged GPS stops at customer locations, and handling of concurrent package activities.

**Key challenges** include the volume mismatch (high-frequency GPS vs. sparse scanner events), risk of over-/under-segmentation of continuous trajectories, incomplete linkages (GPS stops without scanner records for traffic/breaks/parking search), timestamp clock drift, urban GPS multipath errors, and the natural loops created by failed deliveries and mid-shift maintenance. Robust ETL pipelines with domain-specific location intelligence are required.

**Process Discovery**  
Discovery algorithms reveal the *as-is* end-to-end process (Depot departure → sequence of travel/service cycles → possible interruptions → Depot return):

- **Inductive Miner** (or Inductive Miner–infrequent) produces sound, hierarchical process trees or Petri nets that correctly handle concurrency, exclusive choices (Success vs. Failed), and loops (re-attempts, repeated stops).
- **Heuristics Miner** captures frequency-based dependencies and is robust to noise common in real logistics logs.
- **Fuzzy Miner** or directly-follows graphs provide intuitive visualizations of complex “spaghetti” processes, abstracting low-frequency arcs while highlighting the dominant flow: Start Shift → Route Assigned → Depart Depot → (Traveling / Low Speed / Unscheduled Stop)* → Arrive Customer → (Delivery Success | Delivery Failed) → Depart Customer → … → Arrive Depot → End Shift.

The resulting models explicitly surface branches for failed deliveries, unscheduled maintenance interruptions, overtime extensions, and driver-initiated sequence changes. Multi-perspective views overlay resources (drivers/vehicles) and data attributes (locations, speeds).

**Conformance Checking**  
The discovered models and event log are compared against the planned routes, stop sequences, and time windows from the dispatch system using alignment-based conformance checking (optimal alignments) and token-based replay.  

Deviations systematically examined include:
- Sequence/order deviations (reordered, skipped, or inserted stops).
- Unplanned activities (Unscheduled Stops, prolonged Idle, Low Speed periods, detours evidenced by excess GPS distance).
- Missing planned activities.
- Timing deviations (travel or service times significantly exceeding planned estimates, arrivals outside customer time windows, overtime).
- Resource or capacity deviations.

Fitness and precision scores, deviation counts per case, and cost annotations (extra time/fuel) quantify the gap between plan and reality.

### 2. Performance Analysis and Bottleneck Identification

**Key Performance Indicators (calculable directly from the enriched event log)**  
- **On-Time Delivery Rate (OTDR)**: Percentage of Delivery Success events falling inside the dispatch time window (joined on Package ID). Segmentable by vehicle, driver, route, time-of-day, weekday.
- **Average Time per Delivery Stop (Service Time)**: Mean duration Arrive Customer → Depart Customer; separated for Success vs. Failed.
- **Travel Time vs. Service Time ratio**: Aggregate moving time (GPS speed > 0 or inter-stop intervals) versus total customer dwell + idle time.
- **Failed Delivery Rate** and re-attempt frequency.
- **Vehicle Utilization Rate**: (Moving + service time) / total shift duration (Start Shift → End Shift); idle percentage from GPS.
- **Traffic Delay Frequency/Duration**: Count and length of Low Speed or prolonged low-speed GPS segments, geo-aggregated.
- **Overtime Rate** and average overtime minutes.
- **Distance/Fuel proxies**: Total distance (GPS integration), distance per package, idle-time fuel waste proxy.
- **Packages or stops completed per productive hour**.

These metrics are obtained via timestamp differences, attribute filtering, and performance perspectives in process mining tools.

**Bottleneck Identification Techniques**  
- Dotted-chart analysis visualizes all Vehicle-Day cases over time, colored by activity or resource, exposing late starts, clustering of delays, and overtime patterns.
- Performance overlays on the discovered process model (mean/median sojourn times, waiting times, frequencies on activities and arcs) immediately highlight high-duration steps.
- Geo-enrichment and clustering of Low Speed/Idle GPS points identify traffic hotspots and parking-difficulty zones; heatmaps quantify spatial concentration of delays.
- Multi-perspective and resource analysis ranks drivers and vehicles by the KPIs above.
- Variant analysis compares high-performing (high OTDR, low cycle time) versus low-performing process variants.
- Temporal aggregation by hour-of-day and weekday surfaces recurring congestion windows.

Impact is quantified by attributing total excess minutes (or estimated fuel/cost) to each bottleneck type and measuring the resulting loss in daily delivery capacity. Typical bottlenecks surface in peak-hour urban corridors, high-variance customer service times, failed-delivery loops, and mid-shift unscheduled stops.

### 3. Root Cause Analysis for Inefficiencies

Process mining moves beyond symptoms to validated causes:

- **Suboptimal/static route planning**: High sequence and timing deviations in conformance checking, plus variants in which adaptive drivers outperform rigid plans, indicate that dispatch routes do not incorporate historical performance or real-time conditions.
- **Inaccurate travel-time estimates**: Systematic positive bias (actual leg times ≫ planned) computed for every consecutive stop pair, strongly correlated with time-of-day and location.
- **Recurring traffic congestion**: Geo-temporal clustering of Low Speed events; cases filtered by high traffic-delay attributes show clear drops in OTDR and rises in cycle time.
- **High service-time variability**: Wide distributions of Arrive-to-Depart durations, often linked to parking search (GPS circling), multi-package stops, or building access; attribute correlation identifies customer types or locations driving the variance.
- **Vehicle reliability issues**: Unscheduled Stop / Engine Warning events (cross-referenced with maintenance logs) produce large cycle-time inflation, incomplete routes, and overtime; cumulative usage attributes (km, idle time) enable early predictive signals.
- **Driver behavior and skill differences**: Resource-performance ranking and statistical comparison reveal systematic OTDR, service-time, and deviation differences across Driver IDs. Trace clustering followed by decision mining extracts the behavioral patterns of top performers.
- **Failed-delivery loops**: Frequency of Delivery Failed → later re-attempt sequences, extra distance incurred, and correlation with time-window mismatch or time-of-day.

Supporting techniques include trace clustering + performance profiling of variants, decision/rule mining on enriched attributes, and multi-level analysis (Vehicle-Day, package, individual travel leg). These analyses quantify the relative contribution of each root cause (e.g., traffic explaining X % of total delay minutes).

### 4. Data-Driven Optimization Strategies

Three concrete strategies are proposed, each directly targeting quantified bottlenecks and root causes:

**Strategy 1: Dynamic, Traffic-Adaptive Routing**  
Targets frequent Low Speed delays, excess travel time, and cascading lateness. Addresses static plans and inaccurate travel estimates. Process-mining evidence (performance overlays, geo-heatmaps of congestion, conformance timing deviations, high- vs. low-traffic variants) supplies both the historical patterns and real-time signals needed for continuous re-optimization. Expected impact: higher OTDR, lower Travel/Service ratio, reduced fuel (less idling and detours), and improved vehicle utilization through more reliable completion of planned stops.

**Strategy 2: Historical-Performance Territory Redesign and Sequence Optimization**  
Targets excessive total travel distance, unbalanced workloads, and cascading delays caused by suboptimal static territories and stop orders. Root cause is planning that ignores actual historical service times, successful sequences, and delay clusters. Discovery models, geographic performance aggregation, and comparative analysis of high-performing Vehicle-Day variants identify efficient territory boundaries and sequencing heuristics. Expected impact: lower total travel time and fuel, more balanced utilization and reduced overtime, higher baseline OTDR, and secondary reduction in failed deliveries through better density matching.

**Strategy 3: Predictive Maintenance Scheduling + Proactive Failed-Delivery Mitigation (with driver coaching)**  
Targets mid-shift Unscheduled Stops and high Failed Delivery rates that destroy capacity. Root causes are reactive maintenance and poor matching of time windows/customer availability. Maintenance-event correlation with preceding GPS/usage patterns enables predictive schedules; failed-delivery frequency, re-attempt loops, and time-window success probabilities guide refined window assignment and customer communication. In addition, resource-performance and variant analysis of top drivers supplies best-practice patterns (efficient sequencing adaptations, shorter service times) for targeted coaching of high-variance drivers. Expected impact: fewer disruptions and overtime, lower failed-delivery rate and re-attempt travel, higher OTDR and vehicle availability, and sustained cost reduction.

The three strategies are complementary and can be prioritized by the quantified delay/cost contribution of each root cause (Pareto analysis).

### 5. Considering Operational Constraints and Monitoring

All strategies explicitly respect hard constraints present in the data:
- Driver working hours and overtime (Start/End Shift events and remaining available time become constraints in the dynamic optimizer).
- Vehicle capacities (dispatch package counts and actual progression are monitored to avoid overload).
- Customer time windows (treated as hard or soft constraints; historical success probabilities refine assignments).
- Additional detectable constraints (depot hours, mandatory breaks inferred from long idle periods, speed limits from GPS) are likewise enforced.

**Continuous Monitoring Plan**  
After implementation, a process-mining dashboard (updated daily or via streaming) tracks:
- The full set of KPIs defined in Section 2 (OTDR, Travel/Service ratio, Failed Delivery Rate, Utilization, Overtime, traffic-delay minutes, etc.), segmented by route/territory/driver/time-of-day.
- Process views: performance-spectrum or animated process maps, variant explorer (pre- vs. post-change), dotted charts, geographic delay/failure heatmaps, and resource rankings.
- Conformance scores against the updated planned models and predictive risk indicators (cases likely to miss windows or generate overtime).

Threshold-based alerts flag KPI degradation or emerging deviation patterns. Monthly re-discovery and root-cause cycles detect concept drift (new congestion, changing customer behavior). Where feasible, controlled A/B roll-outs on subsets of the fleet enable rigorous before-after comparison. This transforms process mining from a one-time diagnosis into a continuous operational capability that sustains gains and rapidly surfaces new issues.

This end-to-end approach—grounded in rigorous multi-source event-log construction, discovery, conformance, performance, and root-cause techniques—provides Speedy Parcels with transparent, quantifiable insights and actionable levers to improve punctuality while lowering fuel and maintenance costs.
