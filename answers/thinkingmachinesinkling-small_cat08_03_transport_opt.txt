Compare planned inter-stop times with actual; if actuals are consistently 30% higher during 08:00–09:30, estimates are flawed. |
| **High Variability in Customer Service Time** | Analyze the distribution of `Arrive Customer` to `Depart Customer` durations by customer address and by region. High variance (e.g., 30 seconds to 15 minutes for similar stops) suggests uncontrollable factors (parking, access, customer readiness) rather than driver efficiency. Outliers at specific addresses indicate site-specific issues (e.g., secure buildings). |
| **Vehicle Breakdowns / Predictable Maintenance Needs** | Filter cases with `Unscheduled Stop`. Link to `Maintenance Logs`. If `V12` (or specific vehicle classes) shows a recurring pattern—e.g., unscheduled stops occur after 180 km or 6 hours of operation—we identify usage-based failure modes. Compare case duration of routes with/without unscheduled stops to quantify impact (e.g., +90 minutes, overtime logged). |
| **Driver Behavior / Skill Differences** | Group cases by `Driver ID`. Control for route difficulty (planned distance, number of stops, urban density). If `D105` has significantly higher travel times but similar service times on comparable routes, this indicates driving behavior (speed, route adherence). Conversely, if one driver achieves faster total case times with identical sequences, they represent a “best-practice variant” to standardize. |
| **Failed Delivery Attempts & Redelivery Cost** | Analyze `Delivery Failed` events (`P9879` at Stop 5). If failures occur disproportionately at certain stops, times (e.g., late afternoon when customers are not home), or zones, the root cause is a mismatch between delivery windows and customer availability—not driver error. Check if failed deliveries lead to new cases (redelivery the next day), which doubles travel and fuel costs. |

**Specific Analytical Techniques:**
*   **Variant Analysis (High vs. Low Performers):** Filter top 15% route-days (lowest duration, highest OTD) and bottom 15%. Compare process maps to see if top performers skip unnecessary delays or choose better sequences.
*   **Dwell-Time Distribution Analysis:** Identify “long-tail” stops; cross-reference scanner notes (if any) to see if failures or access issues explain them.
*   **Correlation with External Traffic:** Augment the event log with external traffic-state data per geofence/time, then use statistical models or simple correlation to confirm traffic as a primary delay driver, not just correlation.

### 4. Data-Driven Optimization Strategies

We propose **three concrete strategies** directly derived from the process insights above.

#### **Strategy A: Dynamic Route Re-Optimization Based on Real-Time Traffic and Discovered "Fast Variants"**
*   **Target Inefficiency:** Unpredictable travel delays (traffic jams like `08:10:55`), suboptimal static routes, and sequence deviations caused by drivers improvising.
*   **Root Cause Addressed:** Static dispatch plans do not adapt to real-world conditions; drivers’ successful deviations (detected in process discovery) reveal better paths that planning never considers.
*   **How PM Supports:** The discovered process model shows which *actual* sequences result in lower travel times. By analyzing the GPS trajectory of high-performing variants, we generate a “learned travel-time matrix” for each leg (time-of-day and traffic-state dependent). Conformance checking reveals which deviations are “good” (faster) vs. “bad” (disorganized).
*   **Implementation:** Integrate real-time traffic APIs with dispatch. Instead of a fixed sequence, use a **Dynamic Vehicle Routing Problem (DVRP)** approach: update the planned sequence every 30–60 minutes based on current traffic conditions, remaining stops, and customer windows. Use the discovered “fast variants” as initial heuristics for the routing engine.
*   **Expected KPI Impact:** Reduces travel time vs. service time ratio (less idling/driving); improves OTD by reducing unpredictable delays; lowers fuel consumption per km; reduces overtime (`17:05:00` end shift).

#### **Strategy B: Predictive Maintenance and Reliability-Based Vehicle/Rider Allocation**
*   **Target Inefficiency:** Unscheduled stops (`11:05:00` `Engine Warning Light`), breakdown-related delays, rising maintenance costs, and vehicle downtime.
*   **Root Cause Addressed:** Reactive maintenance; assigning high-risk vehicles (based on usage patterns) to long, time-sensitive urban routes.
*   **How PM Supports:** Correlating the event log (`Vehicle` events, `Unscheduled Stop`, `Maintenance Logs`) reveals usage-intensity profiles. If vehicle `V12` or a specific model shows a high rate of unscheduled stops after high-mileage days, we have predictive rules. Performance analysis shows that routes with unscheduled stops have significantly longer total duration and lower OTD.
*   **Implementation:** Shift from fixed-interval maintenance to **usage-based/predictive maintenance** triggered by GPS-derived operational stress (miles driven, number of stops, idle time). Additionally, implement **vehicle reliability scoring**: allocate the most reliable vehicles to high-density, high-value routes; reserve older/less reliable vehicles for shorter suburban loops where delays are less costly.
*   **Expected KPI Impact:** Drastically reduces unplanned stop frequency/duration; lowers maintenance and repair costs; improves vehicle utilization rate; improves OTD by eliminating last-minute route interruptions.

#### **Strategy C: Data-Driven Time-Window Management and Proactive Customer Communication**
*   **Target Inefficiency:** High rate of failed deliveries (`Delivery Failed`, `P9879`—Customer Not Home); high variability in service time; customer complaints about late/missed deliveries.
*   **Root Cause Addressed:** Inaccurate delivery promises; time windows that do not match actual customer availability or driver performance; lack of customer awareness/interaction at the point of delivery.
*   **How PM Supports:** Conformance checking shows actual delivery times vs. planned windows. Variant analysis reveals that routes with high failure rates have longer total durations (possibly due to repeat attempts or leaving packages for tomorrow). Analysis of dwell times by customer address identifies which stops are consistently unpredictable.
*   **Implementation:** 
    1.  **Narrow and Accurate Windows:** Use historical performance distributions (mean + 1 standard deviation of actual service + travel time from the log) to promise realistic 2-hour windows instead of over-promising.
    2.  **Proactive Communication:** Use GPS geofencing (when vehicle is within ~500m of customer) to trigger SMS/app notifications (“Driver approaching—please confirm availability”). Offer one-click rescheduling to avoid failed attempts.
    3.  **Safe Delivery Protocols:** For addresses with recurring failures (identified via process mining), establish pre-authorized safe-drop instructions or alternative pickup points, removing the need for a second visit.
*   **Expected KPI Impact:** Reduces failed delivery rate; lowers service time variability (fewer “not home” waits); improves OTD; reduces re-delivery fuel and labor costs; improves customer satisfaction.

*(Supplementary: **Driver Performance & Territory Standardization.** From variant analysis, identify top-performing drivers and standardize their sequence and service practices. Provide targeted coaching for drivers with high travel times or inconsistent scan patterns, using the discovered best-practice process as a benchmark.)*

### 5. Considering Operational Constraints and Monitoring

#### **Accounting for Operational Constraints**
Optimizations cannot violate real-world logistics boundaries:
*   **Driver Working Hours / Labor Rules:** The event log contains `Start Shift` (`07:01:15`) and `End Shift` (`17:05:00`) with overtime logged. Any dynamic routing or added stops must respect maximum driving/working hours (e.g., EU driving time rules or company contracts). Our optimization model must include a **hard constraint**: total planned duration + estimated dynamic delays <= available shift time. If dynamic routing saves 30 minutes, we can either reduce overtime (cost saving) or add a high-value stop (utilization gain), but we cannot extend beyond legal limits.
*   **Vehicle Capacities:** The `Dispatch System` assigns packages per vehicle. The process model must ensure optimized routes do not exceed volume/weight limits; if a dynamic reroute implies visiting a new customer, the dispatch system must verify load feasibility.
*   **Customer Time Windows:** Hard windows (e.g., business delivery 09:00–17:00, residential 18:00–20:00) are constraints in the planned model. Dynamic routing can reorder stops *within* the feasibility window but cannot violate hard commitments.

#### **Continuous Monitoring Plan (Process Mining Control Tower)**
After implementing changes, we establish a **continuous process mining dashboard** to prevent regression and detect new issues.

**Dashboard Views and Metrics:**
1.  **Conformance Monitor:** Daily/Weekly **Fitness Score** per vehicle-day and per driver; list of new deviation types (e.g., “New Traffic Pattern at X” or “New Unplanned Stop Type”).
2.  **Performance Spectrum:** Average duration per activity (`Travel`, `Service`, `Traffic Delay`, `Unscheduled Stop`) over time. Alert if `Traffic Delay` duration increases in a previously optimized corridor.
3.  **Variant Tracker:** Top 10 process variants executed this week. If a new, slow variant emerges (e.g., drivers avoid a new road due to construction), the system flags it.
4.  **KPI Scorecards:**
    *   **Punctuality:** OTD % (target improvement).
    *   **Efficiency:** Travel/Service Ratio; Fuel/Package; Unscheduled Stop Rate.
    *   **Service Quality:** Failed Delivery Rate; Average Dwell Time (variance).
    *   **Asset Use:** Vehicle Utilization Rate; Overtime Hours %.
5.  **Geo-Heatmap Overlay:** Real-time or daily mapping of GPS-identified delays, unscheduled stops, and failed deliveries to visualize new bottleneck hotspots.

**Feedback Loop:**
*   **Weekly Review:** Compare process variants before and after implementation. If dynamic routing is adopted, we expect the “Standard Sequence” variant to change to a “Traffic-Adaptive Sequence” with lower average duration. If the number of unplanned sequence deviations *decreases*, the optimization is succeeding.
*   **Predictive Alerting:** If maintenance logs combined with GPS usage show a vehicle approaching the failure threshold, the dashboard automatically recommends preventive scheduling—closing the loop on Strategy B.
*   **Sustainability Check:** Monitor for “optimization fatigue”: if drivers revert to old patterns (low conformance to the new optimized plan), targeted coaching (Strategy D) is triggered.

By maintaining this process-mining control loop, Speedy Parcels transforms from a reactive operation using static plans into a data-driven, continuously improving logistics system.
