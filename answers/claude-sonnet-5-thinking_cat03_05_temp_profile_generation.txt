# Temporal Profile: Global Supply Chain Process

## Approach

Since this is a linear-style process (SS  OP  RC  QI  CA  PT  PK  WS  DT  AS), every activity eventually follows every prior activity in at least one trace. I estimated **direct-transition** times based on realistic supply-chain domain knowledge (e.g., long lead time for component ordering, quick post-inspection steps, long gap before after-sales support), then derived the **multi-step** pairs by summing the average times of the intermediate direct transitions and combining their variances (assuming approximate independence between consecutive step durations, i.e., `combined_std  sqrt(sum of variances of intermediate steps)`).

This produces internally consistent estimates: the more steps between two activities, the higher the average time and (generally) the higher the variability.

## Reasoning behind base (direct) transitions

| Transition | Rationale | Avg (approx) | Std (approx) |
|---|---|---|---|
| SSOP | Supplier confirmation to PO placement | 2 days | 12 hrs |
| OPRC | Manufacturing + shipping lead time (biggest bottleneck) | 14 days | 3 days |
| RCQI | Quick internal handoff | 3 hrs | 1 hr |
| QICA | Awaiting QA sign-off, scheduling assembly | 12 hrs | 4 hrs |
| CAPT | Move to test bench | 8 hrs | 2 hrs |
| PTPK | Pass testing, prep for packaging | 4 hrs | 1 hr |
| PKWS | Move to storage | 2 hrs | 30 min |
| WSDT | Awaiting shipment scheduling | 2 days | 12 hrs |
| DTAS | Customer usage period before support request | 30 days | 7 days |

## Usage Note

With this profile and a chosen `ZETA` (e.g., 2 or 3), any observed inter-activity duration `t` for pair `(X, Y)` in a trace would be flagged as a deviation if:

```
t < AVG - ZETA * STDEV   or   t > AVG + ZETA * STDEV
```

For example, if a trace shows `OP  RC` taking only 1 hour (instead of ~14 days ± 3 days), this would be flagged as a significant deviation, potentially indicating a data-quality issue or an atypical expedited shipment.
