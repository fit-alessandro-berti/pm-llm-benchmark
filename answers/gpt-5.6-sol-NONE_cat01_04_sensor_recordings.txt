## Activity rationale

### 1. Idle / Machine Ready — Events 1–3
The machine is at approximately room temperature, pressure remains at its baseline, and both vibration and material flow are zero. The tool is at position 0, while energy consumption is only 0.40–0.50 kWh. This is characteristic of a powered but inactive machine awaiting a work cycle.

### 2. Cutting Metal — Events 4–6
This phase shows a coordinated operational ramp-up:

- Temperature increases from 50 to 58 °C.
- Vibration rises from 30 to 38 Hz.
- Material flow stays continuously high at 5 units/s.
- Tool position advances from 5 to 10 mm.
- Energy rises from 2.0 to 3.0 kWh.

High vibration and progressive tool movement are consistent with active material removal. The increasing temperature and energy indicate growing mechanical load or friction.

### 3. Assembling / Positioning Parts — Events 7–8
The readings become stable at moderate levels:

- Temperature: 40 °C
- Pressure: 2 bar
- Vibration: 10 Hz
- Material flow: 2 units/s
- Tool position: 15 mm
- Energy: 2 kWh

Compared with cutting, this phase has much less vibration and lower flow, suggesting controlled placement, clamping, fitting, or assembly. The increased pressure may reflect pneumatic clamping or holding parts in position.

### 4. Welding / Joining — Event 9
Event 9 is a distinct high-intensity pulse:

- Temperature peaks at 80 °C.
- Vibration peaks at 50 Hz.
- Energy reaches 5 kWh.
- Material flow falls to zero.
- Tool position remains fixed at 15 mm.

The sharp thermal and energy spike is strongly indicative of welding or another localized joining operation. The lack of material flow and stationary position distinguish it from the earlier cutting phase.

### 5. Cooling / Process Transition — Event 10
Immediately after welding, temperature drops to 30 °C, vibration falls to 1 Hz, and energy falls to 1 kWh. This likely represents cooling, tool disengagement, or a brief transition before inspection. It is better treated as a transition state than as a main value-adding activity.

- Temperature remains at 25 °C.
- Vibration is low at 5 Hz.
- Material flow is only 1 unit/s.
- Tool position is fixed at 20 mm.
- Energy remains low at 1.5–1.6 kWh.

This signature suggests a light-duty operation such as probing, dimensional checking, sensor scanning, or test handling. The fixed tool position and repeatability support an inspection interpretation.

### 7. Idle / Cycle Complete — Events 13–15
All active signals return to their initial baseline. The tool returns to position 0, material flow and vibration stop, and energy consumption drops to approximately 0.4–0.5 kWh. This marks completion of the production cycle.
