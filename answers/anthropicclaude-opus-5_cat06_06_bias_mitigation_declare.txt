## 1. Where the bias can hide

In the given model nothing prevents a trace such as

```
StartApplication  CheckApplicantRace  Reject  FinalDecision
```

i.e. a decision that is *directly* enabled by the disclosure of a sensitive attribute, with **no** intervening objective check, no manual review, and no recorded rationale. The original model also never mandates the existence of any fairness control, so a "fast lane" (auto-decision) and a "slow lane" (manual review) can emerge and correlate with demographics.

The mitigation strategy below adds four families of control-flow constraints:

1. **Mandatory controls** – a `BiasMitigationCheck` must exist in every case, and exactly one `FinalDecision` is recorded.
2. **Insulation of decisions from sensitive-attribute events** – `chainresponse` / `chainprecedence` / `nonchainsuccession` guarantee that a sensitive event can never be *immediately* followed by an outcome; a bias check always sits in between.
3. **Equal treatment of protected groups** – `coexistence` / `altprecedence` force `ManualReview` (and its audit trail) whenever a decision concerns a protected-group applicant, and forbid "review skipping" shortcuts.
4. **Accountability** – every `Approve` / `Reject` must be followed by `DocumentDecisionRationale` and a `FairnessAudit` record, so outcomes are always explainable and auditable.

## 3. Rationale, constraint by constraint

| Added constraint | Semantics | Why it limits bias |
|---|---|---|
| `existence(BiasMitigationCheck)`, `existence(FairnessAudit)` | both occur in every trace | Fairness controls become non-optional; they cannot be applied selectively to some demographic groups only. |
| `absence(AutoRejectBySensitiveProfile)` | activity never occurs | Structurally eliminates the purely profiling-driven rejection path from the process. |
| `exactly_one(FinalDecision)`, `exactly_one(BiasMitigationCheck)` | exactly one occurrence | Prevents "decision shopping" (re-deciding until the unfavourable outcome appears) and prevents a check being run once per group and skipped elsewhere. |
| `responded_existence(CheckApplicant*, BiasMitigationCheck / FairnessAudit)` | if the sensitive attribute is read, a mitigation + audit event must exist | Any use of protected data leaves a mandatory fairness trace. |
| `response(CheckApplicant*, BiasMitigationCheck)` | mitigation occurs **after** the disclosure | Ensures the check happens *downstream* of the exposure, i.e. it can actually neutralise it. |
| `altresponse(CheckApplicant*, BiasMitigationCheck)` | a *fresh* check after each disclosure | Stops re-using one old check as a fig leaf for several later sensitive-data accesses. |
| `chainresponse(CheckApplicant*, BiasMitigationCheck)` | mitigation is the *immediately next* event | No decision, scoring or routing step can slip in between attribute disclosure and mitigation. |
| `nonchainsuccession(CheckApplicant*, {Approve, Reject, FinalDecision, RequestAdditionalInfo})` | those events never directly follow a sensitive check | Kills the canonical discriminatory pattern `CheckApplicantRace  Reject`, and symmetrically also ` Approve` (positive discrimination) and ` RequestAdditionalInfo` (extra burden imposed on a group). |
| `chainprecedence(BiasMitigationCheck, {Approve, Reject, FinalDecision})` | the event immediately before every outcome is the bias check | Every outcome is directly derived from an attribute-blind evaluation, not from demographic information. |
| `precedence(BiasMitigationCheck, all outcomes)` / `succession(BiasMitigationCheck, FinalDecision)` | outcomes only after mitigation; mitigation always leads to a decision | Guarantees ordering (no decision before the check) and completeness (the check is never a dead end). |
| `coexistence(Reject_Minority / Approve_Minority, ManualReview + FairnessAudit)` | protected-group outcomes always accompanied by human review and audit | Equalises the level of scrutiny: protected-group cases can never be decided by an unreviewed automatic path. |
| `coexistence(ManualReview, BiasMitigationCheck)` | review and mitigation always together | A human review cannot itself become the vehicle of implicit bias — it is always paired with the objective check. |
| `precedence` + `altprecedence` + `chainprecedence(ManualReview, *_Minority)` | each protected-group outcome is immediately preceded by its **own** review | Guarantees a one-to-one, non-recycled four-eyes check per decision. |
| `noncoexistence(SkipManualReview, *_Minority)` and `nonsuccession(CheckApplicant*, SkipManualReview)` | shortcut incompatible with protected-group decisions / never reachable after attribute disclosure | Removes the "fast lane vs. slow lane" asymmetry that is the most common source of disparate treatment. |
| `noncoexistence(AutoRejectBySensitiveProfile, FinalDecision)` | redundant safety net w.r.t. `absence` | Defence in depth: even if the forbidden activity were logged, it can never lead to a recorded outcome. |
| `response` / `responded_existence` / `chainsuccession(Approve\|Reject, DocumentDecisionRationale)` | every decision is immediately documented | Makes each outcome explainable and comparable across groups — a prerequisite for detecting residual disparate impact. |
| `succession(ManualReview, FairnessAudit)`, `altsuccession(BiasMitigationCheck, FairnessAudit)` | each control is closed by an audit record | Provides the evidence base for periodic statistical fairness monitoring. |

## 4. How this reduces bias overall

* **Insulation:** the sensitive-attribute events are structurally *sandwiched* by an attribute-blind `BiasMitigationCheck` (`chainresponse` before, `chainprecedence` before the outcome). No path exists in which the disclosure of race, gender or age is the immediate cause of a decision or of an extra documentation request.
* **Equal scrutiny:** through `coexistence` / `altprecedence` / `noncoexistence`, protected-group decisions and general decisions converge on the *same* amount of review — the classic "minorities get the slow, stricter lane / majorities get the automatic approval" divergence becomes unsatisfiable.
* **Symmetry:** the negative constraints forbid the direct link from a sensitive check to **both** `Reject` and `Approve`, so the model prevents favourable as well as unfavourable discrimination.
* **Accountability & measurability:** mandatory `DocumentDecisionRationale` and `FairnessAudit` events turn every case into an auditable record, enabling downstream conformance checking and disparate-impact statistics.

**Caveat / next step.** DECLARE, as used here, constrains *control flow* only; it can enforce that a check happens but not that the check is *substantively* unbiased. Attribute-level guarantees (e.g. "the rejection rate conditioned on `ApplicantRace` is equal") require **MP-Declare / data-aware DECLARE** with activation and correlation conditions over event payloads, e.g. `Response[CheckApplicantRace, BiasMitigationCheck] | A.race != "" | T.model_version = A.model_version`. It is also advisable to run a consistency (satisfiability) check on the enriched model — e.g. with RuM or a pm4py/LTLf-to-automaton encoding — because the combination of many `chain*` and `non*` constraints can easily produce a vacuous or unsatisfiable model.
