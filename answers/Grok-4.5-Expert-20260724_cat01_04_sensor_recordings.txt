**Labeled Process from Event Log Analysis**

The sensor data shows a clear cyclic sequence on Machine M1. Distinct phases emerge from coordinated changes in temperature, vibration, material flow, tool position, pressure, and energy. These phases map to the following high-level manufacturing activities:

### 1. Idle / Standby (Events 1–3 and 13–15)
- **Readings**: Temperature ~20–21 °C, pressure 1 bar, vibration 0 Hz, material flow 0, tool position 0 mm, energy ~0.40–0.50 kWh.
- **Rationale**: All sensors are at baseline minima with the tool fully retracted. This is the machine waiting state before and after a work cycle. The near-identical values at the start and end of the log confirm a complete cycle returning to the same idle condition.

### 2. Cutting Metal (Events 4–6)
- **Readings**: Temperature rises sharply (50 → 58 °C), vibration high and increasing (30 → 38 Hz), material flow steady at 5 units/s, tool position advancing (5 → 10 mm), energy climbing (2.00 → 3.00 kWh), pressure mild (1–1.5 bar).
- **Rationale**: High vibration combined with continuous material feed and progressive tool movement is characteristic of a cutting or machining operation. Frictional heating explains the temperature rise and elevated energy use. This is the first active phase after idle.

### 3. Assembling Parts (Events 7–8)
- **Readings**: Temperature drops to a stable 40 °C, pressure rises to 2 bar, vibration falls to a moderate 10 Hz, material flow reduces to 2 units/s, tool position held at 15 mm, energy steady at 2.00 kWh.
- **Rationale**: Lower vibration and reduced but still present material flow, together with increased pressure while the tool is held stationary, indicate mechanical joining or positioning of parts rather than aggressive material removal. Energy remains moderate, consistent with assembly rather than high-power processes.

### 4. Welding (Event 9)
- **Readings**: Temperature spikes to 80 °C, vibration jumps to 50 Hz, material flow drops to 0, tool position remains at 15 mm, energy peaks at 5.00 kWh, pressure stays at 2 bar.
- **Rationale**: The sudden extreme temperature and energy surge with zero material flow and very high vibration (while the tool is locked in position) is the classic signature of a welding or high-energy joining process. No feedstock is moving because the process is applying intense localized energy to already-positioned parts.

### 5. Quality Inspection (Events 10–12)
- **Readings**:  
  - Event 10: Rapid cool-down (30 °C), near-zero vibration and flow, tool still at 15 mm, energy falls to 1.00 kWh.  
  - Events 11–12: Temperature stabilizes at 25 °C, low vibration (5 Hz), slight material flow (1 unit/s), tool advances to 20 mm, energy ~1.50–1.60 kWh.
- **Rationale**: After the welding energy spike, the machine cools and then performs gentle tool movement with minimal vibration and only trace material flow. These conditions match a non-destructive inspection or measurement step (e.g., dimensional check, surface scan, or light probing) rather than productive material processing. The tool is repositioned slightly, consistent with moving a sensor or probe across the workpiece.

### Process Summary
The inferred process model for one cycle on M1 is:

**Idle → Cutting Metal → Assembling Parts → Welding → Quality Inspection → Idle**

This sequence is fully consistent with the sensor dynamics:
- Cutting produces heat + vibration + feed.
- Assembly applies pressure with reduced dynamics.
- Welding creates the highest energy/temperature spike with no feed.
- Inspection uses low-energy, low-vibration probing after the heat-intensive step.
- The machine returns to the exact idle baseline, confirming cycle closure.

The labels are derived purely from the relative magnitudes and transitions in the six numeric channels; no external process knowledge beyond standard manufacturing physics was required.
