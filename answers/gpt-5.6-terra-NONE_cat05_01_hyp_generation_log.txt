Analyze the PostgreSQL event log and related tables as a process-mining and operational-risk investigation.

Your objectives are to:

1. Identify anomalous, non-compliant, or undesirable process behaviors relative to the expected order process:

   `Register Order  Perform Credit Check  Validate Stock  Confirm Shipment  Ship Goods  Issue Invoice  Receive Payment`

2. For each anomaly you find:
   - State which cases/events are affected.
   - Explain why the behavior violates or deviates from the expected flow.
   - Assess potential operational, financial, compliance, customer-service, or fraud risk.
   - Form plausible hypotheses for the root cause, such as:
     - workflow/system configuration defects,
     - manual workarounds,
     - insufficient staff training,
     - missing controls or approval gates,
     - timestamp or data-quality problems,
     - resource authorization/segregation-of-duties issues,
     - policy violations.

3. Propose PostgreSQL SQL queries that would help investigate and quantify each hypothesis. Queries may use:
   - `order_event_log`,
   - `orders`,
   - `resources`.

Your SQL should be production-oriented where possible:
- Use `event_id` as a deterministic tie-breaker when timestamps are equal.
- Clearly distinguish event sequence based on `(timestamp, event_id)`.
- Consider duplicate events, missing required activities, out-of-order activities, unusual cycle times, unauthorized resource/department assignments, and suspicious patterns by customer, order type, order value, or resource.
- Where appropriate, provide aggregate queries to measure how widespread each issue is.

Do not assume that the supplied sample is representative of the whole dataset. Clearly distinguish observations directly supported by the supplied data from hypotheses requiring further investigation.
