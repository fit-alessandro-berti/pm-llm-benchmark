## Where bias appears

### 1. Explicit preference for community-group membership
Membership in the **Highland Civic Darts Club** produces a documented **+10 score adjustment**:

- **C001:** 710  720, approved  
- **C004:** 690  700, approved  

No equivalent adjustment is available to applicants without that affiliation. Unless club membership has a validated relationship to repayment risk, this is preferential treatment based on social affiliation rather than underlying creditworthiness.

The adjustment can be outcome-determinative. For example, C001’s unadjusted score of 710 is lower than C003’s 715, yet C001 receives the bonus and is approved while C003 is rejected.

### 2. Apparent preference for local residents
All three local residents are approved:

- C001: approved
- C002: approved
- C004: approved

Among nonresidents, C003 is rejected and only the substantially higher-scoring C005 is approved. Particularly notable comparisons are:

| Comparison | Underlying score | Status | Outcome |
|---|---:|---|---|
| C004 | 690 | Local, club member | Approved after adjustment to 700 |
| C003 | 715 | Nonlocal, no affiliation | Rejected |
| C002 | 720 | Local, no affiliation | Approved |
| C005 | 740 | Nonlocal, no affiliation | Approved |

C003 has stronger apparent creditworthiness than C004 but receives a worse result. The log does not show a numerical residency adjustment, so locality may be embedded in the final rules, used as an unstated threshold modifier, or confounded with club membership.

### 3. Cumulative advantage
C001 and C004 receive both favorable characteristics:

- local residency; and
- preferred community affiliation.

This can create stacked advantages: a score bonus plus potentially more favorable decision rules. Conversely, applicants lacking both characteristics may need materially higher credit scores to receive the same outcome.

## Fairness and equity implications

- **Unequal treatment at similar risk levels:** Applicants with similar or better unadjusted scores receive different outcomes based on geography or affiliation.
- **Proxy discrimination risk:** Residency and club membership may correlate with race, ethnicity, income, neighborhood, social class, or access to local networks, creating disparate impacts even if protected characteristics are not explicitly used.
- **Access-based inequity:** Joining a particular club may require proximity, knowledge, fees, time, or social connections. People without those opportunities are structurally disadvantaged.
- **Geographic exclusion:** Favoring local residents can resemble geographic redlining if location is not demonstrably relevant to legitimate credit risk.
- **Automation does not remove the bias:** The community bonus is applied by the scoring engine and the final outcome by the rules engine. The manual reviews do not appear to correct or challenge these preferences.

## Important limitation

The sample contains only five cases and does not expose the full decision rule, so it cannot prove the exact size or cause of the residency effect. Reviewer-specific bias also cannot be established because reviewers do not record the final decisions. Nevertheless, the explicit club bonus and the score-conditioned outcome differences are strong warning signs.

A fair-process audit should remove or rigorously justify the affiliation bonus, disclose all residency rules, and run counterfactual tests—holding every fact constant while changing only `LocalResident` or `CommunityGroup`. If the outcome changes, the process is directly dependent on those characteristics and requires legal and fairness review.
