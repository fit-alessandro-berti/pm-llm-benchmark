Based on standard Procure-to-Pay (P2P) best practices and internal control principles, the ideal sequence is: 
*Requisition $\rightarrow$ Requisition Approval $\rightarrow$ Purchase Order Issued $\rightarrow$ Goods Received $\rightarrow$ Invoice Received $\rightarrow$ Invoice Matching $\rightarrow$ Payment Released $\rightarrow$ Case Closed.*

Analyzing the provided event log, **Case 1001** follows the standard, healthy P2P flow. However, **Cases 1002, 1003, and 1004** contain significant process anomalies and control breakdowns. 

Here is the detailed analysis of the anomalies found in the event log and why they are problematic:

---

### **Case 1002: Retroactive Approvals and Premature Payment**
**Anomalies:**
1. **Late Requisition Approval:** "Approve Purchase Requisition" occurs *after* the Purchase Order is issued, the Invoice is received, and the Payment is released (Feb 3 at 08:00, whereas payment was released Feb 3 at 07:15).
2. **Payment Before Goods and Invoices (Premature Payment):** "Release Payment" happens on Feb 3, but "Receive Goods" does not occur until Feb 4, and "Match Invoice to PO" happens on Feb 5.

**Why it is problematic:**
* **Lack of Internal Controls:** Approving a requisition *after* a payment has already been sent reduces the approval process to a meaningless "rubber stamp" exercise. It defeats the purpose of preventive controls designed to verify if a purchase is legitimate before company funds are committed or spent.
* **Financial Risk (Pay and Pray):** Paying a vendor before receiving the goods means the organization has no leverage if the goods arrive damaged, late, or not at all. Furthermore, paying before matching the invoice to the PO creates a high risk of overpayment, paying fraudulent invoices, or paying for items never ordered.

---

### **Case 1003: Payment Before Receiving Invoice and Goods**
**Anomalies:**
1. **Payment Before Invoice & Goods:** "Release Payment" occurs on Feb 8. However, the "Vendor Invoice Received" does not happen until Feb 9, and "Receive Goods" happens on Feb 10. 
2. **Out-of-Order Invoice Matching:** Invoice matching occurs on Feb 11, *after* the payment has already been released (Feb 8).

**Why it is problematic:**
* **Cash Flow and Fraud Risk:** Disbursing funds without a vendor invoice means the company is paying blindly, potentially based on estimates or unverified statements. 
* **Missing 3-Way Match Verification:** The foundational control of P2P is the "3-way match" (matching the PO, receiving report, and vendor invoice). Performing the match *after* the payment has been released completely invalidates the control, exposing the business to duplicate payments, incorrect pricing, and billing for unreceived items.

---

### **Case 1004: Maverick Purchasing (PO Issued After Goods Received)**
**Anomalies:**
1. **Goods Received Before PO Issued:** "Receive Goods" happens on Feb 6 at 09:00, but the "Issue Purchase Order" does not occur until Feb 6 at 09:15.

**Why it is problematic:**
* **Maverick/Tail Spend:** Receiving goods or services before a Purchase Order is officially issued indicates unauthorized purchasing behavior (maverick buying). 
* **Legal and Contractual Risks:** Operating without a PO means the company may have received goods without agreed-upon terms, conditions, pricing, or legal protections. It also bypasses budget checks, which can lead to unexpected departmental overspending.

---

### **Summary of Business Impacts:**
* **Weakened Compliance:** Cases 1002, 1003, and 1004 demonstrate severe compliance failures that would likely cause an organization to fail internal/external financial audits (e.g., Sarbanes-Oxley / SOX compliance).
* **Increased Financial Leakage:** Paying ahead of receiving goods, invoices, and matching substantially raises the risk of fraud, duplicate disbursements, and financial loss.
