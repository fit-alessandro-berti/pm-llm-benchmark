## Proposed High-Level Process

The low-level events can be aggregated into five manufacturing stages for each `CaseID`:

1. **Material Preparation and Setup**
2. **Welding Assembly**
3. **Weld Quality Inspection**
4. **Protective Coating and Curing**
5. **Final Visual Inspection**

---

## Event-to-Stage Mapping

| High-Level Activity | Included Low-Level Events | Grouping Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Material Preparation and Setup** | Retrieve raw metal sheet; Scan barcode; Place metal sheet on table; Align metal sheet; Preheat metal sheet | These actions prepare and position the raw material for processing. They occur at the beginning of the case, are temporally close, and establish material identity, location, alignment, and temperature. |
| **Welding Assembly** | Pick up welding tool; Weld corner A; Weld corner B | These activities form one structural assembly operation. Tool pickup is directly related to the subsequent welding actions, which are performed by the same operator and produce the joined component. |
| **Weld Quality Inspection** | Measure weld integrity | This is a distinct quality-control checkpoint performed by a quality sensor after welding. The integrity score is an inspection result rather than a production action. |
| **Protective Coating and Curing** | Apply protective coating; Dry coating | Both activities belong to the surface-finishing phase. Applying the coating and thermally drying it are sequential operations performed to protect and stabilize the assembled component. |
| **Final Visual Inspection** | Visual check | This is the final acceptance check after assembly and finishing. It is performed by a separate quality resource and produces a pass/fail result. |

## Grouping Rules

The aggregation can be implemented using the following rules:

```text
Retrieve raw metal sheet
through
Preheat metal sheet
    -> Material Preparation and Setup

Pick up welding tool
through
Weld corner B
    -> Welding Assembly

Measure weld integrity
    -> Weld Quality Inspection

Apply protective coating
through
Dry coating
    -> Protective Coating and Curing

Visual check
    -> Final Visual Inspection
```

The boundaries are based primarily on:

- **Manufacturing purpose:** preparation, joining, inspection, finishing, and acceptance.
- **Process sequence:** each group represents a recognizable phase in the product lifecycle.
- **Resource type:** operators and preparation equipment are followed by welding resources, quality sensors, coating equipment, and final inspection personnel.
- **State transition:** the product changes from raw material to positioned material, assembled component, inspected weldment, coated component, and finally accepted product.
- **Temporal proximity:** events within a phase occur close together, although short gaps between phases may represent waiting or handoff time.

The two inspection activities could alternatively be rolled into a single **Quality Assurance** stage. However, keeping them separate is more informative because weld integrity inspection is an in-process check, while visual inspection is a final acceptance check after coating and curing.
