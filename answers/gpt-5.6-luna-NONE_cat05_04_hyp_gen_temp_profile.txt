## 1. Anomalies in the temporal profile

- **R  P:** Mean of approximately 25 hours with a standard deviation of only 1 hour. Such low variation may indicate a rigid batch schedule, synthetic timestamps, or an approval process occurring at a fixed daily time.
- **P  N:** Mean of 7 days with a standard deviation of 2 days. This is a substantial delay and indicates high variability, potentially caused by notification backlogs, manual handling, or missing/interpreted event timestamps.
- **A  C:** Mean of 2 hours despite the intended intermediate steps. This may indicate claims being closed without evaluation or approval, or the model may be measuring non-consecutive event pairs in a misleading way.
- **E  N:** Mean of only 5 minutes with a 1-minute standard deviation. This may be valid for automated notifications, but it could also indicate that notification is generated automatically, evaluation timestamps are recorded late, or required approval is being bypassed.
- **N  C:** Mean of 30 minutes with low variation. This may reflect an automated closure process or a scheduled batch operation.
- **Inconsistent process ordering:** The profile includes intervals such as `A  C`, `E  N`, and `P  N`, but does not establish whether all claims follow the intended `R  A  E  P  N  C` sequence.

## 2. Possible hypotheses

1. **Batch-based approvals:** Approvals may be processed at a fixed time each day, producing the unusually stable `R  P` interval.
2. **Notification backlog:** Approved claims may wait in a queue before notifications are sent, explaining the long and variable `P  N` duration.
3. **Missing or delayed event logging:** Events may be entered manually or written asynchronously, making recorded times differ from actual operational times.
4. **Skipped process steps:** Some claims may be closed directly after assignment, or notifications may be sent after evaluation without a recorded approval event.
5. **Automated transitions:** Evaluation-to-notification and notification-to-closure intervals may be short because downstream actions are system-triggered.
6. **Resource bottlenecks:** Specific adjusters, regions, or claim types may experience delays because of workload, specialization requirements, or staffing constraints.
7. **Data-quality problems:** Duplicate events, incorrect timestamps, timezone conversions, or events recorded out of order could distort the temporal profile.
8. **Claim-type-specific workflows:** Auto and home claims may follow different approval or notification procedures, making aggregate averages misleading.

## 3. Verification queries

### A. Calculate intervals between relevant activities

The following query pairs the first occurrence of each activity per claim:

```sql
WITH activity_times AS (
    SELECT
        claim_id,
        MIN(timestamp) FILTER (WHERE activity = 'R') AS receive_time,
        MIN(timestamp) FILTER (WHERE activity = 'A') AS assign_time,
        MIN(timestamp) FILTER (WHERE activity = 'E') AS evaluate_time,
        MIN(timestamp) FILTER (WHERE activity = 'P') AS approve_time,
        MIN(timestamp) FILTER (WHERE activity = 'N') AS notify_time,
        MIN(timestamp) FILTER (WHERE activity = 'C') AS close_time
    FROM claim_events
    GROUP BY claim_id
)
SELECT
    claim_id,
    EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (assign_time - receive_time)) AS r_to_a_seconds,
    EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (evaluate_time - receive_time)) AS r_to_e_seconds,
    EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (approve_time - receive_time)) AS r_to_p_seconds,
    EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (close_time - assign_time)) AS a_to_c_seconds,
    EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (notify_time - evaluate_time)) AS e_to_n_seconds,
    EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (notify_time - approve_time)) AS p_to_n_seconds,
    EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (close_time - notify_time)) AS n_to_c_seconds
FROM activity_times;
```

### B. Find claims outside profile-based ranges

Using a threshold of two standard deviations:

```sql
WITH activity_times AS (
    SELECT
        claim_id,
        MIN(timestamp) FILTER (WHERE activity = 'R') AS r_time,
        MIN(timestamp) FILTER (WHERE activity = 'A') AS a_time,
        MIN(timestamp) FILTER (WHERE activity = 'E') AS e_time,
        MIN(timestamp) FILTER (WHERE activity = 'P') AS p_time,
        MIN(timestamp) FILTER (WHERE activity = 'N') AS n_time,
        MIN(timestamp) FILTER (WHERE activity = 'C') AS c_time
    FROM claim_events
    GROUP BY claim_id
),
intervals AS (
    SELECT
        *,
        EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (p_time - r_time)) AS r_to_p,
        EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (n_time - p_time)) AS p_to_n,
        EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (c_time - a_time)) AS a_to_c,
        EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (n_time - e_time)) AS e_to_n
    FROM activity_times
)
SELECT
    claim_id,
    r_to_p,
    p_to_n,
    a_to_c,
    e_to_n
FROM intervals
WHERE
      r_to_p NOT BETWEEN 90000 - 2 * 3600 AND 90000 + 2 * 3600
   OR p_to_n NOT BETWEEN 604800 - 2 * 172800 AND 604800 + 2 * 172800
   OR a_to_c NOT BETWEEN 7200 - 2 * 3600 AND 7200 + 2 * 3600
   OR e_to_n NOT BETWEEN 300 - 2 * 60 AND 300 + 2 * 60;
```

### C. Identify claims closed shortly after assignment

```sql
WITH event_times AS (
    SELECT
        claim_id,
        MIN(timestamp) FILTER (WHERE activity = 'A') AS assigned_at,
        MIN(timestamp) FILTER (WHERE activity = 'C') AS closed_at
    FROM claim_events
    GROUP BY claim_id
)
SELECT
    c.claim_id,
    c.customer_id,
    c.claim_type,
    c.claim_amount,
    e.assigned_at,
    e.closed_at,
    e.closed_at - e.assigned_at AS assignment_to_close
FROM claims c
JOIN event_times e ON e.claim_id = c.claim_id
WHERE e.assigned_at IS NOT NULL
  AND e.closed_at IS NOT NULL
  AND e.closed_at - e.assigned_at <= INTERVAL '2 hours'
ORDER BY assignment_to_close;
```

### D. Check whether evaluation or approval was skipped

```sql
WITH activity_flags AS (
    SELECT
        claim_id,
        BOOL_OR(activity = 'A') AS has_assign,
        BOOL_OR(activity = 'E') AS has_evaluate,
        BOOL_OR(activity = 'P') AS has_approve,
        BOOL_OR(activity = 'N') AS has_notify,
        BOOL_OR(activity = 'C') AS has_close
    FROM claim_events
    GROUP BY claim_id
)
SELECT *
FROM activity_flags
WHERE has_close
  AND (
       NOT has_evaluate
       OR NOT has_approve
      );
```

### E. Find excessive approval-to-notification delays

```sql
WITH event_times AS (
    SELECT
        claim_id,
        MIN(timestamp) FILTER (WHERE activity = 'P') AS approved_at,
        MIN(timestamp) FILTER (WHERE activity = 'N') AS notified_at
    FROM claim_events
    GROUP BY claim_id
)
SELECT
    c.claim_id,
    c.claim_type,
    c.claim_amount,
    e.approved_at,
    e.notified_at,
    e.notified_at - e.approved_at AS approval_to_notification
FROM claims c
JOIN event_times e ON e.claim_id = c.claim_id
WHERE e.approved_at IS NOT NULL
  AND e.notified_at IS NOT NULL
  AND e.notified_at - e.approved_at > INTERVAL '11 days'
ORDER BY approval_to_notification DESC;
```

### F. Correlate timing anomalies with claim type

```sql
WITH event_times AS (
    SELECT
        claim_id,
        MIN(timestamp) FILTER (WHERE activity = 'P') AS approved_at,
        MIN(timestamp) FILTER (WHERE activity = 'N') AS notified_at
    FROM claim_events
    GROUP BY claim_id
),
durations AS (
    SELECT
        c.claim_type,
        EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (e.notified_at - e.approved_at)) AS p_to_n_seconds
    FROM claims c
    JOIN event_times e ON e.claim_id = c.claim_id
    WHERE e.approved_at IS NOT NULL
      AND e.notified_at IS NOT NULL
)
SELECT
    claim_type,
    COUNT(*) AS claim_count,
    ROUND(AVG(p_to_n_seconds), 2) AS avg_p_to_n_seconds,
    ROUND(STDDEV_SAMP(p_to_n_seconds), 2) AS stdev_p_to_n_seconds,
    MIN(p_to_n_seconds) AS min_p_to_n_seconds,
    MAX(p_to_n_seconds) AS max_p_to_n_seconds
FROM durations
GROUP BY claim_type
ORDER BY avg_p_to_n_seconds DESC;
```

### G. Correlate delays with event resources

```sql
WITH approval_resources AS (
    SELECT DISTINCT ON (claim_id)
        claim_id,
        resource AS approval_resource
    FROM claim_events
    WHERE activity = 'P'
    ORDER BY claim_id, timestamp
),
event_times AS (
    SELECT
        claim_id,
        MIN(timestamp) FILTER (WHERE activity = 'P') AS approved_at,
        MIN(timestamp) FILTER (WHERE activity = 'N') AS notified_at
    FROM claim_events
    GROUP BY claim_id
)
SELECT
    ar.approval_resource,
    COUNT(*) AS claim_count,
    AVG(EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (et.notified_at - et.approved_at))) AS avg_delay_seconds,
    STDDEV_SAMP(EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (et.notified_at - et.approved_at))) AS stdev_delay_seconds,
    COUNT(*) FILTER (
        WHERE et.notified_at - et.approved_at > INTERVAL '11 days'
    ) AS excessive_delay_count
FROM approval_resources ar
JOIN event_times et ON et.claim_id = ar.claim_id
WHERE et.approved_at IS NOT NULL
  AND et.notified_at IS NOT NULL
GROUP BY ar.approval_resource
ORDER BY excessive_delay_count DESC;
```

### H. Detect out-of-order events

```sql
WITH ordered_events AS (
    SELECT
        claim_id,
        activity,
        timestamp,
        LAG(activity) OVER (
            PARTITION BY claim_id
            ORDER BY timestamp, event_id
        ) AS previous_activity,
        LAG(timestamp) OVER (
            PARTITION BY claim_id
            ORDER BY timestamp, event_id
        ) AS previous_timestamp
    FROM claim_events
)
SELECT *
FROM ordered_events
WHERE
       (previous_activity = 'A' AND activity = 'R')
    OR (previous_activity = 'E' AND activity = 'A')
    OR (previous_activity = 'P' AND activity IN ('R', 'A', 'E'))
    OR (previous_activity = 'N' AND activity IN ('R', 'A', 'E', 'P'))
    OR (previous_activity = 'C');
```

### I. Check whether fixed times explain the low R-to-P variation

```sql
WITH times AS (
    SELECT
        claim_id,
        MIN(timestamp) FILTER (WHERE activity = 'R') AS received_at,
        MIN(timestamp) FILTER (WHERE activity = 'P') AS approved_at
    FROM claim_events
    GROUP BY claim_id
)
SELECT
    EXTRACT(HOUR FROM approved_at) AS approval_hour,
    EXTRACT(DOW FROM approved_at) AS approval_day_of_week,
    COUNT(*) AS approval_count
FROM times
WHERE received_at IS NOT NULL
  AND approved_at IS NOT NULL
GROUP BY approval_hour, approval_day_of_week
ORDER BY approval_count DESC;
```

A concentration of approvals at the same hour or on particular weekdays would support the batch-processing hypothesis.
