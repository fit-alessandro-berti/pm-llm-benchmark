**Anomalies identified in the event log (Cases 1002, 1003, and 1004; Case 1001 follows the expected compliant path)**

Standard Procure-to-Pay (P2P) best practices and internal controls follow a controlled sequence: Create/Approve Purchase Requisition (PR)  Issue Purchase Order (PO)  Receive Goods (Goods Receipt / GR)  Vendor Invoice Received  Match Invoice to PO (ideally 3-way match of PO + GR + Invoice)  Release Payment  Close. Approvals authorize commitment of funds; the PO authorizes the vendor; GR confirms physical receipt of value; matching and payment only occur after verification. Deviating from this creates control gaps, risk of unauthorized spend, potential fraud/overpayment, inventory/accounting mismatch, and audit/compliance issues.

### Case 1002 – Multiple severe control failures
Sequence (abbreviated): Create PR  Issue PO (no prior approval)  Vendor Invoice Received  **Release Payment**  Approve PR (far too late)  Receive Goods  Match  Close.

- PO issued without PR approval (unauthorized commitment).  
- Invoice received and **payment released before goods receipt, before matching, and before the (late) PR approval**.  
- Matching occurs only after money has already left.

**Why problematic**: Complete breakdown of segregation of duties and 3-way matching. High risk of paying for unordered/unreceived goods, paying an unapproved requisition, fictitious invoices, or fraud. Accounting records will be unreliable (payment without GR). Severe internal-control and SOX/audit exposure.

### Case 1003 – Premature payment
Sequence: Create/Approve PR  Issue PO  **Release Payment**  Vendor Invoice Received  Receive Goods  Match  Close.

- Payment released days before invoice receipt **and** before goods receipt.  
- Matching performed only after payment.

**Why problematic**: Classic “pay before receive / pay before match” violation. Funds leave without confirmation that goods/services were delivered or that the invoice is legitimate and prices/quantities/terms match the PO. Exposes the company to loss if goods never arrive, if there are discrepancies, or if the invoice is fraudulent. Violates fundamental AP controls.

### Case 1004 – Goods received before PO
Sequence: Create/Approve PR  **Receive Goods**  Issue PO (after GR)  Invoice  Match  Payment  Close.

- Goods received and accepted before any PO exists.

**Why problematic**: “Blind” receipt without a purchasing document. There is no authorized order for the warehouse/AP to check against, making quantity/price/quality validation impossible at the point of receipt. Creates “after-the-fact” purchasing (which defeats budget/approval controls), complicates matching, risks accepting unordered or incorrect goods, and raises inventory and liability questions. While emergency after-the-fact POs sometimes occur, they are non-standard and usually require special exception handling and heightened review; treating them as normal is a process anomaly.

### Case 1001 (for reference – no material anomaly)
Create PR  Approve PR  Issue PO  Receive Goods  Invoice  Match  Payment  Close.  
Timestamps and order are consistent with standard P2P controls and 3-way match before payment.

**Summary of root issues across anomalous cases**:
- Authorization controls bypassed (PO or payment without proper prior approval).  
- Payment released without verified delivery and/or without matching.  
- Temporal order of core documents inverted (GR before PO; payment before GR/invoice/match).  

These patterns would typically appear as conformance-checking or deviation-analysis findings in process mining of a real P2P event log and would trigger exception reports, root-cause analysis (policy gaps, system configuration, training, or fraud risk), and remediation of both process and system controls.
