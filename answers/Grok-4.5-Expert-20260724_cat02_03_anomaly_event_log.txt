**Anomalies in the Procure-to-Pay (P2P) event log**

Standard P2P flow (simplified best-practice sequence):
1. Create Purchase Requisition  
2. Approve Purchase Requisition  
3. Issue Purchase Order  
4. Receive Goods  
5. Vendor Invoice Received  
6. Match Invoice to PO (ideally a 3-way match of PO + goods receipt + invoice)  
7. Release Payment  
8. Close Case  

Payment should occur only after a proper match, and a purchase order should normally exist before goods are received. Deviations from this create control, compliance, fraud, and financial risks.

### Case 1001
**No significant anomalies.**  
The sequence follows the expected order: requisition → approval → PO → goods receipt → invoice → match → payment → close. Timing is reasonable.

### Case 1002 – Multiple severe anomalies
- **PO issued without prior PR approval**  
  (Create PR → Issue PO, then Approve PR only later).  
  This bypasses the authorization control that is supposed to exist before a commitment is made to a vendor.

- **Payment released extremely early**  
  (Vendor Invoice Received → Release Payment occurs *before* PR approval, *before* goods receipt, and *before* any invoice matching).  
  This is a critical control failure. Paying without confirmation that goods were received and without a 3-way match exposes the organization to paying for goods never delivered, overpaying, or paying fraudulent invoices.

- **PR approval occurs after the PO has already been issued and payment has already been released.**  
  The approval is effectively meaningless at that point.

**Why problematic:** High risk of unauthorized commitments, payments for non-existent or incorrect deliveries, and potential fraud or compliance violations (e.g., SOX, internal control frameworks).

### Case 1003 – Critical payment anomaly
- **Payment released before invoice receipt, before goods receipt, and before any matching.**  
  Sequence: Issue PO → Release Payment → Vendor Invoice Received → Receive Goods → Match Invoice.

**Why problematic:** The organization has paid the vendor with no documentary evidence that an invoice exists or that goods/services were delivered. This is a classic “pay first, check later” failure that can lead to loss of funds, difficulty recovering money, and audit findings.

### Case 1004 – Goods receipt before PO
- **Goods received before a Purchase Order is issued.**  
  Sequence: Approve PR → Receive Goods → Issue PO → Invoice → Match → Payment.

**Why problematic:**  
- Receiving goods without a corresponding PO means there is no formal commitment or agreed price/quantity on record at the time of receipt.  
- This weakens the subsequent 3-way match (the PO is created after the fact).  
- It can indicate process workarounds, emergency purchases that were never properly authorized, or attempts to retrospectively legitimize an informal transaction.  
- Inventory, liability, and matching controls become unreliable.

### Summary of key risk themes
| Case  | Main anomalies                                      | Primary risks                                      |
|-------|-----------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------|
| 1001  | None                                                | —                                                  |
| 1002  | PO without approval; payment before match/goods/approval | Unauthorized spend, fraud, control override       |
| 1003  | Payment before invoice and before goods receipt     | Paying without evidence of delivery or liability |
| 1004  | Goods receipt before PO                             | Weak matching, retrospective authorization        |

Cases 1002 and 1003 contain the most serious control breaches (especially premature payment). Case 1004 shows a sequencing violation that undermines the integrity of the subsequent matching step. These patterns are typical red flags in process-mining analyses of real P2P event logs and would normally trigger investigation or process redesign.
