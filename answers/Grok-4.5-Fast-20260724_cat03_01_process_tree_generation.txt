**Process Tree:**

```
->(
  ->('A', 'RG', 'IA'),
  ->('TI', 'TC'),
  X('CE', 'FBP'),
  *(tau, ->('SC', X('TM', 'SPT'), X('FCE', 'FBA'))),
  +('AI', 'NC'),
  ->('FAS', 'DDI'),
  +('BL', 'ICP', 'MRF')
)
```

**Explanation of structure (corresponding to the numbered stages):**

- Stages 1–2 are sequential prefixes: `->('A','RG','IA')` followed by `->('TI','TC')`.
- Stage 3 is the exclusive initial diagnostic: `X('CE','FBP')`.
- Stage 4 is the (optional, repeatable) abnormal-results loop. Because the loop body is entered only when the preceding diagnostic is abnormal, the loop is written `*(tau, body)` where  
  `body = ->('SC', X('TM','SPT'), X('FCE','FBA'))`.  
  This yields the language  
  `ID · (SC · (TM|SPT) · (FCE|FBA))*`  
  (zero or more iterations of the specialist/treatment/re-diagnostic fragment).  
  (Using `*(body, tau)` would force at least one iteration and would therefore be incorrect.)
- Stage 5 is the true-concurrency pair of advanced activities: `+('AI','NC')`.
- Stage 6 is the final administrative sequence: `->('FAS','DDI')`.
- Stage 7 is the three-way true-concurrency clearance block: `+('BL','ICP','MRF')`.

All operators are the binary/n-ary process-tree operators defined in the query; every leaf is either an activity from the expected set or the silent step `tau`. The parent/children links required by the `ProcessTree` constructor follow the nesting above.
