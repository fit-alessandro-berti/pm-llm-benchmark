## Case-by-case assessment

| Case | Normative compliance | Desiderata compliance | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Compliant | Follow-up call not recorded | Normatively valid; potentially misses one recommended practice |

| **3** | Compliant | Credit check late, applicant informed late, and no follow-up call | Valid process sequence, but suboptimal performance |

### Case 1

**Normative rules:** Compliant.

The required approved-loan sequence is followed:

> Application Received  Credit Check  Risk Assessment  Approved Decision  Inform Applicant  Finalize Documentation  Close Case

There are no skipped, reversed, or contradictory mandatory activities.

**Desiderata:**

- Credit Check occurred 30 minutes after receipt, within the recommended 24 hours.
- Applicant was informed 2 hours after the decision, within the recommended 48 hours.
- No Follow-up Call is recorded.

Thus, Case 1 satisfies the timing recommendations but appears to miss the optional follow-up call. Because that call is only encouraged, this is not a normative violation. It is also possible that a follow-up was handled informally or simply was not recorded.

### Case 2

**Normative rules:** Noncompliant for multiple reasons.

1. **Risk Assessment occurred before Credit Check.**  
   Risk Assessment was performed at 08:20, while Credit Check occurred at 09:00. This directly violates the mandatory ordering rule.

2. **Finalize Documentation occurred after rejection.**  
   The loan was rejected at 09:30, but documentation was finalized at 10:00. A rejected case must proceed toward closure without finalizing documentation.

3. **Inform Applicant is missing.**  
   The applicant must be informed after the loan decision and before the subsequent branch-completion activities. No such event is recorded before closure.

The observed trace was:

> Application Received  Risk Assessment  Credit Check  Rejected Decision  Finalize Documentation  Close Case

The required rejected-loan trace was:

> Application Received  Credit Check  Risk Assessment  Rejected Decision  Inform Applicant  Close Case

These are serious normative deviations, not merely suboptimal behavior.

**Desiderata:**

- Credit Check occurred within one hour of receipt, so the 24-hour recommendation was met.
- The 48-hour notification recommendation cannot be credited because no Inform Applicant event exists. More importantly, the missing notification is already a normative violation.
- No Follow-up Call is recorded, so the optional follow-up recommendation was not met.

**Possible interpretations:**

- The Risk Assessment and Credit Check timestamps may reflect delayed or inaccurate event logging rather than actual execution order.
- “Finalize Documentation” may have been mislabeled and might instead mean administrative archiving of rejection materials.
- The notification may have occurred through an unlogged channel.

These explanations could indicate data-quality or activity-labeling issues, but without supporting evidence, the case must be treated as normatively noncompliant.

---

### Case 3

**Normative rules:** Compliant.

The required approved-loan sequence is followed correctly:

> Application Received  Credit Check  Risk Assessment  Approved Decision  Inform Applicant  Finalize Documentation  Close Case

Although several activities were delayed, the mandatory order and approval-specific branch logic were respected.

**Desiderata:** Multiple recommendations were not met.

1. **Credit Check was late.**  
   It occurred approximately **49 hours** after application receipt, exceeding the recommended 24-hour window.

2. **Applicant notification was late.**  
   The decision occurred on January 14 at 12:00, and notification occurred on January 16 at 15:00—approximately **51 hours later**, exceeding the recommended 48-hour window.

3. **No Follow-up Call was recorded.**

These deviations are not severe violations because the requirements are recommendations rather than hard constraints. They nevertheless indicate suboptimal timeliness and customer communication.

**Possible interpretations:**

- The credit-check delay could have resulted from weekends, third-party bureau availability, missing information, or operational backlog.
- The notification delay may have resulted from additional internal review, difficulty contacting the applicant, or non-business-hour calculation conventions.
- A separate follow-up call may have been unnecessary because the notification itself provided sufficient alignment, or the call may have occurred without being logged.

## Overall conclusion

- **Normatively violating case:** **Case 2**
- **Normatively compliant cases:** **Cases 1 and 3**
- **Desiderata shortfalls:**
  - **Case 1:** No recorded Follow-up Call.
  - **Case 2:** No recorded Follow-up Call; applicant notification is absent altogether, which is also a stricter normative violation.
  - **Case 3:** Credit Check exceeded 24 hours, notification exceeded 48 hours, and no Follow-up Call was recorded.

Case 2 requires corrective attention because it breaches mandatory process logic. Cases 1 and 3 primarily present opportunities for improving recommended customer-service and timeliness practices.
