**This visualization illustrates the concept of *Proclets* (lightweight interacting process fragments/objects with their own life-cycles), applied to a healthcare laboratory scenario.**

Proclets (introduced by Wil van der Aalst and colleagues) provide a way to model complex processes as collections of simpler, loosely coupled process instances that communicate explicitly, rather than forcing everything into one large monolithic workflow. This is especially useful for domains like healthcare that involve one-to-many relationships, different levels of granularity, and dynamic interactions.

The figure has three parts:

### (a) Two proclet classes connected through two channels
This is the core process model (using a YAWL-style notation for control flow inside each proclet).

- **Left side — “Lab visit” proclet class**: Instantiated once per patient lab visit / blood sample collection.  
  - Roles: doctor (Order entry, and later Send report), lab assistant (Take blood sample, Receive result / Judge results, Do tests again / Receive updated result, Send report).  
  - Typical flow: Order entry → Take blood sample → (send order performatives) → Receive result(s) → Judge results.  
  - After judging: either “Everything fine” (leading toward finishing the related tests and sending the final report) or “Do tests again” (triggering re-testing). Updated results can be received if needed. Ends with Send report and a termination marker (black square).

- **Right side — “Lab test” proclet class**: Instantiated once *per individual test* performed on the sample (e.g., one instance for an HGB test).  
  - Role: chemical analyst.  
  - Flow: Created → Perform test → Make report → (send result) → possibly Perform test again + Provide updated report → Finish lab test (black square termination).

- **Channels** (the dashed boxes mediating interaction):  
  - **Order system**: Used mainly to create / order lab tests (and related control messages) from the Lab visit side to Lab test instances.  
  - **HIS** (Hospital Information System): Used to send results, updated results, and finish signals back from Lab test instances to the Lab visit instance.

Ports (connection points between tasks and channels) are annotated with pairs such as `1:1`, `1:*`, `*:1`, etc. These encode:
- **Cardinality** (how many recipients/senders a performative involves — `*` = zero or more, `1` = exactly one, `+` = at least one, `?` = zero or one).
- **Multiplicity** (how many times a performative may be exchanged via that port over the lifetime of a proclet instance).

White rectangles are typically human tasks; gray ones are often automated or decision-related. Circles represent conditions/places. Black triangles or filled shapes mark starts/ends or special interaction points. The diagram shows how one higher-level Lab visit can dynamically create and interact with multiple lower-level Lab test instances, including possible re-testing loops.

### (b) Class diagram containing the two proclet classes
A simple UML-style structural view:
- A **Lab visit** *requires* **Lab test**(s).
- Multiplicities: Lab visit side is `1..1`, Lab test side is `1..*`.

This captures the natural one-to-many relationship: one patient visit / blood sample can require multiple individual lab tests.

### (c) Example of a performative
Performatives are the structured messages (inspired by speech-act theory) that proclets exchange over channels. Each has attributes such as time, channel, sender, receiver(s), action, content, scope, and direction.

The table shows a concrete example:
- **Time**: 11:00  
- **Channel**: Order system  
- **Sender**: Lab visit – John  
- **Receivers**: Lab test – HGB John  
- **Action**: Create  
- **Content**: “Can you perform a HGB test for John?”  
- **Scope**: Private  
- **Direction**: OUT  

This is how a Lab visit instance requests the creation of a specific Lab test instance (here, a hemoglobin test for patient John). Received performatives are typically stored in a knowledge base local to the proclet so it can make decisions (e.g., “have I received a result for every test I ordered?” before allowing “Judge results” to fire).

**In short**, the figure demonstrates the power of the proclets approach: divide a complex process into natural interacting fragments at the right levels of granularity, make interactions (via channels + performatives + ports with cardinalities/multiplicities) first-class citizens, and thereby handle dynamic multiplicities and object-centric behavior more cleanly than a single flat workflow model.
