### Anomalies Identified in the Temporal Profile Model

1. **Receive to Approve (R to P)**
   - **Anomaly:** The average time is approximately 25 hours, which is significantly longer than the expected 1 day (86400 seconds). The standard deviation is very low (3600 seconds or 1 hour), suggesting this deviation is not due to random variation but possibly an enforced schedule or systematic delay.
   - **Potential Reason:** This could be due to a mandatory waiting period or a review process that is strictly enforced, regardless of the typical workflow. It might also indicate that the system waits for certain conditions to be met before proceeding to approval, which are not captured in the standard profile.

2. **Approve to Notify (P to N)**
   - **Anomaly:** There is a prolonged average delay of 7 days with a high standard deviation of 2 days. This is notably longer and more variable than expected.
   - **Potential Reason:** This delay could be attributed to a backlog in notification processes, possibly due to manual or bottlenecked communication channels. Alternatively, it might indicate that some claims are being closed prematurely without the required notification steps being completed.

3. **Assign to Close (A to C)**
   - **Anomaly:** The average time between assign and close is only about 2 hours, with a standard deviation of 1 hour. This suggests that many claims are being closed quickly after assignment, potentially bypassing necessary evaluation or approval steps.
   - **Potential Reason:** This may indicate that claims are being closed prematurely, possibly due to automated systems that close claims without proper evaluation. It could also point to a lack of oversight or a process where claims are incorrectly marked as closed.

4. **Evaluate to Notify (E to N)**
   - **Anomaly:** The average time between evaluation and notification is just 5 minutes, which is much shorter than the typical 5 minutes to 1 hour indicated by the standard deviation.
   - **Potential Reason:** This could suggest that there is a rush to notify customers after evaluation, possibly due to automated notifications that do not align with the actual business process timeline. It might also indicate that the evaluation step is being bypassed or that notifications are sent prematurely.

### Hypotheses on the Observed Anomalies

1. **R to P Anomaly:**
   - **Hypothesis 1:** The system enforces a mandatory waiting period for approval, possibly to ensure quality checks are met. This waiting period is not reflected in the standard temporal profile.
   - **Hypothesis 2:** There might be a manual review process that is not automated, causing delays that are not captured in the model.

2. **P to N Anomaly:**
   - **Hypothesis 1:** The notification process is experiencing bottlenecks, leading to delays in communicating the approval status to customers.
   - **Hypothesis 2:** The system may be closing claims automatically without completing the notification step, resulting in the long average delay.

3. **A to C Anomaly:**
   - **Hypothesis 1:** There is an automated system that closes claims immediately after assignment, possibly due to insufficient evidence or incomplete claims.
   - **Hypothesis 2:** The process may be skipping the evaluation step, leading to premature closure.

4. **E to N Anomaly:**
   - **Hypothesis 1:** The notification process is overly rapid, possibly due to automated systems sending notifications immediately after evaluation.
   - **Hypothesis 2:** The evaluation process might be bypassed or completed too quickly, leading to an immediate notification step.

### Proposed SQL Verification Queries

To investigate these anomalies, the following SQL queries can be used on the `claim_events` table:

1. **Identify Claims with Anomalies in R to P Timing**
   ```sql
   SELECT claim_id, 
          AVG(EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (timestamp)) - EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM LAG(timestamp) OVER (PARTITION BY claim_id ORDER BY timestamp))) AS avg_time_seconds,
          STDDEV(EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (timestamp)) - EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM LAG(timestamp) OVER (PARTITION BY claim_id ORDER BY timestamp))) AS stddev_time_seconds
   FROM claim_events
   WHERE (activity = 'R' AND next_activity = 'P')
   GROUP BY claim_id
   HAVING avg_time_seconds > 90000 OR stddev_time_seconds < 600;
   ```

2. **Identify Claims with Anomalies in P to N Timing**
   ```sql
   SELECT claim_id, 
          AVG(EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (timestamp)) - EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM LAG(timestamp) OVER (PARTITION BY claim_id ORDER BY timestamp))) AS avg_time_seconds,
          STDDEV(EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (timestamp)) - EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM LAG(timestamp) OVER (PARTITION BY claim_id ORDER BY timestamp))) AS stddev_time_seconds
   FROM claim_events
   WHERE (activity = 'P' AND next_activity = 'N')
   GROUP BY claim_id
   HAVING avg_time_seconds > 604800 OR stddev_time_seconds > 172800;
   ```

3. **Identify Claims Closed Immediately After Assignment (A to C Anomaly)**
   ```sql
   SELECT claim_id, 
          AVG(EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (timestamp)) - EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM LAG(timestamp) OVER (PARTITION BY claim_id ORDER BY timestamp))) AS avg_time_seconds,

   ```

4. **Identify Claims with Anomalies in E to N Timing**
   ```sql
   SELECT claim_id, 
          AVG(EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (timestamp)) - EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM LAG(timestamp) OVER (PARTITION BY claim_id ORDER BY timestamp))) AS avg_time_seconds,
   ```

These queries will help identify specific instances of the anomalies by analyzing the timestamps of activities within each claim. By examining these results, adjustments to the process can be made to address any identified inefficiencies or irregularities.
