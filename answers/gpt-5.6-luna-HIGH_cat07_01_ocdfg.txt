The visualization is an **object-centric process flow** showing how several business object types move through purchasing, sales, returns, payment, and invoicing activities.

- **Source objects at the top:**  
  - VendorInvoiceItem — **1.2M objects**  
  - ReturnOrderItem — **0.8M**  
  - PurchaseRequisitionItem — **2.0M**  
  - SalesOrderItem — **3.5M**  
  - PurchaseOrderItem — **5.0M**

- Each source flows into a **Start** event, after which the streams merge into shared activities. Colored lines and dots identify the object streams:
  - Purple: purchase requisitions
  - Blue: purchase orders/payment-related objects
  - Orange: sales orders and fulfillment
  - Teal: vendor invoices/returns
  - Red: return-order or closure-related objects

- **Requisition and purchasing flow:**  
  Purchase requisitions go through **Create Requisition**—with an overall volume of **3.0M**—and then **Approve Requisition**, which has **5.7M** objects. This branch connects to activities such as **Send Order**, **Check Stock**, **Process Return**, and **Approve Payment**.

- **Sales-order fulfillment flow:**  
  Sales orders and purchase orders converge at **Create Order**, with **8.5M** objects. The primary sequence is:
  **Create Order  Pack Goods  Ship Goods  Confirm Delivery  Close Order**.  
  The activities contain approximately **2.0M**, **1.8M**, **1.7M**, and **1.7M** objects respectively.

- **Returns, payment, and invoicing:**  
  Return-related objects pass through **Process Return** (**0.8M**), while payment and invoicing proceed through **Approve Payment** (**1.5M**) and **Create Invoice** (**2.0M**).

- The diagram includes **transition times in milliseconds**. Examples include:
  - 300 ms from Purchase Requisition start to Create Requisition
  - 400 ms from Sales Order start to Create Order
  - 300 ms from Create Order to Pack Goods
  - 200 ms from Pack Goods to Ship Goods
  - 220 ms from Ship Goods to Confirm Delivery
  - 180 ms from Confirm Delivery to Close Order

Several branches terminate in separate **End** nodes, reflecting different completion paths for orders, invoices, and other process variants.
