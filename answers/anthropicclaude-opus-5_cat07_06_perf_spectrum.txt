## Overview

This is a **performance spectrum** (the kind of diagram produced by tools such as the Performance Spectrum Miner for processmining event logs). It appears to be built from the wellknown **Road Traffic Fine Management** event log, since the three activities shown are *Send Fine*, *Insert Fine Notification* and *Send for Credit Collection*.

**Structure of the chart**

- The **horizontal axis is time**, running from `2000-01-25 01:00:00` on the left, through `2006-09-09 02:00:00` in the middle, to `2013-04-24 02:00:00` on the right — roughly 13 years of process execution.
- Three **horizontal black lines** represent activities, stacked in the order they occur in the process: *Send Fine* (top), *Insert Fine Notification* (middle), *Send for Credit Collection* (bottom).
- Each thin diagonal/vertical line is **one case (one fine)**: it starts at the time the upper activity occurred and ends at the time the following activity occurred. The **slope encodes waiting/processing time** — nearvertical lines mean the handover was fast, strongly slanted lines mean a long delay.
- Line **colour encodes duration classification**: grey for "normal/faster" handovers, shades of pink to saturated red for the slowest ones.

## What the picture shows

**Segment 1 — Send Fine  Insert Fine Notification (upper band)**

Extremely dense, almost vertical grey strokes spread more or less continuously across the whole 13year period. The nearvertical orientation means notifications are inserted a short, fairly uniform time after the fine is sent. The density varies: there are visible vertical "stripes" of many simultaneous cases alternating with narrow white gaps, suggesting **bursty / batched issuing of fines** rather than a perfectly smooth arrival stream. A few strokes lean noticeably to the right (isolated cases with much longer delays).

**Segment 2 — Insert Fine Notification  Send for Credit Collection (lower band)**

This segment looks completely different: long, strongly slanted lines forming a series of **triangular "fan" or "sawtooth" shapes**. Each fan is made of many lines starting at different times along the *Insert Fine Notification* line but all converging to a single point (or a very narrow interval) on the *Send for Credit Collection* line.

- This convergence pattern is the classic signature of **batching**: creditcollection requests are not sent casebycase but accumulated and dispatched together in periodic bulk runs (roughly once a year / at irregular but discrete moments, judging by the ~10–12 fans across the timeline).
- Because of the batching, **waiting time depends heavily on where in the batching cycle a case arrives**: cases whose notification was inserted just after a batch run wait a very long time (steep, long lines, often coloured red), while cases arriving just before a run wait only briefly (short, steepertovertical lines near the convergence point).
- The **red colouring is concentrated in the interior of the fans and along their long leading edges**, confirming that these are the slow handovers; the reddest, most saturated cluster is around the middle of the timeline (roughly 2005–2007), where a very dense fan converges near 2007.

## Takeaways

1. The first handover is fast and continuous; the bottleneck of the process sits in the second handover.
2. *Send for Credit Collection* is executed in **large periodic batches**, producing systematic, highly variable waiting times (from days to years) that are an artefact of the batching policy rather than of case complexity.
3. Workload is unevenly distributed over the 13 years, with visible gaps (periods with few or no fines recorded) and denser clusters, e.g. around 2001–2003 and again after 2008.
