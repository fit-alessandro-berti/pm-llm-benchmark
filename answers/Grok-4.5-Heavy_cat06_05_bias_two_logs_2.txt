**Group B (Unprotected Group) exhibits the bias.**

Group A applies no special adjustments: every case has ScoreAdjustment = 0, ProfessionalAssociation = None, and decisions rest on the raw SkillScore, PersonalityScore, and CulturalFit values. Group B explicitly applies a non-merit “+5 (Association Boost)” to CulturalFit for candidates who belong to the LocalBusinessLeadersClub. That preferential rule is absent from Group A and is the clear source of bias.

### How the bias manifests
The bias is injected at the CulturalFitCheck activity (performed by the HR Analyst) and then propagates unchanged through FinalInterview and HiringDecision.

- **U001** (club member): raw CulturalFit 60 → adjusted 65; SkillScore 80 → Hired.  
- **U003** (club member): raw CulturalFit 58 → adjusted 63; SkillScore only 75, PersonalityScore 65 → Hired.  
- **U002** (no association): CulturalFit remains 60; SkillScore 78 → Not Hired.

In Group A the same raw CulturalFit threshold of ~60 consistently leads to rejection (P002), while candidates with CulturalFit 65 are hired. The boost therefore lowers the effective bar exclusively for club affiliates. All three Group-B cases are LocalResident = TRUE; the two who receive the boost are the only ones with the club affiliation. Group-A candidates (all non-residents, no affiliation) have no pathway to the same adjustment.

Even within the small sample the directional effect is visible: average SkillScore of hired candidates is ~83.5 in Group A versus ~77.5 in Group B. The process therefore hires lower-scoring affiliates while rejecting non-affiliates with comparable or superior raw scores.

### Implications for fairness and equity
- **Procedural fairness** is violated because identical activities are governed by different rules depending on group membership and club affiliation.  
- **Individual fairness** is violated because candidates with similar or better objective scores are treated differently solely because of an association that is not shown to be job-related.  
- **Group fairness / equity** is compromised: the protected group (non-local residents) has no access to the boost, while the unprotected group contains an embedded privilege for members of a local professional network. This creates the conditions for disparate impact even when overall hire rates happen to be equal (2/3 in both groups).  
- The practice undermines meritocracy, reduces the likelihood of diverse hiring outcomes, and risks legal exposure under equal-opportunity or anti-discrimination frameworks that prohibit preferential treatment based on non-job-related characteristics or that produce unjustified disparate impact on protected classes.  

In short, the event log for Group B embeds an explicit, non-merit preference that systematically advantages a subset of the unprotected group and disadvantages everyone else—exactly the form of bias that fair hiring processes are designed to eliminate.
