### Explanation of Logic

#### 1. Case Identification Logic
In process mining, a "case" should represent a logical unit of work. I analyzed the temporal and contextual flow of the user's actions and identified two distinct, coherent cases:

*   **Case 1: Data_Gathering_and_Drafting** — This case captures the user's preparatory work. The user opens a scratchpad document (`Document1.docx`), drafts initial ideas, and then engages in a multi-application research loop: checking email for meeting details, reviewing a PDF draft for key findings, and updating an Excel budget. The loop concludes when the user integrates the budget reference back into `Document1.docx` and closes it. This represents a complete "data gathering and drafting" subprocess.
*   **Case 2: Quarterly_Report_Finalization** — This case captures the actual authoring of the final deliverable. The user opens `Quarterly_Report.docx` briefly at the start (likely to check requirements), switches away to do the prep work in Case 1, and then returns to it to write the Executive Summary and close the document. 

*Note: While one could argue the entire log is one massive case (Authoring Quarterly Report), splitting it into the "Prep/Draft" phase and the "Finalization" phase yields much cleaner, more analyzable process models, as it isolates the iterative data-gathering loop from the linear final-authoring phase.*

#### 2. Activity Naming Logic
Raw system logs contain low-level OS actions (e.g., `FOCUS`, `TYPING`, `SWITCH`). To make the event log valuable for process analysis, I translated these into higher-level, business-meaningful activities based on the application context:

*   **`FOCUS`**  **`Open Document`**: Represents the initiation of work on a specific artifact.
*   **`TYPING`**  Context-dependent:
    *   In Word/Chrome  **`Compose Text`** (Authoring written content).
    *   In Excel  **`Update Data`** (Modifying structured data).
*   **`SCROLL`**  **`Review Content`** (Passive consumption of information).
*   **`HIGHLIGHT`**  **`Annotate Content`** (Active marking/evaluation of information).
*   **`CLICK`**  Context-dependent:
    *   Open Email  **`Read Email`**
    *   Reply  **`Compose Reply`**
    *   Send  **`Send Email`**
*   **`SWITCH`**  **`Switch Application`**: Retained as a distinct activity to allow analysts to measure context-switching overhead and application handoffs.
*   **`SAVE`**  **`Save Work`**: Standardized persistence action.
*   **`CLOSE`**  **`Close Document`**: Represents the conclusion of work on a specific artifact.

#### 3. Coherent Narrative
The event log tells a clear story of modern knowledge work: A user starts by opening their final report (`Case 2`), realizes they need to gather information, and pivots to a scratchpad (`Case 1`). They oscillate between communication (Chrome), reference material (Acrobat), and data (Excel) before synthesizing it into their scratchpad. Once the prep work is closed, they context-switch back to their main report (`Case 2`) to finalize the executive summary, demonstrating a classic "prepare-then-author" workflow.
