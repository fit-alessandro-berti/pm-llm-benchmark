## 1. Overview of the Abstraction Approach

Before naming anything, I looked for **boundaries** in the low-level stream. Four signals consistently coincide in this log:

| Signal | What it tells us |
|---|---|
| **Change of resource / resource role** | Operator A (material handling)  Operator B (welding)  Quality Sensor #1 (measurement)  Coating Unit #3 / Heating Unit #2 (finishing)  Operator C (inspection). Each role change marks a handover, i.e. a phase boundary. |
| **Temporal gaps** | Intra-phase gaps are 2–15 s; the gap between "Preheat" (08:00:20) and "Pick up welding tool" (08:01:00) is 40 s — an order of magnitude larger. Large relative gaps = phase transitions. |
| **Object / state transformation** | Each block leaves the workpiece in a new state: *staged & heated*  *joined*  *verified*  *coated & cured*  *released*. A high-level step should correspond to one state change. |
| **Semantics of the activity label** | "Pick up welding tool" is a *setup* action that only makes sense as a preamble to "Weld …"; "Dry coating" only makes sense as the completion of "Apply protective coating". Such setup/teardown events must be absorbed into the step they serve, not treated as steps. |

Both cases (A1, B2) exhibit the **identical 12-event pattern**, so the mapping can be expressed as a deterministic activity  phase dictionary, with time/resource signals used only to validate the segmentation and to handle deviations (rework, retries).

## 2. Proposed High-Level Activities

### HL-1 — **Material Preparation & Setup**
| Low-level events | Resources |
|---|---|
| Retrieve raw metal sheet · Scan barcode · Place metal sheet on table · Align metal sheet · Preheat metal sheet | Operator A, Robot Arm #2, Heating Unit #1 |

**Rationale:** All five events serve one purpose — bringing a raw part into a *weld-ready* state. They form one contiguous 15-second block, involve only material-handling/staging resources, and none of them alters the part's geometry. The barcode scan is identification/traceability for *this* material, so it belongs here rather than being a standalone "Registration" step. Preheating is a *precondition of welding*, but it is performed by the staging equipment before the welding operator arrives, so it closes the preparation block (its role is documented in the step's attributes).

**Derived attributes:** PartID, Barcode, TableID, Alignment result, Preheat temperature, start/end timestamp, duration.

---

### HL-2 — **Component Joining (Welding Assembly)**
| Low-level events | Resources |
|---|---|
| Pick up welding tool · Weld corner A · Weld corner B | Operator B |

**Rationale:** Single resource, single tool (W-12), single value-adding transformation (the part becomes structurally joined). "Pick up welding tool" is tool setup with zero business meaning on its own and is 5 s before the first weld — it is absorbed as the *start* of the step. The two corner welds are repetitions of the same operation type and should be aggregated into one step with a `WeldCount`/`WeldPoints` attribute rather than shown as separate process steps.

**Derived attributes:** ToolID, WeldType, WeldPoints = [Corner A, Corner B], WeldCount = 2, duration.

---

### HL-3 — **In-Process Weld Quality Verification**
| Low-level events | Resources |
|---|---|
| Measure weld integrity | Quality Sensor #1 |

**Rationale:** Kept as its own high-level step even though it is a single event, because (a) it is performed by a *different resource class* (automated quality sensor, not the welding operator), (b) it is a **control point** whose outcome can gate the process (a low IntegrityScore should trigger rework/scrap), and (c) analysts typically want conformance, pass-rate and rework metrics on this measurement. Merging it into HL-2 would hide the decision point.
*Alternative (coarser) view:* if you only need 4 stages, fold it into HL-2 as "Weld & Verify" and keep IntegrityScore as an attribute.

**Derived attributes:** IntegrityScore, PassFail (score  threshold), Sensor ID.

---

### HL-4 — **Surface Treatment (Coating & Curing)**
| Low-level events | Resources |
|---|---|
| Apply protective coating · Dry coating | Coating Unit #3, Heating Unit #2 |

**Rationale:** Two different machines, but one indivisible chemical/thermal treatment: an applied coating is not a finished coating until it is cured. They are tightly coupled in time (15 s apart), always co-occur, and always in this order. Grouping them yields one meaningful "part is protected" state change instead of two half-steps. Note that the *heating* here is functionally different from the preheating in HL-1 (curing vs. forming preparation) — hence a different high-level step despite similar equipment type.

**Derived attributes:** CoatingType, Cure temperature, application-to-cure delay, total treatment duration.

---

### HL-5 — **Final Quality Inspection & Release**
| Low-level events | Resources |
|---|---|
| Visual check | Operator C |

**Rationale:** Terminal control point performed by a dedicated inspector after all transformations are complete. It determines whether the case leaves the process as *accepted*. It is deliberately **not** merged with HL-3: HL-3 is an in-process, automated, weld-specific measurement; HL-5 is a manual, end-of-line, whole-product acceptance decision. Different purpose, different resource, different downstream consequence.

**Derived attributes:** Inspector, Check outcome, Case end timestamp, total case lead time.

## 3. Abstraction Rules (applicable to the full log)

**R1 — Activity mapping dictionary (primary rule):**

```
Retrieve raw metal sheet     -> Material Preparation & Setup
Scan barcode                 -> Material Preparation & Setup
Place metal sheet on table   -> Material Preparation & Setup
Align metal sheet            -> Material Preparation & Setup
Preheat metal sheet          -> Material Preparation & Setup
Pick up welding tool         -> Component Joining
Weld corner *                -> Component Joining
Measure weld integrity       -> Weld Quality Verification
Apply protective coating     -> Surface Treatment
Dry coating                  -> Surface Treatment
Visual check                 -> Final Quality Inspection & Release
```

**R2 — Instance formation:** within a CaseID, sort by timestamp and cut a new high-level *instance* whenever the mapped phase changes **or** the gap to the previous event of the same phase exceeds a threshold (e.g. > 3 × median intra-phase gap,  60 s here). This makes repeated visits to the same phase (rework) appear as separate instances, e.g. `Component Joining #1`, `Component Joining #2`.

**R3 — Event construction:**
- `start = min(timestamp)`, `complete = max(timestamp)` of the member events (emit as a lifecycle interval).
- `Resources = distinct resources`, with a `PrimaryResource` = resource of the value-adding event (e.g. welder, coating unit).
- `AdditionalInfo` keys are lifted into typed attributes; numeric values kept as-is, repeated values aggregated (count, min/max/avg).
- `LowLevelEventCount` retained for drill-down and traceability.

**R4 — Setup/teardown absorption:** events matching `Pick up *`, `Place *`, `Load *`, `Release *`, `Dry *`, `Cure *` are never standalone steps; they attach to the adjacent value-adding event of the same phase.

**R5 — Deviation handling:**
- IntegrityScore < threshold followed by further weld events  mark `Rework = true` on the case and keep the loop `Joining  Verification  Joining  Verification`.
- Visual check = Failed followed by no further events  case ends in `Rejected`.
- Missing expected phase  flag `IncompleteAbstraction` rather than silently merging neighbours.

## 5. Why This Abstraction Is Useful

- **12 low-level events  5 process steps**, giving a directly readable end-to-end model instead of a spaghetti of micro-actions.
- **Waiting time becomes visible:** value-adding time per case is ~40 s, but lead time is ~115–120 s; the gaps between HL-1HL-2 (40 s, waiting for Operator B) and HL-3HL-4 (10 s) are now measurable queue/handover times, not hidden inside event sequences.
- **Two explicit control points** (HL-3 automated, HL-5 manual) enable first-pass-yield and rework analysis.
- **Resource analysis per stage** becomes meaningful (Operator B is the likely bottleneck / handover delay source; Table T-3 and Tool W-12 are shared across cases  potential contention).
- **Traceability is preserved:** every high-level event keeps pointers to its constituent low-level events, so drill-down for root-cause analysis remains possible.
