- **Map matching** (snapping coordinates to the road network) and **trip segmentation** into drive vs. stop episodes.

- **Clock skew** between GPS, handhelds, and dispatch servers  wrong event ordering; mitigate with tolerance windows and sequence fields where available.
- **GPS drift / urban canyons** in dense high-rise areas  false "stop" detections; requires smoothing and map matching.
- **Missing scans** (drivers forget "Arrive Customer")  impute from geofence events but flag as *inferred* to preserve data provenance.

- **Macro-level**: Start Shift  (Loading)  Depart Depot  [Travel  Stop]*  Arrive Depot  End Shift.

| **On-Time Delivery (OTD)** | % successful deliveries within window | Success timestamp vs. dispatch window |
| **First-Attempt Success Rate** | % delivered on first try | Success on attempt 1 ÷ all attempts (package-journey view) |
| **Avg. Service Time per Stop** | Dwell time at customer | Depart Customer  Arrive Customer; decompose into parking (geofence-arrival  scan) and interaction (scan  depart) |
| **Travel-to-Service Ratio** | Driving vs. stopped time | Sum of travel-leg durations ÷ sum of dwell times per case |
| **Stops per Route-Hour** | Route productivity | Deliveries ÷ (Arrive Depot  Depart Depot) |
| **Excess Travel Time** | Delay beyond free-flow | Actual leg time  free-flow baseline (off-peak history or routing engine) |
| **Idle Ratio** | Engine-on, zero speed | Idle time ÷ shift time (fuel proxy) |
| **Fuel per km / per Package** | Cost efficiency | Telematics fuel if available; else model from distance, speed profile, idle time |
| **Vehicle Utilization** | Productive use of fleet | Active route time ÷ available time; packages ÷ capacity |
| **Failed Delivery Rate & Re-delivery Cost** | Waste from failed attempts | Failures ÷ attempts; extra km/minutes traced via package-journey cases |
| **Unscheduled Downtime** | Breakdown impact | Frequency/duration of unscheduled stops; breakdowns per 1,000 km; MTBF from maintenance logs |
| **Overtime Rate** | Shift overruns | End Shift vs. scheduled end |
| **Departure Punctuality** | Depot process health | Depart Depot vs. planned departure |

### 2.2 Bottleneck Identification Techniques

1. **Performance-annotated process maps**: compare mean vs. median sojourn times — a large gap signals a long tail worth investigating; inspect 90th-percentile dwell times per activity.
2. **Transition-level analysis**: for every *Depart Stop i  Arrive Stop i+1* transition, compute excess travel time vs. free-flow baseline and rank transitions by **total attributable excess hours** (Pareto analysis). This isolates *where* travel time is lost, not just that it is lost.
3. **Dotted charts and performance spectrum**: plot events over time-of-day to expose recurrent congestion windows, depot loading queues (Route Assigned  Depart Depot lag), and afternoon slowdowns.
4. **Systematic slice-and-dice** — this directly answers "where are the bottlenecks": compute every KPI per **route/territory, time-of-day bucket, driver, vehicle type, geohash, and activity type**. High variance across a dimension flags it as a bottleneck carrier. E.g., if service time varies strongly by *zone* but little by *driver*, the bottleneck is location-driven (parking, building access); if it varies by *driver* on the *same* route, it's behavior-driven.
5. **Spatial clustering** of low-speed/unscheduled-stop events (geohash/DBSCAN)  a traffic hotspot map.
6. **Quantification**: for each bottleneck, attributable hours/week = frequency × average excess. Then run **what-if estimates**: recompute route completion times assuming free-flow on the worst leg or removal of the top hotspot, and translate into OTD points, fuel liters, and overtime hours. *(Illustrative: if 14% of shift time is low-speed travel concentrated in the 08:00–09:30 inner-ring window, shifting departures 45 minutes earlier could recover ~20–30 min per route — to be validated against the actual log.)*

## 3. Root Cause Analysis for Inefficiencies

For each hypothesis, the validation method is as important as the hypothesis itself:

**a) Suboptimal static route planning.**
*Validation:* Compare planned vs. actual km and duration per route; mine **alignment reorder patterns** — if many drivers independently deviate in the *same* way on a route and outperform compliant cases, the plan is structurally wrong. **Variant analysis** contrasting the top-quartile vs. bottom-quartile routes reveals which sequences actually work.

**b) Inaccurate travel time estimation.**
*Validation:* Compute residual = actual  planned leg duration, segmented by time-of-day and zone. A systematic bias curve (e.g., plan assumes 25 km/h but CBD legs average 12 km/h at peak) confirms static estimation as the root cause, and the residuals themselves become training data for better estimates.

**c) Traffic congestion patterns.**
*Validation:* Correlate low-speed event density (location × hour) with late arrivals via regression; separate **recurrent congestion** (predictable  a planning problem) from **incidents** (unpredictable  a real-time response problem). The hotspot map from Section 2 localizes this.

**d) Service-time variability at customers.**
*Validation:* Distribution analysis of dwell times by customer type (apartment vs. house, business vs. residential), zone, and repeat offenders; decompose into parking search (geofence-arrival  first scan) vs. interaction time. Long-tail customers can be identified individually and given wider windows or special handling.

**e) Vehicle breakdowns / maintenance.**
*Validation:* Sequence analysis of events *preceding* unscheduled stops (e.g., "Engine Warning Light" events days before failures); correlate breakdown frequency with mileage-since-last-service, accumulated idle hours, and vehicle age (survival analysis). Measure **case-level impact**: routes with mid-shift breakdowns complete N fewer stops and show cascading lateness — this quantifies the true cost of reactive maintenance.

**f) Driver behavior / skill differences.**
*Validation:* Compare drivers **controlling for route and zone** (same route, different drivers — a natural fixed-effects setup). Compare idle ratios, parking time, speed profiles, deviation propensity, and scan discipline (missing scans also corrupt the data itself). This distinguishes genuine skill gaps from assignment effects.

**g) Failed delivery attempts.**
*Validation:* Failure rate by delivery hour (midday "customer not home" peaks), zone, and repeat-failure customers; package-journey traces quantify the re-attempt cost (extra km next day plus displaced stops). Correlate failure probability with window width and absence of ETA notifications.

**h) Depot/loading delays.**
*Validation:* Analyze Start Shift  Depart Depot duration variance, then test the **cascade hypothesis**: correlation between departure delay and first-window misses. A strong cascade means 15 minutes at the depot costs hours downstream.

**Cross-cutting validation toolkit:** comparative/discriminative process discovery (separate models per cohort, then diff them), decision mining at deviation points (under what conditions do drivers re-sequence?), statistical tests with effect sizes (not just p-values), and predictive models whose feature importances corroborate the suspected causes.

## 4. Data-Driven Optimization Strategies

### Strategy 1 — Dynamic, Learning-Based Routing and Re-Sequencing
- **Targets:** Excess travel time, missed time windows, fuel consumption, overtime.
- **Root cause:** Static routing built on uniform travel-time assumptions (causes b, c).
- **Process mining support:** Residual analysis (b) provides training data for ML travel-time predictions per road segment × time-of-day; hotspot maps (c) identify corridors to avoid at specific hours; beneficial deviations mined in conformance checking reveal driver-validated better sequences.
- **Implementation:** Retrain travel-time models monthly from GPS history; dispatch re-optimization engine; driver app proposing re-sequencing when predicted delay threatens a window (limited to a few changes per shift for acceptance).
- **Expected KPI impact:** OTD , travel-to-service ratio , excess travel time , fuel/km , overtime .

### Strategy 2 — Territory and Route Template Redesign with Staggered Starts
- **Targets:** Route imbalance, chronic congestion exposure, depot loading queues.
- **Root cause:** Territories drawn without accounting for real service times, parking difficulty, and congestion rhythms (causes a, d, h).
- **Process mining support:** Stops-per-hour and dwell-time distributions per zone show true territory "weight"; performance spectra show when each zone is cheapest to serve; Start Shift  Depart Depot lag quantifies the loading bottleneck.
- **Implementation:** Re-cluster territories using historical service-time and congestion priors; redesign route templates with time-window- and service-time-aware sequencing; stagger shift starts and introduce evening pre-loading to eliminate the morning queue and let vans depart before peak congestion.
- **Expected KPI impact:** Stops/route-hour , departure punctuality , overtime , vehicle utilization .

### Strategy 3 — Failed-Delivery Reduction via Predictive Windows and Proactive Communication
- **Targets:** Failed attempts and re-delivery cost (often the single largest hidden cost in last-mile).
- **Root cause:** Customer absence at attempt time; windows misaligned with actual availability; no ETA communication (cause g).
- **Process mining support:** Failure-rate heatmaps by hour × zone; package-journey traces quantifying re-attempt cost; repeat-failure customer identification.
- **Implementation:** Train a no-show risk model (customer history, time-of-day, zone); offer dynamically feasible windows at checkout based on actual route plans; send live ETAs with reschedule options; enable delivery preferences (neighbor, safe place) for high-risk stops; de-prioritize high-no-show-risk stops to times when the customer is historically home.
- **Expected KPI impact:** First-attempt success , re-delivery km , cost per successful delivery , OTD  (fewer displaced stops).

### Strategy 4 — Condition-Based Predictive Maintenance
- **Targets:** Mid-shift breakdowns, unscheduled downtime, maintenance cost.
- **Root cause:** Calendar-based maintenance ignoring actual usage intensity — urban stop-start driving and idling stress vehicles faster than mileage suggests (cause e).
- **Process mining support:** Pre-breakdown event sequences (warning-light events as leading indicators); survival analysis linking breakdowns to mileage-since-service, idle hours, and usage patterns; quantified case-level impact of breakdowns.
- **Implementation:** Condition-based service triggers per vehicle; schedule maintenance strictly off-shift; rotate fleet usage to equalize wear; alert on vehicles crossing risk thresholds.
- **Expected KPI impact:** Unscheduled downtime , route completion rate , breakdowns per 1,000 km , maintenance cost .

### Strategy 5 — Targeted Driver Coaching and Process Standardization
- **Targets:** Service-time variability, excessive idling, harmful deviations, missing scans.
- **Root cause:** Behavior/skill differences isolated from route effects (cause f).
- **Process mining support:** Driver comparisons on identical routes; per-driver DFGs revealing idiosyncratic loops; idle-ratio and parking-time benchmarks; scan-discipline metrics.
- **Implementation:** Coaching programs focused on the specific behaviors where a driver deviates from peer benchmarks (not generic training); standard work instructions for stop execution; positive reinforcement via gamified KPIs.
- **Expected KPI impact:** Service-time variance , idle ratio  (fuel ), data quality , safety .

## 5. Operational Constraints and Continuous Monitoring

### 5.1 Respecting Operational Constraints

- **Driver working hours:** Route optimization must treat shift length and mandatory breaks as *hard constraints*. Conformance rules encode labor compliance (max shift, required breaks as expected activities); the overtime KPI acts as a guardrail so punctuality gains don't come from exploiting drivers.
- **Vehicle capacity and type:** Dispatch assignments and any dynamic re-routing must respect load capacity and vehicle-specific restrictions (e.g., emission-zone-compliant vans for the inner city). Capacity utilization is monitored to spot chronic over/under-sizing.
- **Customer time windows:** Distinguish hard vs. soft windows; the optimizer may violate soft windows at a penalty but never hard ones. Window changes (Strategy 3) require customer consent via the communication channel.
- **Change management:** Dynamic re-sequencing is capped (e.g., 3 changes/shift) to avoid driver fatigue and resistance; driver-facing KPIs are framed for coaching, not surveillance, with worker representation involved early.

### 5.2 Continuous Monitoring Plan

**Pipeline:** Daily (ideally near-real-time) incremental event-log ingestion with automated data-quality checks (missing-scan rate, GPS gaps, clock drift) — bad data must fail loudly, not silently.

**Dashboard layers:**
1. **KPI tiles:** OTD, first-attempt success, stops/route-hour, travel-to-service ratio, idle ratio, unscheduled stops, overtime rate — vs. baseline and target.
2. **Live process map:** Discovered model refreshed weekly with performance overlays; **conformance view** showing deviation rate and top deviation types per route/driver.
3. **Bottleneck radar:** Top excess-time transitions and hotspots this week vs. rolling baseline (catches new congestion patterns from roadworks, seasonality).
4. **Fleet health:** Breakdown risk scores from the predictive maintenance model; upcoming condition-based service triggers.
5. **Customer view:** Today's high no-show-risk stops and active ETA exception list.

**Proactive alerting (predictive process monitoring):**
- Mid-route models predict, per active case, the probability of missing remaining windows  dispatcher alert *before* the failure, enabling intervention (re-sequencing, customer notification, stop reassignment).
- Deviation-rate thresholds trigger review (e.g., unscheduled stops > X% of cases).

**Sustainability and evaluation:**
- **Stepped-wedge or A/B rollout** of each strategy with matched control routes; compare pre/post KPIs with statistical rigor — process mining provides the counterfactual via matched historical cases.
- **Concept drift detection:** monthly re-discovery of process models; alert when variant structure or bottleneck ranking shifts materially (new behavior, gaming, or external change).
- **Balanced scorecard guardrails:** no single KPI is optimized in isolation (e.g., OTD gains must not coincide with rising overtime or idle ratio).
- **Governance loop:** monthly review where planners, fleet managers, and driver representatives examine dashboard insights and feed confirmed beneficial deviations back into route templates — closing the loop between discovery, action, and re-discovery.

**Summary:** The engagement follows the classic process mining cycle — integrate and abstract the multi-source log, discover the *actual* process, check conformance against the *planned* process, quantify bottlenecks and validate root causes, then intervene with five mutually reinforcing strategies (dynamic routing, territory redesign, failed-delivery reduction, predictive maintenance, driver coaching) — all sustained by a monitoring architecture that treats the event log as a living asset rather than a one-time analysis.
