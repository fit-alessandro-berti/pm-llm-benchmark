A typical controlled P2P sequence is:

**Create requisition  Approve requisition  Issue purchase order  Receive goods and receive invoice  Perform two-/three-way match  Release payment  Close case**

The invoice may arrive before or after the goods, but payment normally should not occur until the invoice has been validated against the PO and, where applicable, the goods receipt.

### Case-level findings

| Case | Assessment | Anomalies and risks |
|---|---|---|
| **1001** | Normal | The requisition is approved before the PO is issued. Goods and invoice are recorded before matching, and payment is released only after the match. This follows the expected control sequence. |
| **1002** | Highly anomalous | **PO issued before requisition approval.** This bypasses the authorization control and could represent unauthorized or “maverick” purchasing. **Payment released before requisition approval, goods receipt, and invoice matching.** The organization paid without confirming authorization, delivery, quantity, price, or invoice validity. The match occurred two days after payment, making it a retrospective control rather than a preventive one. |
| **1003** | Highly anomalous | **Payment released before the vendor invoice was received**, and also before goods receipt and matching. There is no recorded invoice to support the payment at the time it was made. This creates risks of erroneous, fraudulent, duplicate, or unrecoverable payment. It might be legitimate only if this were an explicitly authorized advance/prepayment process, but no such activity or approval appears in the log. |
| **1004** | Anomalous | **Goods were received before the purchase order was issued.** This suggests a retrospective PO or that goods were ordered outside the formal procurement process. Even though the requisition had been approved, there was no recorded PO governing price, quantity, delivery terms, or supplier commitment at receipt. The subsequent invoice match and payment are otherwise in the expected order. |

### Main control concerns

1. **Authorization bypass:** Case 1002 commits the organization before requisition approval.
2. **Payment before validation:** Cases 1002 and 1003 release funds before matching the PO, receipt, and invoice.
3. **Retrospective purchasing documentation:** Case 1004 records the PO only after goods have arrived.
4. **Ineffective after-the-fact controls:** A match performed after payment, as in Cases 1002 and 1003, may detect an error but cannot prevent the improper payment.

Thus, **1001 appears conforming**, while **1002 and 1003 contain severe payment-control violations**, and **1004 shows a purchasing-sequence/control violation**.
