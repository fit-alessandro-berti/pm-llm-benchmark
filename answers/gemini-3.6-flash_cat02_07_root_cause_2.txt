### 2. Root Cause Analysis

Analysis of the log attributes reveals three primary root causes contributing to the extended lead times:

#### Root Cause 1: Complexity-Driven Rework Loops ("Ping-Ponging" for Documents)
There is a direct correlation between **Complexity**, the **Number of "Request Additional Documents" activities**, and total duration.
* **Low Complexity Cases (2001, 2004):** Require **0** document requests; completed in ** under 1.5 hours**.
* **Medium Complexity Case (2002):** Requires **1** document request; extends duration to **~26 hours**.
* **High Complexity Cases (2003, 2005):** Require **2 to 3** document requests; extends duration to **48–77 hours**.

#### Root Cause 2: Piecemeal Document Gathering by Adjusters
In **Case 2005**, `Adjuster_Lisa` makes **three separate requests for additional documents** on three consecutive days:
1. *Apr 1, 11:30* – 1st Request
2. *Apr 2, 17:00* – 2nd Request (29.5 hours later)
3. *Apr 3, 15:00* – 3rd Request (22 hours later)

This indicates that claims handlers are requesting information piecemeal (one item at a time) rather than performing a comprehensive initial audit and requesting all necessary documents in a single interaction.

#### Root Cause 3: Management Approval Bottlenecks (Resource-Specific Delay)
* **Manager_Ann** handles Low and Medium complexity cases and approves them almost instantly (within 15–45 minutes of the prior step).
* **Manager_Bill** handles High-complexity cases and creates significant queue times:
  * **Case 2003:** Received documents/last activity on *Apr 1 at 17:00*, but `Manager_Bill` did not approve until *Apr 2 at 16:00* (**23-hour delay**).
  * **Case 2005:** Last document received on *Apr 3 at 15:00*, but `Manager_Bill` approved on *Apr 4 at 10:00* (**19-hour delay**).

#### Regional Differences:
* **Region A vs. Region B:** Region itself is not a direct primary bottleneck (both regions handle Low complexity cases in ~1.5 hours). However, Region B hosted the worst-performing case (2005) due to the combination of high complexity, multiple document requests by `Adjuster_Lisa`, and queueing for `Manager_Bill`.

### 4. Proposed Mitigation Strategies

To reduce case durations and remove bottlenecks, the following interventions are recommended:

1. **Implement Dynamic Upfront Intake Checklists (Reduce Rework Loops):**
   * *Problem:* High-complexity claims lack clear documentation requirements at submission.
   * *Solution:* Update the intake portal/CSR script to automatically require specific supporting documents based on claim complexity at the time of submission (e.g., dynamic forms for High Complexity claims).

2. **Enforce Single-Touch Document Requests (Adjuster Efficiency):**
   * *Problem:* Adjusters ask for missing files one by one across multiple days.
   * *Solution:* Implement a policy requiring adjusters to perform a complete review before contacting the customer. Standardize an "Information Request Checklist" so all missing items are requested in a single batch.

3. **Establish Management Approval SLAs (Resource Capacity Optimization):**
   * *Problem:* `Manager_Bill` creates a ~20-hour bottleneck on high-value/high-complexity approvals.
   * *Solution:* Implement Service Level Agreements (SLAs) for approvals (e.g., mandatory review within 4 hours). If a high-complexity manager's queue exceeds a threshold, automatically reassign or delegate approval authority to available managers (e.g., `Manager_Ann`).
