# Process Mining Analysis: Inferring Activities from Numeric Sensor Data

## 1. Approach

Since no activity labels exist, I treated the log as a multivariate time series and looked for **contiguous segments of similar sensor "signatures."** Three signals turned out to be the most informative:

| Signal | Interpretive role |
|---|---|
| **Tool Position** | Acts as a *stage counter*. It increases monotonically (0  5  7  10  15  20) and then resets to 0. Resets are strong **case boundaries**; plateaus indicate a stationary operation. |
| **Energy (kWh)** | Proxy for *process intensity*. Baseline  0.4–0.5; work phases 1.5–5.0. The single 5.0 peak marks the most energy-dense operation. |
| **Temperature + Vibration** | Distinguishes *mechanical* work (high vibration, moderate heat) from *thermal* work (very high heat, high vibration but **zero material flow**). |
| **Material Flow** | Distinguishes material-consuming steps (cutting, feeding, conveying) from non-consuming steps (welding, inspection, idle). |
| **Pressure** | Rises to 2 bar exactly where vibration drops — signature of **clamping/holding** rather than machining. |

Segmentation rule used: start a new phase whenever 2 signals change by more than ~50% of their prior value, or whenever Tool Position resets.

## 3. Rationale for Each Label

**A — Idle / Standby (Events 1–3).**
Every actuator signal is at its floor: no vibration, no flow, tool retracted at origin, pressure at 1 bar, and energy at the lowest observed value (0.40–0.50 kWh — the "keep-the-lights-on" load). Temperature equals room temperature. Nothing is being transformed; this is the machine powered but waiting, i.e., setup/standby.

**B — Cutting Metal (Events 4–6).**
This is the classic *material-removal* signature:
- **Vibration jumps from 0 to 30–38 Hz** — chatter from a blade/saw/mill engaging metal.
- **Temperature climbs steeply (21  50  55  58 °C)** — friction heat accumulating in the workpiece and tool.
- **Material flow is at its maximum (5 units/s)** — stock is being fed continuously.
- **Tool position advances incrementally (5  7  10 mm)** — a cutting tool progressing into the material (depth of cut).
- **Pressure stays low (1  1.5 bar)** — no clamping force needed yet; the load is mechanical, not pneumatic.
- Energy ramps 2.0  3.0, consistent with increasing engagement.

**C — Assembling Parts (Events 7–8).**
The transition at Event 7 is the key evidence:
- **Vibration drops sharply (38  10 Hz)** — no more cutting; only gentle positioning/pressing motion.
- **Pressure rises to 2 bar** — a pneumatic clamp/press is now holding or joining components.
- **Temperature falls (58  40 °C)** — residual heat dissipating; no new heat source.
- **Tool position freezes at 15 mm across two consecutive events** — the tool is *stationary at a fixture point*, characteristic of holding parts together rather than machining them.
- **Flow reduced to 2** — discrete components being fed, not continuous stock.
- The two events are **identical in every field**, indicating a steady, low-dynamics holding operation.

**D — Welding (Event 9).**
The most distinctive single event in the log:
- **Temperature spikes to 80 °C — the global maximum** — an intense thermal input.
- **Energy = 5.0 kWh — the global maximum**, ~10× idle. Only fusion processes (arc/spot/laser welding) draw this much for such a short duration.
- **Material flow = 0** — nothing is being fed or removed; the machine is joining what is already in place. This is what rules out "cutting" despite the high vibration.
- **Vibration 50 Hz** is consistent with arc instability / electrode or transformer buzz, not tool travel, since **tool position is unchanged at 15 mm** and **pressure remains at 2 bar** (parts still clamped from assembly).
- Logical position in the sequence: assemble  then fuse the assembled joint.

**E — Cooling & Quality Inspection (Event 10).**
- **Temperature collapses 80  30 °C in one interval** — active or forced cooling of the weld.
- **Vibration  1 Hz and flow = 0** — the machine is essentially motionless; nothing is being processed mechanically.
- **Pressure released back to 1 bar** — clamps opened, part free.
- **Tool still at 15 mm**, i.e., the part remains in the station — it hasn't moved on yet.
- **Energy = 1.0 kWh**: above idle (0.5) but far below any work phase. This "low-but-not-idle" draw is the fingerprint of **sensing/measurement equipment** (cameras, probes, gauges) running while actuators are off.
- *Note:* this event is dual-purpose — cool-down and inspection overlap. If you want a stricter model, label it "Post-Weld Cooling / Inspection" as a single composite activity, since only one sample exists.

**F — Packaging / Discharge (Events 11–12).**
- **Tool position advances to 20 mm — the furthest point in the cycle**, i.e., the workpiece has been pushed past all processing stations toward the outfeed.
- **Low temperature (25 °C)** — the part is cool; no thermal or cutting work is occurring.
- **Vibration 5 Hz and flow = 1** — gentle, continuous motion typical of a conveyor, wrapper, or pick-and-place handler moving finished goods.
- **Energy 1.5–1.6 kWh** — modest, sustained motor load, higher than inspection (motion) but far below welding/cutting (no material transformation).
- Two consecutive near-identical events = a steady handling operation, not a transient.

**G — Idle / Reset (Events 13–15).**
All values return exactly to the Phase A baseline, and critically **tool position resets to 0**. This retraction-to-home is the classic **end-of-case marker**: the machine has released the finished product and is ready for the next workpiece. In process-mining terms, this closes the trace.

## 5. Reusable Classification Heuristics

These rules can be applied to future raw logs from the same line:

| Rule (in priority order) | Inferred activity |
|---|---|
| Energy  0.6 AND Vib = 0 AND Flow = 0 AND Tool = 0 | **Idle / Reset** |
| Temp > 70 AND Energy > 4 AND Flow = 0 | **Welding** |
| Vib  25 AND Flow  4 AND Tool increasing AND Temp rising | **Cutting** |
| Pressure  2 AND Vib  15 AND Tool constant AND Flow 1–3 | **Assembling** |
| Vib  2 AND Flow = 0 AND 0.6 < Energy  1.2 AND large negative Temp | **Cooling / Inspection** |
| Temp  30 AND Vib 3–8 AND Flow  1 AND Tool at max | **Packaging / Discharge** |

## 6. Caveats and Confidence

| Label | Confidence | Why / What would confirm it |
|---|---|---|
| Idle, Cutting, Welding | **High** | Extreme, unambiguous signatures (baseline floor; max flow+vibration+advancing tool; max temp+energy with zero flow). |
| Packaging | **Medium-High** | Position at end of cycle + max tool travel + low gentle load. Confirmed by an outfeed part-counter or barcode-scan event. |
| Assembling | **Medium** | Pressure rise + tool hold is suggestive of clamping/pressing, but could also be "part fixturing/pre-weld clamping" — a preparatory step rather than true assembly. |
| Cooling & Quality Inspection | **Low-Medium** | Only **one** event; inspection is inferred from *absence* of mechanical activity plus above-idle energy. It could equally be pure cool-down (with inspection done off-line), or two merged activities. Adding a vision-system status flag or a pass/fail field would resolve this. |

**Other limitations:** only one machine and one case are observed, so the model cannot reveal variants, rework loops, or concurrency. The 5-second sampling interval may hide short sub-steps (e.g., tool change between cutting and assembling). To strengthen the model, I would (a) cluster many cycles with k-means/HMM on the six sensors plus their first differences, (b) use tool-position resets as automatic case IDs, and (c) validate the six inferred labels against a handful of operator-annotated cycles.
