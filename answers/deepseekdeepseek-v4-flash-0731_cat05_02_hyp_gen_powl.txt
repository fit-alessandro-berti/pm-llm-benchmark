## 1. Anomalies in the POWL model

The intended process is:

`R  A  E  P  N  C`

The POWL model allows several departures from this:

1. **Loop between Evaluate and Approve**  
   The `LOOP(E, P)` structure means `E` and `P` can repeat as a cycle, e.g. `E-P-E-P-E`.  
   This violates the intended single evaluation and single approval. It also allows an evaluation to occur after an approval, or an approval to occur multiple times.

2. **Customer notification can be skipped**  
   The XOR node `XOR(N, skip)` permits the silent transition instead of `Notify Customer`.  
   Since `skip` is a silent transition, it leaves no event in the database. Therefore a claim can go through the process without any `N` activity.

3. **Close Claim can happen prematurely**  
   The partial order has:
   - `R -> A`
   - `A -> loop`
   - `loop -> xor`
   - `A -> C`

   There is **no edge** from `xor` to `C`, and there is a direct edge `A -> C`.  
   Because a partial order only enforces the listed edges, `C` is not required to happen after `loop` or after `xor`.  
   As a result, a trace like:

   `R  A  C`

   is allowed by the model, so the claim can be closed before evaluation, approval, or notification.

## 2. Hypotheses for why these anomalies exist

### H1: Partially implemented business rule change

The process may have been changed from an older iterative review process to a simpler one, but the model was only partly updated. The old model may have allowed repeated evaluations/approvals, and a modeler forgot to replace the loop with a strict sequence.

### H2: Miscommunication between departments

The claims department may believe customer notification is optional or handled outside the main workflow. Similarly, closing a claim immediately after assignment may be a real shortcut used for simple claims, but it was not communicated as an official business rule.

### H3: Workflow modeling error

The direct edge `A -> C` and missing `xor -> C` are likely modeling mistakes. The modeler may have accidentally connected the wrong nodes, or forgot to add the final ordering constraint that `C` must occur after `N`.

### H4: Weak model validation in the tool

The POWL modeler may not have checked that only safe partial orders are allowed. The tool may permit incomplete process models where the final activity is not constrained to be last, and mandatory activities are not enforced.

```sql
WHERE f.no_eval
   OR f.no_approval
   OR f.no_notify
   OR f.approval_count > 1
GROUP BY
    COALESCE(a.name, f.resource),
    a.specialization,
    a.region
ORDER BY anomalous_claims DESC;
```

These queries can confirm whether the anomalies are only theoretical model flaws or whether they are actually occurring in the insurance claim process. Even if the database has no examples, the POWL model is still unsafe because it permits these behaviors.
