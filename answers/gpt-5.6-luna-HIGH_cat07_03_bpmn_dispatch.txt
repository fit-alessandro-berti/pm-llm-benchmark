The visualization is a **BPMN-style swimlane process diagram** describing how a hardware shop dispatches goods.

### Participants/swimlanes
- **Logistics**: handles parcel insurance.
- **Secretary / Computer Hardware Shop**: clarifies shipment details, arranges logistics, and prepares labels.
- **Warehouse**: packages the goods and handles shipment preparation.

### Process flow
1. The process starts with **“Ship goods.”**
2. A parallel gateway splits the work into two concurrent branches:
   - The warehouse begins **“Package goods.”**
   - The secretary **“Clarif[ies] shipment method.”**
3. The process checks whether **special handling** is required:
   - **No**: the secretary checks whether insurance is required, using stored **customer information**.
     - If insurance is necessary, the parcel is sent to Logistics to **“Insure parcel.”**
     - Regardless of insurance, the secretary proceeds to **“Write package label.”**
   - **Yes**: the secretary obtains **three offers from logistic companies**, then **selects a logistics company and places the order.**
4. The logistics-related branches converge.
5. In parallel, the warehouse packages the goods. A **48-hour timer** is attached to this task:
   - If packaging is delayed for 48 hours, the warehouse **notifies the customer about the delay**.
6. The packaging/delay branch and the logistics branch synchronize at a parallel gateway.
7. The warehouse then **fills out shipment paperwork**, **moves the package to the pickup area**, and the process ends at **“Shipment prepared.”**

Overall, the diagram emphasizes parallel work between the secretary/logistics functions and the warehouse, with an optional insurance step and a delay notification if packaging takes more than 48 hours.
