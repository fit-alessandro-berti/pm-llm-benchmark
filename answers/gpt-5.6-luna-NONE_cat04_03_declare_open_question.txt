The DECLARE model describes a highly controlled loan-origination process. It combines mandatory activities, prohibited behavior, ordering rules, and response requirements to ensure that applications are handled consistently, audibly, and in accordance with lending and compliance obligations.

## 1. Core activities and lifecycle

A realistic process would generally begin with:

1. **Receive_Application (A)**  
   This represents formally accepting an application into the bank’s or lender’s workflow. It may include identity information, requested loan amount, purpose of borrowing, consent for credit checks, and initial documentation.

2. **Preliminary_Credit_Check (B)**  
   This is an initial assessment of creditworthiness, possibly involving a credit-bureau inquiry, affordability screening, debt-to-income analysis, fraud checks, and basic eligibility rules.

3. **Gather_Additional_Documents (C)**  
   If the preliminary assessment indicates that more evidence is needed, the lender requests items such as payslips, tax returns, bank statements, proof of assets, or business financial statements.

4. **Assemble_Loan_Offer_Package (D)**  
   The lender prepares the proposed loan terms, including principal, interest rate, fees, repayment schedule, collateral requirements, and applicable conditions.

5. **Quality_Assurance_Review (E)**  
   A separate review checks whether the application is complete, calculations are correct, required disclosures are present, and internal policies and regulations have been followed.

6. **Authorize_Contract_Terms (F)**  
   This is the formal approval or finalization of the contract terms by an authorized person or system. It represents a decision point beyond merely preparing an offer.

7. **Transfer_Funds (G)**  
   Once all conditions are met, the lender disburses the funds. This may involve payment to the borrower, a seller, a supplier, or another designated account.

8. **Notify_Customer (H)**  
   The customer is informed of the decision, approved terms, required next steps, or, where relevant, a decline and the applicable explanation.

9. **Proceed_Without_Compliance (Z)**  
   This represents prohibited conduct: continuing with a transaction while ignoring legal, procedural, or compliance requirements. Its explicit prohibition is a strong control against bypassing mandatory checks.

## 2. Mandatory and uniqueness constraints

The **existence** rules require that `Receive_Application` and `Gather_Additional_Documents` occur. In practical terms, the lender must have a recorded application and must collect supplementary evidence where the process requires it. This supports complete case files and reduces the risk of making decisions on insufficient information.

The **absence** rule for `Proceed_Without_Compliance` prohibits any deliberate bypass of compliance controls. This could cover ignoring sanctions screening, accepting incomplete documentation, failing to verify identity, or approving a loan outside delegated authority.

The **exactly_one** rule for `Preliminary_Credit_Check` is particularly important. It requires one—and only one—preliminary credit check. Businesswise, this prevents:

- progressing without an initial risk assessment;
- duplicating checks unnecessarily;
- creating inconsistent results across multiple preliminary assessments;
- potentially causing repeated credit-bureau inquiries that could affect the applicant.

In a real implementation, “exactly once” would need to be interpreted carefully. A lender may perform later underwriting or verification checks, but the model distinguishes those from the single formal preliminary check.

The **init** rule requires `Receive_Application` to be the first modeled activity. This establishes a clear case start and prevents downstream actions from occurring against an unregistered or unidentified application.

## 3. Response and coexistence rules

The **response** rule requires `Receive_Application` to be followed at some point by `Preliminary_Credit_Check`. This ensures that every received application enters the risk-assessment path rather than remaining unprocessed.

The **coexistence** rule requires `Gather_Additional_Documents` and `Authorize_Contract_Terms` to occur in the same case. This suggests that additional evidence is not merely collected for recordkeeping; it must be considered before final contract authorization. A lender should not request documents and then approve terms without using or validating them.

The **responded_existence** rule connects `Assemble_Loan_Offer_Package` to `Quality_Assurance_Review`: once an offer package is prepared, it must receive a QA review. This creates a maker-checker control, where the person or system preparing the offer is not the sole authority validating it.

## 4. Ordering and succession rules

The **precedence** constraint requires `Quality_Assurance_Review` to occur before `Authorize_Contract_Terms`. This prevents final authorization before the file and proposed terms have been checked.

The **succession** rule requires `Preliminary_Credit_Check` to be followed by `Gather_Additional_Documents`. This expresses a practical workflow in which the preliminary assessment identifies missing or necessary evidence, which is then collected.

The **altsuccession** rule also connects `Gather_Additional_Documents` to `Quality_Assurance_Review`. Depending on the precise DECLARE semantics, this can require an appropriate follow-up review after document gathering, or permit one of the modeled follow-up paths. Operationally, it emphasizes that newly received documents must be incorporated into a review rather than left unexamined.

These rules together create a broad sequence:

**Application received  preliminary assessment  additional documentation  offer preparation and review  authorization  communication and disbursement.**

## 5. Longer-range chain constraints

The chain rules impose relationships across multiple intermediate events.

- **chainresponse:** `Assemble_Loan_Offer_Package` must eventually lead to `Transfer_Funds`. Preparing an offer is therefore connected to completion of the lending lifecycle, rather than being an isolated activity.
- **chainprecedence:** `Authorize_Contract_Terms` must be preceded by `Preliminary_Credit_Check`, possibly with other activities in between. This ensures that authorization is ultimately grounded in an initial credit assessment.
- **chainsuccession:** `Quality_Assurance_Review` must be followed by `Assemble_Loan_Offer_Package`. This may reflect a process in which QA validates the material needed to support or complete the offer package.

These constraints provide traceability. A case cannot simply contain the right activities in an arbitrary order; it must demonstrate a meaningful relationship between risk assessment, documentation, offer preparation, review, and authorization.

## 6. Controls involving disbursement and notification

The **altresponse** rule links `Transfer_Funds` to `Notify_Customer`. In practical terms, a disbursement should be accompanied by, or lead to, customer communication. The customer should know that funds have been released, under what terms, and what repayment obligations apply.

The **altprecedence** rule connects `Notify_Customer` and `Transfer_Funds` in the opposite direction. Depending on the formal interpretation of the alternative-precedence template, this can require notification to be appropriately preceded by a related disbursement path, or establish an alternative ordering relationship. Operationally, the pair of rules signals that notification and disbursement must be coordinated and not treated as unrelated events.

The **noncoexistence** rule prevents `Transfer_Funds` and `Receive_Application` from occurring together in the same modeled case. In a loan process this should be interpreted carefully. It likely intends to prevent funds from being disbursed merely because an application was received, without the necessary intervening controls. The user’s example—preventing disbursement before the application is even received—is more naturally enforced by an explicit precedence rule requiring `Receive_Application` before `Transfer_Funds`. As written, noncoexistence means the two activities cannot both occur in the same trace, which would be unusually strict for a normal loan process and may indicate that the model or constraint label needs refinement.

The **nonsuccession** rule prevents `Notify_Customer` from being directly followed by `Preliminary_Credit_Check`. This avoids an implausible sequence in which the lender communicates a final or intermediate decision and only afterward performs the initial credit assessment.

The **nonchainsuccession** rule prevents `Authorize_Contract_Terms` from eventually being followed by `Notify_Customer`, depending on the exact template semantics. In ordinary lending, authorization would normally be followed by some form of customer notification, so this rule may be intentionally prohibitive—for example, if notification is handled before authorization—or it may be a modeling inconsistency. It should be validated with process owners because it appears potentially at odds with the stated real-life meanings.

## 7. Compliance and quality objectives

The overall model supports several likely business and regulatory goals:

- **Know-your-customer and anti-money-laundering compliance:** identity verification, sanctions screening, fraud detection, and documented decision-making.
- **Responsible lending:** assessing affordability and creditworthiness before approval.
- **Fair lending and consumer protection:** applying consistent eligibility criteria and preserving evidence for audit or complaint handling.
- **Data quality:** requiring supporting documents and independent review.
- **Segregation of duties:** separating preparation, review, authorization, and disbursement.
- **Operational risk reduction:** preventing premature payments, missing approvals, or unauthorized contract terms.
- **Auditability:** ensuring that each material decision has a traceable predecessor and follow-up.
- **Customer transparency:** ensuring that decisions, terms, and fund transfers are communicated.
- **Fraud prevention:** preventing applications from bypassing identity, documentation, or approval controls.

The support and confidence values of `1.0` indicate that these constraints are presented as fully reliable and universally applicable within the modeled process. In a real process-mining setting, however, such values would normally be based on observed event logs and should be reviewed against exceptions, manual work, rejected applications, withdrawn applications, and legitimate alternative paths.

## 8. Overall interpretation

The model aims to make the loan lifecycle deterministic and controlled:

**register the application, perform one preliminary credit assessment, obtain necessary evidence, prepare and review the offer, authorize the terms, communicate appropriately, and only then complete the financial transaction.**

Its strictness reflects the fact that loan decisions affect both the lender and the customer, involve regulated financial activity, and can create significant legal, financial, and reputational risk. Some rules—especially those governing disbursement, notification, and post-authorization behavior—should be checked for semantic consistency with the intended business process. DECLARE constraints are precise, and a rule that sounds like “prevent disbursement before receipt” may not be equivalent to a noncoexistence rule. Validating those interpretations with compliance officers and process owners would be essential before implementing the model as an automated control.
