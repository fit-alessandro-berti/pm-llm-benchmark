### 3. Impact on Cycle Times and Recommendations

#### How These Factors Increase Cycle Times:
1.  **Hand-off Latency:** Every time a ticket moves between roles (Triage $\rightarrow$ Assignment $\rightarrow$ Escalation), momentum is lost. If the receiving queue is unmonitored or overloaded, tickets sit idle, directly inflating the total cycle time.
2.  **Lack of Real-time Work-in-Progress (WIP) Limits:** Agents appear to hold tickets or leave them unworked for hours (e.g., Case 104's 3.5-hour wait before investigation), suggesting poor task-switching or high workloads.
3.  **Escalation Black Holes:** Escalations to Level-2 represent the highest delays (Case 105 taking 2 days). Without strict tracking, escalated tickets fall to the bottom of the queue.

#### Recommendations for Process Improvement:
1.  **Establish SLAs for Escalations & Hand-offs:** Set maximum time thresholds for how long a ticket can sit unassigned after triage, or uninvestigated after escalation to Level-2.
2.  **Review Level-2 Staffing and Workload:** Since escalated tickets disproportionately drive up resolution times, assess whether Level-2 is understaffed or if routing rules need optimization to catch complex issues faster.
3.  **Implement Automated Queue Alerts:** Configure the ticketing system to trigger alerts if a ticket remains in any state (such as *Assign to Level-1* or *Escalate to Level-2*) for longer than 30–60 minutes without active work.
4.  **Evaluate 24/7 or Extended Coverage:** If overnight pauses are standard operating procedure, set proactive expectations with customers regarding resolution windows that span multiple days, or consider staggered shifts to prevent overnight stagnation.
