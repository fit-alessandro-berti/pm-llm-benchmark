## 1. Cases with significantly longer resolution times

| Case | Received | Closed | Total Cycle Time | Assessment |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 101 | Mar 1, 08:00 | Mar 1, 10:15 | **2 h 15 m** | Normal |
| 102 | Mar 1, 08:05 | Mar 2, 09:15 | **25 h 10 m** | Delayed |
| 103 | Mar 1, 08:10 | Mar 1, 09:30 | **1 h 20 m** | Fastest / normal |
| 104 | Mar 1, 08:20 | Mar 2, 08:30 | **24 h 10 m** | Delayed |
| 105 | Mar 1, 08:25 | Mar 3, 09:30 | **49 h 05 m** | Most delayed |

Cases **102, 104, and 105** are clear outliers compared with the normal cases, 101 and 103, which close within approximately 1–2 hours.

- Case 102 is about 11–19 times slower than the normal cases.
- Case 104 is similarly delayed despite having no escalation.
- Case 105 is the largest outlier, taking just over two days.

## 2. Potential root causes

### A. Escalations are associated with substantial delay

Cases **102** and **105** both involve escalation to a Level-2 agent.

#### Case 102
Key waiting periods:

| Transition | Delay |
|---|---:|
| Assign to Level-1  Escalate to Level-2 | 2 h 30 m |
| Escalate to Level-2  Investigate | 2 h 30 m |
| Investigate  Resolve | **19 h** |

The escalation introduces handoffs and queue time. The largest delay occurs after Level-2 investigation, where resolution takes 19 hours.

#### Case 105
Key waiting periods:

| Transition | Delay |
|---|---:|
| Initial investigation  Escalation | 50 m |
| Escalate to Level-2  Further investigation | **28 h** |
| Further investigation  Resolve | **19 h** |

Case 105 indicates a severe Level-2 bottleneck. The ticket waits 28 hours after escalation before Level-2 investigation begins, then waits another 19 hours to be resolved.

This may indicate:

- Insufficient Level-2 staffing or capacity.
- A queue backlog for escalated tickets.
- Escalations being assigned without immediate ownership.
- Missing escalation prioritization or service-level targets.
- Complex tickets requiring information or action from another team.

---

### B. Long delays between investigation and resolution

A notable repeated pattern appears in cases **102, 104, and 105**:

| Case | Investigate  Resolve Delay |
|---|---:|
| 102 | **19 h** |
| 104 | **19 h** |
| 105 | **19 h** |

The same 19-hour gap suggests a systematic process issue rather than purely random ticket complexity. Possible explanations include:

- Work is paused overnight and resumes the next business day.
- Investigations are completed but resolutions are not recorded promptly.
- Tickets wait in a “ready for resolution” queue.
- Agents require approval, verification, customer confirmation, or a specialist response before resolving.
- There is no ownership or SLA enforcement after investigation has begun.

Because this pattern also affects case 104, which was not escalated, escalation alone cannot explain all long cycle times.

---

### C. Delay before investigation begins

Case **104** has a substantial waiting period before work starts:

| Transition | Delay |
|---|---:|
| Receive  Triage | 40 m |
| Triage  Assignment | 30 m |
| Assign  Investigate | **3 h 30 m** |

The 3.5-hour delay from assignment to investigation suggests that assignment does not necessarily result in immediate work. Potential causes include:

- Level-1 agent workload or queueing.
- Assignment to an unavailable agent.
- Tickets being assigned but not actively monitored.
- Lack of a response-time target between assignment and first investigation.
- Lower priority classification during triage.

By contrast, the fast cases start investigation shortly after assignment:

- Case 101: 40 minutes after assignment.
- Case 103: 15 minutes after assignment.
- Case 105: 10 minutes after assignment.

---

### D. Closure itself is not the bottleneck

The final **Resolve  Close** step takes only 15–30 minutes in every case. Therefore, ticket closure is operating reasonably efficiently and is not responsible for the large overall delays.

The delays occur primarily:

1. Before investigation starts.
2. During escalation handoff and Level-2 queueing.
3. Between investigation and resolution.

---

## 3. Insights and recommendations

### Primary bottlenecks

1. **Level-2 escalation queue / handoff delays**
   - Especially visible in Case 105, with a 28-hour wait before post-escalation investigation.
   - Escalation appears to add substantial idle time and may lack clear ownership.

2. **Investigation-to-resolution delay**
   - The repeated 19-hour delay across Cases 102, 104, and 105 is the strongest evidence of a systematic bottleneck.
   - This may be an overnight operating-hours effect, an approval queue, a customer-response dependency, or delayed update discipline.

3. **Assigned-but-not-started work**
   - Case 104 shows that assigning a ticket does not guarantee prompt investigation.
   - The assignment-to-investigation stage should be monitored as a distinct service target.

### Recommended actions

- **Track stage-level SLAs**, not just total ticket resolution time:
  - Receive to triage.
  - Triage to assignment.
  - Assignment to first investigation.
  - Escalation to specialist acceptance/investigation.
  - Investigation to resolution.

- **Introduce explicit ownership for escalations.**
  - A Level-2 team member should acknowledge or accept an escalated ticket promptly.
  - Escalated tickets should have queue-aging alerts and priority rules.

- **Analyze the 19-hour investigation-to-resolution pattern.**
  - Determine whether it reflects overnight pauses, approval requirements, waiting for customer responses, or internal resolution queues.
  - If tickets are waiting for customers, record a separate “Await Customer” status so that waiting time is distinguishable from internal delay.

- **Reduce the assignment-to-investigation delay.**
  - Implement workload balancing, agent availability checks, automated reassignment, or reminders for tickets not touched shortly after assignment.
  - Escalate unattended assigned tickets after a defined threshold.

- **Improve Level-2 capacity or triage quality.**
  - If Level-2 demand exceeds capacity, add staffing, rotate on-call specialists, or prioritize aged escalations.
  - If many tickets are escalated unnecessarily, improve Level-1 knowledge bases, training, and diagnostic procedures.

Overall, the data indicates that the primary performance problem is not ticket closure, but rather **queueing and waiting after assignment, after escalation, and after investigation**. Case 105 is the clearest example of this problem, while Case 104 confirms that long resolution times can occur even without escalation.
