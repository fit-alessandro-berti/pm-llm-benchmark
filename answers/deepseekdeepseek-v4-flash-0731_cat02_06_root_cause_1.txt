## 2. Potential root causes of the delays

### A. Long waits before investigation
- **Case 104** was assigned to a Level-1 agent at 09:30, but investigation did not start until 13:00 — a **3h 30m idle gap**.
- **Case 102** was assigned at 09:00, escalated at 11:30, and only investigated at 14:00. So there was **5 hours from assignment to actual investigation**.

Even without an escalation, tickets can be delayed simply because investigation is not started promptly.

### B. Escalation bottlenecks
- **Case 105** was escalated to Level-2 at 10:00, but the next investigation did not happen until **the next day at 14:00** — a **28-hour wait**.
- **Case 102** also lost time in the escalation queue: escalation to Level-2 at 11:30, but investigation only started at 14:00.

This suggests the Level-2/escalation team is either understaffed, not prioritized, or lacks a clear handoff process.

### C. Overnight gap between investigation and resolution
In all three slow cases, the ticket was investigated in the early afternoon and then not resolved until the next morning:

- **Case 102**: Investigated at 14:00  resolved at 09:00 next day = **19h wait**
- **Case 104**: Investigated at 13:00  resolved at 08:00 next day = **19h wait**
- **Case 105**: Investigated at 14:00  resolved at 09:00 next day = **19h wait**

This is a major pattern: tickets are investigated, but then they sit overnight instead of being resolved or explicitly handed over.

### D. Escalation is not the only cause
Case 104 had **no escalation**, yet it was still slow. This means the main issue is not simply “escalations are bad.” The broader problem is **long inactive waiting time between process steps**, especially around handoffs and end-of-day boundaries.

## 3. How these factors increase cycle time and recommendations

### How delays increase cycle time
Every handoff creates a potential waiting queue. When a ticket is:

- assigned but not investigated promptly,
- escalated but not picked up by Level-2,
- investigated but not resolved before the end of the day,

then the total cycle time grows even though no actual work is being done. The ticket’s clock keeps running while it sits idle.

### Recommendations

1. **Set waiting-time targets for each step**
   - Investigation should start within e.g. 1 hour of assignment.
   - Escalated tickets should be picked up by Level-2 within 2 hours.

2. **Create an escalation hot-queue**
   - Escalated tickets should be treated as high priority, not placed at the end of the Level-2 queue.
   - A warm handoff from Level-1 to Level-2 should include a summary of what was already tried.

3. **Avoid overnight gaps after investigation**
   - If a ticket is investigated by early afternoon, there should be an expectation to either resolve it the same day or formally hand it over to the next shift.
   - Consider follow-the-sun support or a clear end-of-day handoff process.

4. **Automate assignment and triage**
   - Case 104 lost 3h 30m between assignment and investigation.
   - Automated skill-based routing could assign tickets to available agents immediately after triage and reduce queue waiting time.

5. **Track waiting times, not just total time**
   - Monitor segment-level delays such as:
     - Assignment  Investigation
     - Escalation  Level-2 Investigation
     - Investigation  Resolution
   - This makes bottlenecks visible early.

A key takeaway: the slow tickets are not slow because there are necessarily more activities; they are slow because of **long inactive gaps** between activities, especially around escalations and overnight transitions.
