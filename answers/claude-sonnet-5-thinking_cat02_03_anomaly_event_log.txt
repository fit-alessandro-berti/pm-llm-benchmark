# Analysis of Procure-to-Pay Event Log

## Standard Reference Process
The typical P2P flow follows this logical sequence:
**Create PR  Approve PR  Issue PO  Receive Goods  Vendor Invoice Received  Match Invoice to PO  Release Payment  Close Case**

Each step exists to enforce financial controls (e.g., three-way match: PO, Goods Receipt, Invoice) before money leaves the organization.

---

## Case 1001 —  Normal/Compliant Case
Follows the textbook sequence exactly, with logical time gaps (approval before PO, goods before invoice, match before payment). This serves as the baseline for comparison.

---

## Case 1002 —  Anomalous: Missing Segregation of Duties / Approval Bypass
**Sequence:** Create PR  **Issue PO**  Vendor Invoice Received  **Release Payment**  **Approve PR (after the fact)**  Receive Goods  Match Invoice to PO  Close Case

**Problems:**
1. **PO issued before PR approval** — A purchase order was sent to a vendor before the requisition was formally approved. This violates the basic control that spending should be authorized *before* commitment is made.
2. **Payment released before goods were received and before invoice matching** — This is a serious control violation. Paying a vendor before confirming that goods were actually delivered (and before verifying the invoice against the PO) exposes the organization to fraud, duplicate payments, or payment for goods never received.
3. **Approval occurs retroactively** — The PR is approved *after* PO issuance and even after payment, suggesting the approval step was just a rubber-stamp/paperwork formality rather than a genuine control checkpoint.
4. **Match Invoice to PO happens after payment** — Matching should occur *before* payment, not as a post-hoc audit trail formality. This defeats the purpose of the three-way match control.

**Business Risk:** This case pattern is consistent with process manipulation to bypass approval controls — a classic red flag in fraud detection and SOX/internal audit reviews.

---

## Case 1003 —  Anomalous: Payment Released Before Goods/Invoice/Match
**Sequence:** Create PR  Approve PR  Issue PO  **Release Payment**  Vendor Invoice Received  Receive Goods  Match Invoice to PO  Close Case

**Problems:**
1. **Payment released before the vendor invoice was even received** — This is highly unusual and risky. Normally, payment should never precede invoicing, since there's nothing yet to reconcile against.
2. **Payment occurs before goods receipt** — Again violates the fundamental principle of not paying for goods/services until delivery is confirmed.
3. **Match Invoice to PO happens last, after payment** — The matching step is rendered meaningless as a control since money has already changed hands. This is essentially a "check-the-box" activity with no preventive value.

**Business Risk:** This looks like an advance/prepayment scenario that wasn't properly flagged as such, or a process violation where payment was pushed through without verification — increasing risk of paying incorrect amounts, paying for undelivered goods, or enabling vendor fraud.

---

## Case 1004 —  Minor Anomaly: Goods Received Before PO Was Issued
**Sequence:** Create PR  Approve PR  **Receive Goods**  **Issue PO**  Vendor Invoice Received  Match Invoice to PO  Release Payment  Close Case

**Problems:**
1. **Goods received before the PO was issued** — This suggests goods were physically received without a formal purchase order in place, which could indicate:
   - An informal/verbal order was placed and the PO was created retroactively to "paper over" the transaction (a common audit finding).
   - Vendor shipped goods speculatively or based on an earlier unofficial agreement.
2. While the rest of the process (invoice  match  payment) follows the correct order, the **PO-after-goods-receipt** pattern undermines the purpose of the PO as a pre-authorization/commitment document.

**Business Risk:** Lower severity than Cases 1002/1003 since payment still followed proper invoice matching, but it still represents a control gap — the organization received goods without a legally binding purchase agreement in place at time of receipt, which can create disputes over pricing/terms.

---

## Summary Table of Anomalies

| Case | Anomaly Type | Severity | Key Issue |
|------|-------------|----------|-----------|
| 1001 | None | — | Compliant baseline case |
| 1002 | PO before approval; Payment before goods/match; late approval | **High** | Multiple control bypasses, possible fraud indicator |
| 1003 | Payment before invoice and goods receipt | **High** | Payment made with no verification basis at all |
| 1004 | Goods received before PO issued | **Medium** | Retroactive PO / unauthorized goods receipt |

---

## Key Takeaways
- **Case 1001** should be used as the "happy path" template for conformance checking.
- **Cases 1002 and 1003** exhibit the most severe compliance risks because they involve **payment release before adequate verification (goods receipt and/or invoice matching)** — a major internal controls violation, often targeted in SOX and anti-fraud audits.
- **Case 1004** reflects a **process discipline gap** (missing PO-first workflow) rather than a financial control failure, but still warrants correction to prevent unauthorized purchasing.
- A recommended remediation would be enforcing **system-level hard controls** (e.g., ERP workflow blocks) so that payment cannot be released until both goods receipt and invoice match are completed, and POs cannot be issued/backfilled after goods receipt.
