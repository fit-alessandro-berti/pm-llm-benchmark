The XOR branch creates a potential source of **procedural and outcome bias**:

- Applicants selected for `CheckLocalAffiliation` (D) receive a “subtle score uplift.”
- Applicants routed through `skip` do not receive that uplift.
- If the selection of D is based on locality, community membership, geography, referrals, or other correlated characteristics, otherwise similar applicants may receive different scores and therefore different loan terms or decisions.

Even if the targeted characteristic is not legally protected, it may be a **proxy** for protected characteristics. For example, local residence, neighborhood, language, family background, or membership in a community organization may correlate with race, ethnicity, nationality, religion, socioeconomic status, or disability. Thus, a seemingly modest preference can produce disparate treatment or disparate impact.

The bias can also be amplified downstream. A small scoring advantage may move borderline applications out of `ManualReview`, change approval probabilities, improve interest rates or credit limits, or affect the terms assigned in `FinalDecision`. Repeated across many applications, this creates systematic advantages for one group and disadvantages for others. Applicants may also be unable to understand or challenge the difference if the routing rule and uplift are opaque.

Fairness concerns arise even when the favored group is not legally protected:

1. **Equal-treatment concern:** comparable applicants are evaluated under different rules.
2. **Proxy discrimination:** the criterion may indirectly encode protected attributes.
3. **Accountability concern:** an unexplained advantage undermines transparency and contestability.
4. **Feedback-loop risk:** favored applicants may perform better or receive better terms, reinforcing the belief that the rule is justified.

A fairer design would require a documented, relevant, and evidence-based reason for the affiliation check; apply it consistently rather than selectively; separate legitimate verification from preferential scoring; and validate the process using subgroup outcome, error-rate, approval-rate, and pricing analyses. The model should also log why D was selected, disclose material factors to applicants, and prohibit an automatic uplift unless it is demonstrably relevant, proportionate, legally permissible, and monitored for indirect discrimination.
