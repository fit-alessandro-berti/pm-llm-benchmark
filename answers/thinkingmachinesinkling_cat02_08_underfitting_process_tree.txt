**Trace that the model wrongly accepts**

```
Receive_Order, Check_Inventory, Ship_Order, Authorize_Payment, Package_Items, Close_Order
```

*(Any permutation of Authorize_Payment, Package_Items, and Ship_Order inside the success branch is allowed; this is one concrete example.)*

---

### Why the model allows it

Parse the tree:

1. `Root` = **SEQUENCE**  `Receive_Order`  `Check_Inventory`  `XOR`  `Close_Order`
2. The `XOR` chooses the **successful** branch (`successful_subtree`).
3. `successful_subtree` = **PARALLEL** of  
   `Authorize_Payment`, `Package_Items`, `Ship_Order`

Because the three activities are in a `PARALLEL` block, the process-tree semantics permit them to occur in **any order** and with **any interleaving**—all three must happen, but none is ordered before another.

So after `Check_Inventory`, the trace can legally execute `Ship_Order`, then `Authorize_Payment`, then `Package_Items`, and finally the root’s `Close_Order`.

---

### Why it violates domain knowledge

In a real Order-to-Cash flow, once inventory is confirmed sufficient the steps must happen in a strict causal/physical sequence:

1. **Authorize Payment** – confirm the customer is charged / payment is secured.  
2. **Package Items** – gather and pack the goods.  
3. **Ship Order** – hand the packed goods to the carrier.

The trace above breaks this in multiple ways:

- **Ship_Order happens first** – goods are shipped before they have even been packaged or paid for.  
- **Authorize_Payment happens after shipping** – the order is sent out before payment is authorized, creating a financial risk.  
- **Package_Items happens last** – packaging occurs after the order has already been shipped, which is physically impossible.

The root cause in the model is that `successful_subtree` uses `Operator.PARALLEL` instead of `Operator.SEQUENCE`, removing all ordering constraints among those three critical steps.

*(Note: The model also allows the cancel path to execute `Close_Order` twice—once inside `cancel_subtree` and once at the root—producing traces such as `..., Cancel_Order, Close_Order, Close_Order`, which is also domain-invalid. But the misordered success path above is the clearest violation of the required “natural order of operations.”)*
