### 2. Root Causes of Performance Issues

By analyzing the time intervals between individual activities, three primary bottlenecks emerge:

#### Factor A: Level-2 Escalation Queue & Handover Bottlenecks
* **Impacted Cases:** Case 102, Case 105
* **Details:**
  * **Case 102:** Spent **2.5 hours** waiting between L1 assignment and escalation, plus another **2.5 hours** waiting for L2 to begin investigation.
  * **Case 105:** Experienced a massive **28-hour gap** between being escalated (`2024-03-01 10:00`) and the Level-2 agent beginning the investigation (`2024-03-02 14:00`).
* **Root Cause:** Level-2 specialized agents appear to have severe capacity constraints or lack a clear SLA/alerting system when escalated tickets arrive in their queue.

#### Factor B: Delays Prior to Initial Investigation (L1 Queue Time)
* **Impacted Cases:** Case 104
* **Details:**
  * Case 104 was **never escalated**, yet took over 24 hours to resolve.
  * After assignment to a Level-1 agent at 09:30, it sat idle for **3.5 hours** before the investigation began at 13:00 (compared to 10–40 minutes in normal cases).
* **Root Cause:** Inefficient queue management or workload distribution at Level-1, causing assigned tickets to linger unworked during business hours.

#### Factor C: Late-in-the-Day Investigation Gaps (Off-Hours Stagnation)
* **Impacted Cases:** Case 102, Case 104, Case 105
* **Details:**
  * In all three delayed cases, the `Investigate Issue` activity started mid-day/afternoon (13:00 for Case 104, 14:00 for Case 102, 14:00 on Day 2 for Case 105) and was not marked as `Resolve Ticket` until 08:00–09:00 the following morning.
* **Root Cause:** Tickets assigned or investigated late in the workday pause overnight. This introduces an artificial **17–19 hour jump** in elapsed clock time due to operating hours/shifts rather than active effort.

### 3. Impact Analysis & Recommendations

#### Impact on Operations
1. **Inflated Turnaround Times:** Inactive wait times account for >80% of total cycle time in delayed cases.
2. **Customer Dissatisfaction:** Delayed escalations (e.g., Case 105 taking over 2 days) lead to missed service level agreements (SLAs).
3. **Misleading Metrics:** Simple cycle-time averages mask the difference between active working time and queue/overnight waiting time.

---

#### Process Improvement Recommendations

1. **Optimize Level-2 Escalation Routing & Capacity:**
   * **First-Contact Triage Improvement:** Enhance initial triage accuracy so complex cases bypass Level-1 entirely if they clearly require Level-2 expertise, saving up to 2.5 hours of initial misrouting.
   * **L2 Queue Alerts:** Implement automatic notifications for Level-2 queues when an escalated ticket remains unassigned/uninvestigated for more than 1 hour.

2. **Automate Assignment and Set Response SLAs:**
   * Introduce active response time limits (e.g., maximum 30-minute window between *Assign to Agent* and *Investigate Issue*). If an agent does not pick up the ticket within 30 minutes, automatically reassign it to an available pool.

3. **Incorporate Business Hours (Calendar Shifts) in Performance Tracking:**
   * Adjust process monitoring software to measure duration in **working hours** rather than absolute clock hours. This separates actual delays from overnight non-working hours, providing clearer performance visibility.

4. **End-of-Day Workload Rebalancing:**
   * Implement a brief end-of-day triage sweep around 15:00 to reassign pending investigations to evening shifts or prioritize them first thing next morning.
