### Process Mining & Queue Mining Analysis Plan
**Role:** Process Analyst | **Objective:** Reduce Patient Waiting Times & Visit Duration via Data-Driven Optimization

---

### 1. Queue Identification and Characterization

To apply queue mining effectively, we must first translate raw event logs into quantifiable queue dynamics. The core insight in queue mining is distinguishing between **service time** (value-added work) and **waiting time** (non-value-added delay).

#### Calculating Waiting Times
Using the event log, we reconstruct the timeline for each `Case ID`. For every consecutive pair of activities $(A_i, A_{i+1})$ within a patient's visit, the waiting time (queue time) is calculated as:

$$ \text{WaitTime}_{(A_i \to A_{i+1})} = \text{Timestamp}(A_{i+1}, \text{START}) - \text{Timestamp}(A_i, \text{COMPLETE}) $$

*   **Definition:** This delta represents the time the patient spends idle in the system between the completion of one task and the initiation of the next. It captures queuing for resources, handover delays, and administrative hold-ups.
*   **Zero/Negative Values:** We filter out cases where $\text{WaitTime} \leq 0$, which may indicate data errors or simultaneous execution capabilities that do not constitute a queue.

#### Key Metrics for Characterization
Relying solely on averages can mask severe patient experiences. We will calculate a suite of metrics per queue link:

1.  **Central Tendency:**
    *   **Median Wait Time:** Robust measure of typical patient experience; less skewed by extreme outliers.
    *   **Mean Wait Time:** Useful for aggregate capacity planning but sensitive to spikes.
2.  **Tail Risk (Crucial for Satisfaction):**
    *   **P90 / P95 / P99 Percentiles:** These indicate the threshold below which 90% of patients fall. A high P90 relative to the median signals high variability and frequent "bad days" for patients.
    *   **Maximum Wait Time:** Identifies worst-case scenarios that may trigger escalations or complaints.
3.  **Volume & Frequency:**
    *   **Queue Frequency:** Percentage of visits experiencing this specific transition.
    *   **Total Queue Volume:** Sum of wait times across all cases; helps estimate total lost productive hours.
4.  **Queue Load Index:**
    *   Derived metric: $\text{Load} = \text{Average Arrival Rate} \times \text{Average Service Time}$. Queues where Load approaches or exceeds 1.0 are structurally unstable.

#### Identifying Critical Queues
We will prioritize queues using a **Weighted Criticality Score** rather than looking at duration alone. Criteria include:
*   **High Volume × High Delay:** Queues affecting many patients with significant delays (e.g., Registration bottlenecks).
*   **High Percentile Variance:** Queues with low medians but massive P99 values (e.g., "Waiting for Specialist Review"). These cause disproportionate dissatisfaction despite reasonable average performance.
*   **Impact on Critical Path:** Queues that block subsequent high-value activities or extend the total visit duration significantly.
*   **Segmentation Impact:** Queues where specific patient types (e.g., "Urgent") suffer disproportionately longer waits compared to "Normal" flows.

### 2. Root Cause Analysis

Identifying the "where" is insufficient; we must diagnose the "why." Process mining offers advanced analytics to uncover root causes beyond simple timing.

#### Potential Root Causes
*   **Resource Bottlenecks:**
    *   *Capacity Mismatch:* Arrival rates exceed service capacity for specific resources (e.g., limited number of ECG rooms or specialized nurses).
    *   *Skill Mix Issues:* Tasks assigned to highly paid specialists that could be performed by technicians or nurses.
*   **Variability & Uncertainty:**
    *   *Service Time Variance:* High standard deviation in activity durations (e.g., Doctor Consultations vary wildly by specialty or complexity) leads to queue accumulation even if average load is manageable.
    *   *Inter-arrival Variance:* Irregular patient arrivals cause "burstiness," overwhelming resources during peaks.
*   **Process Design & Dependencies:**
    *   *Sequential Rigidities:* Mandatory sequential steps where parallel processing is possible (e.g., Patient must finish check-out before blood test results are reviewed, though results are ready).
    *   *Handover Delays:* Long gaps between departments due to lack of communication or batch processing.
*   **Scheduling & Policies:**
    *   *Deterministic Scheduling:* Appointments scheduled without accounting for variability or no-show rates, leading to cascading delays.
    *   *Priority Handling:* "Urgent" patients interrupting "Normal" flows, potentially increasing wait times for the majority.

#### Process Mining Techniques for Diagnosis
*   **Resource Utilization & Availability Analysis:** Calculate utilization rates per resource. Resources with >85% utilization coupled with growing queues indicate capacity constraints. Overlay **Availability** to see if queues spike when specific staff are absent.
*   **Bottleneck Detection Algorithms:** Apply algorithms that identify activities where the ratio of arrival rate to service rate is highest. Distinguish between **Resource Bottlenecks** (too few staff) and **Process Bottlenecks** (flow design issues).
*   **Variant Analysis & Filtering:** Segment the log by `Patient Type` and `Urgency`. Compare wait times across variants. If "New" patients have 3x the wait in "Nurse Assessment" compared to "Follow-up," the root cause may be workflow complexity or triage rules for new patients.
*   **Correlation Analysis:** Mine correlations between wait times and other attributes. For example, does wait time in "Doctor Consultation" correlate with the preceding activity being "ECG Test"? This might suggest equipment dependency or doctor preference issues.
*   **Social Network Analysis (SNA):** Analyze resource interactions. If certain staff members consistently generate longer downstream queues, it may indicate coordination issues or training needs.

### 3. Data-Driven Optimization Strategies

Based on the analysis, the following three strategies target common healthcare queue dynamics. Each is grounded in data insights.

#### Strategy 1: Dynamic Staffing & Workload Balancing
*   **Target Queue:** Queues associated with high-utilization resources (e.g., Nurses, Diagnostic Techs) during peak hours.
*   **Root Cause Addressed:** Fixed shift schedules do not match variable patient arrival patterns and service time demands.
*   **Data Support:**
    *   Hourly heatmaps of `Arrival Rate` vs. `Service Demand`.
    *   Analysis showing that wait times spike only when specific resources are utilized above a threshold.
*   **Implementation:**
    *   Implement a **Dynamic Staffing Model**. Use historical data to predict hourly demand. Adjust staff schedules to align labor supply with predicted demand curves.
    *   Introduce **Cross-Training**: If data shows "Registration" is fast but "Check-out" is slow, train registration clerks to assist with check-out during surges.
*   **Expected Impact:** Reduction in peak-hour wait times by 20-30%; improved resource utilization balance; no net increase in headcount costs.

#### Strategy 2: Parallelization and Decoupling of Activities
*   **Target Queue:** Queues occurring before high-variance activities (e.g., waiting for Doctor Consultation while diagnostic tests are pending).
*   **Root Cause Addressed:** Strict sequential flow forces patients to wait for upstream completions even if downstream resources are free, or vice versa.
*   **Data Support:**
    *   **Variant Analysis** reveals that certain activities (e.g., Blood Test, ECG) are independent of the Doctor's immediate attention.
    *   Trace analysis shows significant idle time for doctors while patients wait for labs, or patients waiting in rooms while doctors are stuck in consultations.
*   **Implementation:**
    *   **Redesign Flow for "Standard" Visits:** Allow "Pre-Consultation Testing" (Blood/ECG) to occur *before* the doctor sees the patient, or concurrently. The doctor reviews results upon entry.
    *   **Decouple Registration:** For "Follow-up" patients, automate pre-registration or allow self-check-in, reducing the queue at the front desk.
*   **Expected Impact:** Reduction in total visit duration by 15%; smoothing of resource load; reduction in "Waiting for Doctor" queue length as patients are engaged in testing while the doctor remains busy.

#### Strategy 3: Stochastic Appointment Scheduling with Buffer Management
*   **Target Queue:** Queues caused by schedule compression or cascading delays (common in "Doctor Consultation" blocks).
*   **Root Cause Addressed:** Deterministic scheduling assumes constant service times, ignoring the high variance observed in process mining.
*   **Data Support:**
    *   Distribution analysis of `Doctor Consultation` service times showing heavy tails (some visits take 2x the average).
    *   Correlation between early delays and cumulative end-of-day lateness.
*   **Implementation:**
    *   **Stochastic Scheduling:** Replace fixed 15-minute slots with probabilistic booking windows based on service time distributions.
    *   **Buffer Slots:** Insert calculated buffer slots based on the probability of overrun. If P95 service time is 20 minutes but average is 15, schedule buffers accordingly.
    *   **Booking Rules:** Prioritize "Urgent" patients in dedicated slots to prevent disruption of the normal flow, validated by impact analysis on "Normal" patient waits.
*   **Expected Impact:** Reduction in schedule variability by 25%; improved predictability of wait times; reduction in overtime costs associated with delayed closures.

### 4. Consideration of Trade-offs and Constraints

Optimization in healthcare requires balancing efficiency with safety, quality, and cost.

#### Trade-offs
*   **Cost vs. Wait Time:** Adding staff reduces wait times but increases operational costs.
    *   *Mitigation:* Focus on **efficiency gains** (parallelization, dynamic staffing) rather than pure headcount expansion. Quantify ROI by comparing cost savings from reduced overtime/no-shows against investment.
*   **Speed vs. Care Quality:** Compressing service times risks rushing consultations.
    *   *Mitigation:* Monitor **Clinical Outcome KPIs** and **Compliance Rates** alongside wait times. Ensure parallelization does not compromise patient privacy or data integrity. Use process mining to detect deviations in documentation completeness.
*   **Shifting Bottlenecks:** Improving one queue may overwhelm the next.
    *   *Mitigation:* Perform **System-wide Simulation**. Before implementation, simulate the entire process to ensure the new bottleneck is acceptable or can be addressed simultaneously.
*   **Staff Workload & Morale:** Dynamic changes can increase cognitive load or stress.
    *   *Mitigation:* Involve staff in design. Use data to show how changes reduce frustration (e.g., fewer angry patients). Monitor burnout indicators.

#### Balancing Objectives
*   **Multi-Objective Optimization:** Define a utility function that weights Wait Time, Cost, and Quality. Optimize for the Pareto frontier.
*   **Segmentation:** Accept slightly longer waits for low-complexity "Follow-up" cases if it significantly improves outcomes for "Urgent" or "New" complex cases, provided overall satisfaction targets are met.
*   **Pilot Programs:** Implement changes in specific specialties or time slots first to validate trade-offs before full rollout.

---

### 5. Measuring Success

Continuous monitoring is essential to sustain improvements and detect drift.

#### Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)
1.  **Patient Experience KPIs:**
    *   **P90 Wait Time per Stage:** Primary metric for satisfaction. Target: Reduce by X%.
    *   **Total Visit Duration:** Mean and Median time from Registration Start to Check-out Complete.
    *   **On-Time Arrival Rate:** Percentage of patients seen within 5 minutes of their expected window (for scheduled visits).
2.  **Operational Efficiency KPIs:**
    *   **Resource Utilization:** Balanced utilization (target 75-85%) without excessive idle time or saturation.
    *   **Throughput:** Patients processed per provider per day/hour.
    *   **Queue Depth:** Real-time count of patients waiting in critical areas.
3.  **Quality & Safety KPIs:**
    *   **Process Compliance Rate:** % of visits adhering to the optimized process variant.
    *   **Clinical Error/Backlog Rate:** Ensuring speed did not introduce errors or missed follow-ups.

#### Ongoing Process Monitoring
*   **Real-Time Dashboards:** Deploy a process mining dashboard connected to the event stream. Display live queue lengths, current wait times, and alerts when thresholds are breached (e.g., "Nurse Queue > 5 patients").
*   **Automated Alerting:** Trigger notifications to floor managers when specific queues exceed limits, enabling immediate intervention (e.g., calling in float staff).
*   **Periodic Re-Mining:** Schedule monthly/quarterly process mining runs to:
    *   Verify KPI trends.
    *   Detect **Process Drift** (e.g., staff reverting to old habits).
    *   Identify new bottlenecks emerging from previous optimizations.
    *   Update predictive models for staffing and scheduling.
*   **Control Charts:** Use statistical process control to distinguish between common cause variation and special cause incidents, ensuring sustained stability.

By rigorously applying these queue mining principles, the clinic can move from reactive complaint management to proactive, data-driven flow optimization, delivering a superior patient experience while maintaining fiscal responsibility.
