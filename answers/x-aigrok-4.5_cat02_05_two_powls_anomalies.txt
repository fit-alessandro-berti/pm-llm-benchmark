The standard (normative) Hire-to-Retire process follows a clear causal/logical sequence reflecting the nature of recruitment and employment: a vacancy must first be advertised (`Post_Job_Ad`), after which a pool of applicants is filtered (`Screen_Candidates`), the shortlisted applicants are examined in depth (`Conduct_Interviews`), a hiring choice is taken on the basis of that examination (`Make_Hiring_Decision`), a successful candidate is integrated into the organisation (`Onboard_Employee`), the permanent payroll record is created (`Add_to_Payroll`), and the case is finally closed. Optional branches (e.g., rejection after the decision) and iteration (e.g., a second interview round) may exist, but they must never invert the principal causal dependencies nor introduce nonsensical multiplicities (multiple irrevocable on-boardings, optional remuneration of an already-hired employee, \ldots).

**Anomalies in Model 1**  
- After `Screen_Candidates` the activities `Conduct_Interviews` and `Make_Hiring_Decision` are placed in a purely concurrent partial order. Consequently an execution is possible in which the hiring decision is taken before any interview has occurred (or even before an interview has started).  
- Because `Conduct_Interviews` has no outgoing edges it may also finish after the decision, after onboarding, or even after case closure—an entropic interleaving that makes systematic evaluation of candidates impossible.  
These are serious order anomalies, yet every activity still appears exactly once and the suffix that starts with a (possibly premature) decision is perfectly sequential and complete (`Onboard_Employee` \(\to\) `Add_to_Payroll` \(\to\) `Close_Case`).

**Anomalies in Model 2**  
- `Screen_Candidates` possesses only an incoming edge from the initial posting; it is therefore concurrent with every subsequent activity. Screening can therefore finish after interviews, after the decision, after onboarding, or can be “lost” in the concurrent interleaving—effectively making interviewing of unscreened applicants possible.  
- A loop envelope `*(Onboard_Employee, \(\tau\))` forces the employee to be onboarded at least once and permits an arbitrary number of additional, identical onboarding executions.  
- An exclusive-choice construct `X(Add_to_Payroll, \(\tau\))` renders addition to the payroll optional even though onboarding has already taken place.  
Taken together these constructs produce three independent severe violations: missing causal guards, nonsensical multiplicity of an irrevocable life-cycle step, and the possibility of creating an unpaid (or nowadays illegally unpaid) employment relation.

**Which model is closer to the normative process?**  
Model 1 is the closer of the two. Its only material defect is a missing precedence edge between interviewing and deciding; once that edge is supplied the model becomes a correct total order of the classic Hire-to-Retire life-cycle. Model 2, conversely, simultaneously erodes the screening prerequisite, injects an unbounded loop around an inherently non-repeatable activity, and makes the legally compulsory payroll step optional. Those three anomalies strike at the semantic integrity of the process and cannot be repaired by a single local change; they therefore constitute a more distant—and practically more dangerous—deviation from the normative Hire-to-Retire workflow.
