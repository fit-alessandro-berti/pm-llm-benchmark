# Analysis of Procure-to-Pay Event Log Anomalies

## Standard P2P Reference Flow
The expected sequence is:
**Create PR  Approve PR  Issue PO  Receive Goods  Vendor Invoice Received  Match Invoice to PO  Release Payment  Close Case**

Key control principle: **Payment should never be released before the invoice is matched to the PO, and goods receipt should precede invoicing/payment** (three-way match: PO, Goods Receipt, Invoice).

---

## Case 1001 —  Normal/Compliant
Follows the exact expected sequence with logical timestamps. This is the baseline "healthy" case.

---

## Case 1002 —  Anomalous (Missing Approval Control + Sequence Violation)

**Issue 1: PO issued before PR approval**
```
Create PR (08:30)  Issue PO (08:40)  ...  Approve PR (Feb 3, 08:00)
```
The Purchase Order was issued **before** the requisition was approved. This violates the maker-checker/segregation-of-duties control — a PO should never be issued for unapproved spend. This is a classic **bypassed approval control**, risking unauthorized purchasing.

**Issue 2: Payment released before invoice matching and before goods receipt**
```
Vendor Invoice Received (Feb 3, 07:00)  Release Payment (07:15)  Receive Goods (Feb 4)  Match Invoice to PO (Feb 5)
```
Payment was released **before goods were even received** and **before the three-way match** was performed. This is a severe **internal control violation** — it exposes the organization to paying for goods that were never received, paying incorrect amounts, or paying fraudulent invoices. The "Match Invoice to PO" and "Receive Goods" steps happening *after* payment renders them meaningless as controls (they become retroactive paperwork rather than actual verification).

---

## Case 1003 —  Anomalous (Payment Before Invoice/Goods Receipt)

```
Issue PO (Feb 2)  Release Payment (Feb 8)  Vendor Invoice Received (Feb 9)  Receive Goods (Feb 10)  Match Invoice to PO (Feb 11)
```

This is the most severe anomaly in the log: **payment was released before the invoice even existed and before goods were received**. This suggests either:
- A **prepayment** was made without being flagged/tagged as such, or
- A **data entry/process error**, or
- Fraudulent manipulation (e.g., paying a vendor and fabricating supporting documents afterward)

Additionally, there's a large time gap between PO issuance (Feb 2) and Release Payment (Feb 8) with no supporting documentation — the payment has no evidentiary basis at the time it was made. This is a critical audit finding: **payment without invoice or proof of delivery**.

---

## Case 1004 —  Anomalous (Goods Received Before PO Issued)

```
Approve PR (Feb 5, 10:15)  Receive Goods (Feb 6, 09:00)  Issue PO (Feb 6, 09:15)
```

Goods were received **before** the Purchase Order was formally issued. This indicates:
- **Uncontrolled/unauthorized procurement** — goods were ordered or received through informal channels (e.g., phone call, verbal agreement) without a formal PO in place.
- Risk of **price discrepancies**, since there's no PO to benchmark invoice amounts against at time of receipt.
- Common real-world root cause: "maverick buying" where operational staff bypass procurement to expedite delivery, later regularized with a backdated PO.

## Summary Table

| Case | Anomaly Type | Risk |
|------|-------------|------|
| 1001 | None | Baseline compliant case |
| 1002 | PO before Approval; Payment before Match/Goods Receipt | Unauthorized spend; payment fraud/error risk |
| 1003 | Payment before Invoice & Goods Receipt | Payment without documentation; potential fraud |
| 1004 | Goods Receipt before PO Issued | Maverick buying; no formal authorization at time of order |

## Business Impact
These anomalies collectively point to a **breakdown of the three-way match control** (PO–Goods Receipt–Invoice) that is fundamental to P2P governance. In real audits, such patterns are red flags for:
- **Segregation of duties violations**
- **Fraud risk** (e.g., paying fictitious vendors before verification)
- **Financial misstatement risk** (accruals/liabilities not matching actual obligations)
- **Compliance failures** (SOX, internal audit standards)

Recommended action: flag Cases 1002, 1003, and 1004 for **process conformance review** and investigate whether system controls (e.g., blocking payment posting without prior GR/invoice match) can be enforced to prevent recurrence.
