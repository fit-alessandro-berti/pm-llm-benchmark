**This is a BPMN (Business Process Model and Notation) diagram** that models the “Dispatch of goods” process for a Computer Hardware Shop. It shows how three roles collaborate to prepare a shipment.

### Participants (Swimlanes)
- **Logistics** (top lane)
- **Secretary** (middle lane)
- **Warehouse** (bottom lane)

The entire process is contained in one pool labeled “Dispatch of goods Computer Hardware Shop.”

### Overall Flow
The process begins with a **start event** (“Ship goods”) and immediately splits via a **parallel gateway** (diamond with +). This creates two concurrent paths that must both finish before the final steps can proceed:

1. **Secretary path** – decides how the shipment will be handled and prepares the necessary logistics/insurance/labeling.
2. **Warehouse path** – physically packages the goods (with a delay-handling mechanism).

After both paths complete, they synchronize at a **parallel join**, and the process finishes with paperwork and moving the package.

### Detailed Path Descriptions

**1. Secretary Path**
- **Clarify shipment method** (uses the “Order details” document).
- **Exclusive gateway** (“Special handling?”):
  - **No** (standard handling):
    - **Check if insurance is required** (reads from the “Customer information” data store and Order details).
    - **Exclusive gateway** (“If insurance necessary”):
      - If yes → **Insure parcel** (performed in the Logistics lane).
      - Always → **Write package label**.
    - The insure and label paths merge.
  - **Yes** (special handling):
    - **Get 3 offers from logistic companies**.
    - **Select logistic company and place order**.
- The standard and special-handling branches merge at an exclusive gateway, then flow down to the synchronization point.

**2. Warehouse Path**
- **Package goods**.
  - A **48-hour timer boundary event** is attached. If packaging takes longer than 48 hours, the process triggers **Notify customer about delay**.
  - The notification path rejoins the main packaging flow (at a gateway), so the delay handling does not stop packaging.
- The packaging path continues to the synchronization point.

**3. Synchronization and Completion**
- A **parallel join** (AND gateway) waits for both the Secretary path and the Warehouse path to finish.
- **Fill out Shipment paperwork**.
- **Move package to pickup area**.
- **End event**: “Shipment prepared”.

### Key BPMN Features Used
- Parallel split/join for concurrent work (packaging happens at the same time as logistics decisions).
- Exclusive gateways for decisions (special handling? insurance needed?).
- Boundary timer event for exception handling (customer notification on delay).
- Data objects / data store (Order details, Customer information).
- Cross-lane message/sequence flows (especially when Logistics is involved only for insurance).

### Business Meaning
The diagram models an efficient dispatch process that:
- Runs packaging and logistics arrangement in parallel.
- Handles both normal and special shipments differently.
- Optionally insures parcels based on customer/order data.
- Automatically notifies the customer if packaging is delayed beyond 48 hours.
- Ensures all preparatory steps are complete before the package is considered ready for pickup.

In short, it is a classic order-fulfillment / shipping-preparation process that balances efficiency (parallelism), flexibility (decisions), and customer communication (delay notification).
