## Proposed High-Level Process

The low-level events can be aggregated into four coherent manufacturing stages for each `CaseID`:

| Sequence | High-Level Activity | Included Low-Level Events |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | **Material Preparation and Setup** | Retrieve raw metal sheet; scan barcode; place metal sheet on table; align metal sheet; preheat metal sheet |
| 2 | **Welding and Structural Assembly** | Pick up welding tool; weld corner A; weld corner B |
| 3 | **Weld Quality Inspection** | Measure weld integrity |
| 4 | **Protective Coating and Curing** | Apply protective coating; dry coating |
| 5 | **Final Visual Inspection** | Visual check |

---

## 1. Material Preparation and Setup

**Low-level events:**

- Retrieve raw metal sheet
- Scan barcode
- Place metal sheet on table
- Align metal sheet
- Preheat metal sheet

**Rationale:**

These events prepare the raw material for the main manufacturing operation. Retrieving and scanning identify and trace the material, while placing and aligning it establish the correct physical position. Preheating completes the setup required before welding.

Although several resources participate—Operator A, Robot Arm #2, and Heating Unit #1—the events are closely connected in time and all support the same objective: preparing the metal sheet for assembly.

**Suggested aggregate name:**  
**Material Preparation and Setup**

---

## 2. Welding and Structural Assembly

**Low-level events:**

- Pick up welding tool
- Weld corner A
- Weld corner B

**Rationale:**

Picking up the welding tool is the immediate preparation for the welding operation. Welding both corners is part of the same structural joining task and is performed by the same operator using the same tool and welding method.

These events form one manufacturing action because they create the physical assembly or joint in the component.

**Suggested aggregate name:**  
**Welding and Structural Assembly**

---

## 3. Weld Quality Inspection

**Low-level event:**

- Measure weld integrity

**Rationale:**

The integrity measurement is a dedicated quality-control activity performed by Quality Sensor #1. It occurs directly after welding and evaluates whether the assembly meets the required weld-quality criteria.

It is kept separate from welding because it has a different purpose, resource, and outcome: welding creates the joint, while measurement verifies it.

**Suggested aggregate name:**  
**Weld Quality Inspection**

---

## 4. Protective Coating and Curing

**Low-level events:**

- Apply protective coating
- Dry coating

**Rationale:**

Applying the polyurethane coating and drying it are sequential parts of the same surface-treatment operation. The first event applies protection to the assembled component, while the second uses controlled heat to cure or dry the coating.

The two events are grouped despite being performed by different machines because they represent one logical finishing phase.

**Suggested aggregate name:**  
**Protective Coating and Curing**

---

## 5. Final Visual Inspection

**Low-level event:**

- Visual check

**Rationale:**

The visual check is the final acceptance activity performed by an operator after assembly and finishing are complete. It verifies the overall condition of the product, including visible welding and coating issues.

It is separated from the weld-integrity measurement because it is a broader, final inspection rather than a specialized sensor-based weld test.

**Suggested aggregate name:**  
**Final Visual Inspection**

---

## Aggregated Trace Example

For both `A1` and `B2`, the resulting high-level trace is:

```text
Material Preparation and Setup
     Welding and Structural Assembly
     Weld Quality Inspection
     Protective Coating and Curing
     Final Visual Inspection
```
