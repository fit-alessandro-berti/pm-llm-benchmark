## 1. Normative-rule violations

### Case 1 — No normative violation

Case 1 follows the required sequence:

1. Application Received  
2. Credit Check  
3. Risk Assessment  
4. Loan Decision — Approved  
5. Inform Applicant  
6. Finalize Documentation  
7. Close Case  

The approval branch is also handled correctly: documentation is finalized before the case is closed.

### Case 2 — Normative violations

Case 2 violates several strict rules:

- **Risk Assessment occurred before Credit Check.**  
  Risk Assessment took place at 08:20, while Credit Check occurred at 09:00. This reverses the required order.

- **Inform Applicant is missing.**  
  The applicant must be informed after the loan decision, but no such activity appears in the log.

- **Documentation was finalized after a rejection.**  
  The loan was rejected at 09:30, yet Finalize Documentation occurred at 10:00. This is explicitly prohibited.

- **The rejection branch was not followed.**  
  A rejected case should be closed without finalizing documentation. Instead, documentation was finalized before closure.

The event at 09:30 does occur after the recorded Risk Assessment, so that particular ordering relation is satisfied. However, the earlier reversal between Credit Check and Risk Assessment still makes the overall process nonconforming.

### Case 3 — No normative violation

Case 3 follows the required strict sequence:

1. Application Received  
2. Credit Check  
3. Risk Assessment  
4. Loan Decision — Approved  
5. Inform Applicant  
6. Finalize Documentation  
7. Close Case  

Although some activities occurred later than recommended, no mandatory activity was skipped or performed in the wrong order. Since the loan was approved, documentation was finalized before closing.

## 2. Desiderata violations

### Case 1

- **Credit Check timing:** Satisfied. It occurred 30 minutes after application receipt, within the recommended 24-hour window.
- **Applicant notification timing:** Satisfied. The applicant was informed two hours after the decision, within the recommended 48-hour window.
- **Follow-up Call:** No Follow-up Call is recorded.

The missing Follow-up Call is a minor desideratum deviation, not a normative violation. The case remains fully compliant with the strict process rules.

### Case 2

The log does not provide enough information to confirm compliance with the timing desiderata:

- The Credit Check occurred one hour after receipt, so it was within the 24-hour recommendation.
- The applicant was not recorded as being informed. This is primarily a **normative violation**, not merely a desideratum failure. Because there is no notification event, its timing cannot be assessed.
- No Follow-up Call is recorded, which is a minor desideratum deviation if the absence is accurate.

### Case 3

Case 3 fails the following recommendations:

- **Credit Check timing:**  
  The application was received on January 12 at 09:00, but the Credit Check occurred on January 14 at 10:00—approximately 49 hours later. This exceeds the recommended 24-hour window.

- **Applicant notification timing:**  
  The decision was made on January 14 at 12:00, but the applicant was informed on January 16 at 15:00—approximately 51 hours later. This exceeds the recommended 48-hour window.

- **Follow-up Call:**  
  No Follow-up Call is recorded.

These are suboptimal behaviors, but they do not constitute serious process violations because the mandatory sequence and decision-dependent closing logic were still respected.

## 3. Possible interpretations or justifications

- **Case 1:** The process appears to have operated normally. The absence of a Follow-up Call could be justified if the call is only an optional alignment activity or if other communication made it unnecessary.

- **Case 2:**  
  - The early Risk Assessment might have been an informal or preliminary assessment rather than the formal assessment required by the process. However, unless the process distinguishes those activities, the recorded event still violates the stated ordering rule.
  - The Credit Check may have been performed earlier but entered into the system late. This could explain the apparent reversal, but it would require supporting evidence outside the event log.
  - The missing Inform Applicant event could reflect an unlogged phone call, email, or system-recording failure. Without such evidence, it must be treated as missing.
  - Finalizing documentation after rejection could be a workflow error, a misclassified activity, or documentation related to rejection processing rather than loan completion. Under the stated rules, however, it remains prohibited.

- **Case 3:** The delays could be explained by operational backlog, incomplete information, external verification, or difficulty reaching the applicant. Such explanations may account for the desiderata violations, but they do not change the fact that the recommended timing targets were missed.
