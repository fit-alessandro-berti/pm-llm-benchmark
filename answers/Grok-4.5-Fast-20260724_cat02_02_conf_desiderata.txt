These deviations are explicitly characterized as suboptimal rather than serious process failures.

**3. Possible interpretations / justifications consistent with the given constraints**

- **Case 1** is the ideal execution: all normative steps appear in the correct order, the approval path correctly inserts Finalize Documentation before Close Case, and both timing windows are comfortably inside the recommended limits. The missing Follow-up Call is the only (minor) desideratum gap; it may simply have been judged unnecessary once the applicant had already been informed and documentation finalized the same day.
- **Case 2** shows clear process deviations. Possible real-world explanations (none of which excuse the normative breaches) could include an analyst starting the risk assessment on incomplete data, a system or manual error that recorded activities out of order, or an attempt to “fast-track” a rejection that nevertheless still performed the forbidden finalization step. The absence of the Inform Applicant step further suggests either an incomplete log or a genuine omission of a mandatory communication.
- **Case 3** respects every hard sequencing and decision rule, so the case is normatively correct. The long delays (Credit Check ~2 days later, Inform Applicant ~2 days after the decision) and the missing Follow-up Call are typical of workload backlogs, weekend effects, or prioritization of other cases. They represent efficiency or customer-experience shortcomings rather than control-flow violations.

In summary, only Case 2 breaks the mandatory process logic; Case 3 (and, to a lesser extent, the other cases) illustrate the softer desiderata gaps that the process owners would ideally reduce.
