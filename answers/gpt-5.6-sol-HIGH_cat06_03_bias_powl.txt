The XOR is not inherently biased; the bias arises from how the branch is selected and what consequence follows from taking it. If applicants who pass through `CheckLocalAffiliation` receive a score uplift while others take the silent branch, then two otherwise equally creditworthy applicants may receive different outcomes solely because one is a local resident or belongs to a recognized community group.

### Why this creates subtle bias

- **Rewards social connectedness rather than creditworthiness.** Established applicants with recognized affiliations gain an advantage, while newcomers, migrants, socially isolated people, or members of less-recognized groups do not.
- **Can act as a proxy for protected characteristics.** Even if “local affiliation” is not legally protected, it may correlate with race, ethnicity, nationality, religion, age, or socioeconomic status. The rule could therefore produce disparate impacts on protected groups.
- **The benefit can compound downstream.** A small scoring uplift may determine who enters manual review, how an underwriter interprets a borderline case, whether a loan is approved, and what interest rate or terms are offered. Small differences can become materially significant near decision thresholds.
- **It can introduce anchoring in manual review.** Reviewers may treat the uplift as evidence of trustworthiness, reinforcing the initial advantage.
- **It creates a feedback loop.** Favored applicants obtain more credit and build stronger financial histories, while excluded applicants lose the same opportunity, perpetuating existing inequalities.

The fact that the favored category is not legally protected does not automatically make the practice fair—or lawful in every jurisdiction. Fairness concerns arise whenever similarly situated applicants are treated differently for a factor that lacks a legitimate and demonstrable relationship to repayment risk. Legal risk may also remain if the factor causes indirect or disparate-impact discrimination.

A useful counterfactual question is: **Would the decision or loan terms change if the applicant’s financial characteristics remained identical but their local/community affiliation changed?** If so, the model violates a basic notion of individual fairness.

Appropriate safeguards include removing the uplift unless it has a documented, legitimate, and proportionate risk rationale; testing approval rates, pricing, and error rates across protected and intersectional groups; auditing both XOR-branch selection and downstream manual decisions; using less exclusionary financial indicators; and providing explanations and an appeal mechanism. Logs should record the branch taken and its exact contribution to the final decision so the effect is auditable.
