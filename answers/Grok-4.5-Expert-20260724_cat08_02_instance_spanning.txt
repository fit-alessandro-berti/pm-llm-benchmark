**1. Identifying Instance-Spanning Constraints and Their Impact**

Instance-spanning constraints create dependencies across different order instances (cases). Process mining starts from the event log, which already records case ID, activity, start/complete timestamps, resource, and the key attributes (Order Type, Destination Region, Requires Cold Packing, Hazardous Material).

**Shared Cold-Packing stations**  
Filter the log to cases where `Requires Cold Packing = TRUE`. Project only the Packing activity (start and complete events) together with the station resource. Use resource-centric analysis (or a dotted chart sorted by cold-packing station) to detect overlapping intervals on the same station. Queueing time for a case is the interval from the moment the preceding activity completes until Packing starts, provided that interval coincides with another case occupying a cold station. Quantify impact with:  
- Mean/median waiting time attributable to cold-station contention (and its distribution by hour-of-day / day-of-week).  
- Utilization of the limited stations and fraction of time all stations are busy.  
- Number of orders delayed beyond a target SLA solely because of cold-station occupancy.  

**Shipping Batches**  
Group cases by Destination Region and by the Batch identifier recorded on the Shipping Label Generation event (or reconstruct batches by clustering cases whose Shipping Label events occur within a short temporal window and share the same region). For each case, measure the “batch waiting time” as the difference between the moment its Quality Check completes and the moment Shipping Label Generation fires. Separate pure batch-formation delay from ordinary processing delay. Metrics: average batch size, average wait for the last member of a batch, fraction of total cycle time spent waiting for batch mates, and correlation between batch size and on-time delivery rate.

**Priority Handling (Express orders)**  
Identify all Express cases and the resources they claim. For every standard case that uses the same resource (especially packing stations or QC staff), detect preemption or insertion: an Express start that occurs while a standard case is active on that resource, followed by a resume or delayed complete of the standard case. Metrics: frequency of preemptions, extra waiting time injected into standard orders, and the resulting increase in cycle-time variance for standard orders.

**Hazardous-Material simultaneous limit**  
Filter to cases with `Hazardous Material = TRUE`. Construct a global concurrency timeline for the activities Packing and Quality Check across all such cases. Count the number of concurrent hazardous orders in those two activities at every timestamp. Measure the fraction of time the concurrency approaches or exceeds 10, the number of hazardous orders forced to wait because the limit is reached, and the resulting throughput reduction (orders that could have started Packing/QC but were held).

**Differentiating within-instance vs. between-instance waiting**  
For any activity transition (e.g., Item Picking complete → Packing start):  
- Within-instance waiting is the residual time after subtracting any detected resource-contention or batch-formation interval; it is explained by the case’s own attributes or by ordinary queueing behind its own previous activity.  
- Between-instance waiting is isolated by correlating the idle interval with concurrent occupancy of the required resource by other cases (resource-perspective alignment) or with the formation of a batch (region + temporal clustering). Performance-spectrum or multi-instance dotted-chart visualizations make the distinction visually and quantitatively clear. Attribute-level filtering (cold vs. non-cold, hazmat vs. non-hazmat, express vs. standard) further isolates the contribution of each constraint.

**2. Analyzing Constraint Interactions**

The four constraints are not independent.  

- An Express order that also requires cold packing claims one of the scarce cold stations with priority. This lengthens the queue for every subsequent standard cold-packing order and can cascade into later batch-formation delays for the North/West regions that frequently contain cold items.  
- Batching by region can concentrate multiple hazardous orders into the same temporal window. If those orders reach Packing or Quality Check together, they rapidly consume the regulatory concurrency budget of 10, forcing other hazardous orders (even Express ones) to wait and potentially violating the very priority rule that was meant to protect them.  
- Priority preemption of a standard order that is already inside a forming batch can break batch completeness, causing the remaining members to wait longer or forcing the system to close an undersized batch, which reduces route-optimization benefits.  
- High utilization of cold stations during peak hours can push more hazardous cold orders into the same narrow time windows, amplifying the regulatory concurrency pressure.

Understanding these interactions is essential because a local improvement (e.g., simply adding two more cold stations) may shift the bottleneck to the hazardous concurrency limit or destroy batching efficiency. Only a joint view—obtained by multi-perspective process mining that simultaneously tracks resource, case attributes and inter-case temporal relations—reveals the true system-level trade-offs and prevents sub-optimization.

**3. Developing Constraint-Aware Optimization Strategies**

**Strategy A – Predictive dynamic allocation and soft reservation of cold-packing stations**  
Primary constraints addressed: Shared Cold-Packing + Priority Handling.  
Mine historical demand patterns (hourly/ daily profiles of cold-order arrivals by region and by Express flag). Train a lightweight online predictor that, for the next 30–60 min, forecasts the expected number of cold Express and cold standard orders. Based on the forecast, dynamically reserve a subset of the five cold stations for Express traffic and release the remainder to standard traffic under a weighted fair-share policy. When an Express order arrives and no reserved station is free, it may still preempt a standard order, but the preemption is logged and the standard order is immediately re-queued with elevated priority.  
Data leverage: the same event log used for discovery supplies the training features; real-time event streams keep the forecast current.  
Expected outcome: reduced cold-station waiting time for both Express and standard orders, lower preemption frequency, and more predictable cycle times without adding physical capacity.

**Strategy B – Adaptive, constraint-aware batching with dual triggers and hazmat-aware sizing**  
Primary constraints addressed: Shipping Batches + Hazardous-Material limit.  
Replace static “wait-for-region” batching with two triggers: (1) a maximum wait timer (learned from the distribution of historical batch-waiting times that still meet the delivery SLA) and (2) a dynamic size target that shrinks when the current global count of concurrent hazardous orders is already high. Before closing a batch, a quick concurrency check is performed; if adding the remaining hazardous members would push the facility over the limit of 10, the batch is split or the hazardous members are deferred to the next eligible batch.  
Data leverage: historical batch-size versus on-time-delivery curves and the concurrency timeline mined earlier determine the timer thresholds and the safe size functions.  
Expected outcome: shorter average batch waiting times, fewer hazardous concurrency violations, and still acceptable route density because undersized batches are closed only when necessary.

**Strategy C – Global capacity-token scheduling that jointly respects priority, cold stations and the hazardous limit**  
Primary constraints addressed: all four, with emphasis on their interactions.  
Model the five cold stations and the ten “hazardous concurrency slots” as explicit capacity tokens. Every order, upon completion of Item Picking, requests the tokens it needs (cold token if required, hazardous token if flagged). A central dispatcher grants tokens according to a multi-objective rule: Express orders receive highest priority, then the order that has already waited longest relative to its SLA, subject to the hard capacity limits. When a token is released, the dispatcher re-evaluates the waiting set. Minor process redesign: insert a lightweight “Token Acquisition” activity between Item Picking and Packing; the activity is instantaneous when tokens are free and records the wait otherwise, making the between-instance delay fully visible in future logs.  
Data leverage: the mined process model plus the quantified contention metrics supply the initial token capacities and the priority weights; continuous mining updates the weights.  
Expected outcome: simultaneous reduction in cold-station queues, controlled hazardous concurrency, fewer disruptive preemptions, and higher overall throughput because capacity is allocated globally rather than locally.

**4. Simulation and Validation**

Build a discrete-event simulation model whose control flow is the process model discovered from the event log (Petri net or BPMN). Resource pools mirror the real stations and staff; the five cold stations and the hazardous concurrency limit of 10 are implemented as hard capacity constraints. Batching logic, priority preemption rules and the three proposed strategies are encoded as alternative decision policies.  

Arrival processes, activity-duration distributions and attribute frequencies (cold, hazmat, Express, region) are fitted directly from the three-month log, preserving temporal correlations (e.g., peak-hour cold-order surges).  

Key simulation experiments:  
- Baseline replication to validate that the model reproduces the observed waiting-time distributions and concurrency profiles.  
- Scenario runs that activate each strategy (and combinations) under normal and peak load.  
- Sensitivity analysis on the number of cold stations, the hazardous limit, and the batch timer.  

Primary KPIs tracked while the constraints remain enforced: end-to-end cycle time (mean, 95th percentile), on-time delivery rate, cold-station and hazardous-slot utilization, frequency of preemptions, and average batch waiting time. Only strategies that improve the KPIs without violating the hard regulatory limit or creating new hidden queues are candidates for pilot implementation.

**5. Monitoring Post-Implementation**

Deploy a live process-mining dashboard fed by the operational event stream. Core views:  

- Resource contention dashboard: real-time and historical queue-length and waiting-time distributions for cold-packing stations, broken down by Express vs. standard.  
- Batch-formation dashboard: average and 95th-percentile batch waiting time per region, current open-batch sizes, and fraction of batches closed by timer versus size.  
- Hazardous concurrency gauge: continuous count of orders in Packing or Quality Check that carry the hazardous flag, with alerts when the count exceeds 8 (early warning) or 10 (hard violation).  
- Priority-impact view: number of preemptions per hour and the extra cycle time inflicted on the preempted standard orders.  
- Composite SLA dashboard: end-to-end cycle-time percentiles and on-time rates stratified by the four attribute combinations (cold/hazmat/Express/region).  

Automated conformance checks continuously verify that the observed token usage and batch logic still match the designed policies. Drift detection on waiting-time distributions triggers re-mining and possible re-tuning of the predictive models or token weights. Together these metrics confirm that the instance-spanning constraints are being managed rather than merely endured, and they supply the feedback loop for further data-driven refinement.
