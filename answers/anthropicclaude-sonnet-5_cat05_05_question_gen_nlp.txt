# 20 Thought-Provoking Questions on the Smartphone Supply Chain Process

1. How does the company balance the trade-offs between just-in-time replenishment for high-demand markets and the risk of stockouts during unexpected demand surges or supply disruptions?

2. What contingency plans exist if one of the critical suppliers (e.g., the Taiwan microprocessor fabricator) experiences a sudden shutdown due to geopolitical tensions, natural disasters, or pandemics?

3. How does reliance on suppliers from five different countries affect the company's exposure to currency fluctuations, and what hedging strategies might mitigate this risk?

4. To what extent does the choice between air freight and cargo ship transportation reflect a broader strategic trade-off between speed, cost, and carbon footprint?

5. How might increasing geopolitical tensions (e.g., trade wars, tariffs) between the countries involved in the supply chain impact lead times, costs, and sourcing decisions?

6. What are the hidden costs and risks associated with maintaining 24/7 manufacturing operations in Malaysia, particularly regarding worker welfare, labor law compliance, and machine maintenance?

7. How could the company leverage machine learning models not just for demand forecasting, but also for predicting supplier risks or potential quality failures before they occur?

8. In what ways does the complexity of coordinating customs, insurance, and regulatory compliance across multiple countries create bottlenecks, and how might blockchain or other digital solutions streamline this?

9. How does the strategy of aggregated monthly shipments for slower-moving markets impact working capital, inventory holding costs, and the ability to respond to sudden demand changes?

10. What ethical and reputational risks arise from depending on suppliers in regions with varying labor and environmental standards, and how can the company ensure consistent compliance across all tiers?

11. How resilient is this supply chain to a "black swan" event (e.g., a major earthquake in Taiwan or Japan), and what redundancy measures are—or should be—in place?

12. How does the decision to serve VIP or pre-order customers with direct shipments affect overall logistics complexity, cost structures, and customer satisfaction compared to standard retail distribution?

13. What role should sustainability metrics (e.g., carbon footprint, e-waste, recyclability) play in supplier selection and transportation mode decisions, and how might this reshape long-term supply chain design?

14. How can the company better align R&D design choices with supply chain constraints (e.g., component availability, lead times) to avoid costly redesigns or delays during sourcing?

15. What are the potential vulnerabilities in relying heavily on data analytics and machine learning for demand planning, especially if the underlying data is incomplete, biased, or disrupted?

16. How might a product recall be managed efficiently given the global distribution network, and what systems need to be in place to trace affected units back to specific suppliers or production batches?

17. How does the company weigh the benefits of centralizing assembly in Malaysia against the risks of single-point-of-failure disruptions, and would regional assembly hubs offer a viable alternative?

18. What governance structures or communication protocols are necessary to ensure real-time visibility and coordination across teams operating in different time zones, languages, and regulatory environments?

19. How can predictive analytics be used not only for inventory optimization but also for anticipating shifts in consumer preferences that could affect future R&D and sourcing decisions?

20. To what extent do warranty claims and after-sales support data feed back into R&D and quality assurance processes, and how could this feedback loop be strengthened to reduce future defects or design flaws?
