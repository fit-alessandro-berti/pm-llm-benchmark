### 2. Root Cause Analysis

By examining the timestamps between activities, we can isolate specific bottlenecks causing these delays.

#### A. The "Afternoon Cliff" (Post-Investigation Delays)
Three delayed cases (102, 104, 105) share a distinct pattern: the gap between `Investigate Issue` and `Resolve Ticket` is approximately **19 hours**, crossing into the next day.
*   **Case 104:** Investigate at 13:00  Resolve next day at 08:00.
*   **Case 102:** Investigate at 14:00  Resolve next day at 09:00.
*   **Case 105:** Investigate at 14:00  Resolve next day at 09:00.
*   **Contrast:** Fast cases (101, 103) investigate and resolve within the same timeframe (e.g., 101 investigates at 10:00 and resolves at 10:00).

**Insight:** There appears to be a hard cutoff or shift-change policy where tickets investigated after roughly **13:00–14:00** are not resolved immediately but are pushed to the next business day. This suggests a lack of "end-of-day" resolution protocols or urgent handling for late-afternoon investigations.

#### B. Inconsistent Escalation Response Times
Cases involving escalation show high variability in how quickly specialized teams engage.
*   **Case 102:** Escalated at 11:30  Level-2 Investigates at 14:00. **Wait: 2.5 hours.**
*   **Case 105:** Escalated at 10:00  Level-2 Investigates at 14:00 (next day). **Wait: 28 hours.**

**Insight:** Despite escalating earlier, Case 105 suffered a massive 28-hour delay for Level-2 engagement, whereas Case 102 was handled in 2.5 hours. This indicates severe inconsistency in Level-2 team workload, prioritization, or alert mechanisms. Case 105 may have been deprioritized or fell through the cracks due to overload.

#### C. Pre-Investigation Bottlenecks (Case 104)
Case 104 has no escalation but is still delayed by ~24 hours.
*   **Assign to Agent:** 09:30
*   **Investigate Issue:** 13:00
*   **Gap:** 3.5 hours.

#### D. Triage Delays

### 3. Recommendations & Insights

To reduce cycle times and eliminate these bottlenecks, the following actions are recommended:

#### 1. Address the "Afternoon Cliff"
*   **Implement End-of-Day Handoff Protocols:** Create a process where tickets investigated after 13:00 that cannot be resolved immediately are flagged as "Urgent" or automatically reassigned to an on-call agent to prevent overnight stagnation.
*   **Review SLA Definitions:** If the current workflow forces overnight waits, update Service Level Agreements (SLAs) to reflect realistic resolution windows or introduce overtime/shift coverage for critical tickets.

#### 2. Stabilize Escalation Workflows
*   **Analyze Level-2 Capacity:** Investigate why Case 105 waited 28 hours while Case 102 waited 2.5 hours. It is likely Level-2 is under-resourced or lacks a triage mechanism for incoming escalations.
*   **Automate Escalation Alerts:** Ensure escalations trigger immediate notifications to Level-2 supervisors or managers to guarantee acknowledgment within a strict SLA (e.g., <2 hours).
*   **Prioritization Matrix:** Define clear criteria for escalation priority so that Level-2 can distinguish between critical delays (like Case 105) and standard requests.

#### 3. Optimize Level-1 Assignment and Load Balancing
*   **Monitor Agent Utilization:** The 3.5-hour delay in Case 104 suggests uneven workload distribution. Implement real-time dashboards to monitor agent queues and auto-route tickets to available agents to minimize pre-investigation wait times.
*   **Capacity Planning:** If Case 104's delay was due to a single agent being overwhelmed, consider dynamic routing rules that balance load more effectively.

#### 4. Continuous Monitoring
*   **Bottleneck Detection:** Set up automated alerts for any case where the gap between `Assign` and `Investigate` exceeds 1 hour, or the gap between `Investigate` and `Resolve` crosses a shift boundary. This allows management to intervene proactively rather than reacting to closed delayed tickets.

By addressing the post-investigation cutoff, stabilizing escalation response times, and balancing agent workloads, the process can move from the current average of ~25+ hours for problematic cases back toward the efficient ~2-hour benchmark seen in Cases 101 and 103.
