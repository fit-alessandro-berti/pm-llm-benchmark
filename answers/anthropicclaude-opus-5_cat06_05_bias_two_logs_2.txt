# Bias Analysis: Group A vs. Group B Hiring Logs

## 1. Short answer

**Group B's process is the biased one** — but the harm lands on Group A. Group B contains an extra, undisclosed data transformation (`ScoreAdjustment = +5 (Association Boost)`) that is *structurally inaccessible* to Group A, because 0/3 Group A candidates are — and, as non-residents, plausibly cannot be — members of the "LocalBusinessLeadersClub."

Critically, this is **not visible in the control flow**. Both groups execute the identical trace:

`ApplicationReceived  BasicScreening  SkillTest  PersonalityAssessment  CulturalFitCheck  FinalInterview  HiringDecision`

with the same resources and near-identical throughput times (A: 100–121 min; B: 100–105 min). A conformance check on activity sequence would return **zero deviations**. The discrimination lives entirely in the **data perspective and the decision logic** — which is exactly where fairness audits most often fail to look.

---

## 2. Reconstructing the decision rule

| Case | Group | Skill | Personality | CF (raw) | Adjustment | CF (final) | Decision |
|------|-------|-------|-------------|----------|------------|------------|----------|
| P001 | A | 85 | 70 | 65 | 0 | 65 | **Hired** |
| P002 | A | 78 | 75 | 60 | 0 | 60 | Not Hired |
| P003 | A | 82 | 68 | 65 | 0 | 65 | **Hired** |
| U001 | B | 80 | 72 | 60 | +5 | 65 | **Hired** |
| U002 | B | 78 | 70 | 60 | 0 | 60 | Not Hired |
| U003 | B | 75 | 65 | 58 | +5 | 63 | **Hired** |

Decision mining on this table yields a single clean rule:

> **Hire  CulturalFit(final)  ~63**

Skill and personality scores are *not* discriminating variables — U003 has the **lowest skill (75) and lowest personality (65) of all six candidates** and is hired, while P002 (78/75) is rejected. The only variable that separates hires from rejections is the *post-adjustment* cultural fit score.

Since the adjustment is the difference between passing and failing the threshold in **both** Group B hires, the boost is not a cosmetic tiebreaker — it is **outcome-determinative in 100% of the cases where it is applied.**

---

## 3. How the bias manifests

### (a) Outcome parity masks the unfairness
Selection rate is **2/3 (66.7%) in both groups**. Statistical parity difference = 0. Disparate impact ratio = 1.0. A naive fairness dashboard would report *"no bias detected."*

The parity is an illusion produced by a **differential merit bar**:

| | Mean Skill of hires | Mean Personality of hires | Mean raw CF of hires |
|---|---|---|---|
| Group A | 83.5 | 69.0 | 65.0 |
| Group B | 77.5 | 68.5 | **59.0** |

Group A candidates must earn a raw 65 to be hired; Group B members of the club need only a raw 58. **Group A is being held to a bar roughly 6–7 points higher on the same instrument.** Parity in *rates* coexists with a large gap in *required qualifications* — a classic signal that an unearned advantage is compensating for lower measured performance.

### (b) Counterfactual unfairness (the smoking gun)
- **P002** (Skill 78, Pers 75, CF 60)  **Not Hired**
- **U001** (Skill 80, Pers 72, CF 60)  **Hired**

Same raw cultural fit. P002 has a *higher* personality score. The sole differentiator is club membership. Flip P002's association attribute to `LocalBusinessLeadersClub` and their CF becomes 65  **Hired**. The decision is counterfactually dependent on a group-correlated attribute, which violates counterfactual fairness and, in most legal frameworks, is direct evidence of a discriminatory rule.

Even starker: **U003 is dominated by P002 on every raw measure** (75<78 skill, 65<75 personality, 58<60 cultural fit) yet is hired while P002 is not. The process produces a strict merit inversion.

### (c) Proxy / redundant encoding
`ProfessionalAssociation = LocalBusinessLeadersClub` is perfectly nested inside `LocalResident = TRUE` (2/3 in Group B, 0/3 in Group A). The club is a **proxy variable**: the process never mentions the protected attribute, but conditioning on membership is functionally equivalent to conditioning on residency/group status. Removing the protected attribute from the model would not remove the discrimination — a textbook case of *fairness through unawareness* failing.

Note also the "unprotected-group" internal control, **U002**: a local resident with no club membership is rejected on CF 60. This confirms the mechanism is *membership*, not group label per se — which is precisely what makes proxy discrimination legally and technically insidious. The rule is facially neutral and internally consistent; its impact is not.

### (d) The adjustment targets the softest criterion
The boost is applied not to the objective, machine-scored `SkillTest` but to `CulturalFitCheck` — the most subjective, least validated, human-assessed construct in the pipeline (Resource: HR Analyst). Layering a discretionary points bonus onto an already-subjective measure compounds opacity: there is no way to distinguish "genuine fit" from "affiliation credit" in the final number. The log even overwrites the field (`65 (60+5)`, `63 (Adjusted)`), so downstream activities (FinalInterview, HiringDecision) consume the *inflated* value with no visible provenance at the point of use.

---

## 4. Is the boost ever defensible?

Only if the organization can demonstrate **job-relatedness and business necessity** — i.e., that club membership predicts job performance *beyond* what skill/personality/fit already measure, validated on data, and that no less-discriminatory alternative exists. Nothing in this log supports that:

- If membership genuinely signals a competency, that competency should be **measured directly** for all candidates, not awarded as an untethered 5-point bonus.
- The boost is applied uniformly regardless of tenure, role, or activity in the club — the hallmark of a status credential, not a performance predictor.
- Eligibility is **geographically gated**, so even a valid predictor would be unavailable to non-residents, making the criterion a barrier rather than a measure.

The most plausible reading is **affinity/network bias** institutionalized as a scoring rule — rewarding social proximity to incumbent decision-makers ("Business Leaders Club") and thereby reproducing the existing composition of the workforce.

---

## 5. Implications

**Fairness.** Individual fairness is violated (similar candidates, different outcomes). Counterfactual fairness is violated. Group fairness *appears* satisfied only because the metric chosen is insensitive to the compensating merit gap — a warning that single-metric fairness monitoring is inadequate.

**Legal exposure.** A facially neutral criterion with disparate impact on a protected class, applied without validated job-relatedness, is the standard fact pattern for disparate-impact liability. The explicit `ScoreAdjustment` field is, in effect, self-documented evidence.

**Equity and feedback loops.** Club membership is itself downstream of prior access — who already has local standing, connections, and time. Rewarding it converts prior advantage into hiring advantage, and each cohort hired this way strengthens the club's pipeline. The disparity compounds rather than decays.

**Data integrity.** Overwriting `CulturalFit` with the adjusted value destroys the raw signal for downstream evaluators and for future model training. Any predictive model trained on these outcomes will learn the association bonus as a legitimate performance correlate and reproduce it at scale, now without an auditable `ScoreAdjustment` column to expose it.

**Caveat.** With n=3 per group, no statistical claim is supportable. But the finding here is **not statistical — it is structural**: a deterministic rule that is outcome-determinative whenever triggered and available to only one group. The bias is provable from the policy itself, independent of sample size. A larger log would quantify magnitude, not establish existence.

---

## 6. Recommended remediation

1. **Suspend the association boost** pending a documented validation study; absent evidence of job-relatedness, remove it permanently.
2. **Never overwrite raw scores.** Persist `CulturalFit_raw`, `Adjustment`, `Adjustment_reason`, and `CulturalFit_final` as separate immutable fields.
3. **Publish the decision threshold** (~63) and apply it to raw, unadjusted scores.
4. **Audit the data perspective, not just control flow.** Add decision-mining and proxy-detection checks (test each auxiliary attribute for correlation with protected status) to the standard conformance pipeline.
5. **Monitor a metric bundle**, not parity alone: selection rate *plus* mean qualifications of hires per group, counterfactual flip rate, and consistency (do dominated candidates ever beat dominating ones?).
6. **Restructure cultural fit** into behaviorally anchored, job-linked criteria with rubrics, or drop it — a subjective score that is also the sole decision driver is an unacceptable single point of failure.
7. **Retrospective review** of P002-type cases (rejected candidates who would have been hired under equal treatment) and remedy where feasible.

**Bottom line:** Group B's log contains the biased mechanism; Group A's candidates bear its cost. Equal hire rates conceal a structurally unequal standard, and the fairness failure is invisible to control-flow conformance checking and to statistical-parity testing alike.
