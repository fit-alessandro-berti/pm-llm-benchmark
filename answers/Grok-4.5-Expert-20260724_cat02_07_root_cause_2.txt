**Cases with significantly longer durations**

I calculated end-to-end lead time (Submit Claim → Close Claim) for each case:

- **2001** (Region A, Low): ~1.5 hours  
- **2004** (Region B, Low): ~1.4 hours  
- **2002** (Region B, Medium): ~26 hours  
- **2003** (Region A, High): ~48 hours  
- **2005** (Region B, High): ~77 hours  

The clear outliers are **2003** and especially **2005**. Case 2002 is moderately extended. Cases 2001 and 2004 complete within a couple of hours and show no performance issue.

**Attribute analysis and root-cause patterns**

| Case | Complexity | # “Request Additional Documents” | Region | Key resources involved | Lead time |
|------|------------|----------------------------------|--------|------------------------|-----------|
| 2001 | Low        | 0                                | A      | Adjuster_Mike, Manager_Ann | Short    |
| 2004 | Low        | 0                                | B      | Adjuster_Lisa, Manager_Ann | Short    |
| 2002 | Medium     | 1                                | B      | Adjuster_Lisa, Manager_Ann | Medium   |
| 2003 | High       | 2                                | A      | Adjuster_Mike, Manager_Bill | Long     |
| 2005 | High       | 3                                | B      | Adjuster_Lisa, Manager_Bill | Very long|

Key correlations:

1. **Complexity is the dominant driver**  
   - Low-complexity claims never request additional documents and finish the same morning.  
   - Medium complexity triggers one request and adds roughly one extra day.  
   - High complexity triggers repeated requests (2–3 times) spread across multiple days; each successive request further delays the Approve → Pay → Close path.

2. **Region is secondary / not causal by itself**  
   Both Region A and Region B contain a long high-complexity case and a short low-complexity case. Region does not systematically lengthen or shorten lead time once complexity is controlled for.

3. **Resource effects are intertwined with complexity**  
   - Adjuster_Lisa handles the two longest B-region cases (Medium + High) and issues the highest number of document requests.  
   - Adjuster_Mike handles the long high-complexity case in A and also issues two requests.  
   - Manager_Ann approves the three faster cases; Manager_Bill approves the two slowest cases.  
   The pattern suggests that the same adjusters who work high-complexity claims are the ones repeatedly looping on “Request Additional Documents,” rather than a pure “bad resource” effect independent of complexity.

**Why these attributes produce long lead times**

- High-complexity claims appear to arrive with incomplete or ambiguous documentation. The current process reacts by issuing successive, incremental requests instead of a single comprehensive request after the initial Evaluate Claim step. Each new request introduces multi-day waiting time before the next activity can fire.  
- The repeated requests themselves become a bottleneck: the same adjuster (Lisa or Mike) stays on the case, so any capacity constraint or sequential working style of that resource amplifies the delay.  
- Manager assignment may be a downstream symptom rather than a root cause (the longer cases simply reach the approval queue later and are routed to Manager_Bill).

**Recommendations to mitigate the root causes**

1. **Complexity-aware intake checklist**  
   At the Evaluate Claim step, apply a mandatory, complexity-tiered document checklist. For High complexity, force a single exhaustive request rather than allowing iterative follow-ups. This should collapse the 2–3 request loops into one.

2. **Adjuster work-allocation rules**  
   - Route High-complexity cases to a dedicated “complex-claims” queue with a smaller caseload per adjuster.  
   - Track the metric “number of additional-document requests per case” as a performance indicator; coach or re-train adjusters who repeatedly issue >1 request on the same case.

3. **Process redesign for High complexity**  
   - Insert an explicit “Compile Complete Package” activity after the first (and only) document request, with a hard SLA.  
   - Consider parallel rather than sequential document collection (e.g., customer portal that accepts multiple uploads against a checklist).

4. **Monitoring**  
   Add real-time dashboards that flag any case that has already received two “Request Additional Documents” events; escalate those cases immediately so they do not silently accumulate multi-day waits.

Implementing the above should bring High-complexity lead times much closer to the Medium-complexity baseline and eliminate the multi-day document-request loops that currently dominate overall process duration.
