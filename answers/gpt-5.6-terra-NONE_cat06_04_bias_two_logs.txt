**Group B (Unprotected Group) exhibits the apparent bias.**

### Evidence of bias

In **Group A**, every applicant has:

- `LocalResident = FALSE`
- `CommunityGroup = None`
- `ScoreAdjustment = 0` (or N/A during non-scoring steps)

Their preliminary scores remain unchanged through the final decision:

- P001: 720  720  Approved  
- P002: 710  710  Rejected  
- P003: 740  740  Approved  

In **Group B**, applicants marked as local residents and members of the *Highland Civic Darts Club* receive a discretionary-looking score increase:

- U001: 720  **730** due to `+10 (Community Boost)`  Approved
- U003: 695  **705** due to `+10 (Community Boost)`  Approved

Meanwhile, U002, although a local resident, has no community-group membership and receives no adjustment:

- U002: 710  710  Rejected

### How the bias manifests

The process gives a positive scoring benefit to applicants associated with a particular community group. Since that group appears only in the unprotected-group log, the benefit is unavailable to Group A applicants.

This can produce unequal outcomes for applicants with comparable—or even weaker—preliminary scores:

- **P002** in Group A has a preliminary score of **710** and is rejected.
- **U003** in Group B has a lower preliminary score of **695**, but receives a community-based +10 adjustment to 705 and is approved.

Even though the exact decision threshold or all decision rules are not shown, this pattern strongly suggests that group-linked attributes influence outcomes beyond the preliminary score.

### Role of `LocalResident` and `CommunityGroup`

`LocalResident` and `CommunityGroup` may function as direct or proxy variables for protected-group membership, geographic origin, ethnicity, social affiliation, or access to local networks. In this log:

- `LocalResident = TRUE` is present only for Group B.
- Membership in the named civic club is also present only for Group B.
- The score boost is tied specifically to community-group membership.

Thus, Group B members receive an additional pathway to approval that Group A does not have. If Group A members are systematically less likely—or unable—to qualify for that community affiliation, then the “Community Boost” creates **disparate treatment** or, at minimum, a substantial risk of it.

### Conclusion

The discriminatory mechanism is the **+10 Community Boost** applied to certain Group B applicants based on community affiliation. It can convert lower preliminary scores into approvals while Group A applicants receive no comparable adjustment. The resulting process is not purely score-based and may systematically favor local, community-connected applicants over the protected group.
