A normative Hire-to-Retire hiring sequence would typically be:

\[
\text{Post Job Ad} \rightarrow \text{Screen Candidates} \rightarrow \text{Conduct Interviews} \rightarrow \text{Make Hiring Decision} \rightarrow \text{Onboard Employee} \rightarrow \text{Add to Payroll} \rightarrow \text{Close Case}
\]

There may be legitimate alternatives—for example, a rejection decision may lead directly to case closure—but interviewing should normally happen before the hiring decision, onboarding should follow a positive decision, and payroll registration should normally be mandatory for a hired/onboarded employee.

## Model 1 analysis

### Behavior implied by the model

Model 1 imposes:

- `Post_Job_Ad  Screen_Candidates`
- After screening, both:
  - `Screen_Candidates  Make_Hiring_Decision`
  - `Screen_Candidates  Conduct_Interviews`
- `Make_Hiring_Decision  Onboard_Employee  Add_to_Payroll  Close_Case`

Crucially, there is no ordering relation between `Conduct_Interviews` and `Make_Hiring_Decision`, nor between `Conduct_Interviews` and the later onboarding/payroll/closure chain.

Therefore, after screening, the interview and hiring-decision paths may occur concurrently. The model allows traces such as:

```text
Post_Job_Ad,
Screen_Candidates,
Make_Hiring_Decision,
Onboard_Employee,
Add_to_Payroll,
Close_Case,
Conduct_Interviews
```

It also allows the decision to be made before the interview.

### Anomalies in Model 1

1. **Hiring decision can occur before interviews**
   - This is the main anomaly.
   - In a normal recruitment process, interviews provide important information for the hiring decision. A decision before interviewing is generally illogical or at least highly exceptional.
   - The correct relation should normally be:

   ```text
   Screen_Candidates  Conduct_Interviews  Make_Hiring_Decision
   ```

2. **Interviews are not required to finish before onboarding**
   - Since `Interview` has no dependency on `Decide`, `Onboard`, `Payroll`, or `Close`, onboarding can start before interviews are completed.
   - This could result in an employee being onboarded and paid while their interview is still pending.

3. **Case may close before the interview**
   - The model permits `Close_Case` before `Conduct_Interviews`.
   - This violates process integrity because an activity associated with the recruitment case may occur after the case has been formally closed.

4. **No explicit negative-decision/rejection path**
   - The model always leads from `Make_Hiring_Decision` to onboarding and payroll.
   - In reality, a decision can be negative, in which case the process should close without onboarding or adding someone to payroll.
   - This is a deviation, although it may be acceptable if the model is intended to represent only successful hires.

### Severity

The decision-before-interview and close-before-interview possibilities are significant ordering errors. However, Model 1 still makes onboarding, payroll registration, and closure mandatory after the decision, which preserves the core structure of a successful hire lifecycle.

## Model 2 analysis

### Behavior implied by the model

Model 2 imposes:

- `Post_Job_Ad  Screen_Candidates`
- `Post_Job_Ad  Conduct_Interviews`
- `Conduct_Interviews  Make_Hiring_Decision`
- `Make_Hiring_Decision  loop_onboarding`
- `loop_onboarding  xor_payroll`
- `xor_payroll  Close_Case`

The onboarding loop is:

```text
*(Onboard_Employee, skip)
```

This means onboarding is performed at least once, and may be repeated. The silent transition allows the loop to end after an onboarding iteration.

The payroll XOR is:

```text
X(Add_to_Payroll, skip)
```

Thus, payroll registration is optional: either the employee is added to payroll or that activity is skipped.

### Anomalies in Model 2

1. **Screening and interviews can occur in parallel**
   - Both `Screen_Candidates` and `Conduct_Interviews` depend only on `Post_Job_Ad`.
   - There is no edge:

   ```text
   Screen_Candidates  Conduct_Interviews
   ```

   Therefore, interviews may begin or even finish before candidate screening is complete.

   This is a deviation from normal practice because screening is usually used to select candidates who should proceed to interviews. Interviewing candidates before screening them is inefficient and potentially inappropriate.

2. **Onboarding may repeat indefinitely**
   - The loop permits repeated execution of `Onboard_Employee`.
   - Re-onboarding can occasionally make sense—for example, correcting documentation, repeating orientation, or handling a rehire—but unrestricted repetition is not normative.
   - In a standard model, onboarding would normally occur once, perhaps with specific rework activities modeled separately if needed.

3. **Payroll registration is optional**
   - The XOR allows:

   ```text
   Make_Hiring_Decision  Onboard_Employee  Close_Case
   ```

   without `Add_to_Payroll`.

   This is a severe anomaly if the decision represents a successful hire. An onboarded employee who is not added to payroll cannot normally be paid, which undermines a central operational obligation of the hiring process.

4. **No explicit decision outcome**
   - As in Model 1, the model does not distinguish between a positive hiring decision and a rejection.
   - Every decision flows into onboarding, so rejected applicants are not explicitly represented.
   - More importantly, Model 2 combines mandatory onboarding with optional payroll, which is inconsistent for successful hires.

### Severity

The optional payroll activity is a fundamental integrity issue. A process should not normally allow a person to be onboarded as an employee but skip payroll setup. The screening/interview concurrency is also problematic, although less severe than allowing payroll to be omitted after onboarding.

---

## Which model is closer to a normative process?

**Model 1 is closer to a normative Hire-to-Retire process.**

### Justification

Model 1 has a serious sequencing defect: it does not enforce that interviews occur before the hiring decision, onboarding, payroll, or closure. Nevertheless, its main successful-hire path retains several essential constraints:

```text
Make_Hiring_Decision  Onboard_Employee  Add_to_Payroll  Close_Case
```

Thus, once the process proceeds toward hiring, onboarding and payroll are mandatory and occur before closure. This preserves the essential employment-administration logic.

Model 2 correctly ensures that interviews precede the hiring decision, but it introduces more consequential problems:

- interviews can occur before screening;
- onboarding may repeat without restriction;
- most critically, payroll setup can be skipped after onboarding.

The last issue is particularly severe because payroll registration is normally indispensable for a newly hired employee. It permits an incomplete and operationally invalid hiring outcome: a person is treated as onboarded but is never entered into the payroll system.

## Summary

| Model | Main anomalies | Overall assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Model 1 | Decision may precede interview; interview may happen after onboarding or case closure; no rejection branch | Imperfect ordering, but preserves mandatory onboarding and payroll for successful hires |
| Model 2 | Screening and interviews may overlap; onboarding may repeat; payroll is optional; no rejection branch | More structurally problematic, especially because it allows onboarding without payroll |
| Better alignment | **Model 1** | Closer to the expected successful-hire lifecycle despite its interview/decision ordering flaw |

A corrected normative structure would usually enforce:

```text
Post_Job_Ad
 Screen_Candidates
 Conduct_Interviews
 Make_Hiring_Decision
 X(
      Onboard_Employee  Add_to_Payroll,
      Reject_Candidate
  )
 Close_Case
```

This captures both the normal successful-hire route and the possibility of a rejection outcome.
