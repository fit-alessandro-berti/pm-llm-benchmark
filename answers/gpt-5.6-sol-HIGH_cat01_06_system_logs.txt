## Transformation Logic

### Case identification

Cases were defined using the primary business object being handled:

- Each Word document is a separate document-editing case.
- The Annual Meeting email and its reply form one email-handling case.
- The PDF is a PDF-review case.
- The Excel workbook is a spreadsheet-editing case.
- Repeated access to the same object remains in the same case, even when another application interrupts the work. Thus, both periods of work on `Document1.docx` belong to `DOC-DOCUMENT1`.

The inbox access at 09:01:45 was assigned to the Annual Meeting email case because the immediately following action identifies that thread. `SWITCH` events were attributed to the destination object rather than duplicated across source and destination cases.

### Activity abstraction

Low-level actions were translated into business-readable activities:

- `FOCUS` and destination-side `SWITCH`  **Access Document**, **Access Workbook**, **Access Inbox**, or **Access PDF**
- `TYPING`  **Edit Document**, **Edit Workbook**, or **Compose Email Reply**
- `SCROLL`  **Read Email** or **Review PDF**
- Context-specific `CLICK` actions  **Open Email**, **Start Email Reply**, or **Send Email Reply**
- `HIGHLIGHT`  **Annotate PDF**
- `SAVE` and `CLOSE` retain their business meaning as save and close activities

No completion events were invented for the PDF or workbook cases where the source log did not contain an explicit close or completion action.
