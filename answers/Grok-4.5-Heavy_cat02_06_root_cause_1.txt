**Root causes / patterns causing the delays**  
Three clear, recurring factors appear in the long cases (supported by inter-event gaps):  

1. **Escalations to Level-2 (present in 102 and 105, the two longest)**  
   - 102: Assign L1 (09:00) → Escalate (11:30) = 2.5 h; Escalate → Investigate = 2.5 h.  
   - 105: Assign (09:00) → Investigate L1 (09:10) → Escalate (10:00); then Escalate → next Investigate (14:00 next day) = **~28 h** wait.  
   Early steps (Receive → Triage → Assign) are similar and short across *all* cases; the major added time appears after escalation.

2. **Consistent ~19 h overnight gaps between Investigate and Resolve (present in *all three* long cases)**  
   - 102: Investigate 14:00 day 1 → Resolve 09:00 day 2.  
   - 104: Investigate 13:00 day 1 → Resolve 08:00 day 2.  
   - 105: Investigate 14:00 day 2 → Resolve 09:00 day 3.  
   Short cases complete Investigate → Resolve in 0.5–1 h on the same morning and never cross into overnight. This pattern strongly indicates work does not progress (or is not staffed) outside certain hours, so any ticket whose investigation starts later in the day automatically carries over.

3. **Post-assignment delays before investigation starts (especially visible in 104)**  
   - 104: Assign L1 (09:30) → Investigate (13:00) = 3.5 h (no escalation).  
   - 102 also shows 2.5 h from Assign to Escalate.  
   Combined with the overnight effect, a late start of investigation guarantees a multi-day cycle.

No evidence of rework loops, repeated activities, or major differences in the initial Receive/Triage/Assign stages. The short cases stay entirely within Level-1 and finish the same morning.

**How these factors increase cycle time + recommendations**  
Escalation introduces hand-off and Level-2 queue time; when that is compounded by an investigation that starts mid/late day, the overnight pause is forced and the ticket spans one or more extra calendar days. Case 105 illustrates the worst interaction (escalation + very long L2 wait + overnight). Case 104 shows that even without escalation, a delayed investigation start plus the overnight rule is enough to produce a ~24 h cycle.

Actionable insights:  
- Improve triage/routing accuracy so complex tickets go directly (or earlier) to Level-2, reducing unnecessary L1 hand-offs.  
- Set and monitor SLAs for Level-2 response after escalation; increase Level-2 capacity or create a fast-track queue if waits regularly exceed a few hours.  
- Reduce post-assignment idle time (better workload balancing, auto-assignment, or visibility of agent availability).  
- Address the overnight carry-over: either start investigations earlier in the day, introduce limited after-hours / on-call coverage for in-progress tickets, or explicitly prioritize tickets that are already under investigation so they finish same-day.  
- Track process metrics such as time-to-first-investigation, escalation wait time, and % of tickets resolved same-day; these will surface the bottlenecks more systematically than total cycle time alone.

These changes target the exact points where the log shows time accumulating and should shrink the multi-day tails without altering the efficient path already used by simple tickets.
