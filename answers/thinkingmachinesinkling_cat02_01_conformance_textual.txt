## Case-by-case analysis

### Case 1
**Sequence:** Receive  Check  Authorize  Package  Ship  Close  
**Verdict:** **Correct** (sufficient-inventory path).  
All ordering rules are respected and Close is final.

### Case 2
**Sequence:** Receive  Check  **Package**  **Authorize**  Ship  Close  
**Verdict:** **Violation.**  
- **Package occurs before Authorize payment.** The norm states that authorization must happen once inventory is confirmed and **before** packaging (“Authorize payment … should only occur … before packaging”; “Package items should only occur after authorization”).  
- Consequently, items are packaged without confirmed payment approval, breaking the logical dependency between authorization and packaging.

### Case 3
**Sequence:** Receive  Check  Authorize  **Cancel**  **Ship**  Close  
**Verdict:** **Violation** (multiple breaches).  
1. **Contradictory branching:** After Check, the process should branch *either* to the fulfillment chain (Authorize  Package  Ship) *or* directly to Cancel. Here both Authorize and Cancel occur. Since cancellation implies insufficient inventory, authorizing payment first makes no sense—payment should only be authorized when inventory is sufficient.  
2. **Ship after Cancel:** The description explicitly says it is illogical to continue with fulfillment activities (such as shipping) once an order is canceled.  
3. **Ship without Package:** Even ignoring the cancellation, Ship occurs with no preceding Package step, violating “Package items … before shipping.”  
4. **Close is correctly last, but the preceding steps are invalid.**

### Case 4
**Sequence:** Receive  Check  Cancel  Close  
**Verdict:** **Correct** (insufficient-inventory path).  
The process branches immediately to cancellation after the inventory check and ends with Close, with no improper fulfillment activities.

### Case 5
**Sequence:** Receive  Check  Authorize  **Ship**  Close  
**Verdict:** **Violation.**  
- **Missing “Package items”:** The norm requires that “only after items have been properly packaged is it appropriate to proceed with Ship order.” Here Ship follows Authorize directly, skipping packaging entirely.  
- **Ordering breach:** Because Package is absent, Ship does not occur after Package, violating the fulfillment chain.
