# Process Mining & Optimization Strategy: Speedy Parcels

As a Process Mining Consultant specializing in transportation and logistics, I have formulated a comprehensive roadmap to analyze Speedy Parcels' last-mile delivery operations. By leveraging the multi-source event data, we can unearth hidden inefficiencies, isolate root causes of delays and escalating costs, and deploy sustainable data-driven optimizations.

---

## 1. Process Discovery and Conformance Checking

### Data Preprocessing and Integration
To create a cohesive event log, we must harmonize heterogeneous data sources into a standardized format (such as XES or a structured tabular event log) using a unified **Case ID** (e.g., `Vehicle-Day`, like `V12-20241205`, and a secondary correlation using `Package ID` for granular parcel lifecycle tracking).

*   **Data Alignment:** We will map timestamps sequentially. Dispatch data establishes the baseline plan; scanner events define milestone activities; GPS trackers supply continuous telemetry (speed, location) that can be transformed into lifecycle events (e.g., "Low Speed Detected", "Unscheduled Stop"); and maintenance logs append lifecycle states for vehicle health.
*   **Challenges:** 
    *   *Granularity Mismatch:* GPS data pings every few seconds, whereas scanner events occur sporadically at stops. We must aggregate or filter high-frequency GPS logs (e.g., turning continuous tracking into state events like "Idling" or "In Transit") to prevent process models from becoming cluttered.
    *   *Data Quality & Synchronization:* Clock drift between handheld scanners, vehicle trackers, and dispatch servers requires time-zone normalization and timestamp synchronization.
    *   *Missing Data:* GPS signal loss in urban canyons requires spatial imputation to correctly associate coordinates with customer addresses.

### Process Discovery
Using process discovery algorithms (such as the Inductive Miner or Heuristics Miner) on the integrated event log, we will visualize the **As-Is process**. 

Unlike a clean flowchart, the discovered model will expose the messy reality: depot departure $\rightarrow$ transit $\rightarrow$ unmapped loops $\rightarrow$ customer arrival $\rightarrow$ successful scan (or unexpected "Delivery Failed" $\rightarrow$ extra transit loop) $\rightarrow$ unscheduled maintenance detours $\rightarrow$ depot return. Frequency and performance annotations will highlight the most common paths (variants) and where time is inherently lost.

### Conformance Checking
By applying conformance checking techniques (token-based replay or alignment-based analysis), we will compare the *As-Is* discovered model against the *To-Be* model derived from the *Planned Routes* in the Dispatch System. 

We will search for three primary deviations:
1.  **Sequence Deviations:** Drivers bypassing planned stop sequences, visiting stops out of order, or returning to zones already covered.
2.  **Unplanned Stops:** Unauthorized or prolonged stops detected via GPS (e.g., extended idling, unlogged breaks, or engine warning light incidents).
3.  **Timing & Window Deviations:** Significant variance between planned arrival times at customer windows and actual scanner-recorded arrival times.

## 2. Performance Analysis and Bottleneck Identification

### Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) & Calculation Methodologies
To measure operational success, we will calculate the following KPIs directly from the event log:
*   **On-Time Delivery Rate:** Percentage of packages where `Arrive Customer` $\le$ Customer Requested Time Window end.
*   **Average Time per Delivery Stop:** Time elapsed between `Arrive Customer` and `Depart Customer` (Service Time).
*   **Travel Time vs. Service Time Ratio:** Total duration spent moving (GPS transit states) divided by total duration spent servicing stops.
*   **Fuel Consumption per km/package:** Derived by correlating GPS distance/speed profiles and vehicle load data with maintenance/fuel logs.
*   **Vehicle Utilization Rate:** Ratio of active delivery hours versus total shift hours or scheduled maintenance windows.
*   **Frequency/Duration of Traffic Delays:** Count and duration of events where GPS speed drops significantly below speed limits along known transit corridors.
*   **Rate of Failed Deliveries:** Percentage of `Delivery Failed` events relative to total delivery attempts, segmented by reason codes (e.g., "Customer Not Home").

### Bottleneck Identification Techniques
Using **Performance Social Network Analysis** and **Bottleneck Mining** (filtering cases by duration and highlighting high-latency edges in the process map), we will isolate bottlenecks. 
*   We will cross-filter bottlenecks by **time of day** (e.g., morning rush hour traffic bottlenecks), **geographical zones** (urban high-density vs. suburban sprawl), **drivers**, and **vehicle types**. 
*   We will quantify the impact by calculating the *waiting time* and *excess duration* introduced by these bottlenecks, translating them directly into added labor hours and excess fuel burn.

## 3. Root Cause Analysis for Inefficiencies

To move beyond symptoms, we will execute advanced process mining analytics:
*   **Variant Analysis:** We will segregate the top 10% performing routes from the bottom 10%. Decision tree mining (e.g., using process mining classification tools like *performance spectrums*) will reveal structural differences—such as whether poor performance stems from high stop density, poor initial sequencing, or specific driver behaviors.
*   **Traffic & Dwell Time Correlation:** By mapping GPS low-speed events against GIS traffic data, we can prove whether late deliveries are caused by external traffic congestion or inefficient internal depot loading processes.
*   **Service Time Variability:** Analyzing the duration between `Arrive Customer` and `Delivery Success` will expose if delays are driven by parking difficulties (long transit from parking spot to door) or cumbersome scanning/signature procedures.
*   **Maintenance Correlation:** We will correlate unpredicted maintenance events (from maintenance logs) with vehicle age, mileage, and specific drivers to see if driving styles accelerate component wear and tear.
*   **Failed Delivery Root Causes:** Analyzing the sequence *prior* to a `Delivery Failed` event will determine if failed deliveries cluster on specific routes where time-window tightening forces premature arrivals when customers are absent.

## 4. Data-Driven Optimization Strategies

Based on the insights gathered, I propose three targeted, data-driven optimization strategies:

### Strategy 1: Dynamic Re-Sequencing & Traffic-Aware Routing
*   **Targeted Inefficiency:** Excessive travel time, high fuel consumption, and traffic-induced delays.
*   **Root Cause:** Suboptimal static route planning that fails to adapt to real-time urban traffic congestion patterns.
*   **Process Mining Support:** Process maps will clearly highlight transit edges with extreme latency during specific windows. 
*   **Expected Impact:** Reduces *Travel Time vs. Service Time ratio* by 15%, cuts fuel consumption per package, and directly improves *On-Time Delivery Rate*.

### Strategy 2: Predictive Maintenance Scheduling
*   **Targeted Inefficiency:** Unscheduled repair stops during shifts, leading to route abandonment, package delays, and costly roadside assistance.
*   **Root Cause:** Reactive maintenance policies where vehicles are only serviced after breakdown or scheduled on rigid, mileage-agnostic intervals.
*   **Process Mining Support:** Event logs capture early indicators—such as recurring "Unscheduled Stop" events, low-speed warnings, and operational strain profiles per Vehicle ID.
*   **Expected Impact:** Decreases unpredicted vehicle breakdowns by 30%, maximizes *Vehicle Utilization Rate*, and reduces emergency maintenance overhead.

### Strategy 3: Proactive Customer Communication & Time-Window Management
*   **Targeted Inefficiency:** High rate of failed delivery attempts (`Delivery Failed: Customer Not Home`), forcing costly re-delivery loops.
*   **Root Cause:** Static delivery windows with poor customer visibility, leading to customers being unavailable when the van arrives.
*   **Process Mining Support:** Variant analysis will show that cases with failed deliveries have significantly longer overall cycle times and double-handling costs.
*   **Expected Impact:** Lowers the *Rate of Failed Deliveries* by 25%, drastically reduces unnecessary mileage, and enhances customer satisfaction scores.

## 5. Considering Operational Constraints and Monitoring

### Accounting for Operational Constraints
Every optimization strategy must operate within strict real-world boundaries:
*   **Driver Working Hours:** Routing algorithms and dynamic schedules will hard-code maximum shift lengths and mandatory break regulations (preventing forced overtime log entries like those seen in the sample data).
*   **Vehicle Capacities:** Volume and weight constraints from the dispatch system will act as hard boundaries during route optimization to prevent overloading.
*   **Customer Time Windows:** Delivery sequencing algorithms will treat time windows as weighted constraints, minimizing penalty costs for early or late arrivals rather than treating them as suggestions.

### Continuous Monitoring and Dashboarding
To ensure sustainability, we will deploy a real-time process mining monitoring dashboard (e.g., via Celonis, Apromore, or custom BI integrations) tracking the following:

*   **Live Operational Dashboard (Operational View):**
    *   Real-time conformance monitor: Flags active routes deviating >15 minutes from the planned path.
    *   Active vehicle status: Monitoring ongoing transit vs. service stop times.
*   **Strategic KPI Dashboard (Management View):**
    *   Weekly *On-Time Delivery Rate* trends.
    *   Average service time and parking/dwell time per zone.
    *   Cost-to-serve metrics (Fuel burn per completed delivery, maintenance downtime frequency).
*   **Anomaly Alerting System:** Automated triggers if a specific driver, vehicle, or route repeatedly exhibits variant patterns associated with high failure or delay rates, enabling proactive management intervention before customer complaints arise.
