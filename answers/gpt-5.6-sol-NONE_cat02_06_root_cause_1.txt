Cycle time is measured from **Receive Ticket** to **Close Ticket**.

| Case | Total cycle time | Escalated? | Assessment |

The two fast cases average about **1h 48m**. Cases **102, 104, and 105** are therefore clear performance outliers, with **105** being the most severe.

## 2. Likely root causes

### Case 102 — escalation and delayed investigation/resolution

Important waiting periods:

- Assignment  escalation: **2h 30m**
- Escalation  investigation: **2h 30m**
- Investigation  resolution: **19h**
- Resolution  closure: **15m**

The largest bottleneck occurs after investigation. Escalation also introduces a substantial handoff delay: five hours pass between Level-1 assignment and the Level-2 investigation.

**Likely causes:** late escalation, Level-2 queue or availability constraints, and delayed follow-up after investigation.

---

### Case 104 — long Level-1 queues despite no escalation

Important waiting periods:

- Receipt  triage: **40m**
- Assignment  investigation: **3h 30m**
- Investigation  resolution: **19h**
- Resolution  closure: **30m**

This case shows that escalation is not the only source of delay. The ticket waits a long time for Level-1 investigation and then remains unresolved overnight.

**Likely causes:** agent workload or queue congestion, slow initial handling, insufficient capacity, or a delay in acting on investigation findings.

---

### Case 105 — severe escalation bottleneck

Important waiting periods:

- Assignment  first investigation: **10m**
- First investigation  escalation: **50m**
- Escalation  second investigation: **28h**
- Second investigation  resolution: **19h**
- Resolution  closure: **30m**

The dominant issue is the **28-hour wait after escalation**. Although the escalation decision itself is made relatively quickly, the Level-2 team does not begin its investigation until the following day. Another 19 hours pass before resolution.

**Likely causes:** Level-2 backlog or limited staffing, poor handoff coordination, lack of escalation priority, and delayed action after specialist investigation.

## 3. Overall patterns and recommendations

### Main patterns

1. **Escalation is strongly associated with longer cycle time.**  
   The escalated cases take approximately **25 hours** and **49 hours**, compared with under three hours for the normal fast cases.

2. **Escalation alone does not explain every delay.**  
   Case 104 takes more than 24 hours without escalation, indicating Level-1 capacity or workflow issues.

3. **Most delay is waiting time rather than closure administration.**  
   Closing takes only 15–30 minutes. The major delays occur:
   - before investigation,
   - after escalation,
   - and between investigation and resolution.

4. **Overnight waits materially increase elapsed cycle time.**  
   Cases 102, 104, and 105 cross one or more day boundaries. This may indicate restricted support hours or unresolved work being deferred to the next day.

### Recommendations

- **Set stage-level service targets**, such as maximum time from assignment to investigation, escalation to Level-2 acceptance, and investigation to resolution.
- **Monitor queue aging**, with alerts for tickets waiting beyond thresholds.
- **Introduce explicit escalation ownership.** A Level-2 agent should acknowledge and accept escalated tickets promptly rather than leaving them in an unowned queue.
- **Improve triage and early routing** so specialist issues are sent directly to Level-2 where appropriate, avoiding unnecessary Level-1 delay.
- **Review Level-2 staffing and availability**, especially because Case 105 shows a 28-hour specialist queue.
- **Investigate overnight handoff practices and operating hours.** Add on-call coverage or prioritized next-shift handling for high-impact tickets where justified.
- **Track waiting time separately from active work time.** Metrics should identify whether delays arise from workload, staffing, dependencies, or technical complexity.
- **Prioritize post-investigation action.** The repeated 19-hour gap before resolution in Cases 102 and 104, plus the same gap in Case 105 after its second investigation, suggests a systematic bottleneck rather than an isolated incident.

Overall, **Case 105 is the highest-priority problem**, followed by **102 and 104**. The primary improvement opportunity is reducing queue and handoff delays around investigation and escalation, not optimizing the final closure step.
