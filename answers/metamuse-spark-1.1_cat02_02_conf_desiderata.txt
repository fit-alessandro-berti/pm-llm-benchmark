Here is a conformance analysis of the log against the two levels of constraints:

### 1. Normative Violations - Strict Rules

The normative model is:

> `Application Received -> Credit Check -> Risk Assessment -> Loan Decision -> Inform Applicant -> [IF Approved: Finalize Documentation] -> Close Case`

| Case | Normative Compliant? | Explanation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Case 1** | **YES - Fully Compliant** | Follows the exact required sequence for an Approved loan. No steps skipped, no order reversed, no forbidden action.  |
| **Case 2** | **NO - Severe Violations** | Has **3 distinct normative violations**: <br> **1. Ordering Violation:** `Risk Assessment [08:20]` occurs BEFORE `Credit Check [09:00]`. This violates rule: *Credit Check must occur before Risk Assessment*. <br> **2. Skipped Mandatory Activity:** `Inform Applicant` is completely missing. The model requires `Inform Applicant` after *any* Loan Decision and before Close Case. <br> **3. Forbidden Action / Decision Logic Violation:** `Finalize Documentation [10:00]` occurs after `Loan Decision (Rejected) [09:30]`. Rule: *If Rejected -> close without finalization. Finalize Documentation after rejection is explicitly forbidden.* |
| **Case 3** | **YES - Fully Compliant** | From a control-flow perspective, the sequence is correct: Application -> Credit Check -> Risk Assessment -> Approved -> Inform Applicant -> Finalize Documentation -> Close Case. No strict rules are broken. |

**Interpretation of Case 2:** This is not a timing issue, it is a true non-compliant execution. Possible explanations: human error / work-around where an assessor started risk evaluation prematurely, system logging error where credit check was actually done earlier but logged late, or lack of enforcement in the IT system. Finalizing documentation after rejection suggests a misunderstanding of procedure or automatic triggering of the finalization sub-process regardless of decision outcome.

### 2. Desiderata Violations - Recommended Best Practices

These are NOT severe violations. They do not make a case non-compliant, they only indicate suboptimal performance.

Desiderata defined:
*   D1: Credit Check within 24h of Application Received
*   D2: Inform Applicant within 48h of Loan Decision
*   D3: Perform a Follow-up Call before Close Case

| Case | Desiderata Compliant? | Explanation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Case 1** | **Largely Compliant / Minor deviation** | D1: PASS - 30 min delay. D2: PASS - 2 hours delay. D3: FAIL - No Follow-up Call logged. This is acceptable, but represents a missed opportunity for service quality. |
| **Case 2** | **Not evaluated / Shadowed** | While it also fails D3 and misses Inform Applicant, the normative violations are so severe that desiderata analysis is secondary. A non-compliant case cannot be considered good even if timing was fast. |
| **Case 3** | **NO - Multiple Desiderata Violations** | D1: FAIL - Application 2024-01-12 09:00 -> Credit Check 2024-01-14 10:00 = ~49 hours > 24h. <br> D2: FAIL - Loan Decision 2024-01-14 12:00 -> Inform Applicant 2024-01-16 15:00 = ~51 hours > 48h. <br> D3: FAIL - No Follow-up Call. <br> **This case is still normatively VALID.** These delays are not serious from a compliance standpoint. |

**Interpretation / Justification of Case 3:** 
The behavior is perfectly legal but suboptimal. Plausible, non-malicious reasons:

*   The 49h delay for Credit Check from Jan 12 to Jan 14 spans a weekend / high workload / external dependency on credit bureau being unavailable.
*   The 51h delay in informing applicant could be due to applicant being unreachable, or batch processing of notifications, or waiting for a manager review before sending communication.
*   The absence of Follow-up Call is, per definition, allowed. It may be that workload was high and the agent prioritized closing cases, or that follow-up was done via email and not logged as a call event.

**Summary:**

*   **Normative failure = Case 2 only.** It must be flagged for corrective action.
*   **Desiderata failure = Case 3 is the clearest example, and Case 1 and 3 both miss the optional Follow-up Call.** These should be flagged for performance improvement, not for compliance breach.
