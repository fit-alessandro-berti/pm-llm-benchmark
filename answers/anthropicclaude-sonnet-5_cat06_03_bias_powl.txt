# Bias Analysis: The Local Affiliation XOR Branch

## Identifying the Mechanism

The XOR construct `xor_local_check = X(D, skip)` creates a structural fork where applicants are split into two populations:

- **Path 1**: Applicant goes through `CheckLocalAffiliation`  receives potential "score uplift"
- **Path 2**: Applicant skips this step entirely  no uplift possible

The critical fairness issue is **not** the existence of local affiliation as a factor, but *how the XOR choice is determined* and *what happens structurally when it fires*.

## Why This Is a Subtle Bias Vector

**1. Asymmetric opportunity, not asymmetric criteria**
The model doesn't score against non-local applicants directly — it simply never gives them the *chance* to gain the uplift. This is more insidious than explicit discrimination because:
- It looks neutral in an audit ("we don't penalize anyone")
- It fails equal *opportunity*, not equal *treatment on paper*

**2. Proxy discrimination risk**
"Local resident + member of a known community group" is very likely to correlate with:
- Race/ethnicity (segregated neighborhoods, insular community networks)
- National origin (immigrant enclaves vs. established local ties)
- Socioeconomic history (long-term residency requires stability/generational wealth)

Even though "local affiliation" is *not itself* a legally protected class, it can act as a **proxy variable** that reintroduces protected-class bias through a back door — this is the classic disparate impact problem in fair lending law (e.g., ECOA, Fair Housing Act in the US).

**3. Where does the XOR decision come from?**
The POWL model is silent on this — and that silence is the danger. In practice, this branch could be triggered by:
- An automated rule (e.g., zip code lookup)  **direct proxy for redlining**
- Human discretion (loan officer decides who "seems local")  **subjective, unaudited bias**
- Self-disclosure  advantages applicants who *know* to mention affiliation, correlating with social capital

## Downstream Impact on Fairness

```
A  loop(B,G)  C  [D or skip]  E  F
                      
              Silent scoring asymmetry compounds
              into ManualReview (E) and FinalDecision (F)
```

Because `D` feeds into `ManualReview` and ultimately `FinalDecision`, any uplift is **not contained** — it propagates through the entire remaining process. Two applicants with identical financial profiles could receive different final terms (interest rate, approval) solely based on whether they took the `D` branch.

## Recommendations

1. **Make the XOR condition transparent and auditable** — log why each applicant took path D vs. skip.
2. **Test for disparate impact**: stratify outcomes by protected-class proxies (race, national origin) to see if `D` selection correlates with them.
3. **Consider removing the discretionary skip** — either apply `D` uniformly to all applicants (equal opportunity) or remove it as a scoring factor entirely if it can't be shown to be a legitimate, non-proxy credit risk indicator.
4. **If kept, cap the "uplift" magnitude** and require it to be independently justified by traditional credit factors, not used as a tiebreaker that compounds unfairly in borderline cases reaching `ManualReview`.

**Bottom line**: The POWL structure exposes a real governance gap — a process-modeling choice (XOR with skip) that *silently encodes* who gets a second look at their community ties, and this omission-based unfairness is exactly the kind of pattern regulators flag as disparate impact even absent explicit discriminatory intent.
