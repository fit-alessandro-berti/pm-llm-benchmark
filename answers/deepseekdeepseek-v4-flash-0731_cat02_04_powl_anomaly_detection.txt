The intended Order-to-Cash flow should be:

**Receive_Order  Check_Credit  Approve_Order  Issue_Invoice  Receive_Payment  Close_Order**

The provided POWL model deviates from this in two important places.

## 1. Credit check can be skipped

```python
xor_credit = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[C, skip])
```

This creates an **XOR-choice between `Check_Credit` and a silent transition**.

The silent transition means the process can go from `Receive_Order` directly to the approval/invoice block without doing `Check_Credit`. This is a workaround for the credit-checking policy.

**Why it matters:**  
Skipping credit checks is financially risky. An order could be approved and invoiced even when the customer has poor credit, leading to unpaid invoices, higher credit risk, and bad debt.

---

## 2. Approval and invoicing are placed in an unbounded loop

```python
loop_approve_invoice = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP, children=[A, I])
```

This wraps `Approve_Order` and `Issue_Invoice` in a **LOOP**.

In POWL loop semantics, the first child is the body, and the second child is the loop-back/redo part. So this loop permits **repeated approve/invoice cycles** instead of enforcing exactly:

```text
Approve_Order  Issue_Invoice  Receive_Payment
```

Depending on the exact POWL interpretation, this can lead to:

- multiple approvals for one order,
- multiple invoices being issued for one order,
- possible exits after approval, meaning an invoice is not guaranteed,
- approval/invoice steps being repeated before payment is received.

**Why it matters:**  
In a normal Order-to-Cash process, an order should be approved once and invoiced once before payment. The loop allows duplicate invoices, repeated approvals, and confusing process behavior. This can create inefficiencies, duplicate financial documents, customer dissatisfaction, and even increased fraud risk.

The model now accepts non-compliant traces such as:
