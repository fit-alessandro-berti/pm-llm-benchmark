## 1. Normative Hire-to-Retire order

For a successful hire, a typical ordering is:

```text
Post Job Advertisement
     Screen Candidates
     Conduct Interviews
     Make Hiring Decision
     Onboard Employee
     Add Employee to Payroll
     Close Case
```

In partial-order terms, the important precedence relations are:

```text
Post < Screen < Interview < Decide < Onboard < Payroll < Close
```

The node order in the Python list does not itself impose precedence. Only the edges in `model.order` do so.

---

## 2. Analysis of Model 1

The relevant relations are:

```text
Post < Screen
Screen < Decide
Screen < Interview
Decide < Onboard < Payroll < Close
```

### Correct aspects

- The job is posted before screening.
- Screening occurs before both the interview and the hiring decision.
- The decision precedes onboarding.
- Onboarding precedes payroll registration.
- The case is closed only after payroll registration.
- All activities are mandatory and occur once; there is no possibility of silently skipping payroll or repeatedly onboarding someone.

### Anomaly

There is no relation:

```text
Interview < Decide
```

Consequently, `Interview` and `Decide` are incomparable in the partial order. This allows traces such as:

```text
Post  Screen  Decide  Onboard  Payroll  Close  Interview
```

or:

```text
Post  Screen  Decide  Interview  Onboard  Payroll  Close
```

Thus, the hiring decision may be made before the interview, or the interview may even occur after the case has been closed. This is a significant sequencing anomaly because a hiring decision should normally take the interview results into account.

The model could be corrected by adding:

```python
model1.order.add_edge(Interview, Decide)
```

After that addition, the model would closely represent the normative successful-hire sequence.

---

## 3. Analysis of Model 2

The partial-order relations outside the operators are:

```text
Post < Screen
Post < Interview < Decide
Decide < loop_onboarding < xor_payroll < Close
```

### Correct aspects

- The job is posted before the interview.
- The interview precedes the hiring decision.
- The decision precedes onboarding.
- Payroll, when selected, occurs after onboarding and before closing.

### Anomalies

#### a. Screening is not required before the interview or decision

There is no edge from `Screen` to `Interview` or from `Screen` to `Decide`.

Therefore, a valid trace could be:

```text
Post  Interview  Decide  Onboard  Payroll  Close  Screen
```

The screening activity is mandatory as a node, but its lack of ordering relations means it can occur very late—even after the case is closed. This violates the normal candidate-evaluation logic.

#### b. Onboarding can repeat

```python
loop_onboarding = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.LOOP,
    children=[Onboard, skip]
)
```

This allows `Onboard_Employee` to be executed repeatedly. Rework or repeated onboarding might be valid in a specialized process, but as modeled there is no business condition explaining why the same employee would be onboarded multiple times. It can therefore cause duplicate or inconsistent employee records.

#### c. Payroll can be skipped

```python
xor_payroll = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[Payroll, skip]
)
```

This allows either:

```text
Add_to_Payroll
```

or a silent transition that bypasses payroll entirely. Hence, the process can close after onboarding without adding the new employee to payroll. This is a fundamental integrity problem in a Hire-to-Retire process: an employee may be onboarded but not registered for payment.

The XOR is also not an appropriate representation of a rejected candidate. A rejection branch should occur at the hiring decision and should avoid onboarding altogether, rather than onboarding someone and then optionally omitting payroll.

---

## 4. Which model is closer to the normative process?

**Model 1 is closer overall.**

Model 1 has one primary sequencing defect: it fails to require the interview before the hiring decision. However, it still:

- performs all core activities,
- requires payroll registration,
- prevents repeated onboarding,
- and closes the case only after payroll.

Model 2 gets the interview-to-decision ordering right, but introduces several broader problems:

- screening is disconnected from the decision path,
- onboarding may repeat,
- payroll is optional,
- and the case may close without payroll registration.

The payroll omission is particularly serious because it violates a central business requirement of hiring an employee. Therefore, although Model 1 needs the additional relation `Interview < Decide`, it requires a much smaller correction than Model 2.

Both models also represent only a successful-hire path. If a complete Hire-to-Retire model must represent rejected candidates, a separate decision branch would be needed so that a negative decision can terminate or close the case without onboarding and payroll.
