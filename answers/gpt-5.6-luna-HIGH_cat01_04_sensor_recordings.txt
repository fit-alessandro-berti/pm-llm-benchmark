## Detailed Interpretation

1. **Initial standby, Events 1–3**  
   The machine is not performing a material-processing operation. The zero vibration, zero material flow, and stationary tool indicate an idle or preparation state.

2. **Cutting, Events 4–6**  
   The simultaneous increase in temperature, vibration, tool position, material flow, and energy indicates forceful contact with material. The increasing tool position suggests motion through the workpiece, supporting a cutting or machining interpretation.

3. **Assembly, Events 7–8**  
   The machine remains active, but the readings are lower and stable. The material flow is reduced and the tool holds a fixed position, suggesting placement or assembly of components rather than cutting.

4. **Welding, Event 9**  
   The extreme temperature and energy spike, together with high vibration and no material flow, indicate a brief, concentrated operation. Welding or another thermal joining process is the most plausible interpretation.

5. **Inspection, Event 10**  
   The process cools rapidly and mechanical activity nearly stops. The fixed tool position and low vibration suggest that the completed item is being checked or measured.

6. **Packaging or transfer, Events 11–12**  
   A small amount of flow and a new tool position indicate gentle handling or movement of the finished item. The low temperature and vibration distinguish this phase from cutting and welding.

7. **Cycle completion, Events 13–15**  
   The return to the original sensor profile indicates that the production cycle has ended and the machine has reset for the next item.

Because the log contains only sensor snapshots and no ground-truth activity labels, these assignments are inferred rather than definitive. The strongest identifications are the cutting phase, due to its sustained high vibration and material flow, and the welding phase, due to its sharp temperature and energy peak.
