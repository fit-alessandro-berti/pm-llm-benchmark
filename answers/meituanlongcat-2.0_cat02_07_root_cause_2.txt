### 1. Identification of Cases with Performance Issues

To identify performance issues, we first calculate the total duration of each case (from "Submit Claim" to "Close Claim"):

*   **Case 2001:** 1.5 hours (Low complexity, Region A)
*   **Case 2004:** 1.25 hours (Low complexity, Region B)
*   **Case 2002:** ~26 hours / 1.1 days (Medium complexity, Region B)
*   **Case 2003:** ~48 hours / 2 days (High complexity, Region A)
*   **Case 2005:** ~77 hours / 3.2 days (High complexity, Region B)

**Findings:** 
Cases 2001 and 2004 are completed swiftly (within ~1.5 hours). Cases 2002, 2003, and 2005 exhibit significant performance issues, taking 1 to 3 days to complete. Case 2005 is the worst performer, taking over 3 days.

---

### 2. Root Cause Analysis by Attribute

By cross-referencing the attributes with the case durations, we can pinpoint the root causes of the delays.

#### A. Complexity Level (Primary Driver)
*   **Observation:** There is a direct correlation between complexity and duration. Low-complexity claims (2001, 2004) bypass the "Request Additional Documents" step entirely, resulting in 1.5-hour turnarounds. Medium-complexity claims (2002) require one document request, tripling the duration. High-complexity claims (2003, 2005) require multiple document requests, extending the process to days.
*   **Root Cause:** High-complexity claims inherently require more back-and-forth. However, the *number* of requests varies. Case 2003 required 2 requests, while Case 2005 required 3 requests, indicating that high complexity is sometimes mishandled, requiring redundant requests.

#### B. Region (Secondary Driver)
*   **Observation:** Comparing similar complexities across regions reveals a disparity. For Low complexity, Region A (1.5h) and Region B (1.25h) perform equally well. However, for High complexity, Region A (Case 2003: 48h) performs significantly better than Region B (Case 2005: 77h). 
*   **Root Cause:** Region B struggles specifically with high-complexity claims. The delay is not uniform across all work types, suggesting a localized operational issue in Region B when dealing with complex tasks.

#### C. Resource (Tertiary Driver - The Bottleneck)
*   **Observation:** Looking at the "Request Additional Documents" phase reveals a stark resource bottleneck:
    *   **Adjuster_Mike (Region A):** In Case 2003, he issued two document requests on the *same day* (11:00 and 17:00), a 6-hour gap.
    *   **Adjuster_Lisa (Region B):** In Case 2005, she issued three document requests with massive gaps between them: 11:30 (Day 1) to 17:00 (Day 2) is a 29.5-hour gap; 17:00 (Day 2) to 15:00 (Day 3) is a 22-hour gap.
*   **Root Cause:** Adjuster_Lisa is a major bottleneck for Region B. The long intervals between document requests suggest she is either overloaded with work, procrastinating on complex claims, or waiting on customers and failing to follow up promptly. 
*   **Secondary Resource Bottleneck (Managers):** Manager_Ann (who handles Low/Medium complexity) approves claims quickly. Manager_Bill (who handles High complexity) takes 9 hours (Case 2003) and 17 hours (Case 2005) to approve claims after the final document request, adding further delay to complex cases.

### 3. Proposed Explanations and Mitigation Strategies

#### Explanation 1: The "Multiple Request" Spiral in High Complexity
High-complexity claims require more documentation, but the process lacks a structured checklist. Adjusters may request documents in batches as they realize they need more information, rather than requesting everything at once. This creates a spiral of waiting for the customer, reviewing, and requesting more.

*   **Mitigation Strategy:** Implement a **standardized document checklist** based on claim complexity. For High complexity claims, the system should require the adjuster to request *all* anticipated additional documents during the first "Request Additional Documents" event. This reduces the process from 3 request cycles to 1, potentially cutting days off the timeline.

#### Explanation 2: Resource Inefficiency and Workload in Region B
Adjuster_Lisa's 22-29 hour gaps between document requests in Case 2005 indicate she is not actively working on the claim during those times. She may be prioritizing low-complexity claims (like 2004, which she handled quickly) over complex ones, or she may be experiencing burnout/workload issues specific to Region B.

*   **Mitigation Strategy:** 
    *   Implement **SLA timers** for follow-ups. If an adjuster requests documents and receives them, the system should flag the claim if no action is taken within 4 business hours.
    *   **Workload Balancing:** Redistribute high-complexity claims if an adjuster's queue is full. Consider pairing junior adjusters (like Lisa) with senior adjustors for complex cases to prevent them from getting stuck.

#### Explanation 3: Managerial Bottlenecks for Complex Approvals
Manager_Bill takes significantly longer to approve high-complexity claims than Manager_Ann takes to approve low/medium ones. This could be because complex claims require deeper scrutiny, or because Manager_Bill has a bottleneck in his approval queue.

*   **Mitigation Strategy:** 
    *   **Parallel Processing:** Allow Finance to pre-process payment details while the claim is awaiting managerial approval, so that once Manager_Bill approves, "Pay Claim" can happen instantly.
    *   **Delegation/Training:** Train Manager_Ann to handle certain high-complexity claims, or empower senior adjusters to approve claims under a certain dollar threshold without manager sign-off, reducing the burden on Manager_Bill.
