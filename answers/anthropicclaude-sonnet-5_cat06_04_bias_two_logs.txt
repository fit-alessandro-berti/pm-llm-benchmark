# Bias Analysis: Group A vs. Group B Event Logs

## Summary Finding
**Group B's log exhibits systematic bias favoring applicants with the `LocalResident = TRUE` and `CommunityGroup = "Highland Civic Darts Club"` attributes**, through a hidden "Community Boost" mechanism embedded in the `ScoreAdjustment` field. This constitutes a proxy discrimination pattern that indirectly disadvantages Group A (the Protected Group).

---

## How the Bias Manifests

### 1. **Structural Asymmetry in the Process Itself**
- In Group A, `ScoreAdjustment` is consistently `0` — no adjustment mechanism is ever applied, regardless of case.
- In Group B, cases with `CommunityGroup = Highland Civic Darts Club` (U001, U003) receive a **+10 "Community Boost"** applied at the `PreliminaryScoring` step, propagating through `ManualReview` and `FinalDecision`.
- This means **an entire scoring pathway (the Boost) is only reachable by Group B members with local/community affiliations** — Group A applicants have no equivalent mechanism available to them, even hypothetically.

### 2. **CommunityGroup as a Proxy Variable**
- `CommunityGroup` correlates perfectly with `LocalResident = TRUE`, which correlates perfectly with Group B membership.
- Since Group A is defined by `LocalResident = FALSE` and `CommunityGroup = None`, **no member of the Protected Group can ever qualify for the Boost** — not due to merit, but due to structural exclusion from the attribute that triggers it.
- This makes `CommunityGroup` a proxy for group membership itself, embedding bias directly into the scoring logic rather than through overt discrimination.

### 3. **Impact on Borderline Cases — Outcome Manipulation**
Compare the two rejected/marginal cases:

| Case | Raw Score | Adjustment | Final Score | Decision |
|------|-----------|-----------|--------------|----------|
| P002 (Group A) | 710 | 0 | 710 | **Rejected** |
| U002 (Group B) | 710 | 0 | 710 | **Rejected** (no CommunityGroup) |
| U003 (Group B) | **695** | +10 | **705** | **Approved** |

This is the critical finding: **U003 had a lower raw PreliminaryScore (695) than P002 (710), yet was approved**, while P002 was rejected. The only differentiating factor is the Community Boost — not creditworthiness, income, or any legitimate risk factor. This demonstrates that the adjustment mechanism can **flip a decision outcome** for Group B members who would otherwise be borderline or rejected, while Group A has no path to a similar reversal.

### 4. **Consistent Direction of Adjustment**
- The Boost is *only* positive (+10) and *only* applied to Group B.
- There is no equivalent adjustment (positive or negative) ever applied to Group A, meaning Group A's outcomes are strictly a function of raw `PreliminaryScore`, while Group B's outcomes benefit from an additional, non-transparent lever.

---

## Why This Constitutes Bias (Not Just Correlation)

1. **Disparate Mechanism Availability**: The process itself (not just outcomes) differs between groups — Group B has an extra scoring stage effectively unavailable to Group A.
2. **Proxy Discrimination**: `CommunityGroup` membership functions as a stand-in for `LocalResident`/Group B identity, allowing indirect discrimination even if `LocalResident` itself isn't used directly in the final decision rule.
3. **Outcome Reversal Evidence**: U003 (695  Approved) vs. P002 (710  Rejected) is direct evidence that identical or better-qualified Group A applicants receive worse outcomes than lower-scoring Group B applicants, purely due to group-correlated attributes.
4. **Lack of Transparency/Justification**: No independent business/legitimate risk rationale is provided in the log for why "Community Group" membership should adjust credit/eligibility scores — this looks like a fairness-washing or affinity-based adjustment rather than a risk-based one.

---

## Conclusion
The **Group B log demonstrates bias in favor of the Unprotected Group**, achieved through the `ScoreAdjustment` field tied to `CommunityGroup` membership (itself tied to `LocalResident`). This creates a systematic advantage that can alter final decisions (as seen in U003), while Group A applicants are evaluated solely on unadjusted scores — creating unequal treatment that disadvantages the Protected Group even though `LocalResident`/`CommunityGroup` are not overtly used as decision variables in the final `Decision` field.
