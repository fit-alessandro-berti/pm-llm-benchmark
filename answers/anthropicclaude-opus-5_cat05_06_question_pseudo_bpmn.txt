# 20 Open-Ended Questions on the Multinational Smartphone Supply Chain Process

## Rationale Behind the Process Design

1. The model places market research and R&D prototyping in strict sequence before any sourcing begins. What is the rationale for this fully sequential front end, and under what competitive conditions would overlapping (concurrent engineering) or even initiating long-lead component negotiations during prototyping create more value than it destroys in rework?

2. The four sourcing streams are modeled as a true parallel split-and-join, meaning shipment to Malaysia cannot begin until *every* component category is secured. Is this "wait for all" semantics an accurate reflection of reality, or does it mask a more nuanced pattern in which partial shipments, staged deliveries, or buffer inventories decouple the suppliers from one another?

3. Why might the process concentrate all assembly in a single Malaysian plant rather than distributing it regionally near the four distribution centers? What hidden assumptions about labor cost, tariff structures, and demand volatility does this centralization encode, and when would those assumptions break?

4. The quality gateway routes failures back to "Assemble Smartphones," implying that rework re-enters the main production stream. What are the implications of this loop for capacity planning, yield reporting, and traceability — and should "scrap" really share a path with "rework" when their downstream consequences differ so fundamentally?

5. Air freight and sea freight are modeled as parallel branches that must *join* before customs clearance. Given that these modes differ by weeks in transit time, is a synchronizing join defensible, or should the flow be modeled as independent, asynchronous streams that each clear customs on their own timeline?

## Potential Improvements and Redesign

6. Where in this end-to-end flow does inventory sit idle the longest, and if you could eliminate or compress exactly one wait state, which would deliver the greatest reduction in cash-to-cash cycle time without materially increasing stockout risk?

7. The final loop "Adjust Orders to Suppliers" appears only at the very end of the process, after sales have already occurred. How could demand signals be injected earlier and more continuously — and what would the process look like if it were redrawn as a closed-loop, sense-and-respond system rather than a linear push chain?

8. Quality is inspected at a single checkpoint after assembly. What would change if quality verification were distributed across incoming component inspection, in-line assembly checks, and post-pack audits, and how would you quantify the trade-off between inspection cost and the cost of late-stage defect discovery?

9. Customs clearance is modeled as one monolithic task, yet it differs enormously across the EU, North America, Asia, and Latin America. What is lost analytically by this abstraction, and how would decomposing it by jurisdiction change your view of where delays actually originate?

10. Could postponement strategies — shipping semi-finished units and completing region-specific configuration (SIM, radio bands, language, packaging, regulatory labeling) at the distribution centers — restructure this process advantageously? What new risks would that introduce?

## Risk Management and Resilience

11. Each component category is sourced from a single named geography. What is the concentration risk embedded in this design, and how would you decide which components justify the carrying cost of dual-sourcing or qualified alternates versus which can safely remain single-sourced?

12. The model contains no explicit exception paths for supplier failure, geopolitical disruption, port closure, or plant shutdown. If you were to add compensating or escalation flows, where would they attach, and what triggers and thresholds would activate them?

13. Because the parallel join forces synchronization, the slowest supplier dictates the start of shipping. How would you quantify the "cost of the critical path supplier," and what contractual or inventory mechanisms could insulate the process from that dependency?

14. What single-point-of-failure risks exist beyond the assembly plant — for example, in a single freight forwarder, a single customs broker, or a single ERP integration — and how would you make those dependencies visible in the process model itself?

15. How should the process distinguish between disruptions that warrant a tactical workaround (expedite, re-route, substitute) and those that warrant a strategic redesign (re-shore, re-qualify, exit a market), and who holds the authority to make that call?

## Decision-Making and Governance

16. The two exclusive gateways — quality pass/fail and demand stable/changed — are stated as binary. What data, decision rules, and thresholds actually drive each, who owns them, and how often are those rules revisited as products, suppliers, and markets evolve?

17. The split between air freight and sea freight is drawn as an unconditional parallel branch, but in practice it is a cost-versus-speed allocation decision. What decision logic should govern the split, and how should it flex as margins, demand signals, and fuel or freight rates change mid-cycle?

18. When capacity is constrained, four downstream channels — retail, e-commerce, VIP/pre-orders, and warehousing — compete for the same finished units. What allocation policy should arbitrate among them, and how should the process make that prioritization explicit rather than implicit?

## Stakeholder Communication and Performance Measurement

19. Which handoffs in this chain cross the greatest organizational, contractual, or cultural boundaries, and what information should flow across each one — in what format, at what cadence, and with what escalation path — to prevent the "silent delay" problem where a slip is discovered only downstream?

20. If you had to select a small set of metrics that genuinely reflect end-to-end health rather than local optimization, what would they be — and how would you guard against the classic pathology in which each function (procurement, manufacturing, logistics, sales) hits its own targets while total cycle time, fill rate, and margin deteriorate?
