## Comparison and bias assessment

The **Group B log shows preferential treatment based on attributes associated with that group**, while the comparative effect is a potential disadvantage to Group A.

### 1. Attribute differences

- Every Group A applicant has:
  - `LocalResident = FALSE`
  - `CommunityGroup = None`
  - No score adjustment

- Every Group B applicant has:
  - `LocalResident = TRUE`
  - Two applicants belong to the `Highland Civic Darts Club`
  - Those two applicants receive a `+10 Community Boost`

The adjustment is explicitly linked to `CommunityGroup`, not to the applicant’s preliminary score. Group A applicants have no corresponding opportunity to receive such a benefit.

### 2. Effect on scores and decisions

For Group B:

- **U001:** 720 becomes 730 because of the community boost, and the application is approved.
- **U002:** Has no community affiliation and receives no boost; the score remains 710 and the application is rejected.
- **U003:** A raw score of only 695 becomes 705 after the boost, and the application is approved.

This suggests that community membership can improve an applicant’s position and may allow an applicant with a lower preliminary score to receive approval. U003 is particularly important because the applicant was approved after receiving the adjustment despite having a substantially lower raw score than approved Group A cases.

### 3. Role of `LocalResident`

All Group B applicants are local residents and all Group A applicants are not. Therefore, `LocalResident` is perfectly correlated with group membership in this sample. The logs do not show a separate local resident who received no community boost, except U002, or a non-local resident who belonged to the community group.

Consequently, it is not possible to determine whether the favorable treatment is caused by:

- Local-resident status,
- Community-group membership,
- Or the combination of both.

The explicit explanation in the log is “Community Boost,” so CommunityGroup is the clearest direct source of differential treatment. LocalResident may nevertheless act as a related eligibility condition or proxy.

### 4. Final approval rates

The observed approval rates are the same:

- Group A: 2 of 3 approved
- Group B: 2 of 3 approved

Thus, these six cases alone do not demonstrate a difference in overall approval rates. However, equal approval rates do not eliminate process bias. Group B applicants receive an advantage during scoring that Group A applicants do not receive, and that advantage could produce larger disparities in a bigger dataset or near a decision threshold.

For example, the matched scores show:

- P001 and U001 both start at 720 and are approved, but U001 receives an additional +10.
- P002 and U002 both start at 710 and are rejected.
- U003 is approved with a raw score of 695 after the community boost.

### Conclusion

The logs indicate **attribute-based preferential treatment in Group B**, specifically a +10 adjustment for membership in the Highland Civic Darts Club. This can be viewed as a potential bias **against Group A**, since the protected group has no equivalent adjustment and all of its members are non-local and unaffiliated in the recorded data. The sample is too small to establish a definitive disparity in final outcomes, but it clearly shows a potentially discriminatory scoring mechanism that should be investigated and justified.
