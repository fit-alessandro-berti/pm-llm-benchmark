**The DECLARE model describes a flexible yet highly constrained loan origination / approval process.** DECLARE is a declarative process modeling language: instead of prescribing every possible path (as in BPMN or Petri nets), it states rules that every valid execution trace must satisfy. The constraints are grounded in linear temporal logic over finite traces and allow variation while still enforcing compliance, sequencing, and quality gates.

### Activities in Practical Terms
- **Receive_Application (A)**: Front-office or digital intake—customer submits a loan request (online form, branch, or broker). This is the natural process start.
- **Preliminary_Credit_Check (B)**: Automated or light-touch bureau pull / internal score to decide whether the application is worth further effort.
- **Gather_Additional_Documents (C)**: Request and collect income proofs, tax returns, collateral valuations, identity documents, etc.
- **Assemble_Loan_Offer_Package (D)**: Underwriter or system builds the concrete offer (rate, term, fees, covenants, amortization schedule).
- **Quality_Assurance_Review (E)**: Independent check that documentation is complete, calculations are correct, and regulatory disclosures are present.
- **Authorize_Contract_Terms (F)**: Formal approval by a credit committee, authorized signatory, or automated decision engine that has the necessary mandate.
- **Transfer_Funds (G)**: Actual disbursement to the customer’s account (or to a third party in purchase financing).
- **Notify_Customer (H)**: Communication of decision, terms, or next steps (approval letter, rejection, or request for more information).
- **Proceed_Without_Compliance (Z)**: Any shortcut that bypasses mandatory controls—explicitly forbidden.

### How the Constraints Shape Real Execution

**Existence & cardinality constraints** force key steps to happen and limit wasteful repetition:
- Existence of Receive_Application and Gather_Additional_Documents guarantees that every case starts with an application and that supporting evidence is collected at least once.
- Exactly-one Preliminary_Credit_Check prevents repeated (and costly) credit pulls on the same application and ensures a single, authoritative risk snapshot.
- Absence of Proceed_Without_Compliance is a hard compliance firewall: no execution path may contain a “skip the rules” activity.

**Init + response/succession constraints** impose a disciplined early sequence:
- The process must begin with Receive_Application.
- Response(Receive_Application → Preliminary_Credit_Check) guarantees that every application is screened.
- Succession(Preliminary_Credit_Check → Gather_Additional_Documents) links screening to document collection: documents are only gathered after a credit check has occurred, and the check is always followed (eventually) by document work.

**Quality and mutual-presence constraints**:
- Responded existence (Assemble_Loan_Offer_Package → Quality_Assurance_Review) ensures that every assembled package is subjected to QA somewhere in the case.
- Coexistence(Gather_Additional_Documents ↔ Authorize_Contract_Terms) ties evidence collection to final authorization: you cannot authorize terms without having gathered documents, and vice versa.
- Precedence(Quality_Assurance_Review → Authorize_Contract_Terms) makes QA a prerequisite for authorization.

**Tighter temporal constraints (alternate / chain)** raise the bar on ordering and adjacency:
- Alternate response/precedence/succession variants (e.g., Transfer_Funds alternate-response Notify_Customer, Gather alternate-succession Quality_Assurance_Review) require that the paired activities alternate properly and that intervening repetitions of the same activity are forbidden. This reduces the chance of “notify then transfer then notify again” or repeated document gathering without a corresponding QA cycle.
- Chain response (Assemble_Loan_Offer_Package immediately followed by Transfer_Funds) and chain succession (Quality_Assurance_Review immediately followed by Assemble_Loan_Offer_Package) create tight hand-offs: once the package is ready and has passed QA, disbursement can follow without intervening unrelated work; similarly, QA and package assembly are forced into immediate succession in one direction.
- Chain precedence (Authorize_Contract_Terms immediately before Preliminary_Credit_Check) is unusually strict and may reflect a specialized control (e.g., a re-authorization or override scenario) or an artifact of the mined log; in a classic sequential flow it would be surprising, but under DECLARE it simply forbids any Preliminary_Credit_Check that is not immediately preceded by an authorization.

**Negative constraints** act as safety interlocks:
- Non-coexistence(Transfer_Funds, Receive_Application) is extremely strong: the two activities may never appear together in the same case. In a conventional successful loan this would be problematic (funds transfer presupposes an application). In practice it may surface from logs that contain only rejected or incomplete cases, or it may be intended to isolate disbursement into a separate “fulfillment” process that is deliberately kept distinct from the origination case. Regardless of origin, the constraint prevents any single trace from containing both events.
- Non-succession(Notify_Customer → Preliminary_Credit_Check) forbids notifying the customer and then performing a credit check—sensible, because notification should occur after the risk decision.
- Non-chain-succession(Authorize_Contract_Terms → Notify_Customer) forbids an immediate “authorize-then-notify” adjacency, forcing at least one intervening activity (most naturally the QA or package-assembly step).

### Interplay, Compliance, Quality, and Transparency
Taken together the rules create a controlled lifecycle:
1. Application is received and must be credit-checked exactly once.
2. Document gathering is mandatory and is synchronized with authorization.
3. Any offer package triggers a QA review; QA itself must precede authorization.
4. Disbursement and customer notification are tightly coupled to each other (alternate response/precedence) yet protected from premature or out-of-order occurrence by the negative constraints.
5. The absolute ban on “Proceed_Without_Compliance” plus the forced QA and single credit-check steps give auditors a clear, machine-checkable guarantee that every completed case passed the required gates.

Because DECLARE constraints are declarative, the process remains flexible: activities that are not constrained relative to one another can still interleave in many orders, supporting knowledge work (underwriters can iterate on documents or offers) while the hard rules stay non-negotiable.

### Business Goals and Regulatory Drivers
Such a stringent model typically arises from:
- **Regulatory pressure** (Basel, consumer-credit directives, anti-money-laundering, fair-lending rules, GDPR/privacy of credit data). Regulators demand evidence that every application was properly screened, that decisions rest on documented information, and that no funds leave the institution without authorization and quality review.
- **Risk and capital management**: a single, early credit check plus mandatory document collection and independent QA reduce the chance of underwriting errors that later become non-performing loans.
- **Operational efficiency and cost control**: limiting credit-bureau calls to exactly one, forbidding useless “proceed-without-compliance” paths, and forcing tight hand-offs (chain constraints) cut cycle time and rework.
- **Auditability and transparency**: every constraint is an explicit, checkable assertion. Conformance checking against an event log can instantly flag deviations, supporting internal audit, external regulators, and litigation defense.
- **Separation of duties / four-eyes principle**: coexistence of document gathering with authorization, the forced QA step, and the prohibition on certain direct successions implement segregation of duties without hard-coding a rigid flowchart.

In short, the DECLARE model turns a complex, knowledge-intensive loan process into a set of non-negotiable business and regulatory invariants while still allowing the natural flexibility that underwriters and relationship managers need. The result is a process that is simultaneously agile and demonstrably controlled.
