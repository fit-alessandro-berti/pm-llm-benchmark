**Model 1 is closer to a normative Hire-to-Retire process.**

### Standard/normative expectations
A typical Hire-to-Retire sequence is essentially linear (with limited justified concurrency):
- Post job advertisement
- Screen candidates
- Conduct interviews
- Make hiring decision
- Onboard the selected employee
- Add to payroll
- Close the case

Screening logically precedes (or at least strongly constrains) interviewing; the decision should rest on screening + interview outcomes; onboarding and payroll are post-decision and should occur exactly once for a successful hire; everything finishes before case closure. Parallelism is acceptable only where it does not break causal or business logic (e.g., limited overlap of late screening and early interviewing).

### Anomalies in Model 1
```python
model1 = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[Post, Screen, Decide, Interview, Onboard, Payroll, Close])
# edges: Post→Screen, Screen→Decide, Screen→Interview, Decide→Onboard→Payroll→Close
```
- After `Screen`, both `Decide` and `Interview` are enabled with no ordering between them (and `Interview` has no outgoing edges). Consequently:
  - The hiring decision can be taken before any interview occurs.
  - Interviews can interleave arbitrarily with (or even occur after) the decision, onboarding, payroll, or closure.
- All activities still occur exactly once and the core post-decision chain (`Decide → Onboard → Payroll → Close`) is preserved and sequential.
- The only real concurrency is the undesirable freedom of `Interview` relative to the rest of the process after `Screen`.

These are clear deviations (especially the possibility of deciding without interviews and of interviewing after the decision or later), but they do not remove mandatory activities, introduce unbounded repetition, or allow skipping of payroll/onboarding.

### Anomalies in Model 2
```python
loop_onboarding = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP, children=[Onboard, skip])  # Onboard at least once, optionally repeatedly
xor_payroll = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[Payroll, skip])       # Payroll optional
model2 = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[Post, Screen, Interview, Decide, loop_onboarding, xor_payroll, Close])
# edges: Post→Screen, Post→Interview→Decide→loop_onboarding→xor_payroll→Close
```
- `Screen` has no outgoing edges, so it may execute at any time after `Post` (including after interviews, the decision, onboarding, or even closure).
- Interviews are enabled directly from `Post` and therefore may precede screening.
- Onboarding is placed inside a loop, so it can be repeated arbitrarily often.
- Payroll may be skipped entirely via the exclusive choice with a silent transition.
- The only enforced sequence is the weaker chain `Interview → Decide → (looped) Onboard → (optional) Payroll → Close`.

These violations are more fundamental: screening can be postponed indefinitely or performed too late to influence the decision; onboarding is no longer a single, well-defined step; and an employee can be onboarded (possibly multiple times) without ever being added to payroll.

### Which model is closer to the normative process, and why
**Model 1 aligns more closely.**  

Its anomalies are essentially ordering/concurrency errors around the interview step. The obligatory activities still appear exactly once, the post-decision fragment is correctly sequential, and nothing essential can be omitted or repeated. In contrast, Model 2 permits (a) screening to be irrelevant to the decision, (b) repeated onboarding, and (c) complete omission of payroll—deviations that strike at the integrity of the hiring outcome itself.  

Hence the partial-order freedom in Model 1, while incorrect, leaves a process that is still recognizably a (flawed) Hire-to-Retire workflow, whereas Model 2’s control-flow operators introduce structural options that a normative Hire-to-Retire process should not allow.
