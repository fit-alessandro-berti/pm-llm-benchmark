## Overview

The image shows a **causal net (Cnet) style process model** — the kind of diagram used in process mining / workflow modelling — together with a **legend** (the shaded grey box in the lower right) that explains the split and joinnotation used in the model.

## The process model (upperleft portion)

Nine rounded rectangles represent activities, each labelled with a letter and a short name:

| Node | Label |
|---|---|
| **a** | register request |
| **b** | examine thoroughly |
| **c** | examine casually |
| **d** | check ticket |
| **e** | decide |
| **f** | reinitiate request |
| **g** | pay compensation |
| **h** | reject request |
| **z** | end |

**Flow structure (left to right):**

- **a (register request)** is the start node. Its output side carries several black dots, from which arcs fan out to **b**, **c** and **d** — i.e. after registration either a thorough or a casual examination is done, together with checking the ticket.
- **b (examine thoroughly)**, **c (examine casually)** and **d (check ticket)** all lead into **e (decide)**, whose input side again shows grouped dots (a join construct combining the examination result with the ticket check).
- From **e (decide)** three outputs are possible:
  - an arc looping back (down and to the left) to **f (reinitiate request)**,
  - an arc up to **g (pay compensation)**,
  - an arc down to **h (reject request)**.
- **f (reinitiate request)** feeds back into **b**, **c** and **d**, forming the rework/iteration loop of the model (the long curved arcs on the far left).
- **g** and **h** both point to **z (end)**, which has a join on its input side.

Every arc endpoint at a node is drawn as a small **filled black dot**, and some of these dots are linked to one another by short curved arcs — this is exactly the binding notation explained in the legend.

## The legend (grey box)

Six small example fragments, arranged in two rows of three, with a blank rounded rectangle standing for an activity:

**Top row – splits (dots on the outgoing side):**
- **XORsplit** – three separate dots on the right of the box, each with its own outgoing arrow, no connecting arcs  exactly one branch is taken.
- **ANDsplit** – the three dots are linked together by curved arcs  all branches are taken.
- **ORsplit** – dots linked by arcs in a way that expresses that *any nonempty subset* of branches may be taken.

**Bottom row – joins (dots on the incoming side):**
- **XORjoin**, **ANDjoin**, **ORjoin** – the mirror images of the above, with the arrows entering the box from the left and the dots on its input side.

So the legend tells the reader how to interpret the dot/arc clusters attached to nodes such as **a**, **e**, **f** and **z** in the model: whether the branching there is exclusive (XOR), parallel (AND) or inclusive (OR).
