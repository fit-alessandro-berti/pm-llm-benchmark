**2. Performance Analysis and Bottleneck Identification**  

**Relevant KPIs and calculation from the event log**  
- On-Time Delivery Rate: fraction of “Delivery Success” events whose timestamp falls inside the customer time window (from Dispatch attributes); overall and per route/driver/zone.  
- Average Time per Delivery Stop (service time): mean sojourn between “Arrive Customer” and “Depart Customer” (further split by Success vs. Failed).  
- Travel Time vs. Service Time ratio: sum of durations of consecutive Travel/Driving intervals (activity sequences derived from GPS speed and location change) divided by total service time; also distance between consecutive customer locations / time.  
- Fuel Consumption proxy (or per km/package): total distance (GPS cumulative or odometer) / packages delivered; idle/low-speed duration as inefficiency multiplier. Actual fuel if available via OBD.  
- Vehicle Utilization Rate: productive time (moving + customer service) / total shift length (Start Shift  End Shift); capacity utilization (packages loaded vs. capacity).  
- Frequency/Duration of Traffic Delays: count and total duration of “Low Speed Detected” / Idle events tagged as traffic (geographic + time-of-day filters).  
- Rate of Failed Deliveries: “Delivery Failed” / total delivery attempts; re-attempt cost (extra travel for same Package ID later).  
- On-Time Depot Return / Overtime Rate; Mean Time Between Failures/repairs (from Maintenance + Unscheduled Stops).  

All are computable via event-log queries (sojourn/waiting/service times per activity or segments, attribute aggregations, filters). Cycle time is depot-departure  depot-return; waiting time is idle at customer or traffic.  

**Techniques for bottleneck identification and impact quantification**  
- Performance-annotated DFGs / process maps color edges/nodes by mean/median duration or waiting time; highlight high-sojourn activities (e.g., Arrive  Depart Customer long on certain packages, Travel segments with high variance).  
- Bottleneck analysis on Petri-net places or via “critical path” style mass-flow analysis of cumulative delay.  
- Dotted charts and timeline visualizations ordered by case start: reveal shift-start congestion, lunch-time piles, end-of-day overtime clusters.  
- Spatial heatmaps of GPS dwell/low-speed points to locate traffic or parking hotspots; time-of-day slicing.  
- Drill-downs by dimensions (route, driver, vehicle age/type, weekday, weather inferred attributes).  
- Quantify impact: percentage contribution of a bottleneck segment to total cycle time or delay (e.g., “traffic cluster on corridor X adds 18 % average overtime”), cumulative cost proxies (extra fuel km + driver overtime hours), or packages delayed. Statistical tests (ANOVA across drivers/routes) and Pareto ranking of bottlenecks by severity × frequency.  

Bottlenecks may be structural (always-slow route segments), temporal (morning rush), resource (underperforming drivers or aging vehicles), activity-specific (long customer interaction / parking search), or external (failed deliveries cascading travel).  

**3. Root Cause Analysis for Inefficiencies**  

Beyond location of delays, candidates include:  
- Suboptimal static route planning that ignores historical variability.  
- Inaccurate travel-time estimations (planned times systematically under-estimate actual).  
- Recurring traffic congestion patterns at specific corridors/times.  
- High service-time variability (parking difficulty, customer not ready, complex drop-offs).  
- Mid-shift vehicle breakdowns requiring unscheduled stops (correlating GPS “Engine Warning” + Maintenance Logs).  
- Driver behavior/skill differences (aggressive routing vs. methodical, idle time, parking choices).  
- Failed delivery attempts (customer not home, access issues) generating rework travel.  

Process-mining techniques that validate/confirm:  
- Variant analysis and performance comparison: cluster high-KPI (“on-time, low-fuel”) route-days vs. low-KPI variants; attribute differences (stop sequence disorder, extra unplanned activities, driver, start time). Feature importance (decision trees on case attributes) isolates strongest predictors.  
- Correlation / regression of attributes (traffic-delay duration vs. total lateness; service-time histogram per customer type or zone; vehicle age vs. repair frequency).  
- Temporal profiling: sojourn time distributions conditional on time-of-day or day-of-week; autocorrelation of successive travel times for congestion persistence.  
- Conformance residual analysis: large planned-vs-actual travel residuals localized to particular segments indicate estimation bias.  
- Resource-behavior mining (work patterns of drivers: idle after “Depart Customer,” sequence compliance).  
- Root-cause diagnostics via “why” analysis (e.g., fails when stop density high + narrow streets) or multi-perspective logs linking package attributes (residential vs. commercial).  
- Predictive modeling on historical prefixes to forecast remaining delay given current traffic state, validating that certain observed prefixes systematically lead to overtime.  

These analyses turn raw bottlenecks into causal hypotheses supported by quantitative evidence (e.g., “routes planned with outdated travel times account for 40 % of missed windows; reordering based on historical median travel reduces variance”).
