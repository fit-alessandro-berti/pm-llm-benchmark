## Overview

The image is a threepart academic/technical figure illustrating the **proclet** modelling notation (an interactionoriented extension of Petri nets / workflow nets), using a medical "lab visit / lab test" scenario as the running example. The three panels are labelled (a), (b) and (c) by captions underneath each.

---

## (a) "Two proclet classes connected through two channels" — the largest, topleft panel

Two vertical Petrinetlike process models sit side by side, linked horizontally by two dashed rounded rectangles that act as **channels**.

**Left proclet class — "Lab visit"** (label at top, above a circle containing a black triangle = start):
- `Order entry` — annotated with a **doctor** resource icon on the left
- `Take blood sample` — annotated with a **lab assistant** icon
- a place (empty circle), then `Receive result` (drawn as a **grey/shaded box**)
- `Judge results` (lab assistant icon; the box has a green bar along its bottom edge)
- a branch into two grey tasks: `Everything fine` and `Do tests again`
- `Receive updated result` (grey)
- a place, then `Send report` (lab assistant icon; a red bar along its top edge)
- ends in a circle containing a black square (final state)

**Right proclet class — "Lab test"** (also starting with a circle containing a black triangle):
- `Perform test` — **chemical analyst** icon
- `Make report` — chemical analyst icon
- a place, from which flow splits to `Finish lab test` (grey) and `Perform test again` (chemical analyst)
- `Provide updated report` (chemical analyst)
- ends in a circle containing a black square

**Channels (dashed rounded boxes between the two models):**
- Upper channel: **"Order system"**
- Lower channel: **"HIS"** (hospital information system)

Inside/attached to the channels are small **ports** drawn as rounded rectangles containing cardinality/multiplicity pairs such as `*,1`, `1,1`, `1,?`, `1,*`. Arrows run from tasks in one proclet through these ports and across the channel to tasks in the other proclet — e.g. `Order entry`/`Take blood sample`  Order system  `Perform test`; `Make report`  HIS  `Receive result`; `Do tests again`  HIS  `Perform test again`; `Provide updated report`  HIS  `Receive updated result`. The grey shading marks tasks that involve **interaction** (sending/receiving performatives), while white tasks are internal.

## (b) "Class diagram containing the two proclet classes" — topright panel

A simple UMLstyle class diagram: two plain rectangles, **"Lab visit"** (top) and **"Lab test"** (bottom), joined by a vertical line labelled **"requires"**, with multiplicity **1..1** at the Labvisit end and **1..\*** at the Labtest end — i.e. one lab visit may require one or more lab tests.

## (c) "Example of a performative" — bottom panel

A singlerow table showing the attributes of a message (performative) exchanged over a channel:

| Time | Channel | Sender | Receivers | Action | Content | Scope | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 11:00 | Order system | Lab visit – John | Lab test – HGB John | Create | "Can you perform a HGB test for John?" | Private | OUT |

This concretely illustrates how a proclet instance ("Lab visit – John") creates a new labtest instance by sending a performative through the Order system channel.
