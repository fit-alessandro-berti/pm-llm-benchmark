# Bias Analysis: Group A vs. Group B Event Logs

## Finding: Group B's log exhibits systematic bias favoring "LocalResident" applicants via a disguised proxy variable.

---

## 1. The Mechanism: "CommunityGroup" as a Proxy for LocalResident Status

The **ScoreAdjustment** column introduces a "+10 (Community Boost)" that is applied **exclusively** to applicants who are:
- `LocalResident = TRUE`, **and**
- Members of "Highland Civic Darts Club"

No applicant in Group A (all `LocalResident = FALSE`) ever receives this adjustment — it doesn't even apply as an option (marked "0" or "N/A" for non-members).

This means **CommunityGroup membership is not independent of the protected attribute** — in this dataset, it perfectly correlates with `LocalResident = TRUE`. Effectively, the "Community Boost" is a laundered mechanism for rewarding local-resident status, likely a legally protected/proxy-sensitive attribute (residency-based discrimination is a classic redlining pattern).

---

## 2. Quantifying the Impact on Outcomes

| Case | Base Score | Adjustment | Final Score | Decision |
|------|-----------|------------|-------------|----------|
| U001 | 720 | +10 | 730 | **Approved** |
| U003 | 695 | +10 | 705 | **Approved** |
| P001 | 720 | 0 | 720 | Approved |
| P003 | 740 | 0 | 740 | Approved |

**Critical case: U003**
- Raw/base score = **695** — *lower* than every applicant in Group A (720, 710, 740).
- Without the +10 boost, 695 would likely fail whatever threshold governs approval (compare to P002's 710, which was **Rejected**).
- **With** the artificial boost, U003 reaches 705 and is **Approved**.

This is the smoking gun: **an applicant with a strictly worse preliminary score than a rejected protected-group applicant (P002, 710) is approved, solely due to a residency-linked adjustment.**

---

## 3. Comparative Outcome Pattern

| Group | Approval Rate | Adjustment Applied? |
|-------|---------------|---------------------|
| A (Protected) | 2/3 (67%) | Never |
| B (Unprotected) | 2/3 (67%) | 2/3 cases (both approvals) |

While raw approval *rates* look similar, **the pathway to approval differs structurally**: Group B's approvals are partially manufactured by the score adjustment, whereas Group A's approvals depend purely on merit (preliminary score). This means the *decision threshold effectively differs by group* — Group B enjoys a lower effective bar (~695–705 sufficient) while Group A needs a genuinely higher score (P002 at 710 was rejected with no adjustment available to it).

---

## 4. Why This Constitutes Bias, Not Legitimate Differentiation

1. **No equivalent boost exists for Group A** — there is no "None" community path that grants Group A applicants a comparable adjustment for any trait they could hold instead.
2. **CommunityGroup ("Highland Civic Darts Club") is not a proxy for creditworthiness** — it's a social/local affiliation with no demonstrated relationship to repayment risk, yet it directly modifies the *score* rather than being a separate qualitative note.
3. **The gating variable is LocalResident**, and the boost only triggers when `LocalResident = TRUE`. This creates **disparate treatment**: identical underlying financial profiles receive different scores based on residency — a classic proxy discrimination pattern (similar to redlining based on geography/community affiliation).
4. **Process asymmetry**: In Group A, `ScoreAdjustment = 0` is a fixed, non-negotiable field. In Group B, it is a variable "N/A  boost" pipeline, indicating the underwriting/scoring system treats the two groups on **structurally different decision paths**, not just different data inputs.

---

## Conclusion

**Group B's log shows the bias**, not because outcomes look different in aggregate, but because the **decision-making process itself embeds a residency/community-linked score boost** unavailable to Group A. This artificially lowers the effective approval threshold for local residents (via the Highland Civic Darts Club affiliation), allowing at least one applicant (U003) with a *lower raw score than a rejected protected-group applicant* to be approved. This is a textbook case of **indirect/proxy discrimination**, where a facially neutral variable (CommunityGroup) operationalizes disparate treatment based on the protected attribute (LocalResident).
