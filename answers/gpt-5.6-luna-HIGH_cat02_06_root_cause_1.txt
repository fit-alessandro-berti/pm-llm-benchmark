## 2. Delay patterns and potential root causes

### Case 102: Escalation and long post-investigation wait

Important intervals include:

- Receive to triage: **25 min**
- Triage to assignment: **30 min**
- Assignment to escalation: **2 h 30 min**
- Escalation to investigation: **2 h 30 min**
- Investigation to resolution: **19 h**
- Resolution to closure: **15 min**

The main issues are:

1. **Delay before escalation**  
   There is a two-and-a-half-hour gap after assignment before the ticket is escalated.

2. **Level-2 handoff delay**  
   Another two-and-a-half hours pass between escalation and the start of investigation. This may indicate a Level-2 queue, slow acceptance of the handoff, or incomplete escalation information.

3. **Long investigation-to-resolution interval**  
   The ticket is investigated at 14:00 and not resolved until 09:00 the following day. This strongly suggests an overnight queue, approval dependency, customer response delay, or lack of resolution capacity.

---

### Case 104: No escalation, but a long wait to begin investigation

Important intervals include:

- Receive to triage: **40 min**
- Triage to assignment: **30 min**
- Assignment to investigation: **3 h 30 min**
- Investigation to resolution: **19 h**
- Resolution to closure: **30 min**

This case is important because it was **not escalated**, yet still took more than 24 hours. That suggests escalation is not the only problem.

Potential causes include:

- A Level-1 agent queue after assignment
- Assignment to an unavailable or overloaded agent
- Work scheduled only during certain hours
- A ticket waiting for information or approval before investigation could start
- An overnight delay after investigation

The three-and-a-half-hour wait between assignment and investigation is considerably longer than in the fast cases.

---

### Case 105: Severe escalation and rework delay

Important intervals include:

- Receive to triage: **10 min**
- Triage to assignment: **25 min**
- Assignment to first investigation: **10 min**
- First investigation to escalation: **50 min**
- Escalation to second investigation: **28 h**
- Second investigation to resolution: **19 h**
- Resolution to closure: **30 min**

The dominant bottleneck is the **28-hour delay after escalation**. The second investigation event may indicate a handoff, restart, or rework by the specialized team.

This case may therefore involve:

- A long Level-2 queue
- Slow acceptance of escalated tickets
- Poor-quality or incomplete handoff information
- Rework because the first investigation did not provide enough information
- Insufficient weekend or off-hours support
- A separate resolution queue after the second investigation

Case 105 also has the same approximately **19-hour investigation-to-resolution gap** seen in cases 102 and 104, suggesting a recurring process or staffing pattern rather than a one-off event.

## 3. Common patterns across the log

### A. Delays occur mainly before investigation and before resolution

The fast cases have short intervals:

- Case 101: assignment to investigation in **40 minutes**
- Case 103: assignment to investigation in **15 minutes**
- Case 101: investigation to resolution in **1 hour**
- Case 103: investigation to resolution in **30 minutes**

The slow cases have much longer waits, particularly:

- Assignment or escalation to investigation
- Investigation to resolution

The final closing step is not a major bottleneck. It takes only **15–30 minutes** in every case.

### B. Escalations are associated with longer cycle times

Both escalated cases, 102 and 105, are long-running cases. Escalation introduces additional handoffs and waiting:

- Case 102: approximately **5 hours** from assignment through escalation to investigation
- Case 105: **28 hours** from escalation to renewed investigation

However, case 104 shows that escalation is not necessary for a delay to occur. Level-1 queues and post-investigation delays can also produce long cycle times.

### C. Apparent overnight or off-hours waiting

Cases 102, 104, and 105 all contain a roughly **19-hour gap between investigation and resolution**:

- Case 102: 14:00 to 09:00 next day
- Case 104: 13:00 to 08:00 next day
- Case 105: 14:00 to 09:00 next day

This repeated pattern may reflect:

- No work being performed overnight
- Limited weekend coverage
- Resolution approvals available only during business hours
- A scheduled batch or next-shift resolution process

The dates also suggest that these intervals cross into later calendar days, including the weekend. This should be validated against staffing schedules and business calendars.

### D. Triage delays are present but are not the main cause

Triage takes between **5 and 40 minutes**, which is longer in cases 102 and 104 but much smaller than the 19- or 28-hour waits. Improving triage may help, but it will not address the main cycle-time problem.

## Recommendations

1. **Measure queue time separately from active work time**  
   Add or analyze statuses such as:
   - Awaiting assignment
   - Awaiting Level-2 acceptance
   - Awaiting customer
   - Awaiting approval
   - In investigation
   - Ready for resolution

   The current log shows elapsed time but cannot distinguish actual work from waiting.

2. **Create service-level targets for escalations**  
   Set explicit targets for:
   - Time to accept an escalation
   - Time to begin Level-2 investigation
   - Time from investigation completion to resolution

   Automatic alerts should be triggered when these limits are exceeded.

3. **Improve escalation handoffs**  
   Use structured handoff templates containing the issue description, diagnostic results, priority, customer impact, and required next action. A warm handoff or automatic Level-2 assignment could reduce the 28-hour delay in cases like 105.

4. **Review Level-1 assignment queues**  
   Case 104 indicates that a ticket can wait several hours even without escalation. Workload balancing, automatic assignment, WIP limits, and monitoring of assignment-to-first-investigation time could address this.

5. **Provide off-hours and weekend coverage where appropriate**  
   The recurring next-day resolution pattern suggests that tickets may be waiting for the next staffed shift. An on-call or follow-the-sun model could reduce this delay, particularly for high-priority tickets.

6. **Investigate the resolution queue**  
   Since multiple cases spend about 19 hours between investigation and resolution, review whether this represents:
   - Approval waiting
   - A separate resolution team queue
   - Customer confirmation
   - Scheduled batch processing
   - Insufficient resolution authority for agents

7. **Use percentiles and segmented KPIs**  
   Track median and 90th/95th-percentile cycle times by:
   - Escalated versus non-escalated tickets
   - Agent or support level
   - Time of day and day of week
   - Ticket priority and issue type

Overall, the strongest evidence points to **handoff and queue delays around escalation**, **delayed starts to investigation**, and a recurring **overnight or off-hours resolution bottleneck**.
