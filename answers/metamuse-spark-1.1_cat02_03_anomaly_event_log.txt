In Procure-to-Pay, the standard control-flow is:

**Create Purchase Requisition -> Approve Purchase Requisition -> Issue Purchase Order -> Receive Goods -> Vendor Invoice Received -> Match Invoice to PO [3-way match] -> Release Payment -> Close Case**

This sequence enforces key internal controls: No purchase without approval, no payment without proof that goods were ordered, received, and invoiced at the correct price/quantity.

**Case 1001 is the compliant baseline.** All steps are in the correct logical order.

### Case 1002 - Severe Control Violation - Multiple Anomalies

> 08:30 Create Requisition
> 08:40 **Issue PO** 
> 07:00 Vendor Invoice Received
> 07:15 **Release Payment**
> 08:00 **Approve Requisition** 
> 09:00 Receive Goods
> 08:00 Match Invoice to PO

1.  **Issue PO BEFORE Approve Requisition:** A Purchase Order was issued without approval. This bypasses budgetary control and authorisation. It enables maverick buying and violates Segregation of Duties / SOX compliance.
2.  **Release Payment BEFORE Approve Requisition and BEFORE Receive Goods:** Payment was released before the purchase was even approved and before goods were confirmed received. This creates extreme financial risk - you could pay for something never approved or never delivered. High fraud risk.
3.  **Match Invoice to PO AFTER Release Payment:** The 3-way match is the core preventive control to ensure you only pay what you ordered and received. Here matching happens 2 days *after* payment. The payment was uncontrolled.

This case is non-conformant and would be flagged as a critical audit failure.

### Case 1003 - Payment Before Invoice and Before Goods

> 09:00 Create Requisition
> 09:10 Approve Requisition
> 10:00 Issue PO
> 11:00 **Release Payment**
> 12:00 **Vendor Invoice Received**
> 13:00 **Receive Goods**
> 14:00 Match Invoice to PO

1.  **Release Payment BEFORE Vendor Invoice Received and BEFORE Receive Goods:** This is an advance payment without justification. Standard practice is to pay *after* liability is confirmed by invoice and receipt. Risk: Paying for incorrect amounts, paying twice, or paying for goods that never arrive or are damaged.
2.  **Match Invoice to PO AFTER Release Payment:** Again, the control is performed after the fact. If the invoice or goods receipt does not match the PO, the money is already gone and recovery is difficult.

This indicates a broken process or possible attempt to circumvent accounts payable controls.

### Case 1004 - Goods Received Before PO

> 10:00 Create Requisition
> 10:15 Approve Requisition
> 09:00 **Receive Goods**
> 09:15 **Issue PO**
> 08:00 Vendor Invoice Received

1.  **Receive Goods BEFORE Issue Purchase Order:** Goods arrived before a PO was issued to the vendor. The process is reversed. This means there was no formal, legally binding order with agreed price, terms and delivery when the goods were shipped.

While less severe than paying before receiving, it is still anomalous. It causes issues with inventory management, receiving cannot verify against a PO, no price commitment exists, and it often indicates an off-contract purchase or that the PO was created retroactively to cover a purchase that already happened.

**Summary:**

| Case ID | Conformance | Core Problem |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1001** | **Compliant** | None |
| **1002** | **Critical Anomaly** | PO and Payment before Approval, Payment before Goods Receipt and before 3-way match |
| **1003** | **Critical Anomaly** | Payment before Invoice and before Goods Receipt |
| **1004** | **Moderate Anomaly** | Goods Receipt before PO issuance |

All anomalous cases break the fundamental P2P principle: **Approval -> Commitment -> Receipt -> Verification -> Payment.**
