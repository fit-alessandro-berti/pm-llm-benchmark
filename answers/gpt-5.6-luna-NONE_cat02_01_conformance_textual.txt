### Case assessment

| Case | Assessment | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Conforms** | Follows the complete sufficient-inventory path: **Receive  Check inventory  Authorize payment  Package items  Ship order  Close order**. |
| **2** | **Violates** | **Package items** occurs before **Authorize payment**. The description requires payment authorization after inventory checking and before packaging. |
| **3** | **Violates** | After **Cancel order**, the process performs **Ship order**. Shipping after cancellation is explicitly disallowed. In addition, cancellation normally leads directly to **Close order** in the insufficient-inventory branch. The preceding authorization may also be questionable if cancellation indicates insufficient inventory, though the definite violation is shipping after cancellation. |
| **4** | **Conforms**, assuming insufficient inventory | Follows the cancellation path: **Receive  Check inventory  Cancel  Close**. This is the expected sequence when stock is insufficient. |
| **5** | **Violates** | **Ship order** occurs without a preceding **Package items** activity. The normative process requires packaging after payment authorization and before shipping. |

### Summary

The violating cases are **Cases 2, 3, and 5**.

- **Case 2:** Packaging precedes payment authorization.
- **Case 3:** Shipping occurs after cancellation; cancellation should be followed by closure rather than fulfillment activities.
- **Case 5:** Shipping occurs without packaging.

Cases **1** and **4** conform to the two intended process paths.
