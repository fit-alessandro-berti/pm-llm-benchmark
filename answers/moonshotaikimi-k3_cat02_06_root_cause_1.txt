## 3. Root Causes of Delay

**a) Level-2 queue congestion after escalation (biggest single factor)**
- Case 105 waited **28 hours** between escalation and L2 investigation — the largest single delay segment in the entire log. Case 102 waited 2.5 hours. Escalated tickets appear to sit idle in the L2 queue, suggesting insufficient L2 capacity or no SLA on escalation pickup.

**b) Long wait before first investigation (Level-1 capacity/prioritization issue)**
- Case 104 sat **3.5 hours** between assignment and investigation despite *never being escalated* — proving escalation is not the only cause of delay. Case 102 similarly spent 2.5 hours at L1 before escalating. All tickets arrive in a morning burst (08:00–08:25), which likely creates a backlog; tickets at the back of the queue start investigation late.

**c) Overnight spillover in Investigate  Resolve (calendar/working-hours effect)**
- All three slow cases show an almost identical **~19-hour** InvestigateResolve gap, and in each case resolution lands between 08:00–09:00 the next morning. Investigations that begin after ~13:00 do not complete the same day. Fast cases, by contrast, start investigation before 09:10 and finish within the morning. This points to a business-hours cutoff: late-started work is parked until the next working day.

**d) Escalation compounds delay through handoffs and rework**
- Case 105 shows duplicated effort: L1 investigates (09:10–10:00), escalates, then L2 *re-investigates* from scratch. Each handoff adds a queue wait plus repeated work, which is why escalated cases (102, 105) are the slowest overall.

**Not bottlenecks:** triage, assignment, and closing are fast and consistent (40 min) across all cases.

## 4. Recommendations

1. **Skill-based routing at triage** — identify tickets likely to need L2 and route them directly, skipping the failed L1 attempt. This would eliminate both the L1 queue wait and the duplicated investigation (would have saved most of Case 105's ~30h of escalation overhead).
2. **Escalation SLA with alerting** — flag any escalated ticket not picked up by L2 within e.g. 1 hour; consider expanding or flexing L2 staffing.
3. **Match L1 staffing to the morning arrival peak** so all tickets begin investigation before midday, keeping them inside the same-day resolution window (this alone would have kept Case 104 under ~5 hours).
4. **Address the overnight gap** — extended-hours or follow-the-sun coverage for in-progress investigations, or explicitly measure *working-time* cycle time alongside elapsed time so the calendar effect is visible.
5. **Reduce escalation rate** — equip L1 with better knowledge bases/playbooks for common "complex" issues.
6. **Monitor per-transition waiting times, not just end-to-end SLA** — the delays here are *between* activities (handoffs and queues), which aggregate cycle-time metrics would hide.
