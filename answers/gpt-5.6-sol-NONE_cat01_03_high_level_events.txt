## Proposed high-level process model

| Order | High-Level Activity | Included Low-Level Activities | Rationale |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Material Identification and Preparation** | Retrieve raw metal sheet; Scan barcode; Place metal sheet on table; Align metal sheet; Preheat metal sheet | These events prepare and validate the input material before fabrication. Retrieval and scanning establish material identity and traceability; placement, alignment, and preheating bring the sheet into the required physical state and position. |
| 2 | **Welding Assembly** | Pick up welding tool; Weld corner A; Weld corner B | Tool acquisition is a setup action immediately associated with the welding operation. The corner welds collectively form one coherent fabrication stage performed with the same tool and resource. |
| 3 | **Weld Quality Inspection** | Measure weld integrity | This event verifies the output of the welding stage using a dedicated quality sensor. It is kept separate because it represents a control point that may determine whether processing continues, rework occurs, or the product is rejected. |
| 4 | **Protective Coating and Curing** | Apply protective coating; Dry coating | Applying the coating and thermally drying or curing it are consecutive parts of one surface-finishing stage. The coating is not complete until the drying operation has occurred. |
| 5 | **Final Visual Inspection** | Visual check | This is the final product-level quality gate after fabrication and finishing. It checks the completed item rather than only the weld and produces the final pass/fail result shown in the sample. |

## Suggested aggregation rules

The following rules can be applied to the full event log on a per-`CaseID` basis:

```text
{Retrieve raw material, Scan barcode, Place material, Align material,
 Preheat material}
     Material Identification and Preparation

{Acquire/pick up welding tool, Weld component or location}
     Welding Assembly

{Measure/test weld integrity}
     Weld Quality Inspection

{Apply protective coating, Dry/cure coating}
     Protective Coating and Curing

{Visual check of finished item}
     Final Visual Inspection
```

For every aggregated event, retain:

- `CaseID`
- high-level activity name
- earliest constituent timestamp as `StartTime`
- latest constituent timestamp as `EndTime`
- participating resources
- relevant inputs and measurements
- outcome or status, such as `Passed`, `Failed`, or `IntegrityScore`

Activity semantics should take priority over temporal proximity. For example, the integrity measurement is close in time to welding but belongs to a separate quality-control stage because it has a different purpose, resource type, and potential process outcome. Similarly, visual inspection should remain distinct from coating even though it immediately follows curing.
