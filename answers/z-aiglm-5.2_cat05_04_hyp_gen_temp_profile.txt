### 1. Identified Anomalies in the Temporal Profile

Based on the temporal profile model, the following anomalies are evident:

*   **Unnaturally Rigid Time from Receive to Approve (R to P)**: The average time is approximately 25 hours (90,000 seconds) with a standard deviation of only 1 hour (3,600 seconds). This extremely low variance for a multi-step process (which includes assignment and evaluation) suggests an artificial or automated rigid scheduling rather than organic human processing.
*   **Excessive Delay from Approve to Notify (P to N)**: There is an average delay of 7 days (604,800 seconds) between approving a claim and notifying the customer, with a high standard deviation of 2 days (172,800 seconds). This violates expected customer service standards where notification should closely follow approval.
*   **Premature Closures after Assignment (A to C)**: Claims are closed on average just 2 hours (7,200 seconds) after being assigned to an adjuster. This duration is insufficient to properly execute the intended Evaluation (E) and Approval (P) phases, indicating step-skipping or premature closure.
*   **Too-Rapid Transition from Evaluate to Notify (E to N)**: An average of only 5 minutes (300 seconds) between Evaluation and Notification is suspiciously fast. This suggests that the standard Approval (P) step is either being bypassed or the system is triggering notifications prematurely.

### 2. Hypotheses on the Causes of Anomalies

*   **Automated Batch Processing for Approvals (R to P)**: The near-exact 25-hour turnaround suggests a fixed-duration Service Level Agreement (SLA) timer or a nightly automated batch job that approves claims exactly 24 hours after receipt, bypassing manual review.
*   **Notification Bottlenecks or Third-Party Handoffs (P to N)**: The 7-day delay could be caused by notifications being handled by a separate, manually-operated call center or a weekly mailing batch run. Alternatively, there might be a systemic backlog in the notification queue.
*   **SLA Manipulation or Duplicate Claim Rejection (A to C)**: Adjusters might be prematurely closing claims without proper evaluation to artificially meet closure metrics or SLAs. Alternatively, these rapid closures could be system-generated rejections of duplicate or fraudulent claims immediately after assignment.
*   **Misconfigured Workflow Triggers (E to N)**: The 5-minute gap between E and N might occur because the "Notify" event is configured to trigger automatically upon "Evaluate" status completion, rather than waiting for an explicit "Approve" status. ### 3. Verification Approaches using SQL Queries

To verify these hypotheses, we can query the PostgreSQL database to find specific claims matching these anomalous profiles and correlate them with adjuster, region, and claim type data.

**Query 1: Identify Premature Closures Missing Evaluation and Approval**
This query finds claims that were closed within 2 hours of assignment but completely lacked the Evaluation ('E') and Approval ('P') steps.

```sql
SELECT 
    c.claim_id, 
    c.claim_type, 
    c.submission_date,
    c_assign.timestamp AS assign_time, 
    c_close.timestamp AS close_time,
    EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (c_close.timestamp - c_assign.timestamp)) AS time_diff_seconds
FROM claims c
JOIN claim_events c_assign ON c.claim_id = c_assign.claim_id AND c_assign.activity = 'A'
JOIN claim_events c_close ON c.claim_id = c_close.claim_id AND c_close.activity = 'C'
WHERE EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (c_close.timestamp - c_assign.timestamp)) <= 7200 -- Less than or equal to 2 hours
  AND NOT EXISTS (
      SELECT 1 FROM claim_events ce 
      WHERE ce.claim_id = c.claim_id AND ce.activity IN ('E', 'P')
  );
```

**Query 2: Analyze Long Notification Delays by Adjuster Region and Claim Type**
This query checks if the 7-day delay between Approval ('P') and Notification ('N') is concentrated in specific adjuster regions or claim types, indicating a localized bottleneck.

```sql
SELECT 
    a.region, 
    a.specialization, 
    c.claim_type,
    COUNT(c.claim_id) AS affected_claims,
    AVG(EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (n.timestamp - p.timestamp))) AS avg_delay_seconds
FROM claims c
JOIN claim_events p ON c.claim_id = p.claim_id AND p.activity = 'P'
JOIN claim_events n ON c.claim_id = n.claim_id AND n.activity = 'N'
-- Assuming 'resource' in claim_events holds the adjuster's name or ID matching adjusters table
JOIN adjusters a ON p.resource = a.name 
WHERE n.timestamp > p.timestamp 
  AND EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (n.timestamp - p.timestamp)) > 432000 -- More than 5 days
GROUP BY a.region, a.specialization, c.claim_type
ORDER BY avg_delay_seconds DESC;
```

```sql
SELECT 
    c.claim_id,
```
