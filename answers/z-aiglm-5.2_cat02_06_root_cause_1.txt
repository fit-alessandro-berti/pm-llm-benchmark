### 3. Explanation of Cycle Time Impact and Recommendations

**How these factors increase cycle times:**
1.  **The "Next-Day Resolution" Trap:** When Level-1 agents investigate a ticket late in the day, they may run out of time to complete the resolution before their shift ends, pushing the "Resolve Ticket" step to the next morning. This instantly adds 18-19 hours to a ticket's cycle time.
2.  **Escalation Bottlenecks:** Routing a ticket to Level-2 effectively pauses the clock if the Level-2 queue is mismanaged. Because Level-2 handles more complex issues, any delay here drastically inflates the total cycle time, as seen in Case 105.
3.  **First-Response Latency:** Delays in merely starting the investigation (as seen in Case 104) compound over the lifecycle. If a ticket sits idle before investigation, it is more likely to bleed into the afternoon, triggering the aforementioned "Next-Day Resolution" trap.

**Recommendations to Address Bottlenecks:**
*   **Implement Afternoon Triage/Routing Rules:** If a ticket is assigned and investigated after a certain time (e.g., 12:00 PM), it should be flagged as high-priority to ensure it is resolved the same day, rather than automatically deferred to the next morning.
*   **Audit Level-2 Support Capacity:** The 28-hour wait time in Case 105 is unacceptable. Management should assess the workload of the Level-2 team. Consider cross-training Level-1 agents to handle more complex issues or hiring additional Level-2 staff to clear the escalation backlog faster.
*   **Enforce Strict SLAs on Initial Investigation:** Introduce a rule that tickets must be picked up for investigation within 30-60 minutes of assignment to prevent the 3.5-hour idle time seen in Case 104.
*   **Improve Initial Triage Accuracy:** For Cases 102 and 105, if the issues ultimately required Level-2 expertise, the triage process should be enhanced to identify these sooner.
