The DECLARE model describes the loan application process as a **set of hard behavioral rules** rather than a single fixed flowchart. Since every constraint has support and confidence equal to `1.0`, these are meant to be mandatory rules: every real loan case must satisfy them.

### What the activities mean in real life

| Activity | Practical meaning |
|---|---|
| **Receive_Application (A)** | The customer submits a loan application. This creates the loan case and is the first official event. |
| **Preliminary_Credit_Check (B)** | The bank runs an initial credit/risk screen on the applicant. |
| **Gather_Additional_Documents (C)** | The bank collects supporting documents: income statements, tax returns, IDs, bank statements, etc. |
| **Assemble_Loan_Offer_Package (D)** | The bank prepares the actual loan offer: amount, interest rate, fees, repayment terms. |
| **Quality_Assurance_Review (E)** | A reviewer checks that the file is complete, compliant, and the offer is correct before it is finalized. |
| **Authorize_Contract_Terms (F)** | A person with approval authority formally signs off on the contract terms. |
| **Transfer_Funds (G)** | The loan amount is disbursed to the customer. |
| **Notify_Customer (H)** | The customer is told about the decision, the upcoming transfer, or the transfer confirmation. |
| **Proceed_Without_Compliance (Z)** | A forbidden action: moving forward without required compliance checks. It must never occur. |

## How the constraints shape the real-life scenario

### 1. The foundational rules: start, existence, and prohibition

- `init(Receive_Application)`  
  Every loan case must start with receiving the application. This prevents “phantom” loans or actions taken before the customer has applied. No credit check, document request, or transfer can happen before an application exists.

- `existence(Receive_Application)`  
  Confirms that the application activity must appear at least once. Every loan has an application.

- `existence(Gather_Additional_Documents)`  
  No loan can proceed without documentary evidence. This reflects KYC/AML requirements and basic credit risk management: you cannot approve a loan without understanding the borrower’s financial situation.

- `exactly_one(Preliminary_Credit_Check)`  
  The credit check is done exactly once per case. This avoids both **skipping** the check and **unnecessarily repeating** it. It is a single, clear gate.

- `absence(Proceed_Without_Compliance)`  
  The process explicitly forbids the “shortcut” activity. Even if a manager or urgent situation tries to bypass compliance, the model does not allow it.

These rules alone already create a controlled process: start with an application, check credit once, gather documents, and never skip compliance.

---

### 2. Ordering and quality-gate constraints

- `response(Receive_Application, Preliminary_Credit_Check)`  
  After the application is received, the credit check must follow. This means the sequence is not random: application comes first, then the risk screen.

- `succession(Preliminary_Credit_Check, Gather_Additional_Documents)`  
  The credit check and document gathering are linked in order. The preliminary credit check identifies risk, and that determines what additional documents are needed. Therefore, documents are gathered **after** the credit check, not before.

- `altsuccession(Gather_Additional_Documents, Quality_Assurance_Review)`  
  Document gathering and QA review must alternate in the correct order: documents first, QA review second. Practically, it means **the QA reviewer does not review an empty folder**. The file must be complete before the quality review begins.

- `precedence(Quality_Assurance_Review, Authorize_Contract_Terms)`  
  Contract terms cannot be authorized unless a QA review has already happened. This prevents an approver from signing off on an unchecked or non-compliant offer.

- `coexistence(Gather_Additional_Documents, Authorize_Contract_Terms)`  
  Document gathering and contract authorization are tied together. If additional documents are required, they must be gathered before terms are authorized. Conversely, once the terms are authorized, the document collection stage is considered complete. This enforces completeness.

- `responded_existence(Assemble_Loan_Offer_Package, Quality_Assurance_Review)`  
  If an offer package is assembled, a QA review must be somewhere in the case. This is a safety net: no offer package can exist without quality assurance having happened at some point.

---

### 3. Strict handoff rules around offer and disbursement

- `chainsuccession(Quality_Assurance_Review, Assemble_Loan_Offer_Package)`  
  The QA review must be **immediately before** the offer package is assembled. This is a strict handoff: once QA has approved the file, the next step is to build the offer.

- `chainresponse(Assemble_Loan_Offer_Package, Transfer_Funds)`  
  Once the offer package is assembled, the transfer of funds is the **immediately next** activity. In practical terms, this represents an automated “ready to fund” trigger: after the offer package is finalized, the system immediately disburses the loan.

These constraints compress the final phase of the loan process:

\[
\text{Quality Review} \rightarrow \text{Offer Package} \rightarrow \text{Fund Transfer}
\]

This reduces the risk of a loan being approved but then sitting in limbo, or funds being transferred from an incomplete or unreviewed offer package.

---

### 4. Customer notification and transfer transparency

- `altresponse(Transfer_Funds, Notify_Customer)`  
  Every transfer of funds must eventually be followed by a customer notification. No transfer should happen silently.

- `altprecedence(Notify_Customer, Transfer_Funds)`  
  Every transfer of funds must also have a customer notification **before** it. So the customer is notified both before the transfer, as a kind of “disbursement advice,” and after the transfer, as a confirmation.

Together, these two rules mean the customer is not left in the dark around the movement of money:

\[
\text{Notify before transfer} \rightarrow \text{Transfer} \rightarrow \text{Notify after transfer}
\]

---

### 5. Negative constraints: forbidden sequences and shortcuts

- `nonsuccession(Notify_Customer, Preliminary_Credit_Check)`  
  A customer notification cannot be followed by the preliminary credit check. This makes sense: you should not tell the customer about a decision before the credit check has happened.

- `nonchainsuccession(Authorize_Contract_Terms, Notify_Customer)`  
  Authorizing contract terms cannot be immediately followed by notifying the customer. There must be at least one intermediate step, such as assembling the offer package or transferring funds. This prevents premature customer communication right after an internal approval.

- `noncoexistence(Transfer_Funds, Receive_Application)`  
  Taken literally in DECLARE, this would mean the two activities cannot occur together at all, which would be impossible in a real loan process. The intended real-life rule is probably: **funds cannot be disbursed before an application has been received**. That rule is already guaranteed by `init(Receive_Application)`, but the negative constraint reinforces the idea that the application and disbursement belong to different phases of the process.

## How the rules support compliance and quality

The constraints create a **sequence of gates**:

1. **Application received** – no process before this.
2. **Preliminary credit check** – done exactly once, immediately after application.
3. **Documents gathered** – required for every case, after the credit check.
4. **QA review** – cannot happen before documents are complete.
5. **Terms authorized** – cannot happen before QA review.
6. **Offer package assembled** – immediately after QA review.
7. **Funds transferred** – immediately after offer package.
8. **Customer notified** – before and after transfer.

This ordering protects the business in several ways:

- **No skipped credit checks:** because `exactly_one(Preliminary_Credit_Check)` and `succession(Preliminary_Credit_Check, Gather_Additional_Documents)` mean the credit check is a mandatory early step.
- **No approvals on incomplete files:** because `coexistence(Gather_Additional_Documents, Authorize_Contract_Terms)` and `precedence(Quality_Assurance_Review, Authorize_Contract_Terms)` require both documentation and QA review before contract authorization.
- **No unauthorized fund transfers:** because `chainresponse(Assemble_Loan_Offer_Package, Transfer_Funds)` ensures the transfer only occurs after the offer package is created, and in a wider sense the process flow prevents disbursement before the application.
- **No compliance shortcuts:** because `absence(Proceed_Without_Compliance)` explicitly forbids a bypass activity.
- **Transparent customer experience:** because `altresponse` and `altprecedence` around transfer and notification ensure the customer is informed.

## Likely business goals and regulatory drivers

A process with this many mandatory constraints usually reflects industries with strong regulatory oversight. For a loan application process, the likely drivers include:

### 1. Regulatory compliance

- **KYC/AML rules** require collecting and verifying customer information, income, and source of funds.
- **Consumer protection regulations** require that customers understand the loan terms before they are finalized and before funds are transferred.
- **Fair lending laws** require consistent credit evaluation and documentation, not ad hoc approvals.

### 2. Risk management

- Lending money creates credit risk. The model ensures a preliminary credit check is done before further steps.
- Quality assurance reviews reduce operational risk: errors in interest rates, terms, or legal documents are caught before disbursement.

### 3. Auditability and transparency

- Because every case must start with an application and follow the same order, the bank can audit every loan.
- The forbidden compliance shortcut means there is no “off-book” path to approve a loan.

### 4. Operational efficiency

- Exactly one credit check avoids redundant work.
- Fixed handoffs between QA, offer package assembly, and transfer reduce delays and decision ambiguity.
- Customer notifications around transfers improve trust and reduce complaints.

## A note on strict DECLARE interpretation

If the model is interpreted using formal DECLARE semantics, a few constraints appear problematic. For example, `chainprecedence(Authorize_Contract_Terms, Preliminary_Credit_Check)` would strictly mean that contract authorization must occur immediately before the credit check, which reverses the natural loan lifecycle. Similarly, `noncoexistence(Transfer_Funds, Receive_Application)` would formally forbid both activities from appearing in the same trace, which contradicts the intended process.

In practice, these are likely modeling mistakes or shorthand for intended business rules such as:

- “Do not authorize terms before the credit check has been completed.”
- “Do not disburse funds before the application has been received.”

Despite those issues, the overall model communicates a clear and sensible idea: a loan application process must be **started, checked, documented, reviewed, authorized, funded, and communicated** in a controlled, compliant order.
