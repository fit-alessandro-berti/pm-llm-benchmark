###  **Step-by-Step Loan Approval Process**

The DECLARE model describes a **gated, phased workflow** designed to minimize risk while maintaining operational clarity. Here’s how it unfolds in practice:

2. **Initial Risk Assessment (`response`, `exactly_one`)**  
   Following intake, the system mandates a `Preliminary_Credit_Check` exactly once. This is the first substantive decision gate, evaluating baseline creditworthiness before any resources are committed.

3. **Document Collection (`succession`, `existence`)**  
   Depending on the credit assessment, `Gather_Additional_Documents` is triggered. The model ensures this step occurs at least once and follows the credit check, guaranteeing that underwriters have verified income, identity, and collateral evidence before proceeding.

4. **Offer Preparation (`responded_existence`, `chainsuccession`)**  
   Once documentation is complete, `Assemble_Loan_Offer_Package` is generated. The model ties this activity to subsequent verification steps, ensuring no offer moves forward without oversight.

5. **Quality Assurance (`precedence`, `altsuccession`)**  
   Before any commitment, `Quality_Assurance_Review` validates the offer against internal policies, pricing matrices, and regulatory templates. The `precedence` constraint guarantees QA must occur before final approval, acting as a compliance firewall.

6. **Final Authorization (`precedence`, `chainprecedence`)**  
   `Authorize_Contract_Terms` represents the binding commitment. The model tightly couples this step to earlier phases (via `chainprecedence`), ensuring authorization is directly traceable to the credit assessment and QA validation that justified it.

7. **Disbursement & Closure (`chainresponse`, `altresponse`/`altprecedence`, `nonchainsuccession`)**  
   After authorization, `Transfer_Funds` executes. The model links funding to the offer assembly (`chainresponse`) and ensures `Notify_Customer` follows appropriately. Crucially, `nonchainsuccession` prevents authorization from jumping straight to notification, mandating that the actual fund movement occurs in between.
