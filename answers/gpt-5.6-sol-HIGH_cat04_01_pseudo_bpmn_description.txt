## Process overview

The process handles a customer request from receipt through validation or feasibility analysis, possible management approval and rework, invoicing, and final confirmation.

The main distinction is between:

- **Standard requests**, which follow predefined validation and operational checks.
- **Custom requests**, which require a feasibility decision and, if feasible, preparation of a custom quotation.

Only requests that successfully complete one of those paths continue to the approval and invoicing stage. An infeasible custom request is rejected and ends immediately.

---

## Step-by-step explanation

### 1. Start and request intake

**Start Event  Task A: “Receive Customer Request”**

This marks the arrival of a new customer request. Task A captures the information needed to process it, such as:

- Customer details
- Requested products or services
- Quantities and delivery expectations
- Whether the request is standard or customized
- Commercial or technical requirements

### 2. Determine request type

**XOR Gateway: “Check Request Type”**

An exclusive, or XOR, gateway selects exactly one outgoing path:

- **Type = Standard**  standard-processing path
- **Type = Custom**  custom-processing path

The paths are mutually exclusive; the same process instance does not normally execute both.

---

# Standard request path

### 3. Standard validation

**Task B1: “Perform Standard Validation”**

This checks the request against existing rules and predefined offerings. Typical validation may include:

- Required data is complete
- Product or service codes are valid
- Requested quantities are allowed
- Standard pricing and terms apply
- The request conforms to normal business policies

Unlike a custom request, no unique technical design or feasibility investigation is expected.

### 4. Run operational checks in parallel

**AND Gateway: “Run Parallel Checks”**

An AND gateway starts all outgoing branches simultaneously or independently. It creates two parallel work items:

- **Task C1: “Credit Check”**  
  Determines whether the customer has sufficient credit, acceptable payment history, or appropriate payment terms.

- **Task C2: “Inventory Check”**  
  Determines whether the requested goods or capacity are available.

Because this is a parallel split, both checks are required. This is different from an XOR gateway: the process does not choose between credit and inventory checks.

### 5. Synchronize the parallel checks

**AND Join: “All Parallel Checks Completed”**

The join waits until both C1 and C2 have completed. The process cannot continue merely because one check has finished.

In a complete BPMN model, the outcomes of these checks should also be evaluated. For example, separate handling may be needed if:

- Credit is declined
- Inventory is unavailable
- Only part of the requested quantity can be supplied

The pseudo-model currently states only that the checks must complete, not what happens if their results are unfavorable.

### 6. Calculate delivery date

**Task D: “Calculate Delivery Date”**

After both checks finish, the organization calculates a delivery or fulfillment date based on factors such as:

- Available inventory
- Production or service capacity
- Shipping lead time
- Requested quantity
- Customer location
- Agreed service levels

Task D represents the standard path’s final preparation step before the common approval decision.

---

# Custom request path

### 7. Analyze custom feasibility

**Task B2: “Perform Custom Feasibility Analysis”**

This determines whether the requested customization can be delivered. The analysis may cover:

- Technical viability
- Engineering or design effort
- Required materials and capacity
- Regulatory or contractual constraints
- Cost and profitability
- Delivery lead time
- Dependencies on suppliers or specialist resources

This is more extensive than standard validation because the request falls outside the normal predefined offering.

### 8. Decide whether the customization is feasible

**XOR Gateway: “Is Customization Feasible?”**

Exactly one of two outcomes is selected.

#### If feasible

**Task E1: “Prepare Custom Quotation”**

A tailored quotation is produced. It may include:

- Custom pricing
- Scope and specifications
- Assumptions and exclusions
- Special terms
- Estimated delivery schedule
- Validity period
- Any technical or commercial conditions

After E1, the custom path proceeds to the common approval stage.

#### If not feasible

**Task E2: “Send Rejection Notice”  End Event**

The customer is informed that the organization cannot fulfill the customization. The notice may include a reason or possible alternatives.

This is a terminal path. It does not continue to approval, invoicing, or confirmation because no viable offer or order exists.

---

# Convergence of standard and custom paths

After either:

- **Task D** on the standard path, or
- **Task E1** on the feasible custom path,

the process proceeds to the common approval decision.

Conceptually, this point is an **XOR merge**: only one incoming branch is active for a given request, so the merge should not wait for both D and E1.

An AND join would be incorrect here because it would wait for both the standard and custom branches, even though the initial request-type gateway selected only one of them.

---

# Approval and invoicing

### 9. Determine whether approval is required

**XOR Gateway: “Is Approval Needed?”**

Business rules determine whether management approval is necessary.

Approval could be required because of:

- High order or quotation value
- Low margin or nonstandard discount
- Unusual payment terms
- Credit risk
- Custom technical or contractual commitments
- Long or exceptional delivery commitments
- Policy exceptions
- Regulatory or legal concerns

#### If no approval is needed

The request goes directly to:

**Task G: “Generate Final Invoice”**

#### If approval is needed

The request proceeds to:

**Task F: “Obtain Manager Approval”**

The manager reviews the relevant validation, delivery, feasibility, pricing, risk, and commercial information.

### 10. Decide whether approval is granted

**XOR Gateway: “Is Approval Granted?”**

The approval outcome determines the next action.

#### If approval is granted

The process proceeds to:

**Task G: “Generate Final Invoice”**

#### If approval is not granted

The process proceeds to:

**Task H: “Re-evaluate Conditions”**

This is a rework step rather than an immediate process termination. The organization may revise:

- Pricing or discounts
- Scope of customization
- Quantities
- Delivery commitment
- Payment terms
- Technical assumptions
- Risk mitigations
- Other conditions that caused the rejection

---

# Rework loop

After Task H, the process loops back to the branch-specific preparation step:

- For a **custom request**, return to **Task E1: “Prepare Custom Quotation”**
- For a **standard request**, return to **Task D: “Calculate Delivery Date”**

This preserves the original request type:

- A custom request receives a revised custom quotation.
- A standard request receives revised delivery or related standard conditions.

After E1 or D is performed again, the process returns to the common approval-needed gateway. Consequently:

1. The revised conditions are prepared.
2. Approval requirements are evaluated again.
3. If approval is still required, manager approval is requested again.
4. If approval is rejected again, another rework cycle may occur.
5. The loop ends when approval is granted or when revised conditions no longer require approval.

The model does not currently show a limit or alternative exit from this loop. In practice, a business may add:

- A maximum number of rework cycles
- Escalation to a higher-level manager
- Customer renegotiation
- Cancellation or rejection if acceptable conditions cannot be reached

The route from H to D or E1 should ideally be represented by an explicit XOR gateway such as **“Original Request Type?”**, unless the workflow engine retains the request type and performs this routing automatically.

---

# Finalization

### 11. Generate final invoice

**Task G: “Generate Final Invoice”**

Both successful approval paths converge here:

- Approval was not required, or
- Approval was required and granted

This convergence is also conceptually an XOR merge because only one of those alternatives is active.

Task G creates the final billing or commercial document based on the validated or approved terms.

A business-modeling consideration is that some organizations would generate an **order confirmation, quotation, or pro forma invoice** at this point rather than a final invoice, especially if fulfillment has not yet occurred. The exact document should reflect the organization’s billing policy.

### 12. Send customer confirmation

**Task I: “Send Confirmation to Customer”**

The customer receives confirmation containing relevant final details, such as:

- Accepted scope
- Price
- Delivery date
- Payment terms
- Invoice or reference number
- Custom specifications, where applicable

### 13. End event

The process ends successfully after the confirmation is sent.

A separate earlier end event exists for infeasible custom requests after the rejection notice.

## Gateway summary

| Gateway | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Check Request Type | XOR split | Selects either the standard or custom path |
| Run Parallel Checks | AND split | Starts both credit and inventory checks |
| All Parallel Checks Completed | AND join | Waits for both checks to finish |
| Is Customization Feasible? | XOR split | Selects quotation preparation or rejection |
| Standard/Custom convergence | Implied XOR merge | Merges whichever request-type path was selected |
| Is Approval Needed? | XOR split | Selects direct invoicing or manager approval |
| Is Approval Granted? | XOR split | Selects invoicing or rework |
| Rework routing | Implied XOR split | Returns to D for standard requests or E1 for custom requests |
| Pre-invoice convergence | Implied XOR merge | Merges the no-approval and approved paths |

---

## Key differences between standard and custom requests

| Standard request | Custom request |
|---|---|
| Uses predefined validation rules | Requires a specific feasibility analysis |
| Performs credit and inventory checks in parallel | Evaluates technical and commercial feasibility |
| Calculates a delivery date | Prepares a tailored quotation |
| Rework returns to delivery-date calculation | Rework returns to quotation preparation |
| No explicit standard-path rejection is shown | An infeasible request is explicitly rejected and terminated |

---

## Important modeling clarifications

1. **The standard and custom branches should merge through an XOR merge**, not an AND join, because only one branch is active.
2. **The H-to-D/E1 loop needs an explicit routing rule** based on the original request type.
3. **Unfavorable credit or inventory results are not modeled.** Additional decision gateways may be required.
4. **Approval rejection always causes rework in the current model.** No cancellation or final rejection option is shown.
5. **The rework loop is potentially unlimited** unless escalation, timeout, or retry-limit rules are added.
6. **Custom infeasibility is the only explicit early termination.** A feasible custom request and a completed standard request both proceed into the same approval and invoicing flow.
