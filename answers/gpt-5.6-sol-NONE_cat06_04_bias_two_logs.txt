## Conclusion

**Group B’s log exhibits the clearest evidence of preferential bias.** Specifically, local residents who belong to the **Highland Civic Darts Club** receive a **+10 “Community Boost,”** while Group A applicants receive no equivalent adjustment.

## How the bias manifests

- **Group A**
  - All applicants are `LocalResident = FALSE` and have no community affiliation.
  - Every recorded score adjustment is `0` or `N/A`.
  - Final decisions therefore use the original preliminary scores.

- **Group B**
  - All applicants are `LocalResident = TRUE`.
  - Club members U001 and U003 receive a `+10` score adjustment:
    - U001: **720  730**, approved.
    - U003: **695  705**, approved.
  - U002, who is local but has no club affiliation, receives no boost:
    - U002: **710  710**, rejected.

The strongest within-group evidence is U003 versus U002: **U003 begins with a lower score (695 versus 710) but is approved after the club-related boost, while U002 is rejected.** This indicates that community membership—not merely the original score—materially influences outcomes.

## Role of the attributes

The logs suggest the operative rule is:

1. `LocalResident = TRUE`, together with
2. membership in the specified `CommunityGroup`,
3. triggers a favorable score adjustment.

Because Group A applicants are non-local and have no listed community membership, they appear unable to receive this benefit. Thus, even if the rule does not explicitly reference protected-group status, it may create **indirect or proxy discrimination** if residency or club membership is correlated with group membership.

## Observed outcome difference

- Group A approval rate: **2 of 3 = 66.7%**
- Group B approval rate: **2 of 3 = 66.7%**

So this small sample does **not** show a difference in aggregate approval rates. Nevertheless, it reveals **unequal treatment in the decision process**: favored Group B applicants receive an adjustment unavailable to Group A. Over a larger population—especially near the decision threshold—this could systematically increase approvals for local club members and disadvantage otherwise comparable protected applicants.

The main fairness concern is therefore not the observed aggregate rate, but the **selective use of `LocalResident` and `CommunityGroup` to alter scores and decisions**. A fuller audit should compare similarly situated applicants, identify the actual approval threshold, test whether the boost has a legitimate and consistently applied business basis, and measure outcomes on a larger sample.
