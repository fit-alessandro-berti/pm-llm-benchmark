The model describes a tightly controlled loan process in which mandatory intake, credit assessment, documentation, quality review, authorization, disbursement, and notification activities are governed by DECLARE constraints. In DECLARE, these constraints are declarative: they define what must or must not hold without necessarily prescribing one complete workflow.

## Practical role of the activities

- **Receive Application (A):** Establishes a formal case, customer identity, consent, and audit trail.
- **Preliminary Credit Check (B):** Performs an initial creditworthiness, affordability, fraud, or eligibility assessment.
- **Gather Additional Documents (C):** Obtains evidence needed for underwriting and regulatory checks.
- **Assemble Loan Offer Package (D):** Produces the proposed pricing, repayment schedule, conditions, and disclosures.
- **Quality Assurance Review (E):** Verifies completeness, consistency, policy compliance, and accuracy.
- **Authorize Contract Terms (F):** Records formal approval by an authorized person or system.
- **Transfer Funds (G):** Executes loan disbursement.
- **Notify Customer (H):** Communicates a decision, terms, funding status, or required next steps.
- **Proceed Without Compliance (Z):** Represents a prohibited bypass of mandatory controls.

## Meaning of the constraints

### Cardinality and starting conditions

- **Existence(A)** and **Init(A):** Every case must contain an application receipt, and it must be the first event. This creates a case record before processing begins.
- **Existence(C):** Additional-document gathering must occur at least once. Operationally, this model assumes that every application requires supplementary evidence.
- **Absence(Z):** A compliance bypass may never occur.
- **Exactly one(B):** Every case has one—and only one—preliminary credit check. This prevents both omission and unnecessary repeated checks, which may matter for cost, customer impact, consistency, and credit-bureau rules.

Together with **Response(A,B)**, receipt of an application guarantees a later preliminary credit check. Because A is mandatory and B occurs exactly once, every case receives one controlled initial assessment.

### Document, review, and authorization controls

- **Succession(B,C):** B and C are mutually linked: every credit check must eventually be followed by document gathering, and every document-gathering event must have a prior credit check.
- **Alternate succession(C,E):** Each C must be followed by E before another C occurs, and every E must be associated with a preceding C without an intervening E. This organizes document collection and review into alternating cycles rather than allowing unchecked accumulation.
- **Coexistence(C,F):** C and F either both occur or neither occurs. Since C is mandatory, authorization is effectively mandatory as well.
- **Precedence(E,F):** Authorization cannot occur unless a quality review occurred earlier.
- **Chain precedence(F,B):** Every authorization must be immediately preceded by a preliminary credit check.

These rules aim to prevent authorization without evidence and review. They create checkpoints at underwriting, QA, and formal approval.

### Offer preparation and disbursement

- **Responded existence(D,E):** If an offer package is assembled, a QA review must occur somewhere in the case, either before or after D.
- **Chain succession(E,D):** Every QA review must be immediately followed by offer-package assembly, and every offer-package assembly must be immediately preceded by QA.
- **Chain response(D,G):** Every assembled offer package must be immediately followed by fund transfer.

This produces the local sequence:

\[
E \rightarrow D \rightarrow G
\]

whenever E or D occurs. Conceptually, it represents a reviewed package moving directly into execution, minimizing opportunities for an unreviewed alteration between QA and disbursement.

### Notification controls

- **Alternate response(G,H):** Each fund transfer must eventually be followed by notification before another transfer occurs.
- **Alternate precedence(H,G):** Each notification requires a preceding transfer since the previous notification.

Taken together, G and H alternate in pairs: a transfer must lead to a customer notification, and a notification cannot occur without a relevant prior transfer. This supports customer transparency and proof of disbursement.

- **Non-succession(H,B):** Once the customer has been notified, no preliminary credit check may occur later. The process may not return to initial underwriting after final communication.
- **Non-chain succession(F,H):** An authorization may not be followed immediately by notification. Some intervening execution step is required, preventing the system from communicating completion directly from approval without operational processing.

### Preventing disbursement before intake

- **Non-coexistence(G,A):** G and A may not occur in the same trace.

This deserves special attention. It does **not** merely mean “funds cannot be transferred before the application is received.” Since A is mandatory in every trace, non-coexistence makes G impossible in every valid trace. Thus, as encoded, the model prohibits all fund transfers—not just premature ones.

If the business intention is that disbursement may occur only after receipt, the appropriate relation would normally be:

- **Precedence(A,G)**: every G must have an earlier A,

rather than **Non-coexistence(G,A)**.

Because A is also the initial activity, precedence would correctly ensure that no transfer predates application intake.

## Overall lifecycle represented

Ignoring the apparent disbursement-modeling issue, the intended lifecycle seems to be:

1. Receive and register the application.
2. Perform exactly one preliminary credit check.
3. Gather supporting documentation.
4. Review the evidence and proposed transaction.
5. Authorize the contract terms.
6. Assemble the reviewed offer package.
7. Transfer funds.
8. Notify the customer.
9. Never bypass compliance controls or restart initial credit assessment after notification.

The model emphasizes documented handoffs and separation between assessment, QA, authorization, execution, and communication.

## Compliance and business objectives

Such constraints could be motivated by:

- **Know Your Customer and anti-money-laundering duties:** Ensuring a formal case and supporting evidence exist before approval or payment.
- **Responsible lending and affordability rules:** Requiring a credit assessment and evidence-based underwriting.
- **Consumer-protection requirements:** Supporting accurate terms, disclosures, and timely notification.
- **Internal-control and audit requirements:** Making each decision traceable and preventing informal compliance overrides.
- **Fraud prevention:** Blocking payments without a registered application, supporting records, and approval.
- **Operational risk management:** Requiring QA and authorization before execution and reducing ambiguous rework.
- **Data integrity:** Preventing duplicate preliminary checks and requiring controlled document-review cycles.
- **Segregation of duties:** Separating intake, underwriting, review, authorization, payment, and communication.
- **Customer experience:** Avoiding contradictory decisions and ensuring each transfer is followed by a notification.
- **Cost control:** Limiting redundant credit checks and uncontrolled document collection.

## Important consistency concerns

Beyond the G/A issue, the full set appears internally problematic.

Because **C is mandatory**, coexistence makes **F mandatory**. Every F must be immediately preceded by B. Since B occurs exactly once, the trace contains the adjacent pair:

\[
B,F
\]

But **Succession(B,C)** requires a C after B. Meanwhile, every C must be followed, before another C, by E; and chain succession then forces:

\[
C \ldots E,D,G
\]

Yet G is forbidden because A is mandatory and G cannot coexist with A. Therefore, the model as written likely has **no valid complete trace**.

There is also a conceptual ordering tension: the practical narrative suggests credit check  documents  QA  authorization, while **ChainPrecedence(F,B)** requires B immediately before F, leaving no room for documents or QA between that particular B and authorization. If authorization is meant to follow reviewed documentation, a weaker precedence relation—or a different source activity—would be more plausible.

## Conclusion

The model’s intended policy is clear: every loan begins with formal intake, receives one initial credit assessment, is supported by documentation, passes QA and authorization controls, and ends with controlled disbursement and customer communication, while compliance bypasses are absolutely forbidden. These are sensible objectives for auditability, responsible lending, fraud prevention, and consumer protection.

However, stringent rules are useful only if they are mutually satisfiable and encode the intended semantics. In particular, **NonCoexistence(G,A)** should likely be replaced by **Precedence(A,G)**, and the immediate-predecessor rule between B and F should be reconsidered. Formal satisfiability checking and sample-trace validation would be essential before deploying this model as an operational compliance specification.
