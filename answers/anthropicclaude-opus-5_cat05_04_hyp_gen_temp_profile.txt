# Temporal Profile Audit — Insurance Claim Handling

## 1. Anomalies Detected in the Temporal Profile

Below, each activity pair is scored by its **coefficient of variation (CV = STDEV / AVG)** and by whether the magnitude of the interval is plausible for the work involved.

| Pair | AVG | STDEV | CV | Observation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (R, A) | 1 h | 10 min | 0.17 | Plausible, but tight for a manual assignment step — smells like a scheduled/automatic dispatcher. |
| (R, E) | 24 h | 8 h | 0.33 | Reasonable, used as the "healthy" baseline for comparison. |
| **(R, P)** | **25 h** | **1 h** | **0.04** | **Anomaly: near-deterministic.** Approval lands at almost exactly the same offset from receipt for every claim, regardless of complexity, amount, or evaluation outcome. Real decision work is never this uniform. |
| **(A, C)** | **2 h** | **1 h** | 0.50 | **Anomaly: ordering/scope violation.** AssignClose (2 h) is *shorter* than AssignEvaluate implied by RE (~23 h) and far shorter than RP (25 h). Closure cannot legitimately precede evaluation/approval, so this pair must be dominated by a sub-population of short-circuited claims. |
| **(E, N)** | **5 min** | **1 min** | 0.20 | **Anomaly: too fast and too regular.** Notification is supposed to follow *approval*; here it follows evaluation almost instantly, implying P is skipped or is a no-op rubber stamp. |
| (E, C) | 1 h | 50 min | 0.83 | High relative dispersion; also inconsistent with (E,N)=5 min + (N,C)=30 min  35 min, so a second, faster closure path exists alongside the modelled one. |
| **(P, N)** | **7 d** | **2 d** | 0.29 | **Anomaly: extreme absolute delay.** Notification is a low-effort communication step, yet it dominates total cycle time (7 d vs. ~25 h for everything before it). Also internally contradictory with (E,N) = 5 min. |
| (N, C) | 30 min | 5 min | 0.17 | Very tight — suggests automated closure triggered by the notification event. |

**Cross-pair inconsistencies (arguably the strongest signal):**

- **Additivity is violated.** (R,P)=25 h + (P,N)=7 d  RN  8 d, but (R,E)=24 h + (E,N)=5 min  RN  24 h. Two mutually exclusive populations of claims are being averaged into one model.
- **Monotonicity is violated.** For the prescribed order RAEPNC, every interval from an earlier activity must be  intervals implied for downstream ones. (A,C)=2 h < (R,P)=25 h breaks this.
- **Sub-second/single-digit-minute pairs next to multi-day pairs** indicate a mixture of automated and manual/batched execution inside a single "process".

## 2. Hypotheses

**H1 — Derived or truncated timestamps create artificial determinism (explains RP, CV = 0.04).**
The Receive timestamp may be reconstructed from `claims.submission_date` (a DATE, hence midnight), while Approve is written by a nightly batch job at a fixed hour. The near-constant 25 h offset is then an artefact of two clock-aligned events, not of business behaviour.

**H2 — Approval is an automated/bulk rubber stamp.**
A scheduled job approves everything queued in the last day (auto-approval rule, e.g., below an amount threshold, or a straight-through-processing rule), producing constant latency and near-zero variance.

**H3 — Notification is batched, not event-driven (explains PN = 7 d ± 2 d).**
A weekly correspondence/print/mail run releases notifications. A weekly cadence produces exactly a ~7-day mean with multi-day spread depending on where in the cycle approval landed. Alternatively: an outbound queue backlog, third-party mail vendor SLA, or a step that waits for a document/signature that is not modelled.

**H4 — Two distinct process variants are pooled.**
Variant 1 (fast/digital): RAENC within a day, P absent or instantaneous  drives (E,N)=5 min and (E,C)=1 h.
Variant 2 (regulated/manual): RAEP…7 days…NC  drives (P,N). Averaging them yields a profile that describes neither.

**H5 — Premature or administrative closure (explains AC = 2 h).**
Some claims are withdrawn, rejected as ineligible, duplicated, or cancelled shortly after assignment, and are closed without E/P. If these are logged with the same `activity='C'` as normal completions, they contaminate the profile.

**H6 — Skipped or missing control steps.**
EN in 5 minutes suggests the Approve event is either never logged (logging gap in a system component) or is emitted by the same transaction as Evaluate — i.e. the four-eyes/approval control is not actually being exercised.

**H7 — Resource / capacity effects.**
A small number of adjusters (or a single specialization/region) may hold a queue, producing the long right tail in PN and the wide dispersion in EC. Weekend/holiday effects and shift patterns can also manufacture "7 days" from a 5-business-day cycle.

**H8 — Data quality defects.**
Backfilled or migrated events inserted with load-time timestamps, timezone mixing (fixed ±k-hour offsets), duplicate events, and out-of-order/negative durations would all distort both mean and STDEV.

**H9 — Stale model.**
The profile may have been trained on an old period; a process change (new rules engine, outsourcing of notifications) makes it no longer descriptive of current data.

## 3. SQL Verification Plan (PostgreSQL)

### 3.0 Reusable foundation: materialise the profile and per-claim intervals

```sql
-- Reference profile as data, so every check can be parameterised by ZETA.
CREATE TEMP VIEW temporal_profile AS
SELECT * FROM (VALUES
  ('R','A',   3600.0,    600.0),
  ('R','E',  86400.0,  28800.0),
  ('R','P',  90000.0,   3600.0),
  ('A','C',   7200.0,   3600.0),
  ('E','N',    300.0,     60.0),
  ('E','C',   3600.0,   3000.0),
  ('P','N', 604800.0, 172800.0),
  ('N','C',   1800.0,    300.0)
) AS t(a1, a2, avg_s, stdev_s);

-- First occurrence of each activity per claim (use MIN for "eventually follows" semantics;
-- swap to MAX to test sensitivity to rework loops).
CREATE TEMP VIEW first_evt AS
SELECT claim_id, activity, MIN(timestamp) AS ts, COUNT(*) AS occurrences
FROM claim_events
GROUP BY claim_id, activity;

CREATE TEMP VIEW pair_intervals AS
SELECT p.a1, p.a2,
       f1.claim_id,
       f1.ts AS ts1,
       f2.ts AS ts2,
       EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (f2.ts - f1.ts))            AS delta_s,
       p.avg_s, p.stdev_s,
       (EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (f2.ts - f1.ts)) - p.avg_s)
         / NULLIF(p.stdev_s, 0)                       AS z
FROM temporal_profile p
JOIN first_evt f1 ON f1.activity = p.a1
JOIN first_evt f2 ON f2.activity = p.a2 AND f2.claim_id = f1.claim_id;
```

### 3.1 Is the model even descriptive? (Re-derive it from the log)

```sql
SELECT a1, a2,
       COUNT(*)                                                   AS n,
       ROUND(AVG(delta_s))                                        AS emp_avg_s,
       ROUND(STDDEV_SAMP(delta_s))                                AS emp_stdev_s,
       ROUND(MIN(delta_s))                                        AS min_s,
       ROUND(PERCENTILE_CONT(0.50) WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY delta_s))::bigint AS p50,
       ROUND(PERCENTILE_CONT(0.95) WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY delta_s))::bigint AS p95,
       ROUND(MAX(delta_s))                                        AS max_s,
       MAX(avg_s)   AS model_avg_s,
       MAX(stdev_s) AS model_stdev_s,
       ROUND(STDDEV_SAMP(delta_s) / NULLIF(AVG(delta_s),0), 3)     AS emp_cv,
       COUNT(*) FILTER (WHERE delta_s < 0)                         AS negative_durations
FROM pair_intervals
GROUP BY a1, a2
ORDER BY a1, a2;
```
*Interpretation:* if `emp_avg_s` / `emp_stdev_s` diverge materially from the model, the profile is stale or mis-fitted (H9); `negative_durations > 0` proves ordering/data defects (H8).

### 3.2 Generic outlier extraction (ZETA-based)

```sql
-- ZETA = 3 here; vary it (2, 4, 6) to see how outlier counts scale.
SELECT a1, a2, claim_id, ts1, ts2,
       ROUND(delta_s)  AS delta_s,
       ROUND(z::numeric, 2) AS z_score,
       CASE WHEN z > 0 THEN 'TOO_SLOW' ELSE 'TOO_FAST' END AS direction
FROM pair_intervals
WHERE ABS(z) > 3
ORDER BY ABS(z) DESC
LIMIT 200;

-- Which claims are systemic offenders across many pairs?
SELECT claim_id,
       COUNT(*)                                    AS violating_pairs,
       STRING_AGG(a1 || '->' || a2, ',' ORDER BY a1, a2) AS pairs,
       ROUND(MAX(ABS(z))::numeric, 2)              AS worst_z
FROM pair_intervals
WHERE ABS(z) > 3
GROUP BY claim_id
HAVING COUNT(*) >= 2
ORDER BY violating_pairs DESC, worst_z DESC;
```

### 3.3 H1/H2 — Is RP artificially deterministic?

```sql
-- (a) Dispersion, mode concentration, and rounding signature.
WITH rp AS (SELECT * FROM pair_intervals WHERE a1='R' AND a2='P')
SELECT COUNT(*)                                           AS n,
       COUNT(DISTINCT ROUND(delta_s))                     AS distinct_second_values,
       COUNT(DISTINCT ROUND(delta_s/60))                  AS distinct_minute_values,
       ROUND(STDDEV_SAMP(delta_s)/NULLIF(AVG(delta_s),0),4) AS cv,
       COUNT(*) FILTER (WHERE MOD(delta_s::bigint, 3600) = 0) AS exact_hour_multiples,
       COUNT(*) FILTER (WHERE MOD(delta_s::bigint,   60) = 0) AS exact_minute_multiples
FROM rp;

-- (b) Clock alignment: are R and P pinned to fixed times of day?
SELECT activity,
       DATE_PART('hour', timestamp)                            AS hh,
       COUNT(*)                                                AS n,
       COUNT(*) FILTER (WHERE timestamp::time = TIME '00:00:00') AS midnight_exact,
       COUNT(*) FILTER (WHERE DATE_PART('second', timestamp) = 0
                         AND DATE_PART('minute', timestamp) = 0) AS top_of_hour
FROM claim_events
WHERE activity IN ('R','P')
GROUP BY activity, hh
ORDER BY activity, hh;
```
*Interpretation:* if R clusters at `00:00:00` (derived from `submission_date`) and P clusters in one or two hours, the low STDEV is an artefact (H1). A dominant `resource` on P plus tight clustering supports H2.

```sql
-- (c) Who/what performs P, and does auto-approval track claim_amount?
SELECT ce.resource,
       COUNT(*)                                      AS approvals,
       ROUND(AVG(EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (ce.timestamp - r.ts)))) AS avg_r_to_p_s,
       ROUND(STDDEV_SAMP(EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (ce.timestamp - r.ts)))) AS stdev_r_to_p_s,
       ROUND(AVG(c.claim_amount), 2)                 AS avg_amount,
       ROUND(MAX(c.claim_amount), 2)                 AS max_amount
FROM claim_events ce
JOIN first_evt r ON r.claim_id = ce.claim_id AND r.activity = 'R'
JOIN claims   c  ON c.claim_id = ce.claim_id
WHERE ce.activity = 'P'
GROUP BY ce.resource
ORDER BY approvals DESC;
```
*A resource with hundreds of approvals, ~0 STDEV and a hard `max_amount` ceiling is an auto-approval rule (H2).*

### 3.4 H3/H7 — Is PN batched or backlogged?

```sql
-- (a) Distribution of the delay in days + day-of-week signature of N.
SELECT FLOOR(delta_s/86400)::int                    AS delay_days,
       COUNT(*)                                     AS n,
       ROUND(100.0*COUNT(*)/SUM(COUNT(*)) OVER (), 1) AS pct
FROM pair_intervals
WHERE a1='P' AND a2='N'
GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY 1;

SELECT TO_CHAR(timestamp,'Dy') AS dow, DATE_PART('hour', timestamp) AS hh, COUNT(*)
FROM claim_events WHERE activity='N'
GROUP BY 1,2 ORDER BY COUNT(*) DESC LIMIT 20;

-- (b) Batch fingerprint: many notifications inside the same minute by the same resource.
SELECT DATE_TRUNC('minute', timestamp) AS batch_minute,
       resource,
       COUNT(*)                        AS notifications_in_minute,
       COUNT(DISTINCT claim_id)        AS claims
FROM claim_events
WHERE activity = 'N'
GROUP BY 1, 2
HAVING COUNT(*) > 10
ORDER BY notifications_in_minute DESC
LIMIT 50;

-- (c) Backlog view: open notification queue over time.
SELECT DATE_TRUNC('week', p.ts) AS approval_week,
       COUNT(*)                                                   AS approved,
       COUNT(n.ts)                                                AS notified,
       ROUND(AVG(EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (n.ts - p.ts))/86400.0), 2)   AS avg_delay_days,
       ROUND(MAX(EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (n.ts - p.ts))/86400.0), 2)   AS max_delay_days
FROM first_evt p
LEFT JOIN first_evt n ON n.claim_id = p.claim_id AND n.activity = 'N'
WHERE p.activity = 'P'
GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY 1;
```
*A single dominant weekday/hour for N plus large per-minute batches confirms H3; a rising `avg_delay_days` trend indicates a growing backlog (H7).*

### 3.5 H4/H6 — Two variants, and is Approve being skipped?

```sql
-- Variant discovery: the ordered activity signature of each claim.
WITH trace AS (
  SELECT claim_id,
         STRING_AGG(activity, '->' ORDER BY timestamp, event_id) AS variant,
         MIN(timestamp) AS t_start, MAX(timestamp) AS t_end
  FROM claim_events
  GROUP BY claim_id
)
SELECT variant,
       COUNT(*)                                                     AS claims,
       ROUND(100.0*COUNT(*)/SUM(COUNT(*)) OVER (), 2)               AS pct,
       ROUND(AVG(EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (t_end - t_start))/3600.0), 2)  AS avg_cycle_hours
FROM trace
GROUP BY variant
ORDER BY claims DESC;

-- Claims notified without an approval, or notified before approval.
SELECT e.claim_id, e.ts AS evaluate_ts, p.ts AS approve_ts, n.ts AS notify_ts,
       ROUND(EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (n.ts - e.ts))) AS e_to_n_s,
       CASE WHEN p.ts IS NULL          THEN 'APPROVE_MISSING'
            WHEN n.ts < p.ts           THEN 'NOTIFY_BEFORE_APPROVE'
            ELSE 'OK' END              AS control_status
FROM first_evt e
JOIN first_evt n  ON n.claim_id = e.claim_id AND n.activity = 'N'
LEFT JOIN first_evt p ON p.claim_id = e.claim_id AND p.activity = 'P'
WHERE e.activity = 'E'
  AND (p.ts IS NULL OR n.ts < p.ts OR EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (n.ts - e.ts)) < 600);

-- Same-transaction fingerprint: E and P (or E and N) written within 1 second by one resource.
SELECT a.claim_id, a.activity AS act1, b.activity AS act2,
       a.resource, b.resource,
       EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (b.timestamp - a.timestamp)) AS gap_s
FROM claim_events a
JOIN claim_events b
  ON b.claim_id = a.claim_id
 AND b.timestamp >= a.timestamp
 AND b.event_id <> a.event_id
WHERE a.activity = 'E'
  AND b.activity IN ('P','N')
  AND b.timestamp - a.timestamp < INTERVAL '1 second'
ORDER BY a.claim_id;
```

### 3.6 H5 — Premature / administrative closure (AC)

```sql
-- Claims closed with no Evaluate and/or no Approve before closure.
WITH c AS (SELECT claim_id, MIN(timestamp) AS close_ts FROM claim_events WHERE activity='C' GROUP BY claim_id),
     a AS (SELECT claim_id, MIN(timestamp) AS assign_ts FROM claim_events WHERE activity='A' GROUP BY claim_id)
SELECT c.claim_id,
       a.assign_ts, c.close_ts,
       ROUND(EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (c.close_ts - a.assign_ts))) AS a_to_c_s,
       EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM claim_events x WHERE x.claim_id=c.claim_id AND x.activity='E' AND x.timestamp < c.close_ts) AS had_evaluate,
       EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM claim_events x WHERE x.claim_id=c.claim_id AND x.activity='P' AND x.timestamp < c.close_ts) AS had_approve,
       (SELECT STRING_AGG(DISTINCT x.additional_info, ' | ')
          FROM claim_events x WHERE x.claim_id=c.claim_id AND x.activity='C') AS close_notes,
       (SELECT x.resource FROM claim_events x
          WHERE x.claim_id=c.claim_id AND x.activity='C' ORDER BY x.timestamp LIMIT 1) AS close_resource
FROM c JOIN a ON a.claim_id = c.claim_id
WHERE EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (c.close_ts - a.assign_ts)) < 14400   -- < 4 hours
ORDER BY a_to_c_s;

-- How large is this sub-population, and does it explain the (A,C) profile?
SELECT (had_e AND had_p) AS complete_path,
       COUNT(*) AS claims,
       ROUND(AVG(a_to_c_s)) AS avg_a_to_c_s,
       ROUND(STDDEV_SAMP(a_to_c_s)) AS stdev_a_to_c_s
FROM (
  SELECT a.claim_id,
         EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (MIN(cl.timestamp) - MIN(a.timestamp))) AS a_to_c_s,
         BOOL_OR(e.activity='E') AS had_e,
         BOOL_OR(e.activity='P') AS had_p
  FROM claim_events a
  JOIN claim_events cl ON cl.claim_id = a.claim_id AND cl.activity='C'
  LEFT JOIN claim_events e ON e.claim_id = a.claim_id AND e.activity IN ('E','P')
  WHERE a.activity='A'
  GROUP BY a.claim_id
) s
GROUP BY 1;
```
*If the "incomplete path" bucket has a mean near 2 h while the complete path is much longer, the (A,C) profile entry is describing cancellations, not closures (H5) — and the profile should be split by variant.*

### 3.7 Correlating deviations with claim, resource, adjuster and customer dimensions

```sql
-- Deviation rate by claim type and amount band.
WITH viol AS (
  SELECT claim_id, a1, a2, z FROM pair_intervals WHERE ABS(z) > 3
)
SELECT c.claim_type,
       WIDTH_BUCKET(c.claim_amount, 0, 100000, 5) AS amount_band,
       COUNT(DISTINCT c.claim_id)                 AS claims,
       COUNT(DISTINCT v.claim_id)                 AS claims_with_violation,
       ROUND(100.0*COUNT(DISTINCT v.claim_id)/NULLIF(COUNT(DISTINCT c.claim_id),0), 1) AS pct_violating
FROM claims c
LEFT JOIN viol v ON v.claim_id = c.claim_id
GROUP BY 1, 2
ORDER BY pct_violating DESC NULLS LAST;

-- Deviation rate by the resource that performed the *first* activity of the pair.
SELECT pi.a1, pi.a2, ce.resource,
       COUNT(*)                                              AS observations,
       ROUND(AVG(pi.delta_s))                                AS avg_delta_s,
       ROUND(STDDEV_SAMP(pi.delta_s))                        AS stdev_delta_s,
       COUNT(*) FILTER (WHERE ABS(pi.z) > 3)                 AS violations,
       ROUND(100.0*COUNT(*) FILTER (WHERE ABS(pi.z) > 3)/COUNT(*), 1) AS pct_violating
FROM pair_intervals pi
JOIN claim_events ce
  ON ce.claim_id = pi.claim_id AND ce.activity = pi.a1 AND ce.timestamp = pi.ts1
GROUP BY 1,2,3
HAVING COUNT(*) >= 20
ORDER BY pct_violating DESC, observations DESC;

-- Link to the adjusters dimension. `claim_events` has no adjuster FK, so try both
-- plausible linkages and check coverage before trusting either.
WITH assign AS (
  SELECT ce.claim_id, ce.resource, ce.additional_info,
         NULLIF(REGEXP_REPLACE(ce.additional_info, '\D', '', 'g'), '')::int AS parsed_adjuster_id
  FROM claim_events ce
  WHERE ce.activity = 'A'
),
linked AS (
  SELECT a.claim_id,
         COALESCE(ad1.adjuster_id, ad2.adjuster_id)         AS adjuster_id,
         COALESCE(ad1.specialization, ad2.specialization)   AS specialization,
         COALESCE(ad1.region, ad2.region)                   AS region,
         COALESCE(ad1.name, ad2.name)                       AS adjuster_name
  FROM assign a
  LEFT JOIN adjusters ad1 ON ad1.name = a.resource
  LEFT JOIN adjusters ad2 ON ad2.adjuster_id = a.parsed_adjuster_id
)
SELECT l.region, l.specialization, l.adjuster_name, c.claim_type,
       COUNT(*)                                                   AS claims,
       ROUND(AVG(pi.delta_s)/86400.0, 2)                          AS avg_p_to_n_days,
       ROUND(MAX(pi.delta_s)/86400.0, 2)                          AS max_p_to_n_days,
       COUNT(*) FILTER (WHERE pi.z > 3)                           AS slow_outliers
FROM linked l
JOIN claims c        ON c.claim_id = l.claim_id
JOIN pair_intervals pi ON pi.claim_id = l.claim_id AND pi.a1='P' AND pi.a2='N'
GROUP BY 1,2,3,4
HAVING COUNT(*) >= 10
ORDER BY avg_p_to_n_days DESC;

-- Specialization/claim-type mismatch as a driver of delay.
SELECT c.claim_type, l.specialization,
       COUNT(*) AS claims,
       ROUND(AVG(EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (e.ts - a.ts))/3600.0), 2) AS avg_a_to_e_hours
FROM linked l
JOIN claims c   ON c.claim_id = l.claim_id
JOIN first_evt a ON a.claim_id = l.claim_id AND a.activity='A'
JOIN first_evt e ON e.claim_id = l.claim_id AND e.activity='E'
GROUP BY 1,2 ORDER BY avg_a_to_e_hours DESC;
```

```sql
-- Ordering violations, duplicates, and zero-length gaps.
SELECT 'out_of_order'  AS issue, COUNT(*) FROM pair_intervals WHERE delta_s < 0
UNION ALL
SELECT 'duplicate_activity', COUNT(*) FROM first_evt WHERE occurrences > 1
UNION ALL
SELECT 'zero_gap_events', COUNT(*) FROM (
   SELECT claim_id, timestamp FROM claim_events
   GROUP BY claim_id, timestamp HAVING COUNT(*) > 1) d
UNION ALL
SELECT 'all_events_same_ts', COUNT(*) FROM (
   SELECT claim_id FROM claim_events
   GROUP BY claim_id HAVING MIN(timestamp) = MAX(timestamp) AND COUNT(*) > 1) x
UNION ALL
SELECT 'receive_ts_ne_submission_date', COUNT(*)
FROM claim_events ce JOIN claims c USING (claim_id)
WHERE ce.activity='R' AND ce.timestamp::date <> c.submission_date;

-- Suspicious constant offsets (timezone / batch signature): are gaps concentrated
-- on exact hour boundaries?
SELECT a1, a2,
       ROUND(delta_s/3600.0) AS hours_bucket,
       COUNT(*) AS n
FROM pair_intervals
GROUP BY 1,2,3
HAVING COUNT(*) > 20
ORDER BY a1, a2, n DESC;

-- Regime change / stale-model check: month-over-month drift per pair.
SELECT a1, a2, DATE_TRUNC('month', ts1) AS month,
       COUNT(*) AS n,
       ROUND(AVG(delta_s)) AS avg_s,
       ROUND(STDDEV_SAMP(delta_s)) AS stdev_s
FROM pair_intervals
GROUP BY 1,2,3
ORDER BY a1, a2, month;
```

## 4. How to Read the Results

- **RP stays deterministic after removing midnight-truncated R events and batch-resource P events**  genuine rigid SLA scheduling worth business review. **If it collapses**  the anomaly was a timestamp artefact, and the profile entry should be recomputed on corrected data (or RP dropped as non-informative).
- **N events concentrate on one weekday/hour in large per-minute batches**  replace the single (P,N) Gaussian with a batch-aware model (or measure PN in "batch cycles"), and address the queue operationally rather than flagging every claim as anomalous.
- **Variant analysis shows two dominant traces**  split the temporal profile per variant (and per claim_type / amount band); a single pooled model will keep producing contradictory pairs such as (E,N)=5 min alongside (P,N)=7 d.
- **Short AC claims are overwhelmingly missing E and P**  separate "cancelled/withdrawn" closures from "completed" closures in the event log (distinct activity label or lifecycle attribute) before profiling.
- **EP gaps under 1 second with the same resource**  a control weakness, not a timing curiosity; escalate as a compliance finding rather than a process-mining anomaly.
- **Deviation rates concentrate on a handful of resources, one region, or one specialization**  capacity/skill-routing issue; validate against workload volumes before concluding misconduct.
