Bias appears primarily in the **scoring adjustment**, not in the documented baseline scores or review sequence.

### Where bias manifests

- Applicants affiliated with **Highland Civic Darts Club** receive a **+10 “Community” adjustment**:
  - C001: 710  720, approved
  - C004: 690  700, approved
- Applicants with **no community affiliation** receive no adjustment:
  - C002: remains 720, approved
  - C003: remains 715, rejected
  - C005: remains 740, approved

The adjustment is associated with a particular community group and is applied only to applicants who are also marked **LocalResident = TRUE**. This means the process may be favoring people with a specific social or geographic connection rather than relying solely on credit-related characteristics.

### Potential effect on decisions

A 10-point increase can materially affect borderline cases. C004, for example, moves from 690 to 700 and is approved, suggesting that the community adjustment may have helped the applicant cross an approval threshold. An otherwise similar applicant without that affiliation could remain below the threshold and be rejected.

The comparison between C003 and C004 illustrates the equity concern:

- C003: 715, no affiliation, rejected
- C004: 690, affiliated with the favored group, receives +10 and is approved

Although the cases may differ in unobserved factors, the event log shows a lower preliminary score receiving favorable treatment because of community affiliation. This creates a risk that affiliation is functioning as a proxy for geography, social networks, or local identity.

### Fairness implications

This can disadvantage:

- Applicants who are not members of the recognized community group
- People who recently moved into the area
- Individuals who lack access to local clubs or networks
- Applicants from other geographic areas
- People with equivalent creditworthiness but no qualifying affiliation

The process therefore risks **unequal treatment of similarly situated applicants**. It may also create indirect geographic or socioeconomic discrimination if membership in the club is more accessible to certain populations.

Manual review does not appear to correct the disparity; reviewers preserve the adjustment, and the final rules engine uses the adjusted score. The system should therefore be audited for:

1. Whether the community bonus has a demonstrable, credit-relevant justification  
2. Whether similarly situated applicants receive comparable treatment  
3. Whether the bonus disproportionately benefits a geographic, social, or demographic group  
4. Whether decisions change when the adjustment is removed  
5. Whether a neutral, evidence-based factor could replace community affiliation  

Without such validation, the adjustment undermines transparency and may cause final approvals and rejections to reflect group membership rather than underlying creditworthiness.
