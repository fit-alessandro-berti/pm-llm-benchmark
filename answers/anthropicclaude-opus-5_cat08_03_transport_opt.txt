# Process Mining Programme for Speedy Parcels
## Last-Mile Delivery Punctuality & Cost Optimisation

---

## 1. Data Preparation, Process Discovery and Conformance Checking

### 1.1 Building the integrated event log

**Step 1 — Choose the case notion(s). Deliberately use more than one.**

Last-mile delivery is a classic multi-object process; forcing it into a single Case ID destroys information. I would build one integrated event store and derive four analytical views:

| View | Case ID | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Tour view** | `Vehicle-Day` (e.g. `V12-20241205`) | End-to-end shift process, utilisation, driver hours, overtime |
| **Stop view** | `Vehicle-Day-StopSeq` | Dwell-time decomposition, parking/access time, service time |
| **Package view** | `PackageID` (spanning multiple days/attempts) | On-time delivery, failed attempts  re-delivery loops, true cost-to-serve |
| **Asset view** | `VehicleID` (long-running) | Maintenance, degradation, availability |

Ideally this is stored as an **object-centric event log (OCEL)** with object types *vehicle, driver, tour, stop, package, maintenance order*, from which the four flattened logs are generated on demand. This avoids the convergence/divergence artefacts you get if, for example, 35 package events are duplicated onto one tour case.

**Step 2 — Abstract GPS telemetry into activities.**
Raw pings (every 10–30 s) are *not* events; they are a signal. I would apply:

- **Stop detection**: spatio-temporal clustering (e.g. DBSCAN on lat/lon/time, or speed < 3 km/h for > 90 s)  derive `Stop Start` / `Stop End` interval events with duration and coordinates.
- **Geofencing**: classify each stop by matching against depot polygons, customer address geocodes (radius 30–75 m), fuel stations, known break locations  activity labels `At Depot`, `At Customer`, `Refuel`, `Break`, `Unexplained Stop`.
- **Map matching** of the trajectory to the road network  derive per-leg **distance**, **free-flow reference time**, and hence a **congestion index** = actual leg time ÷ free-flow time. Legs where the index exceeds a threshold become `Traffic Delay` events (far more reliable than the raw "Low Speed Detected" flag, which fires at traffic lights too).
- **Idling**: ignition-on + speed 0 + not at a customer geofence  `Idle` (fuel-cost relevant).

**Step 3 — Correlate scanner, dispatch and maintenance data.**
- Scanner events join to GPS stops on (vehicle, time-window, proximity). A scanner event with no matching GPS stop, or vice-versa, is a data-quality flag *and* a behavioural signal.
- Dispatch data supplies the **normative layer**: planned stop sequence, planned arrival times, customer time windows, package count/volume, capacity, planned driver. Each actual stop is matched to a planned stop to allow deviation measurement.
- Maintenance logs are **interval events**; unscheduled repairs that begin mid-shift must be injected into the tour case (`Unscheduled Maintenance Start/End`), while workshop visits become vehicle-availability events.

**Step 4 — Enrich with context.** Time-of-day bucket, weekday, weather, zone/territory ID, road type mix, urban vs suburban, stop density, package attributes (size, COD, signature-required), driver tenure, vehicle age/model, cumulative km since last service.

### 1.2 Expected data-quality challenges

1. **Clock drift and time-zone/DST inconsistency** between GPS units, handhelds and back-office systems — a 90-second offset destroys dwell-time analysis. Remedy: calibrate per device using depot arrival/ignition-off pairs; normalise everything to UTC.
2. **Scanning discipline ("scan gaming")**: drivers batch-scanning `Delivery Success` for several stops at once, or scanning `Arrive Customer` while still driving. Detectable as scanner timestamps inconsistent with GPS speed/location — treat as both a data-quality issue *and* a finding.
3. **GPS dropout / urban canyon noise** producing phantom stops or teleportation; requires trajectory smoothing and outlier filtering.
4. **Missing `Depart Customer` events**  dwell time must be inferred from GPS stop end (event log **incompleteness**).
5. **Granularity mismatch**: millions of pings vs. a handful of milestones — must be resolved by abstraction, otherwise discovery produces unreadable models.
6. **Address geocoding errors** — a stop 400 m from the geocoded address may be correct parking, not a deviation.
7. **Failed-delivery linkage**: a re-delivery is often a *new* shipment record; without a linkage key the true cost of failures is invisible. Fuzzy matching on address+recipient may be needed.
8. **Fuel data may only exist at fill-up granularity**  allocate per-tour via a model (km, idling minutes, load, gradient) rather than pretending it is event-level.
9. **Snapshot/effect of plan revisions**: dispatch plans get edited during the day; you need the *plan as of departure* plus a change log, otherwise conformance checking compares against a plan retro-fitted to reality.

### 1.3 Process discovery

I would discover models at **three levels of abstraction**, because a single model of everything will be spaghetti:

**(a) Tour lifecycle model (control-flow).** Activities: `Start Shift  Scan at Depot / Load  Route Assigned  Depart Depot  (Travel  Arrive Customer  Delivery Success | Delivery Failed  Depart Customer)*  [Break] [Refuel] [Unscheduled Stop]  Arrive Depot  Unload/Return Failed Parcels  End Shift`.
- Use the **Inductive Miner (infrequent variants filtered)** for a sound, block-structured backbone suitable for conformance checking.
- Use a **performance-annotated Directly-Follows Graph / Heuristics Miner** for exploratory work, because it shows frequencies and mean/median durations on edges — ideal for spotting that, say, 22 % of tours contain a `Refuel` insertion mid-route.
- Use **trace clustering / variant analysis** to separate structurally distinct tour types (urban high-density, suburban long-haul, B2B morning-window tours) before comparing performance — otherwise you compare apples with oranges.

**(b) Stop-level model.** A short, high-value model: `Approach (speed decay)  Park  Walk/Access  Arrive Customer scan  Customer Interaction  Delivery Success/Failed  Return to vehicle  Depart Customer`. Decomposing dwell time into **access/parking time, interaction time and administrative time** is where most last-mile improvement potential hides.

**(c) Spatial process model.** Nodes = geographic zones or road segments; edges = travel legs annotated with median speed and congestion index. This is effectively a process map projected onto a map — it exposes recurring traffic hotspots, one-way/turn-restriction detours, and territory overlap between vehicles (two vans crossing the same street is a routing defect).

**(d) Package-level model** to expose the costly rework loop: `First Attempt Failed  Return to Depot  Re-sort  Second Attempt  …`, with rework rates and the number of attempts distribution.

### 1.4 Conformance checking against the dispatch plan

The dispatch plan gives a **normative model per tour** — a unique planned sequence of stops with planned arrival times. I would run conformance on three perspectives:

**Control-flow conformance.** Convert planned stop sequence into a normative trace and compute **alignments** (or, cheaply, edit distance / Spearman rank correlation between planned and actual stop order). Deviation taxonomy:

| Deviation type | Detection | Business meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Re-sequencing** | Planned rank vs. actual rank divergence | Driver overriding the plan (often justified — mine the reasons) |
| **Skipped stop (move)** | Planned stop with no actual visit | Undelivered parcel, rolled to next day |
| **Inserted unplanned stop** | Actual stop not matching any planned customer/depot/known POI | Personal errands, informal breaks, second depot trips, maintenance |
| **Duplicated visit** | Same customer visited twice | Failed first attempt, forgotten parcel, missorted load |
| **Repeat depot return** | Extra `Arrive Depot` mid-tour | Capacity or missort problem |
| **Resource deviation** | Actual driver  planned driver; vehicle swap | Absence management, unfamiliarity with territory |
| **Data deviation** | Actual load > vehicle capacity | Overloading, plan infeasibility |

**Temporal conformance.** Alignment on order is not enough. I would check declarative/temporal constraints (LTL- or Declare-style, or simply timestamp comparison):
- Arrival within the **customer time window** (early / on-time / late, with magnitude).
- Actual arrival vs. **planned ETA**  per-leg *planning error* $\varepsilon_i = t^{actual}_i - t^{plan}_i$. Plot $\varepsilon$ against stop index: a monotonically growing error means systematic underestimation of travel or service time, not driver failure.
- Legal/HR constraints: mandatory break after N hours driving; shift length  contractual limit.

**Fitness reporting.** Produce a per-tour fitness score, then aggregate by **route, territory, driver, vehicle, weekday, weather** to find *where* the plan and reality systematically diverge. A route with 0.95 fitness and one with 0.55 fitness need very different interventions: the latter usually means **the plan itself is wrong**, not the driver.

## 2. Performance Analysis and Bottleneck Identification

### 2.1 KPI definitions and derivation

| KPI | Definition / formula | Derived from |
|---|---|---|
| **On-Time Delivery Rate** | % packages where `Delivery Success` timestamp  customer window | Scanner + dispatch window |
| **First-Attempt Success Rate** | successful first attempts ÷ total first attempts | Package view |
| **Failed-Delivery / Rework Rate** | `Delivery Failed` ÷ total attempts; avg. attempts per package | Package view |
| **Mean/median dwell time per stop** | `Depart Customer`  `Arrive Customer`; decomposed into park+access, interaction, admin | Stop view + GPS |
| **Travel : Service : Idle : Wait ratio** | Sum of leg durations vs. dwell vs. ignition-on-zero-speed vs. waiting for window opening, ÷ shift length | GPS abstraction |
| **Stops per productive hour** | successful stops ÷ (End Shift  Start Shift  break) | Tour view |
| **Depot turnaround time** | `Depart Depot`  `Start Shift` (in the sample: **44 min**) and `End Shift`  `Arrive Depot` | Tour view |
| **Congestion Index / delay minutes** | (actual leg time  free-flow time), by zone and hour | Map-matched GPS |
| **Planning error** | mean and  of $t^{actual}-t^{plan}$ per leg; **travel-time bias** | Conformance |
| **Distance per stop / per package** | map-matched km ÷ stops; also % "empty" km | GPS |
| **Fuel per 100 km & per package** | telematics or allocated fill-ups, normalised by load and idling | Fuel + GPS |
| **Idling ratio** | idling minutes ÷ ignition-on minutes (direct fuel waste) | GPS |
| **Vehicle utilisation** | (volume/weight loaded ÷ capacity); ignition-on hours ÷ available hours; **availability** = 1  downtime/calendar | Dispatch + maintenance |
| **Unplanned maintenance rate & MTBF** | unscheduled events per 10 000 km; km/hours between failures | Maintenance + GPS |
| **Overtime hours & cost** | shift duration beyond contractual | Driver events |
| **Cost per stop / per package** | (labour + fuel + maintenance + failure rework) ÷ successful stops | All + finance |

Two composite KPIs are worth elevating for management: **cost per successfully delivered parcel (first attempt)** and **promise reliability** (share of parcels delivered within the communicated ETA window).

### 2.2 Techniques for bottleneck identification

1. **Performance-annotated process map** with median, 90th percentile and *aggregate* time per activity/edge. Rank bottlenecks not by average duration but by **total time consumed** (frequency × duration) — that is where the savings are.
2. **Dwell-time decomposition** at stops: separate `parking/access` (GPS-stop start  `Arrive Customer` scan) from `customer interaction` (`Arrive`  `Success/Failed`) from `admin/return-to-vehicle` (`Success`  `Depart`). Typically this reveals that "delivery time" is dominated by parking search and building access, not by the customer.
3. **Waiting-time analysis**: time spent parked before a time window opens (arrived too early — a planning defect) and time waiting at gates/reception (B2B).
4. **Dotted chart / timeline visualisation** of all tours: instantly reveals shift-start clustering, mid-day break patterns, the "afternoon collapse" where lateness accumulates, and vehicles idle in the depot yard.
5. **Bottleneck attribution via multi-dimensional slicing**: compute each KPI by *hour-of-day × zone × driver × vehicle model × weekday × weather*. Then apply **decision-tree / rule mining** with "stop was late (yes/no)" or "dwell > p90" as the target and contextual attributes as features. This produces human-readable rules such as *"stops in Zone 4 after 15:30 with > 3 parcels are late in 68 % of cases"*.
6. **Geospatial hotspot analysis**: cluster delay minutes by road segment and hour  recurring congestion corridors (candidates for avoidance windows) vs. random incidents.
7. **Resource/driver analysis with confounder control**: never rank drivers on raw stops/hour. Normalise for territory stop density, parcel mix and traffic exposure (or use matched pairs — the same route driven by different drivers on different days, which the six-month log will contain many times).
8. **Concept drift detection**: track model and KPI stability over the six months to detect seasonality (peak season), a new depot process, or gradual degradation of a vehicle sub-fleet.
9. **Bottleneck impact quantification.** For each bottleneck, compute an addressable-loss estimate:

 $\text{Annual saving} = f \times (d_{\text{actual}} - d_{\text{benchmark}}) \times c$

 where $f$ = annual frequency, $d_{\text{benchmark}}$ = e.g. the 25th percentile achieved by the best-performing comparable cohort, and $c$ = fully loaded cost per minute (driver + vehicle). Example: if 400 tours/day each waste 12 min on depot turnaround beyond best-practice, at €0.65/min that is ~€800k/year of capacity — or ~2 additional stops per tour per day.
10. **Bottleneck  capacity translation**: express savings in *recovered delivery hours* and *additional stops per tour*, which is the currency operations managers act on.

## 3. Root Cause Analysis

The purpose is to move from "stops in Zone 4 are late" to "the planner's travel-time matrix assumes 28 km/h in Zone 4 at 16:00 whereas the observed median is 17 km/h".

| Hypothesised root cause | Process-mining test | Diagnostic signature |
|---|---|---|
| **Static route planning / stale travel-time matrix** | Compare planned leg duration to actual distribution by zone × hour; compute bias and dispersion | Systematic positive bias growing with stop index; planner uses a single average speed regardless of hour |
| **Under-estimated service time** | Compare planned per-stop allowance to actual dwell distribution segmented by stop type (apartment block, B2B, house, locker) | Plan assumes flat 3 min; apartments median 7 min  cumulative lateness |
| **Traffic congestion** | Correlate congestion index by segment/hour with lateness; separate recurrent from incidental delay | Recurrent morning corridors; note that recurrent congestion is *plannable*, so this is really a planning failure |
| **High service-time variability** | Variance/percentile analysis, decision-tree mining on dwell drivers (building type, parcel count, signature, COD, floor) | p90 dwell 4× median in specific stop categories |
| **Sequence overrides by drivers** | Variant analysis of conformant vs. non-conformant tours | If non-conformant tours perform *better*, the driver's tacit knowledge beats the planner — mine and codify it. If worse, it is an adherence issue |
| **Failed deliveries** | Failure rate by hour-of-day, address type, customer history, driver; survival/propensity modelling | Residential failures peak 09:00–15:00  attempted when nobody is home; failure begets ~1.6 extra attempts + return handling |
| **Vehicle condition** | Pre-failure degradation: rising idle time, falling average speed, increasing unscheduled stops, "engine warning" events in the weeks before a breakdown | Mid-shift breakdowns (as in the 11:05 sample event) cascade: remaining stops rolled  next-day overload  lateness spiral |
| **Depot/loading process** | Analyse `Start Shift  Depart Depot` sub-process: scanning, load sequence, missing parcels, waiting for sorter | 44-min turnaround with high variance; missorts cause mid-route depot returns |
| **Driver skill/behaviour** | Matched-route comparison; harsh-event and idling profiles; parking-time distribution by driver | Best quartile 2.1 min access time vs. worst 5.4 min on the same buildings |
| **Overloading / capacity infeasibility** | Check planned load vs. capacity and stop count vs. feasible stops in shift | Plans that are *mathematically* infeasible  guaranteed overtime |
| **Time-window design** | Cluster windows; measure waiting-before-window and window-induced detours | Narrow, scattered windows force zig-zag routes and idle waiting |

**Methodological discipline:** each hypothesis should be tested as a comparison with controls (same territory, same weekday, similar weather), and reported with effect size, not just significance. Process mining establishes *mechanism* (you can see the delay propagate through the trace); statistics establish *magnitude*.

## 4. Data-Driven Optimisation Strategies

### Strategy 1 — Re-calibrate the planning engine with event-log-derived travel-time and service-time models

- **Target inefficiency:** cumulative lateness across the tour; unrealistic ETAs; overtime; drivers ignoring the plan.
- **Root cause:** static, average-based travel-time matrix and a flat per-stop service allowance in the dispatch system.
- **Evidence from the log:** per-zone, per-hour, per-day-of-week speed distributions from map-matched GPS; dwell-time distributions segmented by stop archetype (single-family house, multi-dwelling, B2B, reception-gated); measured planning bias per leg.
- **Implementation:** feed a **time-dependent travel-time matrix** (zone × zone × 30-min bucket, using the 60–70th percentile rather than the mean to build in reliability buffer) and a **stop-level service-time model** (predicted dwell = f(stop archetype, parcel count, signature required, floor, historical dwell for that address)) into the route optimiser. Re-plan sequences accordingly.
- **Expected KPI impact:** On-Time Delivery +8–15 pp; planning error  reduced sharply; overtime hours 20–30 %; conformance/fitness up (drivers stop overriding a plan they trust); distance per stop 3–6 % from better sequencing.

### Strategy 2 — Dynamic in-day re-optimisation with predictive lateness alerts

- **Target inefficiency:** delay propagation — one traffic jam or one long stop at 09:00 makes 15 afternoon stops late; mid-shift breakdowns leave stops undelivered.
- **Root cause:** plan-and-forget dispatching; no feedback loop; no early-warning mechanism.
- **Evidence:** **predictive process monitoring** trained on six months of partial traces — given the trace prefix (stops completed, current time, current delay, remaining stop profile), predict remaining tour time and per-stop arrival. The log shows the delay signature is detectable by stop 5–8 in most late tours, i.e. hours before the damage occurs.
- **Implementation:** a control tower that (a) alerts when predicted arrival breaches a window, (b) proposes re-sequencing of remaining stops, transfer of stops to a neighbouring van with slack, or proactive customer re-scheduling; (c) auto-triggers recovery workflows on `Unscheduled Stop`/breakdown events. Congestion-hotspot avoidance windows learned from the log are hard-coded as routing penalties.
- **Expected KPI impact:** On-Time +5–10 pp on disrupted days; undelivered-at-end-of-day stops 40 %; reduced customer-service contact volume; smoother workload balance across vehicles.

### Strategy 3 — First-attempt success programme (failure reduction)

- **Target inefficiency:** failed deliveries (`Delivery Failed – Customer Not Home`) — each consumes dwell time, distance, a return-handling step, and 1–2 re-delivery attempts, i.e. roughly 2.5× the cost of a first-time success.
- **Root cause:** attempts scheduled without regard to recipient availability; imprecise ETA communication; no alternatives at the door.
- **Evidence:** failure rate by hour-of-day and address, from the package view; per-address historical success propensity; measured cost of the rework loop (attempts distribution, extra km, extra dwell); correlation between narrow/late notification and failure.
- **Implementation:** (a) a **per-address success-propensity model** feeding the sequencer so that low-availability residential addresses are scheduled into their empirically best time bands (e.g. after 16:30) and reliably available addresses fill the low-propensity hours; (b) narrow, dynamic **ETA notifications** (30–60 min) with self-service re-scheduling; (c) capture and reuse access data (gate codes, safe-place consent, neighbour delivery) as structured attributes at the point of failure; (d) locker/PUDO diversion for addresses with 2 historical failures.
- **Expected KPI impact:** First-Attempt Success +5–12 pp; rework attempts 30–50 %; distance and fuel per *delivered* parcel 4–8 %; effective capacity released without adding vehicles.

### Strategy 4 — Usage-based predictive maintenance and fleet-to-territory matching

- **Target inefficiency:** mid-shift breakdowns (highest-cost failure mode: stops rolled, recovery van, overtime, customer impact) and calendar-based servicing that is simultaneously too early for light-duty vans and too late for heavy-duty ones.
- **Root cause:** maintenance scheduled by date, not by actual duty cycle; degradation signals in the telemetry ignored.
- **Evidence:** derive per-vehicle duty metrics from the log — km, engine hours, **stop-start cycles per km** (brutal in dense urban work), idling hours, average speed trend, harsh-event counts, warning-light events. Correlate with unscheduled-repair history to establish MTBF as a function of duty cycle, and identify pre-failure signatures (e.g. rising idle-speed anomalies and declining average speed in the 2–3 weeks before failure).
- **Implementation:** service intervals driven by a composite wear index rather than calendar; predictive alerts routed to the workshop; **schedule maintenance into discovered low-demand windows** (the log shows depot dwell and low-volume weekdays); assign high-cycle urban territories to vehicle models with the best observed MTBF under that duty cycle.
- **Expected KPI impact:** unscheduled mid-shift failures 30–50 %; vehicle availability +2–4 pp; maintenance cost per km 5–10 %; fewer rolled stops (protects OTD).

### Strategy 5 — Depot turnaround and load-sequencing improvement

- **Target inefficiency:** ~44 minutes of unproductive depot time at shift start (and a further 15 min at end), plus mid-route depot returns caused by missorts.
- **Root cause:** loading not synchronised with route sequence; parcels not staged before driver arrival; scanning duplication.
- **Evidence:** sub-process discovery of the `Start Shift  Depart Depot` segment showing variance across drivers and days; correlation between load-sequence errors and in-van search time (measurable as elevated dwell at first stops); frequency of unplanned depot re-entry events.
- **Implementation:** stage and load in **reverse stop order**, with shelf-position labels generated by the optimiser; stagger shift starts to level sorter load (dotted chart shows the 07:00 crush); eliminate the redundant depot scan by integrating the sorter scan.
- **Expected KPI impact:** depot turnaround 15 min/tour  ~4 % more productive drive time per shift (1–2 extra stops per tour); in-van search time reduction lowers median dwell 20–40 s/stop; earlier departure improves morning window compliance.

### Strategy 6 — Evidence-based driver coaching and codification of best practice

- **Target inefficiency:** spread between best- and worst-quartile drivers on the *same* territories (access time, idling ratio, scanning discipline).
- **Root cause:** tacit knowledge not shared; no feedback; scanning behaviour distorting data and ETAs.
- **Evidence:** matched-route variant analysis; per-driver dwell decomposition; idling ratio; harsh-event and speeding profiles; conformance findings on scanning (scans recorded while moving).
- **Implementation:** publish fair, exposure-normalised peer benchmarks; convert best-practice patterns (parking strategy for known buildings, multi-drop bundling in apartment blocks, pre-arrival preparation) into route notes attached to specific addresses; coach the bottom quartile with their own trace visualisations; enforce scan-at-event discipline (which is a prerequisite for Strategies 2 and 3).
- **Expected KPI impact:** median dwell 8–15 %; idling ratio 20 %  fuel 3–5 %; data quality improvement that raises the accuracy of all ETA-based mechanisms.

## 5. Operational Constraints and Continuous Monitoring

### 5.1 Respecting the constraint envelope

Any optimisation must be **feasibility-checked against hard constraints**, otherwise it will be silently overridden in the depot:

- **Driver working time:** maximum shift, mandatory breaks, statutory rest — encoded as hard constraints in the optimiser and as **declarative conformance rules** monitored post-hoc (e.g. "a `Break` of 30 min must occur within 6 h of `Start Shift`"). Note that the log lets you measure *actual* achievable productive hours (net of breaks and depot time), which is usually well below the contractual figure the planner assumes — a common source of infeasible plans.
- **Vehicle capacity:** volume *and* weight *and* practical loadability. Use the log to derive **effective** capacity (the load level beyond which in-van search time and stop dwell degrade), not just nominal capacity.
- **Customer time windows:** hard for B2B/reception-gated and premium services, soft for standard residential. Strategy 3 explicitly trades *window narrowness* against *route efficiency*; the log quantifies the marginal cost per minute of window tightening, enabling a commercial decision.
- **Regulatory/access constraints:** low-emission zones, weight and time-of-day restrictions, pedestrian zones — derived partly from observed route deviations and encoded as routing constraints.
- **Depot constraints:** sorter availability, dock capacity, dispatch cut-offs (staggered starts must respect these).
- **Labour-relations and privacy constraints:** driver-level analytics must be used for coaching and process improvement, not punitive ranking; anonymise/aggregate in dashboards, involve worker representatives, and be transparent about what is measured (GDPR relevance for GPS and performance data).

**Validation before rollout:** calibrate a **discrete-event simulation / digital twin** directly from the event log (empirical travel-time and service-time distributions, failure probabilities, breakdown rates) and test each strategy in-silico, including sensitivity to peak-season volumes. Then run **controlled pilots** — 2–3 depots or territory pairs, matched control group, 6–8 weeks — with conformance and KPI measurement built in from day one.

### 5.2 Continuous monitoring architecture

A three-tier process-mining dashboard, refreshed daily (near-real-time for tier 2):

**Tier 1 — Executive scorecard (weekly/monthly trend, with control limits)**
On-Time Delivery Rate · First-Attempt Success · Cost per delivered parcel · Stops per productive hour · Fuel per 100 km and per parcel · Vehicle availability · Overtime hours · Benefit realisation vs. business case, per strategy.

**Tier 2 — Operational control tower (intra-day)**
Live tour progress vs. plan (deviation in minutes per vehicle) · predicted window breaches · open exceptions (breakdown, unplanned stop, undelivered stops at risk) · driver-hours risk flags · depot turnaround running average · congestion map with today's actual vs. baseline.

**Tier 3 — Analyst workbench (root-cause depth)**
- **Conformance dashboard:** fitness by route/territory/driver/vehicle; ranked deviation types; trend in plan-override frequency.
- **Performance-annotated process map** with median and p90 durations; bottleneck ranking by total time consumed.
- **Variant explorer:** best vs. worst performing tour variants, with automatic difference highlighting (log-delta analysis).
- **Dwell decomposition monitor:** parking/access vs. interaction vs. admin, by stop archetype.
- **Planning-accuracy monitor:** rolling bias and dispersion of planned vs. actual leg and dwell times — this is the **feedback loop that keeps the travel-time matrix from going stale**, and should trigger automatic recalibration when bias exceeds a threshold.
- **Failure analytics:** failure rate by hour/address/driver, attempts distribution, propensity-model drift.
- **Fleet health:** wear index, predicted-failure watchlist, prediction precision/recall.
- **Data-quality panel:** scan-vs-GPS consistency, missing `Depart Customer` events, GPS coverage, clock drift — because every optimisation above depends on log quality.

**Governance:** weekly operational review (tier 2 exceptions + emerging deviation patterns), monthly performance review (tier 1 + one deep-dive from tier 3), quarterly re-calibration of travel-time/service-time/propensity/wear models with formal **drift detection** (alert if KPI distributions or the discovered process model shift beyond control limits — e.g. peak season, a new customer with different stop archetypes, a road-network change). Each optimisation is tracked as a hypothesis with a target KPI, a measurement definition and a pilot-vs-control result, so that the programme compounds rather than relying on one-off analyses.
