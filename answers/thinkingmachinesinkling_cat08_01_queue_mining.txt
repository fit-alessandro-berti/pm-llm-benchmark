In this event log, each case (Visit ID) records the `START` and `COMPLETE` timestamps of discrete activities performed by specific resources. The **inter-activity waiting time**—the core queue metric—is calculated as:

> **Queue Time (Q)** = Timestamp(`START` of Activity *B*)  Timestamp(`COMPLETE` of Activity *A*)

This represents the idle period where the patient has finished Activity *A* (e.g., Registration) and is waiting for the next resource/activity (*B*, e.g., Nurse Assessment) to become available, accounting for handover, travel, and queueing. Waiting *within* an activity (`COMPLETE`  `START`) is the **service time**; inter-activity gaps are the **queue/wait times** this analysis targets.

The guiding principle should be: **reduce idle (queue) time, not service time.** The strategies above do not propose rushing the `Doctor Consultation` (which could reduce care quality); they eliminate waste *between* activities. To control costs, I recommend a **pilot-and-scale model**: implement Strategy B (parallel flow) in one high-volume specialty (e.g., Cardiology) and Strategy C (segmented scheduling) in one clinic wing, comparing results against a control group using the same event log metrics. Only scale strategies where the reduction in wait time per unit of cost (or staff-hour reallocation) meets a predefined threshold.

### 5. Measuring Success

**Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)**

To validate that queuing issues are resolved sustainably, I would track metrics derived directly from the ongoing event log (`START`/`COMPLETE`):

1.  **Queue-Specific Performance (The Direct Metrics):**
    *   Mean, Median, and **P90 inter-activity wait times** for each critical handover (Registration  Nurse; Nurse  Doctor; Doctor  Test; etc.).
    *   Percentage of cases experiencing waits exceeding clinical/policy thresholds (e.g., >15 min for clinical handovers).

2.  **Overall Flow and Duration Metrics:**
    *   **Average Total Visit Duration:** From `Registration` START to `Check-out` COMPLETE.
    *   **Value-Added Time Ratio:** Sum of all activity service times divided by total visit duration. A successful intervention should increase this ratio by reducing non-value-added queue time.
    *   **Cumulative Queue Time per Visit:** Sum of all inter-activity waits.

3.  **Resource and Operational Metrics:**
    *   **Resource Utilization Rate** (per staff/resource): Target sustainable range (~75–85%). Avoid pushing to 95%, which causes queue instability.
    *   **Staff Overtime / Cost per Visit:** To ensure efficiency gains are not offset by labor cost inflation.

4.  **Patient Experience and Quality:**
    *   **Post-Visit Satisfaction Score** (specifically the "waiting experience" dimension).
    *   **Access Metrics:** No-show rate (improved flow may reduce no-shows); volume of `Urgent` patients seen within target time windows.

**Ongoing Process Monitoring and Sustained Improvement**

*   **Continuous Queue Mining Dashboards:** Deploy automated process mining on weekly event log batches. Generate annotated directly-follows graphs where edges are labeled with current mean/median queue times, and resource nodes are colored by real-time utilization.
*   **Statistical Process Control (SPC):** Use control charts (e.g., X-bar and R charts) for the mean and range of queue times per critical handover. If a queue’s P90 exceeds the upper control limit for three consecutive weeks, trigger an automatic root-cause drill-down (checking for staff absences, equipment failure, or arrival pattern shifts).
*   **Conformance Checking:** After implementing Strategy B (parallel flow), use conformance checking to verify that eligible cases actually follow the redesigned path (e.g., `ECG` before `Doctor Consultation`). High deviation rates indicate staff reverting to old habits or protocol ambiguity, requiring retraining.
*   **Pre- vs. Post-Intervention Log Comparison:** Maintain the 6-month baseline log. Every quarter, compare the new event log against the baseline using the same queue mining algorithms. Quantify percentage improvement in each KPI and publish an internal "Flow Report."

By embedding queue mining into the clinic’s operational intelligence—treating the event log not as a static record but as a living diagnostic tool—management can move from reactive complaint management to proactive, evidence-based flow optimization.
