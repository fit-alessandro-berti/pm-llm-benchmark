A redesigned process could shift from a mostly sequential, rules-driven flow into an event-driven, data-assisted workflow that triages requests early, automates routine work, and reserves human expertise for exceptions and genuinely complex customizations.

## 1. Add an intelligent intake and triage layer

### Replace or enhance Task A: “Receive Customer Request”
Instead of merely receiving the request, create an automated intake subprocess:

**Task A1: Capture and normalize request data**
- Extract data from web forms, emails, uploaded documents, CRM records, and prior order history.
- Use OCR/NLP where requests arrive in unstructured formats.
- Validate mandatory fields immediately.
- Detect duplicate requests and incomplete submissions.

**Task A2: Enrich request with contextual data**
- Retrieve customer segment, contract terms, prior purchases, credit status, product availability, historical customization requests, and service-level commitments.
- Attach relevant product, capacity, logistics, and pricing information.

**New gateway: “Is request data complete and valid?”**
- If no, automatically request missing information from the customer through a portal, chatbot, or email workflow.
- If yes, proceed to intelligent classification.

This reduces manual back-and-forth and prevents incomplete requests from entering downstream work queues.

## 2. Replace the simple request-type gateway with predictive classification

### Enhance Gateway: “Check Request Type”
Rather than relying only on a manually selected “Standard” or “Custom” category, introduce:

**Task A3: Predict request complexity and customization likelihood**
- A predictive model scores the probability that the request will require customization, exception handling, engineering review, special pricing, or non-standard delivery treatment.
- Inputs could include:
  - Product configuration
  - Customer industry and segment
  - Order quantity
  - Requested delivery date
  - Historical exception patterns
  - Free-text request content
  - Previous custom orders from the same customer
  - Inventory constraints
  - Current production capacity

**New gateway: “Predicted routing category”**
Possible routes:
1. **Low-risk standard request**  
   Fully automated straight-through processing.
2. **Standard request with exception risk**  
   Standard validation plus proactive exception monitoring.
3. **Likely custom request**  
   Route directly to a custom-configurator or feasibility subprocess.
4. **High-value/high-complexity request**  
   Assign to an expert account manager or specialist team immediately.

This prevents cases that are likely to become custom from spending time in the standard path before being rerouted later.

### Human override
The predictive routing should not be fully opaque. Include:

**Task A4: Human review for low-confidence classifications**
- If model confidence is below a threshold, send the request to a triage specialist.
- Allow customer service personnel to override the recommendation with a reason code.
- Feed override outcomes back into model monitoring and retraining.

This preserves flexibility and avoids over-reliance on predictive scores.

## 3. Automate and parallelize standard validation

### Enhance Task B1: “Perform Standard Validation”
For clearly standard requests, standard validation should become a rules-based automation service:

**Task B1A: Automated business-rule validation**
- Confirm product eligibility.
- Validate customer contract terms.
- Verify pricing, discounts, payment terms, and regulatory requirements.
- Confirm request quantities and allowable delivery windows.

Cases passing all checks proceed automatically. Failures create specific exception work items rather than requiring a full manual review.

### Improve the parallel checks gateway
The current process runs credit and inventory checks in parallel. That is appropriate, but it can be expanded.

**Gateway (AND): “Run automated fulfillment checks in parallel”**
Potential parallel tasks:
- **Credit Check**: query credit limits, outstanding receivables, and payment behavior.
- **Inventory Check**: assess available inventory and allocated stock.
- **Capacity Check**: review production, warehouse, and delivery capacity.
- **Logistics Check**: evaluate shipping routes, customs constraints, and delivery feasibility.
- **Compliance Check**: validate export, safety, customer-specific, or regulatory restrictions.
- **Fraud/Anomaly Check**: detect abnormal order patterns where relevant.

### New gateway: “Can the order be fulfilled as requested?”
- **Yes:** calculate a confirmed delivery date and proceed.
- **No, but alternative is available:** automatically generate alternatives, such as partial fulfillment, substitute product, different ship date, or expedited shipping option.
- **No, requires custom solution:** reroute to the custom feasibility subprocess.
- **No, cannot be fulfilled:** send a transparent rejection or escalation notice.

This makes the standard path more adaptive rather than treating failures as purely manual exceptions.

## 4. Redesign custom feasibility as a reusable subprocess

### Enhance Task B2: “Perform Custom Feasibility Analysis”
Custom feasibility should be a dedicated subprocess rather than a single task.

**Subprocess: “Custom Solution Assessment”**
Possible steps include:

1. **Configuration analysis**
   - Determine whether the request can be supported by an existing configurable product.
   - Use a product configurator or CPQ system to identify allowable variations.

2. **Technical feasibility assessment**
   - Evaluate engineering, manufacturing, service, integration, or design requirements.
   - Automatically compare requested features against known solution templates.

3. **Capacity and lead-time simulation**
   - Estimate impact on production capacity, supply chain availability, and delivery lead time.
   - Use digital-twin or scheduling models where available.

4. **Profitability and commercial assessment**
   - Evaluate margin, cost-to-serve, required discounts, and strategic customer value.
   - Flag high-value cases for prioritized review.

5. **Risk assessment**
   - Identify dependencies, supplier risks, regulatory risks, and delivery commitments.

### Dynamic resource allocation
Instead of assigning every custom request to the same team, use a work orchestration engine:

**New task: “Assign feasibility case based on skills and workload”**
Assignment can consider:
- Required technical expertise
- Product family
- Customer region or language
- Current queue length
- SLA deadline
- Request priority
- Analyst availability
- Historical performance of available specialists

This can reduce queue bottlenecks and improve consistency. Complex requests can be routed to senior specialists, while repeatable customizations can be handled by lower-cost teams or automation.

### New gateway: “Can customization be fulfilled using a standard configurable option?”
- **Yes:** return to an automated quotation path.
- **No, but feasible with engineering effort:** route to an engineering-review path.
- **No, not feasible:** send rejection with alternatives if possible.

This introduces more nuance than the current simple feasible/not-feasible decision.

## 5. Automate quotation generation and negotiation support

### Enhance Task E1: “Prepare Custom Quotation”
A CPQ-style capability can generate proposals faster and more consistently.

**Task E1A: Generate draft quotation automatically**
- Use configured product options, approved pricing logic, estimated delivery dates, and customer contract terms.
- Include assumptions, exclusions, validity dates, and alternative options.
- For low-risk customizations, issue the quote automatically.
- For higher-risk quotations, prepare a draft for expert review.

### New gateway: “Does quotation exceed delegated authority?”
Approval can be determined dynamically based on:
- Discount level
- Margin threshold
- Total order value
- Non-standard contractual terms
- Engineering effort
- Delivery commitment risk
- Customer strategic importance

This is better than applying a single generic approval question to all cases.

### Improve rejection handling
### Enhance Task E2: “Send Rejection Notice”
Rather than only rejecting the request:
- Explain the reason in customer-friendly language.
- Offer substitute products, adjusted specifications, alternate delivery dates, or a paid engineering study.
- Invite the customer to revise the request through a self-service portal.

This can convert rejected custom requests into viable alternatives and reduce customer frustration.

## 6. Make delivery-date calculation predictive and continuously updated

### Enhance Task D: “Calculate Delivery Date”
Instead of a one-time calculation, implement:

**Task D1: Predict fulfillment date and confidence level**
Use real-time information such as:
- Inventory position
- Supplier lead times
- Production schedules
- Warehouse capacity
- Carrier performance
- Historical delivery delays
- Seasonal demand
- Weather, port congestion, or regional disruption signals

The process should produce:
- A predicted delivery date
- A confidence score or delivery window
- Alternative delivery options when the requested date cannot be met

### New event: “Delivery risk detected”
If supply-chain data changes after a quote or invoice is generated:
- Trigger a notification or re-planning subprocess.
- Recalculate delivery commitments.
- Offer proactive alternatives before the customer discovers the delay.

This improves customer communication and reduces reactive escalation.

## 7. Redesign approval as risk-based, asynchronous, and SLA-managed

### Enhance Gateway: “Is Approval Needed?”
Approval should not depend on a broad manual decision. Replace it with:

**Task: “Evaluate approval requirements”**
A rules engine determines whether approval is needed based on risk and authority rules.

Examples:
- No approval for standard, low-value orders within pre-approved pricing and credit limits.
- Commercial approval for large discounts or low margins.
- Credit approval for exceptions to payment terms.
- Engineering approval for non-standard technical specifications.
- Legal approval for non-standard terms and conditions.
- Executive approval for major strategic commitments.

### Parallelize approvals where possible
Instead of one manager approval task:

**Gateway (AND): “Initiate required approvals in parallel”**
- Commercial approval
- Credit approval
- Engineering approval
- Legal/compliance approval

A workflow engine should track the required approvers and enforce SLA timers.

### Add escalation handling
**Boundary timer event on approval tasks**
- If approval is not completed within a defined SLA, escalate to a backup approver.
- If urgency is high, send reminders or route to an on-call authority.
- If an approval is rejected, automatically classify the reason and route the case to pricing adjustment, scope revision, or customer negotiation.

This reduces delays caused by approval bottlenecks.

## 8. Improve the re-evaluation loop

### Enhance Task H: “Re-evaluate Conditions”
The current loop sends the case back to Task E1 for custom or Task D for standard. A more controlled loop would be:

**Task H1: Determine rejection or exception reason**
Classify reasons such as:
- Insufficient margin
- Credit risk
- Capacity limitation
- Technical infeasibility
- Delivery commitment risk
- Contractual issue
- Missing information

**New gateway: “What remediation path is appropriate?”**
- Pricing or commercial issue  revise quotation.
- Capacity issue  propose alternate delivery date or partial fulfillment.
- Technical issue  engineering redesign or alternative configuration.
- Credit issue  request prepayment, reduced exposure, or finance review.
- Missing information  request clarification from customer.
- No viable remedy  send informed rejection.

This avoids looping blindly and makes each rework cycle purposeful. The process should also track the number of iterations; after a threshold, it can escalate to a senior specialist or account manager.

---

## 9. Generate the invoice only when commercial commitments are stable

### Enhance Task G: “Generate Final Invoice”
Invoice generation can be automated, but it should be preceded by a final consistency check:

**Task G1: Confirm commercial and operational commitments**
- Verify approved pricing.
- Confirm product configuration.
- Verify inventory/capacity reservation.
- Confirm delivery commitment.
- Confirm payment terms.
- Ensure all required approvals are complete.

**Task G2: Generate invoice and order confirmation package**
The package could include:
- Invoice or pro forma invoice, depending on the business model
- Order summary
- Delivery estimate/window
- Configuration details
- Customer-specific terms
- Tracking or self-service portal link

For some organizations, it may be more appropriate to generate an order confirmation before invoicing, especially if invoicing occurs after shipment. The BPMN should reflect the organization’s financial process.

## 10. Expand Task I into proactive customer communication

### Enhance Task I: “Send Confirmation to Customer”
Communication should become event-driven rather than a single final message.

**Customer communication subprocess**
Possible notifications:
- Request received
- Additional information required
- Request classified as custom and under review
- Quote ready
- Approval or revision required
- Order confirmed
- Delivery date updated
- Shipment dispatched
- Delay risk identified
- Request rejected with alternatives

A customer portal can also let customers:
- Upload documents
- Accept or revise quotations
- Choose delivery options
- Track status
- View reasons for pending actions
- Communicate with assigned specialists

This improves transparency and reduces inbound status inquiries.

## 11. Suggested redesigned high-level flow

A simplified optimized flow could look like this:

```text
Start
  --> Capture, validate, and enrich request
  --> Predict complexity/customization likelihood
  --> Gateway: Routing category?

      --> Straight-through standard
          --> Automated validation
          --> Parallel credit, inventory, capacity, logistics, compliance checks
          --> Gateway: Fulfillable as requested?
              --> Yes --> Predict delivery date --> Risk-based approvals if required
              --> Alternative available --> Present alternative to customer
              --> Custom/exception required --> Custom Solution Assessment subprocess

      --> Likely custom or complex
          --> Custom Solution Assessment subprocess
          --> Gateway: Feasible?
              --> No --> Offer alternatives / rejection / paid assessment option
              --> Yes --> Automated draft quotation
                     --> Risk-based parallel approvals if needed

      --> Low-confidence classification
          --> Human triage review
          --> Route to standard, exception, or custom path

  --> Final commitment validation
  --> Generate order confirmation and/or invoice
  --> Notify customer and activate monitoring events
End
```

## 12. Expected impact

### Turnaround time
Turnaround time should improve through:
- Straight-through processing for low-risk standard requests.
- Reduced rework caused by incomplete data.
- Parallel execution of validations and approvals.
- Early identification of custom requests.
- Automated quotation and delivery-date calculation.
- Dynamic assignment of work to available qualified staff.

The biggest gains typically come from eliminating manual handoffs, reducing approval wait times, and preventing standard-path requests from being rerouted late in the process.

### Customer satisfaction
Customer satisfaction can improve because customers receive:
- Faster confirmations and quotations.
- More accurate delivery commitments.
- Better explanations when a request cannot be fulfilled.
- Alternative offers instead of simple rejection.
- Greater visibility through proactive updates and self-service tracking.
- More tailored handling of complex requests.

Predictive routing can be especially beneficial for strategic customers whose requests are more likely to require exception management.

### Operational flexibility
The redesigned process can absorb demand fluctuations more effectively because:
- Work is assigned based on skills, workload, and SLA urgency.
- Repeatable exceptions can be partially automated.
- Specialist resources are used only for cases that truly need them.
- Rules, models, and routing thresholds can be adjusted without redesigning the entire process.

### Operational complexity and risks
The redesign also introduces complexity:
- Integration is needed across CRM, ERP, inventory, credit, CPQ, workflow, logistics, and analytics systems.
- Predictive models require governance, monitoring, retraining, and bias/error controls.
- Automation rules need clear ownership and regular maintenance.
- Exception paths must remain understandable to employees and customers.
- Over-automation can create poor experiences if customers cannot easily reach a human when needed.
- Approval automation must respect delegated authority, audit requirements, and compliance obligations.

A practical implementation approach would be incremental: first automate intake and standard validations, then introduce dynamic work allocation and approval rules, and finally add predictive routing and delivery-risk analytics once sufficient historical data and operational maturity are available.
