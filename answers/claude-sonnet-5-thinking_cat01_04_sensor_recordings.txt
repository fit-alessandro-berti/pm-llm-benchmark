# Process Mining Analysis: Manufacturing Assembly Line

## Methodology
I analyzed transitions in sensor readings—particularly simultaneous spikes/drops in temperature, vibration, and energy consumption—to identify natural "phase boundaries" in the process. Tool position changes helped confirm when the machine was actively performing work versus idling.

---

## Identified Phases & Activity Labels

### **Phase 1: Idle / Machine Startup** 
**Events 1–3**
| Temp | Pressure | Vibration | Flow | Tool Pos | Energy |
|------|----------|-----------|------|----------|--------|
| 20-21°C | 1 bar | 0 Hz | 0 | 0mm | 0.4-0.5 kWh |

**Rationale:** All readings are at baseline/minimal levels. No vibration, no flow, no tool movement — this is the machine at rest, either before the cycle begins or in standby mode.

---

### **Phase 2: Cutting Metal**
**Events 4–6**
| Temp | Pressure | Vibration | Flow | Tool Pos | Energy |
|------|----------|-----------|------|----------|--------|
| 5058°C | 11.5 bar | 3038 Hz | 5 | 510mm | 2.03.0 kWh |

**Rationale:** Sharp temperature rise combined with **high vibration (30-38 Hz)** is a hallmark of cutting operations (friction/blade contact generates heat and mechanical vibration). Steady material flow (5 units/s) indicates material being fed into the cutter, and the tool position steadily advances (510mm), consistent with a cutting tool moving through material.

---

### **Phase 3: Assembling Parts**
**Events 7–8**
| Temp | Pressure | Vibration | Flow | Tool Pos | Energy |
|------|----------|-----------|------|----------|--------|
| 40°C | 2 bar | 10 Hz | 2 | 15mm | 2.0 kWh |

**Rationale:** Vibration drops significantly (10 Hz) compared to cutting, suggesting a gentler mechanical process. **Pressure increases to 2 bar**, consistent with clamping or fitting components together. Tool position jumps to 15mm and **holds steady** across two consecutive readings — indicative of parts being aligned/assembled rather than continuous motion. Flow rate decreases (2 units/s), suggesting less raw material movement and more precision positioning.

---

### **Phase 4: Welding**
**Event 9**
| Temp | Pressure | Vibration | Flow | Tool Pos | Energy |
|------|----------|-----------|------|----------|--------|
| 80°C | 2 bar | 50 Hz | 0 | 15mm | 5.0 kWh |

**Rationale:** This single event shows a **dramatic spike** across nearly all metrics — highest temperature (80°C) and vibration (50 Hz) in the entire log, plus the **highest energy consumption (5.0 kWh)**. Notably, **material flow drops to 0**, since welding doesn’t require continuous feedstock flow (unlike cutting). This intense, brief burst of heat/energy with no material flow is a classic welding signature. Tool position remains unchanged (15mm), suggesting the tool is fixed in place while welding occurs at that point.

---

### **Phase 5: Cooling / Post-Weld Transition**
**Event 10**
| Temp | Pressure | Vibration | Flow | Tool Pos | Energy |
|------|----------|-----------|------|----------|--------|
| 30°C | 1 bar | 1 Hz | 0 | 15mm | 1.0 kWh |

**Rationale:** A rapid drop from the welding spike — temperature falls from 80°C to 30°C, vibration nearly disappears, and energy usage drops sharply. This brief transitional event captures the machine cooling down immediately after the high-intensity welding operation, before the next activity begins.

---

### **Phase 6: Quality Inspection**
**Events 11–12**
| Temp | Pressure | Vibration | Flow | Tool Pos | Energy |
|------|----------|-----------|------|----------|--------|
| 25°C | 1 bar | 5 Hz | 1 | 20mm | 1.5-1.6 kWh |

**Rationale:** Readings are low and stable — minimal vibration (5 Hz), low flow (1 unit/s), and modest energy consumption. This suggests a **non-intensive process**, consistent with inspection (e.g., sensors/cameras scanning the part, or a probe gently checking dimensions). The tool position moves to a new location (20mm), possibly positioning the part under an inspection sensor. The lack of aggressive readings rules out mechanical processing — this is a lighter-touch verification stage.

---

### **Phase 7: Cycle Complete / Return to Idle**
**Events 13–15**
| Temp | Pressure | Vibration | Flow | Tool Pos | Energy |
|------|----------|-----------|------|----------|--------|
| 20-21°C | 1 bar | 0 Hz | 0 | 0mm | 0.4-0.5 kWh |

**Rationale:** Identical to Phase 1 — the machine returns to baseline idle conditions. This suggests the **manufacturing cycle has completed** (e.g., part packaged/removed) and the machine resets in preparation for the next unit.

---

## Summary Process Flow

```
[Idle]  [Cutting Metal]  [Assembling Parts]  [Welding]  [Cooling]  [Quality Inspection]  [Idle/Cycle Complete]
```

## Key Indicators Used for Labeling

| Signal Pattern | Inferred Activity |
|---|---|
| High vibration + rising temp + steady flow + advancing tool | **Cutting** |
| Moderate temp + increased pressure + low vibration + static tool | **Assembling** |
| Extreme temp/vibration/energy spike + zero flow | **Welding** |
| Sharp drop in all metrics post-spike | **Cooling** |
| Low, stable readings + minor tool repositioning | **Quality Inspection** |
| All metrics at baseline | **Idle/Transition** |

This labeling framework could be validated further by cross-referencing with known cycle times, machine specifications, or operator logs if available — but based purely on the numeric sensor patterns, these phase transitions align well with typical assembly-line operations.
