**1. Cases with significantly longer total resolution (cycle) times**

Calculating end-to-end duration from first “Receive Ticket” to last “Close Ticket”:

- Case 101: ~2.25 hours (08:00 → 10:15 on 1 Mar)
- Case 103: ~1.33 hours (08:10 → 09:30 on 1 Mar)
- Case 102: ~25.2 hours (08:05 on 1 Mar → 09:15 on 2 Mar)
- Case 104: ~24.2 hours (08:20 on 1 Mar → 08:30 on 2 Mar)
- Case 105: ~49.1 hours (08:25 on 1 Mar → 09:30 on 3 Mar)

Cases **102, 104 and 105** are outliers (roughly 10–20× longer than the fast cases 101 and 103).

**2. Potential root causes / patterns**

- **Escalation introduces multi-day delays**  
  Cases 102 and 105 both contain “Escalate to Level-2 Agent”. After escalation the subsequent “Investigate Issue” (by Level-2) is delayed by many hours or overnight:
  - 102: Escalate 11:30 → Investigate 14:00 (same day, still +2.5 h) then Resolve only the next morning.
  - 105: Escalate 10:00 on 1 Mar → Investigate only at 14:00 on 2 Mar (~28 h wait) then Resolve on 3 Mar.

- **Long waiting times after assignment / before investigation**  
  Case 104 (no escalation) still shows a large gap: Assign to Level-1 at 09:30 → Investigate only at 13:00 (+3.5 h). The two fast cases have much shorter hand-offs (15–40 min).

- **Overnight / multi-day gaps between Investigate and Resolve**  
  In every long case the time from “Investigate Issue” to “Resolve Ticket” spans the night (≈19 h). The short cases resolve within 30–60 min of starting investigation. This pattern is consistent with work stopping at the end of the business day and resuming the next morning (or with complex tickets simply requiring more calendar time).

- **No evidence of unnecessary rework loops**; the extra time is almost entirely waiting or calendar time rather than repeated activities.

**3. How these factors increase cycle time & recommendations**

Escalation + long Level-2 queue + overnight work stoppages compound: a ticket that could theoretically be finished in a few hours becomes a 1- or 2-day ticket purely because of hand-off latency and the daily work-boundary. Even non-escalated tickets suffer when Level-1 agents take hours to start investigation, after which the same overnight boundary appears.

Practical insights / recommendations:

- Tighten the triage → assignment → first-investigation SLA (target < 30–45 min) and monitor Level-1 queue length in real time; the 3.5 h gap in case 104 is a clear capacity or prioritisation signal.
- Treat escalated tickets as high-priority; introduce an explicit Level-2 response-time target (e.g., investigate within 2–4 h of escalation) and consider a small “fast-track” Level-2 capacity or after-hours coverage for escalated work.
- Examine whether complex investigations can be partially parallelised or handed off with better context so that resolution does not routinely wait until the next calendar day.
- Add process metrics that separately track (a) waiting time before first investigation, (b) escalation latency, and (c) calendar time versus pure working time; this makes the overnight boundary visible and actionable.

Addressing the waiting-time and escalation-handoff bottlenecks would be expected to bring the outlier cases much closer to the 1–2 hour cycle times already demonstrated by cases 101 and 103.
