## Analysis of Potential Bias Points

The original model only ensures a basic process flow (`StartApplication`  possibly `RequestAdditionalInfo`  `FinalDecision`). It does **not** protect against:

1. Sensitive-attribute checks (`CheckApplicantRace`, `CheckApplicantGender`, `CheckApplicantAge`) being immediately followed by a decision (`Approve`, `Reject`) without any intervening fairness safeguard.
2. Demographic-specific decision outcomes (e.g., `Reject_Minority`) occurring without a corresponding `ManualReview`.
3. Direct chains from sensitive-attribute disclosure to negative outcomes (`Reject`) that bypass any bias-mitigation step.

To address these, I added constraints in five categories: **existence**, **coexistence**, **response**, **precedence/chainprecedence**, and **nonsuccession/nonchainsuccession**.

## Rationale for Each Added Constraint

| Constraint Type | Added Rule | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **existence** | `ManualReview`, `BiasMitigationCheck` must exist | Guarantees the fairness-oriented activities are actually part of the process model, not optional add-ons. |
| **responded_existence** | `CheckApplicantRace/Gender/Age`  `BiasMitigationCheck` | If a sensitive attribute is examined anywhere, a bias mitigation step must also occur somewhere in the trace. |
| **coexistence** | `Approve_Minority`/`Reject_Minority` (and analogous gender/age variants)  `ManualReview` | Ensures that any demographic-specific decision is always accompanied by a manual, human-in-the-loop review — reducing automated bias. |
| **response** | `CheckApplicantRace/Gender/Age`  `BiasMitigationCheck` | Reinforces that disclosure of a sensitive attribute triggers an eventual mitigation step before the process can be considered complete. |
| **precedence** | `BiasMitigationCheck` before `Approve`/`Reject` | Ensures no decision is made without a prior bias check having occurred — decisions cannot "skip" fairness verification. |
| **succession** | `BiasMitigationCheck`  `FinalDecision` | Combines precedence and response to guarantee mitigation checks are tightly coupled with the final decision-making step. |
| **chainprecedence** | `BiasMitigationCheck` immediately before `Approve`/`Reject` | Strengthens the precedence constraint by requiring the mitigation check to be the *direct* predecessor of the decision, preventing bypass via intervening irrelevant activities. |
| **nonsuccession** | `CheckApplicantRace/Gender/Age` cannot be eventually followed directly by `Reject` (without mitigation) | Blocks patterns where a rejection decision follows shortly after a sensitive attribute check, which is a strong indicator of discriminatory decision-making. |
| **nonchainsuccession** | `CheckApplicantRace/Gender/Age` cannot be *immediately* followed by `Reject` | The strictest safeguard — directly prevents the highest-risk bias pattern: sensitive attribute check immediately causing rejection. |

### Summary

These additions collectively enforce a **"check-then-mitigate-then-decide"** pattern:
- Sensitive attributes may still be recorded (`CheckApplicantRace`, etc.), since this may be needed for compliance/reporting.
- However, any such check **must** be followed by a `BiasMitigationCheck`.
- Decisions (`Approve`, `Reject`) can **only** occur after this mitigation step, and never directly/immediately following a sensitive-attribute disclosure.
- Demographic-specific decision variants are **forced to coexist** with a `ManualReview`, adding human oversight to automated or attribute-influenced outcomes.

This structure reduces the risk that the sequence of activities encodes or permits a discriminatory shortcut (sensitive attribute  immediate decision), while still allowing the process to legitimately collect demographic data for fairness auditing purposes.
