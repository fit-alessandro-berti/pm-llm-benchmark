**2. Cases that fail to meet the desiderata (recommended but non-strict conditions)**

Desiderata violations are not treated as serious process failures; they only indicate suboptimal behaviour relative to the stated preferences (Credit Check preferably within 24 h of Application Received, Inform Applicant preferably within 48 h of Loan Decision, and a Follow-up Call sometime before Close Case).

- **Case 1**  
  – No Follow-up Call is recorded before Close Case.  
  (Timings of Credit Check and Inform Applicant are well within the recommended windows.)

- **Case 2**  
  – No Follow-up Call is recorded.  
  – Because Inform Applicant is missing, the “prompt notification” desideratum cannot be satisfied either.  
  (The other timing windows are short, but the absence of the required Inform step already places the case outside recommended practice.)

- **Case 3**  
  – Credit Check occurs more than 24 h after Application Received (≈ 49 h later).  
  – Inform Applicant occurs more than 48 h after Loan Decision (≈ 51 h later).  
  – No Follow-up Call is recorded before Close Case.  

All three cases therefore exhibit at least one desideratum deviation, with Case 3 showing the clearest timing shortfalls and every case missing the encouraged Follow-up Call.

**3. Possible interpretations / justifications consistent with the given constraints**

- Case 2’s order inversion (Risk Assessment before Credit Check) and the subsequent Finalize Documentation after a rejection most likely reflect either a process bypass (e.g., an urgent or exceptional handling path that was not formally authorised) or a logging error. Under the stated normative rules neither explanation is acceptable; the observed trace remains non-conformant.  
- Case 3’s delayed Credit Check and delayed notification are readily explained by ordinary operational backlog or resource constraints. Because these are only desiderata, the delays do not invalidate the case, yet they illustrate suboptimal responsiveness.  
- The universal absence of a Follow-up Call across all three cases suggests that this activity is currently treated as optional in practice, even though the process description encourages it. Adding it would improve alignment with the recommended best practices without affecting normative compliance.
