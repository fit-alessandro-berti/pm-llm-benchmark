### Explanation of Logic

**1. Case Identification**
Cases were identified by grouping events around the specific resource (document, email, file) being manipulated. A logical unit of work in a desktop environment typically revolves around a specific artifact. Therefore, we have 5 distinct cases:
- **DOC1**: Work related to `Document1.docx`.
- **QDOC**: Work related to `Quarterly_Report.docx`.
- **EMAIL**: Handling the "Annual Meeting" thread within the `Email - Inbox`.
- **PDF**: Reviewing `Report_Draft.pdf`.
- **EXCEL**: Updating `Budget_2024.xlsx`.

**2. Activity Naming**
Raw low-level inputs (e.g., `TYPING`, `CLICK`, `FOCUS`, `SCROLL`) are often too granular for business process analysis and don't communicate intent. They have been abstracted into standardized, higher-level activity names:
- `FOCUS` / `SWITCH` (when initiating a task) translates to **Open Document/Spreadsheet/PDF/Email Client**.
- `SWITCH` (when returning to a previously opened but paused document) translates to **Resume Work**.
- `TYPING` translates to **Edit Content** or **Compose Reply**, depending on the application context.
- `SCROLL` in an email/PDF translates to **Review Content**.
- `CLICK` actions were translated based on intent: **Open Email**, **Initiate Reply**, or **Send Email**.
- `SAVE` and `CLOSE` are kept as recognizable end-step activities (**Save Document**, **Close Document**, etc.).

**3. Handling Interruptions and Waiting Times**
Instead of explicitly creating "Pause Work" events every time the user switched applications (which would clutter the log), waiting/interruption times are implicitly captured. For instance, in the **DOC1** case, the gap in time between the "Save Document" event at 09:01:15 and the "Resume Work" event at 09:06:00 allows process mining algorithms to automatically calculate the waiting time before the user returned to the document.
