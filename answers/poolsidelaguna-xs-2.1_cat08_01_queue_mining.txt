## **1. Queue Identification and Characterization**

### **Calculating Waiting Times**
In the context of outpatient clinic processes, **waiting time (queue time)** between two consecutive activities is defined as the time elapsed between the **completion of the preceding activity** and the **start of the subsequent activity** for the same patient visit. This captures the time a patient spends idle between tasks, such as waiting for a nurse after registration.

To calculate this:
- For each patient visit (case ID), sort activities chronologically by their timestamps.
- For every pair of consecutive activities (e.g., Registration  Nurse Assessment), compute the difference between the **start timestamp of the next activity** and the **completion timestamp of the previous activity**.
- Sum these intervals for each visit to calculate total waiting time between activities and across the entire visit.

### **Key Metrics**
To characterize the queues, the following metrics are calculated:
1. **Average Waiting Time**: Mean duration of a specific queue across all cases.
2. **Median Waiting Time**: Robust measure of central tendency, less affected by outliers.
3. **Maximum Waiting Time**: Identifies extreme cases of delays.
4. **90th Percentile Waiting Time**: Highlights how long the "worst 10%" of patients wait.
5. **Queue Frequency**: Number of times a specific queue occurs (e.g., "Registration  Nurse Assessment" is the most common transition).
6. **Cases with Excessive Waits**: Count of visits where a queue exceeds a predefined threshold (e.g., 15 minutes), indicating systemic issues.

### **Identifying Critical Queues**
Critical queues are prioritized using a combination of:
- **High Average/Median Wait Times**: Indicates systemic delays (e.g., Doctor Consultation  ECG often takes 20 minutes on average).
- **High Frequency**: Frequent queues may impact many patients (e.g., Registration  Nurse is the most common transition).
- **Impact on Patient Types**: Queues disproportionately affecting critical groups like Urgent or New patients (e.g., delays in ECG for Urgent cases could compromise care).
- **Contribution to Overall Visit Duration**: Queues that account for the largest portion of total waiting time (e.g., Doctor  Test accounts for 50% of the visit’s total delay).

**Example**: If "Doctor Consultation  ECG" has an average wait of 30 minutes and affects 40% of visits, it becomes a priority target.

## **2. Root Cause Analysis**

### **Potential Root Causes**
1. **Resource Bottlenecks**:
   - **Staffing**: Nurses or technicians may be over-allocated. For instance, if only one ECG technician works during peak hours, it creates a queue.
   - **Room/Equipment Utilization**: High demand for limited resources (e.g., only one ECG room) leads to queuing.

2. **Activity Dependencies and Handovers**:
   - Sequential dependencies (e.g., Doctor Consultation must finish before ECG can start) may force patients to wait even if downstream resources are available.

3. **Variability in Activity Durations**:
   - Unpredictable service times (e.g., Doctor Consultations varying from 10–45 minutes) disrupt flow and create instability in queues.

4. **Appointment Scheduling Policies**:
   - Overbooking or poor spacing of appointments may overload early stages (e.g., too many New patients scheduled at 9 AM).

5. **Patient Arrival and Type Differences**:
   - New patients may take longer to complete registration, causing delays for others. Urgent cases might be prioritized inconsistently.

6. **Operational Workflows**:
   - Manual processes (e.g., paper-based check-out) or lack of coordination between departments (e.g., ECG techs not notified of Doctor Consultation completion).

### **Process Mining Techniques for Root Causes**
- **Resource Analysis**: Use activity-resource mappings to identify overused staff/rooms. For example, a heatmap showing Nurse 1 is active 90% of the time during peak hours.
- **Bottleneck Analysis**: Compute throughput times and identify activities with high waiting times or low capacity.
- **Variant Analysis**: Compare patient paths to see if certain flows (e.g., New Patient  Doctor  ECG) have worse queues than others.
- **Conformance Checking**: Compare actual flows to an ideal process model to identify deviations (e.g., patients skipping ECG when not scheduled).
- **Performance Analysis**: Correlate queue times with patient demographics or urgency levels (e.g., Urgent patients wait longer in Doctor Consultation queues).

## **4. Consideration of Trade-offs and Constraints**

### **Trade-offs**
1. **Cost vs. Efficiency**:
   - **Resource Reallocation** requires temporary staffing, but avoids permanent cost increases.
   - **Parallel Processing** may require minor tech training but no infrastructural changes.

2. **Bottleneck Shifting**:
   - Adding staff to Registration might overload Nurse Assessment, requiring further adjustments.

3. **Patient Experience**:
   - Staggered appointments may extend overall clinic hours, but reduce perceived wait times.

### **Balancing Objectives**
- **Cost Control**: Use part-time staff or cross-train existing staff (e.g., clerks assisting with check-out).
- **Quality of Care**: Ensure parallel processing does not compromise thoroughness (e.g., ECGs still reviewed by Doctors).
- **Staff Workload**: Monitor utilization rates to prevent burnout. For example, avoid scheduling clerks beyond 8-hour shifts.

## **5. Measuring Success**

### **Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)**
1. **Average Waiting Time Between Activities**: Track reductions in targeted queues (e.g., Doctor  ECG).
2. **Total Patient Visit Duration**: Measure from Registration Start to Check-out Completion.
3. **Patient Satisfaction Scores**: Post-implementation surveys to assess perceived wait times.
4. **Throughput (Patients/Hour)**: Ensure increased efficiency without compromising care.
5. **Resource Utilization Rates**: Ensure staff/equipment are neither over- nor underutilized.

### **Ongoing Monitoring**
- **Automated Dashboards**: Use process mining tools to update KPIs weekly, highlighting deviations (e.g., sudden increases in Doctor Consultation waits).
- **Feedback Loops**: Monthly reviews with staff to identify operational issues (e.g., equipment failures).
- **Predictive Analytics**: Forecast peak times using historical data to preemptively adjust resources.

By systematically applying these strategies and continuously monitoring outcomes, the clinic can achieve measurable improvements in patient satisfaction and operational efficiency while balancing cost and quality constraints.
