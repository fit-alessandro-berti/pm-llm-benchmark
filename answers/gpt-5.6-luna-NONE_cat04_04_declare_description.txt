The DECLARE model describes a tightly controlled loan-approval process in which every application follows a defined sequence and certain unsafe or noncompliant behaviors are prohibited.

## Practical process flow

### 1. Receive the application

The process must begin with `Receive_Application`, because it is marked as an initial activity (`init`). The bank records the customer’s application and creates the case used to track all subsequent work.

The `existence` constraint also requires this activity to occur. In practice, this supports auditability: the institution should be able to show when the application was received, what information was supplied, and which decisions were made afterward.

### 2. Perform the preliminary credit check

`Receive_Application` has a `response` relationship to `Preliminary_Credit_Check`. This means that whenever an application is received, a preliminary credit check must eventually follow.

The model also specifies `exactly_one` occurrence of `Preliminary_Credit_Check`. Thus, each case should contain one—and not multiple—preliminary checks. This can prevent inconsistent decisions, duplicate processing, or attempts to repeatedly check an applicant until a favorable result is obtained.

The credit check provides an initial assessment of creditworthiness, potential repayment risk, existing debt, and possible fraud indicators.

### 3. Gather additional documents

The model specifies a `succession` relationship from `Preliminary_Credit_Check` to `Gather_Additional_Documents`. In the intended business interpretation, the credit assessment occurs before the institution requests or collects the documents needed for detailed underwriting.

The document-gathering activity is also mandatory under `existence`. Typical documents might include proof of income, identification, tax records, bank statements, or collateral information.

The model further requires `Gather_Additional_Documents` and `Authorize_Contract_Terms` to coexist. Therefore, the bank cannot finalize the contract terms without the document-collection stage being present.

The `altsuccession` constraint connects document gathering with `Quality_Assurance_Review`, requiring the review to occur as the relevant next stage or permitted alternative according to the model’s Declare semantics. Operationally, this means that once the necessary documentation has been gathered, the case must move into a controlled review stage rather than bypassing quality controls.

### 4. Assemble the loan offer package

After the required information has been collected and assessed, the institution prepares the `Assemble_Loan_Offer_Package` activity. This package may include:

- Approved principal amount
- Interest rate
- Repayment schedule
- Fees and conditions
- Required disclosures
- Contractual documentation

The model requires the package to be followed by `Quality_Assurance_Review` through `responded_existence`. It also uses `chainsuccession` to place the sequence:

`Quality_Assurance_Review  Assemble_Loan_Offer_Package`

Depending on the exact Declare interpretation and implementation, these constraints should be read together as requiring a controlled relationship between package preparation and quality review. In practical terms, the offer should not be treated as finalized until the package has passed QA, and the package should be the object reviewed by the QA function.

### 5. Conduct quality assurance

`Quality_Assurance_Review` verifies that the proposed loan is complete, internally consistent, legally compliant, and supported by appropriate evidence.

The `precedence` constraint requires `Quality_Assurance_Review` to occur before `Authorize_Contract_Terms`. This prevents an employee from approving contract terms before another control function has reviewed them.

This separation supports:

- Regulatory compliance
- Segregation of duties
- Detection of calculation or documentation errors
- Prevention of unauthorized approvals
- Consistent application of lending policies

### 6. Authorize the contract terms

`Authorize_Contract_Terms` represents the official approval or finalization of the loan terms.

The model’s `chainprecedence` relationship links authorization and the earlier credit-check stage, reinforcing that authorization must be supported by the earlier assessment rather than occurring independently. Together with the precedence rule for QA, this implies a control path such as:

`Preliminary_Credit_Check  Gather_Additional_Documents  Assemble_Loan_Offer_Package  Quality_Assurance_Review  Authorize_Contract_Terms`

The precise Declare constraints may permit some additional events or variations, but the business intent is clear: authorization is downstream of underwriting evidence and quality control.

### 7. Transfer the funds

`Transfer_Funds` represents disbursement of the approved loan into the customer’s account.

The `chainresponse` constraint requires funds transfer to follow `Assemble_Loan_Offer_Package` through the prescribed chain of events. It is therefore not an independent action that an employee should perform merely because an application exists.

The `noncoexistence` constraint between `Transfer_Funds` and `Receive_Application` is potentially unusual if interpreted literally, because every loan case normally contains both an application and a disbursement. In practical terms, it likely represents a safeguard against transferring funds merely on the basis of an intake event, or it may have been intended to prohibit a direct or premature relationship between those activities. If interpreted strictly as “these activities may not both occur in the same trace,” it would conflict with the described end-to-end loan process. This constraint should therefore be clarified or revised before implementation.

### 8. Notify the customer

`Notify_Customer` informs the customer of the outcome, next steps, or completed disbursement.

The model includes an `altresponse` relationship from `Transfer_Funds` to `Notify_Customer`, indicating that a notification should follow disbursement or serve as an allowed response to it. The `altprecedence` relationship in the opposite direction also links notification and transfer, suggesting that the system must preserve a controlled relationship between these events.

The `nonchainsuccession` constraint prohibits `Authorize_Contract_Terms` from being directly followed by `Notify_Customer`. This means authorization alone should not immediately trigger the final customer notification. There should first be intervening operational steps—such as offer completion, disbursement preparation, or funds transfer—so that the notification accurately reflects the actual state of the loan.

The `nonsuccession` constraint prevents `Notify_Customer` from being directly followed by `Preliminary_Credit_Check`. This avoids confusing customer communications with the beginning of underwriting and helps ensure that a notification is not used as a substitute for a required credit assessment.

## Prohibited behavior

`Proceed_Without_Compliance` is listed under `absence`, meaning it must never occur. This represents a broad prohibition against bypassing mandatory compliance procedures, such as:

- Approving a loan without required documentation
- Ignoring sanctions or identity checks
- Disbursing funds without authorization
- Skipping quality assurance
- Overriding lending rules without proper approval

This rule is especially important for audit, consumer protection, anti-money-laundering controls, and regulatory reporting.

## Overall control logic

Taken together, the model aims to enforce the following business principles:

1. Every case begins with a recorded application.
2. A preliminary credit check is mandatory and occurs once.
3. Required supporting documentation must be collected.
4. A formal offer package must be prepared.
5. The package must pass quality and compliance review.
6. Contract terms cannot be authorized before review.
7. Funds cannot be transferred as an informal or premature action.
8. Customer communications must correspond to the actual processing stage.
9. No one may proceed by bypassing compliance controls.

The support and confidence values of `1.0` indicate that these relationships are treated as fully reliable in the model. In a real process-mining setting, however, such values would normally be derived from observed event logs and might reveal exceptions, rework, cancellations, or process deviations.

## Real-world motivations

These constraints support several practical objectives:

- **Regulatory compliance:** Lending decisions must be documented, justified, and based on required information.
- **Fraud prevention:** Credit checks, identity documents, and controlled authorization reduce the risk of fraudulent applications or account manipulation.
- **Risk management:** The institution should not commit funds until repayment risk and contractual conditions have been assessed.
- **Segregation of duties:** A separate QA review reduces the risk that the same person prepares and approves an improper loan.
- **Operational consistency:** Standard ordering makes cases easier to process, monitor, and audit.
- **Customer satisfaction:** Accurate, timely notifications reduce uncertainty and prevent customers from receiving misleading information.

## Consequences of violations

If activities occur out of order or mandatory activities are omitted, several problems could result:

- **Skipping the credit check:** The bank could approve unsuitable or fraudulent borrowers, increasing default and fraud losses.
- **Missing documents:** The loan may be legally incomplete, impossible to audit, or based on inaccurate income or identity information.
- **Skipping QA:** Errors in rates, fees, disclosures, or contract language could reach the customer.
- **Authorizing too early:** The institution could commit to terms that have not been properly reviewed.
- **Transferring funds prematurely:** Money could be disbursed to an unauthorized recipient or before the contract is valid.
- **Notifying too early:** Customers might be told that a loan is approved or funded when the process is still incomplete.
- **Proceeding without compliance:** The organization could face regulatory penalties, lawsuits, reputational damage, and internal disciplinary action.

Thus, the model represents more than a simple sequence. It defines a controlled loan lifecycle in which evidence, review, authorization, disbursement, and communication must occur in a traceable and defensible manner.
