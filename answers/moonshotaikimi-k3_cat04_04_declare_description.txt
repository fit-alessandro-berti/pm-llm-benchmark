# Reading the DECLARE Model as a Loan Approval Process

Taken as a whole, this model describes a **tightly gated, linear loan origination pipeline** with a single forbidden shortcut. The intended happy path is:

**Receive_Application  Preliminary_Credit_Check  Gather_Additional_Documents  Quality_Assurance_Review  Assemble_Loan_Offer_Package  Authorize_Contract_Terms  Transfer_Funds  Notify_Customer** — with `Proceed_Without_Compliance` banned outright.

## Step-by-Step Walkthrough

1. **Intake (Receive_Application).** The `init` and `existence` constraints guarantee every case begins here — nothing can precede or substitute for formal intake. This anchors the audit trail: regulators (KYC/AML, fair-lending rules) require a documented origination point.
2. **Preliminary credit screen (Preliminary_Credit_Check).** `response(Receive  Credit_Check)` ensures *every* application is screened; `exactly_one` ensures it happens **once and only once** — never skipped, never repeated. Repetition matters: duplicate hard inquiries harm the applicant's credit score, and re-running checks is a classic way to "shop" for a passing result.
3. **Document collection (Gather_Additional_Documents).** `succession(Credit_Check  Gather)` enforces screen-first, collect-second. You only burden viable applicants with document requests — good customer experience and good data hygiene (don't collect PII you have no basis to need).
4. **Quality assurance (Quality_Assurance_Review).** `altsuccession(Gather  QA)` makes collection and verification **strictly alternate**: each batch of documents is verified before another round can begin. `responded_existence(Assemble  QA)` additionally guarantees no offer package ever exists without a QA review somewhere in the case.
5. **Offer assembly (Assemble_Loan_Offer_Package).** `chainsuccession(QA  Assemble)` says the package is built **immediately** after QA — no intervening edits, no stale drafts. The offer reflects exactly the reviewed data.
6. **Authorization (Authorize_Contract_Terms).** `precedence(QA  Authorize)` enforces QA-before-sign-off (the maker–checker principle), and `coexistence(Gather  Authorize)` ties authorization to a complete file: no approval without documentation, and no dossier left undecided.
7. **Disbursement (Transfer_Funds).** `chainresponse(Assemble  Transfer)` pushes disbursement immediately after packaging, minimizing the window where terms can drift or be tampered with.
8. **Notification (Notify_Customer).** `altresponse(Transfer  Notify)` and `altprecedence(Notify  Transfer)` create a strict **one-to-one pairing**: every transfer is acknowledged to the customer before any further transfer, and no notification is ever sent without a real disbursement behind it.

## The Negative Constraints as Guardrails

- `absence(Proceed_Without_Compliance)` — an explicit "never": bypassing mandated checks isn't merely discouraged; any trace containing it is automatically non-conformant. This is the compliance backstop.
- `nonsuccession(Notify  Credit_Check)` — once the customer is notified, no new preliminary credit check may follow: the decision is final, the case is closed, and no post-hoc re-underwriting can occur.
- `nonchainsuccession(Authorize  Notify)` — the customer is never notified *immediately* after authorization; an execution step (disbursement) must intervene, so notifications always describe an actual outcome, not an internal approval.
- `noncoexistence(Transfer, Receive)` — in spirit: no disbursement in a case that is merely at intake stage.

## Real-World Motivations

- **Regulatory compliance:** mandatory intake, screening, QA, and authorization create the documented, auditable chain examiners expect; the `absence` constraint mirrors internal red-line policies.
- **Fraud prevention:** document verification loops catch forged pay stubs; separating *assembly* from *authorization* blocks a single bad actor from creating and approving an offer; per-transfer notification alerts the genuine customer to unauthorized disbursements; `exactly_one` on the credit check prevents decision-gaming.
- **Risk management:** cheap screening precedes costly verification; money moves only after terms are fixed and signed off; the 1:1 transfer/notification pairing makes reconciliation trivial.
- **Operational best practice:** no orphan files (`coexistence`), no post-decision rework (`nonsuccession`), and `chain` constraints eliminate handoff ambiguity and latency between QA, packaging, and payment.
- **Customer satisfaction:** applicants aren't asked for documents prematurely, suffer only one credit pull, and receive prompt, truthful communication that always corresponds to executed events.

## Consequences of Violating the Order

- **Skipping the credit check**  lending blind: adverse selection, default losses, fair-lending exposure.
- **Collecting documents before screening**  wasted effort, applicant drop-off, and data-protection risk from purposeless PII collection.
- **Omitting QA**  defective rates/terms, mis-disclosed APRs, contract disputes and restitution.
- **Authorizing before QA or without documents**  rubber-stamping; errors become binding; a direct internal-fraud vector and audit failure.
- **Transferring funds before authorization**  money leaves against unenforceable terms; recovery is legally painful.
- **Notifying without (or before) transfer**  misinformation, customer harm, potential liability for "promised" funds.
- **Any occurrence of `Proceed_Without_Compliance`**  automatic conformance failure; in practice, findings, fines, and reputational damage.

## An Honest Caveat: Tensions in the Model as Written

A few constraints conflict with the narrative above and would need refinement before this model could drive conformance checking:

1. **`noncoexistence(Transfer_Funds, Receive_Application)`** — since `Receive_Application` is mandatory (`init` + `existence`), this literally forbids *any* transfer, while the chain constraints (`existence(Gather)`  `altsuccession`  `chainsuccession`  `chainresponse`) *force* a transfer to occur. The likely intent was a weaker rule such as `precedence(Receive, Transfer)` — "no disbursement without a completed application."
2. **`chainprecedence(Authorize, Credit_Check)`** — read literally, the credit check may occur only *immediately after* authorization, but the model also forces Credit_Check  Gather  QA  Authorize, creating an unsatisfiable cycle. 3. **Placement of authorization** — because `Assemble` must be *immediately* followed by `Transfer`, the literal model leaves no room for `Authorize_Contract_Terms` between packaging and payment, awkwardly pushing formal authorization after disbursement. The natural fix is a chain of QA  Assemble  **Authorize**  Transfer.

With those three adjustments, the model cleanly realizes the intended process: screened intake, verified documentation, QA-gated offers, segregated authorization, and reconciled disbursement-plus-notification — with compliance bypasses rendered impossible by construction.
