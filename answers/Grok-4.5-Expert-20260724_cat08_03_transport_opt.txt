**1. Process Discovery and Conformance Checking**

**Data preprocessing and integration**  
The raw sources must be unified into a single event log with the standard process-mining columns (Case ID, Activity, Timestamp) plus rich attributes (Vehicle ID, Driver ID, Package ID, Location, Speed, Notes, Planned Stop Sequence, Capacity, Time Window, Maintenance Flag, etc.).

Recommended case granularity is the *Vehicle-Day* (e.g., `V12-20241205`). This captures the complete end-to-end operational day while still allowing package-level sub-traces to be extracted when needed.  

Integration steps:  
- Align timestamps to a common timezone and resolve clock skew between GPS, scanners and the dispatch system (typically ±30–90 s).  
- Map heterogeneous event labels onto a controlled activity vocabulary (e.g., “Depart Depot”, “Arrive Customer”, “Delivery Success”, “Delivery Failed”, “Unscheduled Stop”, “Traffic Delay”, “Maintenance Start/End”, “Return Depot”).  
- Enrich GPS points with derived activities (Idle > 3 min → “Unscheduled Stop” or “Traffic Jam”; speed < 5 km/h for > 2 min in known congestion zones → “Traffic Delay”).  
- Link package events to the Vehicle-Day case via Package ID and temporal proximity.  
- Append planned attributes (route sequence, time windows, vehicle capacity) from the dispatch system as case or event attributes.  
- Flag maintenance intervals from the maintenance logs so they appear as explicit activities or as case attributes that can later be filtered.

Typical challenges:  
- Incomplete scanner coverage (missed “Depart Customer” events).  
- GPS gaps or multipath errors in dense urban canyons.  
- Ambiguous case boundaries when a vehicle is reassigned mid-day.  
- Multiple simultaneous packages at one stop (need activity-instance identifiers).  
- Noise from short GPS pings that must be filtered or aggregated into meaningful activities.

**Process discovery**  
Apply inductive miner or heuristic miner (with frequency and dependency thresholds tuned to logistics data) to the cleaned Vehicle-Day event log. The resulting model (BPMN or Petri net) will show the *actual* control-flow: Depot → Travel → Arrive Customer → (Success | Failed) → Depart → … → Return Depot, together with loops for re-attempts, side branches for unscheduled stops and maintenance, and parallelisms when a driver handles multiple packages at one location.  

Visualisation should include the frequency-annotated graph, the performance-annotated graph (mean/median durations), and a solid-line vs. dashed-line distinction between frequent and infrequent paths. Geo-spatial enrichment (plotting activities on a map) further reveals spatial patterns that pure control-flow models miss.

**Conformance checking**  
Align the discovered (or a hand-crafted “planned”) model against the event log using alignment-based or token-based replay. Key deviation types to surface:  
- Sequence deviations: stops performed out of planned order, or insertion of unplanned stops.  
- Missing/extra activities: omitted deliveries, extra maintenance or personal stops.  
- Timing deviations: significant deviations from planned travel or service times (e.g., > 20 % longer).  
- Resource deviations: vehicle or driver substitutions not recorded in the dispatch system.  

Fitness, precision and generalisation metrics quantify overall compliance; per-case fitness scores identify the worst-performing Vehicle-Days for deeper investigation.

**2. Performance Analysis and Bottleneck Identification**

**Relevant KPIs and their calculation from the event log**  
- On-Time Delivery Rate = fraction of “Delivery Success” events whose timestamp falls inside the customer time window (or within a tolerance).  
- Average Time per Delivery Stop = mean (Depart Customer – Arrive Customer).  
- Travel-Time vs. Service-Time ratio = total inter-stop travel time / total service time.  
- Vehicle Utilisation = (time moving or serving) / (shift length).  
- Failed-Delivery Rate = “Delivery Failed” / total attempted deliveries.  
- Frequency & duration of Traffic Delays = count and sum of “Traffic Delay” / “Low Speed” intervals.  
- Fuel proxy (when direct fuel data are absent) = estimated from speed profiles and idle times; later correlated with actual fuel logs if available.  
- Mean dwell time at depot, mean overtime, etc.

All of the above are obtained by standard process-mining performance projection: throughput-time analysis, waiting-time analysis, and attribute-based filtering/aggregation.

**Bottleneck identification techniques**  
- Performance spectrum / dotted charts ordered by time-of-day or by route cluster reveal systematic slow periods.  
- Bottleneck analysis on the process model highlights activities or transitions with high mean waiting or sojourn times (e.g., “Arrive Customer → Delivery Success” or long “Travel” arcs between certain stop pairs).  
- Spatial heat-maps of idle or low-speed events identify traffic hotspots.  
- Resource perspective (driver or vehicle as resource) shows which drivers or vehicle types systematically exhibit longer service or travel times.  
- Time-of-day and day-of-week slicing isolates rush-hour effects.

Quantification is straightforward: absolute time lost (sum of excess durations), percentage contribution to total cycle time, and monetary proxies (extra fuel, overtime, re-delivery cost).

**3. Root Cause Analysis for Inefficiencies**

Process mining supplies the “where” and “how much”; the “why” is obtained by multi-perspective and comparative analyses:

- **Variant analysis**: cluster high-performing vs. low-performing Vehicle-Days (or routes) by fitness, on-time rate or total duration. Differences in control-flow (extra loops, different stop sequences) or in attribute distributions (traffic density, package density, driver) point to root causes.  
- **Travel-time estimation accuracy**: compare planned inter-stop times (from dispatch) with actual GPS-derived travel times; systematic under-estimation on certain corridors indicates outdated speed profiles or missing real-time traffic.  
- **Service-time variability**: analyse the distribution of dwell times at customer locations; high variance often correlates with parking difficulty, multi-package stops, or customer interaction issues.  
- **Failed-delivery impact**: measure the cascade effect—extra travel for re-attempts, overtime, and reduced capacity for subsequent stops.  
- **Maintenance & breakdowns**: filter cases containing “Unscheduled Stop – Engine Warning”; quantify lost productive time and correlation with vehicle age or cumulative mileage.  
- **Driver behaviour**: resource performance spectra or decision-mining on the choice of next stop can reveal systematic deviations from the planned sequence or excessive idle time.  

Correlation of external attributes (weather, known congestion indices, vehicle type) with delay durations further validates or refutes candidate root causes.

**4. Data-Driven Optimization Strategies**

**Strategy A – Dynamic re-routing engine driven by discovered travel-time distributions**  
- Targets: long travel arcs and traffic-delay bottlenecks.  
- Root cause: static planned times that ignore real-time congestion patterns and historical variability uncovered by process mining.  
- Support: performance-annotated process model + spatial heat-maps supply realistic, time-of-day-dependent travel-time distributions; conformance deviations show which planned sequences are most frequently abandoned.  
- Expected impact: 8–15 % reduction in total travel time, higher on-time rate, lower fuel consumption per package.

**Strategy B – Failed-delivery reduction via predictive time-window refinement and proactive customer messaging**  
- Targets: high failed-delivery rate and consequent re-attempt loops.  
- Root cause: rigid time windows and lack of real-time customer availability signals.  
- Support: variant analysis of failed vs. successful deliveries reveals patterns (time-of-day, location clusters, package type); dwell-time distributions indicate how much buffer is realistically needed.  
- Expected impact: 20–30 % drop in failed attempts, reduced overtime, higher vehicle utilisation, improved customer satisfaction.

**Strategy C – Predictive maintenance scheduling based on usage and anomaly patterns**  
- Targets: unscheduled stops and overtime caused by mid-shift breakdowns.  
- Root cause: reactive maintenance that ignores the actual intensity of use (mileage, idle cycles, low-speed events) visible in the GPS and scanner log.  
- Support: cases containing “Unscheduled Stop – Engine Warning” correlated with cumulative vehicle attributes; process mining can surface early warning patterns (increasing frequency of short low-speed events).  
- Expected impact: fewer mid-shift failures, higher vehicle availability, lower emergency-repair costs, more predictable shift lengths.

(Additional strategies such as territory re-balancing or targeted driver coaching can be derived similarly from resource and spatial analyses.)

**5. Operational Constraints and Continuous Monitoring**

All strategies must respect hard constraints that are already present as attributes in the event log:  
- Driver working-time regulations and overtime caps (filter or constrain solutions that would violate them).  
- Vehicle capacity and residual payload (re-routing must not exceed capacity).  
- Customer time windows (dynamic re-routing and messaging must still honour or renegotiate windows).  
- Depot opening hours and shift start/end times.

**Continuous monitoring plan**  
Deploy a process-mining dashboard (e.g., based on Celonis, Disco, or an open-source stack) that is refreshed daily or near-real-time from the integrated event stream. Core views:  
- Live conformance fitness and top deviation types.  
- KPI trend lines (on-time rate, average stop time, failed-delivery rate, overtime hours, estimated fuel proxy).  
- Performance spectrum and bottleneck heat-map for the current week vs. baseline.  
- Variant comparison of the newly optimised routes versus the historical “best” variants.  
- Alerting on emerging anomalies (sudden rise in unscheduled stops, new traffic hotspots, driver-specific degradation).

Monthly deep-dive reviews re-run full discovery and root-cause analyses to detect concept drift (new congestion patterns, seasonal demand shifts, fleet composition changes) and to recalibrate the optimisation models. This closed-loop approach ensures that improvements remain sustainable and that new inefficiencies are detected early.
