## 2. Detailed Analysis

### Case 1 — Conforming
The trace matches the prescribed fulfillment sequence exactly: *Receive order, Check inventory, Authorize payment, Package items, Ship order, Close order*. Every ordering constraint is satisfied and "Close order" is the final event.

### Case 2 — Violation: Packaging before payment authorization
- **Observed:** `Check inventory  Package items  Authorize payment  Ship order`
- **Rule violated:** "*'Package items' should only occur after authorization…*" and "*'Authorize payment' should only occur after 'Check inventory' and **before packaging**.*"
- **Explanation:** The order of *Package items* and *Authorize payment* is inverted. The items were physically prepared for shipment before the customer's payment method had been validated and approved. This exposes the organization to the risk of investing fulfillment effort (and potentially shipping) on an order whose payment may later be declined. All activities are present and shipping does follow packaging, so this is purely an ordering (precedence) violation, not a missing-activity violation.

### Case 3 — Violation: Shipping after cancellation (and payment authorization on a cancelled order)
- **Observed:** `Check inventory  Authorize payment  Cancel order  Ship order  Close order`
- **Rules violated:**
  1. "*'Ship order' should only occur after packaging and **never after a cancellation**.*" — Here *Ship order* directly follows *Cancel order*, which is the most severe deviation in the log: goods were dispatched for an order that had already been voided.
  2. "*'Package items' should only occur after authorization and before shipping*" — *Ship order* occurred with **no preceding Package items** event, so the mandatory packaging precondition for shipping is missing.
  3. **Path mixing / exclusivity violation:** The description defines two mutually exclusive branches. The presence of *Cancel order* implies the inventory check found insufficient stock, in which case the process "*should directly proceed to Cancel order*." Instead, *Authorize payment* was inserted between *Check inventory* and *Cancel order*, meaning the customer's payment was authorized for an order that could not be fulfilled — a likely refund/financial-exposure problem.
- **Note:** The only constraint this case satisfies is that *Close order* is last.

### Case 4 — Conforming
This is the canonical insufficient-inventory path: *Receive  Check  Cancel  Close*. No fulfillment activities (authorize, package, ship) appear after the cancellation, and *Close order* terminates the case, exactly as prescribed.

### Case 5 — Violation: Missing "Package items" before shipping
- **Observed:** `Check inventory  Authorize payment  Ship order  Close order`
- **Rule violated:** "*Only after items have been properly packaged is it appropriate to proceed with 'Ship order'*" / "*'Ship order' should only occur after packaging.*"
- **Explanation:** The mandatory *Package items* step is entirely absent from the fulfillment branch. Since the case followed the sufficient-inventory path (payment was authorized and the order shipped), the full six-activity sequence was required. Shipping without a recorded packaging step means either the step was skipped in reality or was not recorded — both are compliance failures, since "items must be prepared before they can be shipped."

## 3. Notes on Interpreting the Ambiguities

- **Branch condition is not directly observable.** The log records no inventory outcome attribute, so branch membership must be *inferred* from the activities present: the presence of *Cancel order* implies insufficient stock; the presence of *Authorize payment / Package / Ship* implies sufficient stock. Case 3 contains both signals, which is why it is treated as a branch-mixing violation rather than a mere ordering slip.
- **"Extraordinary circumstances" for early authorization.** The description hints that authorizing payment before conditions are confirmed *might* be defensible. Even under the most permissive reading, however, Case 2 still fails, because packaging-before-authorization is explicitly named as a violation, and Case 3 still fails because "never ship after cancellation" is stated as an absolute.
- **Close order as terminal event.** All five cases satisfy this constraint; it is not a source of any deviation here.
- **No concurrency escape hatch.** The description states strict "only after / before" precedences, so simultaneous or reordered execution of *Authorize payment* and *Package items* cannot be excused as parallelism.

**Final answer: Cases 2, 3, and 5 are non-conforming; Case 3 is the most serious, involving shipment of a cancelled order, an unpackaged shipment, and payment authorization on an unfulfillable order.**
