# Analysis of the Two POWL Models

## Standard / Normative Hire-to-Retire Skeleton

For reference, a normative sequence would look like:

```
Post_Job_Ad  Screen_Candidates  Conduct_Interviews  Make_Hiring_Decision  Onboard_Employee  Add_to_Payroll  Close_Case
```

Each step is a logical prerequisite for the next: you cannot decide before interviewing, cannot onboard someone you haven't decided to hire, and cannot close a case for an employee who was never entered into payroll.

---

## Model 1 – Structural Analysis

Edges:
```
Post  Screen
Screen  Decide
Screen  Interview
Decide  Onboard  Payroll  Close
```

**Key observation:** `Decide` and `Interview` are both direct successors of `Screen`, but **no edge exists between them**. In a partial order, absence of an edge means the two nodes are *concurrent* — their relative execution order is unconstrained.

### Anomaly in Model 1
- **Interview/Decision independence (severe):** The model permits `Make_Hiring_Decision` to occur before, after, or in parallel with `Conduct_Interviews`. This breaks the fundamental causal logic of hiring — a decision is supposed to be *informed by* the interview outcome. Allowing the decision to precede (or run in parallel with) the interview is a serious logical flaw, since it decouples the decision from its evidentiary basis.

Apart from this one ordering violation, the rest of the model (Post  Screen  Onboard  Payroll  Close) is fully sequential and faithful to the normative process. There is no skipping of payroll, no unnecessary repetition, and the overall structure is otherwise sound.

---

## Model 2 – Structural Analysis

Edges:
```
Post  Screen
Post  Interview
Interview  Decide
Decide  loop_onboarding  xor_payroll  Close
```//
where `loop_onboarding = *(Onboard, skip)` and `xor_payroll = X(Payroll, skip)`.

### Anomalies in Model 2

1. **Screening decoupled from Interview/Decision (moderate-to-severe):** `Screen_Candidates` depends only on `Post_Job_Ad`; it has no ordering relation to `Interview` or `Decide`. This means screening can happen concurrently with, or even after, interviewing and deciding — violating the expected logic that candidates must be *screened* before being *interviewed* and *evaluated*.

2. **Repeatable onboarding (minor):** The loop `*(Onboard, skip)` guarantees `Onboard_Employee` executes at least once, but allows it to repeat indefinitely. This is a **mild** anomaly — unusual, but not necessarily harmful (it could be interpreted as "re-onboarding" steps), and it does not violate the completeness of the process.

3. **Skippable payroll (severe):** The XOR construct `X(Payroll, skip)` allows `Add_to_Payroll` to be omitted entirely. This is a **critical anomaly** since it permits the process to reach `Close_Case` for an employee who was onboarded but **never entered into the payroll system** — undermining the very purpose of the hiring process (i.e., that hiring results in compensated employment).

---

## Comparative Assessment

| Aspect | Model 1 | Model 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Ordering violation between screening/interview/decision | Interview vs. Decide unordered (1 violation) | Screening vs. Interview/Decide unordered (1 violation) |
| Skippable core step | None | Payroll may be skipped entirely |
| Repetition anomalies | None | Onboarding may loop unnecessarily |
| Number of anomalies | 1 | 3 |
| Severity of anomalies | High (single ordering flaw) | High + Moderate + Low (compounded issues) |

Both models exhibit an ordering flaw of comparable severity (a missing causal dependency among the "screen–interview–decide" cluster). However, **Model 2 introduces two additional deviations**, one of which — the ability to skip `Add_to_Payroll` entirely — is arguably the most damaging anomaly across both models, since it allows the process to terminate (`Close_Case`) without ever compensating the hired employee. This directly contradicts the essential outcome of a "Hire-to-Retire" process: successfully hired and onboarded employees must be integrated into the payroll system.

---

## Conclusion

**Model 1 aligns more closely with the normative Hire-to-Retire process.**

- It contains a single anomaly — the lack of a causal link between `Conduct_Interviews` and `Make_Hiring_Decision` — which, while serious, is an isolated deviation in an otherwise fully sequential and logically consistent model.
- **Model 2**, in contrast, compounds a similar ordering issue (`Screen_Candidates` decoupled from `Interview`/`Decide`) with a structurally optional payroll step and an unnecessary onboarding loop. The skippable payroll step in particular represents a **fundamental integrity violation**, since it allows the process to complete without fulfilling the core obligation of adding a new hire to payroll.

Given that Model 2 has more anomalies overall, and one of them (skippable payroll) is arguably more damaging to the *fundamental purpose* of the process than Model 1's single flaw, **Model 1 should be considered the more normative and reliable representation of the Hire-to-Retire process**, despite not being fully correct itself.
