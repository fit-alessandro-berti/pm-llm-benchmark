## 1. Normative-rule violations

### Case 1 — Compliant
Case 1 follows the required sequence:

1. Application Received  
2. Credit Check  
3. Risk Assessment  
4. Loan Decision — Approved  
5. Inform Applicant  
6. Finalize Documentation  
7. Close Case  

No normative violations are present. The approved branch correctly finalizes documentation before closing.

### Case 2 — Violations present
Case 2 violates several strict rules:

- **Risk Assessment occurred before Credit Check.**  
  The risk assessment at 08:20 preceded the credit check at 09:00, violating the required ordering.

- **The required process order is therefore broken.**  
  Even though a credit check was eventually performed, it did not occur before the risk assessment as required.

- **Inform Applicant is missing.**  
  The process requires informing the applicant after the loan decision. No such activity appears in the log.

- **Documentation was finalized after a rejection.**  
  The loan was rejected at 09:30, but documentation was finalized at 10:00. This is explicitly prohibited.

The case closing itself occurred after the decision, but it does not make the process compliant because the rejection branch requires closing without finalizing documentation and requires the applicant to be informed first.

### Case 3 — Normatively compliant
Case 3 follows the required order:

1. Application Received  
2. Credit Check  
3. Risk Assessment  
4. Loan Decision — Approved  
5. Inform Applicant  
6. Finalize Documentation  
7. Close Case  

The activities are in the correct order, and because the decision was approved, documentation was finalized before closing. There are no normative violations.

The long delays do not violate the stated hard rules because the timing requirements are recommendations rather than strict constraints.

## 2. Desiderata violations

### Case 1 — No apparent desiderata violation
- The credit check occurred 30 minutes after application receipt, within the recommended 24-hour window.
- The applicant was informed two hours after the loan decision, within the recommended 48-hour window.
- No follow-up call is recorded, but the follow-up call is encouraged rather than required. Its absence is therefore, at most, a minor desiderata shortcoming.

### Case 2 — Follow-up and prompt notification concerns
- **No Follow-up Call is recorded.**  
  This is a recommended practice, not a strict requirement.
- **Inform Applicant is missing.**  
  This is primarily a normative violation, not merely a desideratum issue, because informing the applicant is mandatory after the decision.
- The credit check occurred one hour after application receipt, so it met the recommended timing window. However, its ordering relative to the risk assessment was incorrect.

### Case 3 — Desiderata violations
Case 3 violates the recommended timing expectations:

- **Credit Check was delayed.**  
  The application was received on January 12 at 09:00, but the credit check occurred on January 14 at 10:00—approximately 49 hours later, exceeding the recommended 24-hour window.

- **Applicant notification was delayed.**  
  The loan decision occurred on January 14 at 12:00, while the applicant was informed on January 16 at 15:00—approximately 51 hours later, exceeding the recommended 48-hour window.

- **No Follow-up Call is recorded.**  
  This is another minor desideratum shortcoming.

These are suboptimal behaviors but do not constitute hard process violations, since the required activity order was preserved.

## 3. Possible interpretations or justifications

- **Case 1:** This appears to be an ideal or near-ideal execution. The absence of a follow-up call can reasonably be treated as acceptable because the call is optional and no other issue is present.

- **Case 2:** The early risk assessment might reflect preliminary or provisional work performed before the formal credit check. However, under the stated rules, it still counts as a violation because risk assessment must occur after the credit check. The post-rejection documentation finalization could be explained as an administrative mistake or an attempt to process closure paperwork, but it remains explicitly disallowed. The missing applicant notification may indicate an incomplete event log or an omitted activity; if the log is complete, it is a direct normative violation.

- **Case 3:** The delays could be explained by operational backlogs, unavailable credit information, or difficulty reaching the applicant. These explanations may account for the desiderata violations, but they do not change the assessment that the timing was suboptimal. Since the sequence itself remained correct, Case 3 is still normatively compliant.
