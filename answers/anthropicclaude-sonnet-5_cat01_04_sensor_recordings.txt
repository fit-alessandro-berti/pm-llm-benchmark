# Process Mining Analysis: Manufacturing Assembly Line Log

## Step 1: Identifying Phases Through Sensor Pattern Analysis

By examining transitions in temperature, vibration, flow, tool position, and energy consumption, I identified **6 distinct phases** with clear boundary signatures.

---

## Labeled Activities

### **Phase 1: Machine Idle / Startup (Events 1–3)**
| Event | Temp | Pressure | Vibration | Flow | Tool Pos | Energy |
|-------|------|----------|-----------|------|----------|--------|
| 1–3   | 20-21| 1        | 0         | 0    | 0        | 0.40-0.50 |

**Rationale:** Near-ambient temperature, zero vibration, zero material flow, and minimal energy draw indicate the machine is powered on but not performing work — a baseline/idle state before the cycle begins.

---

### **Phase 2: Cutting Metal (Events 4–6)**
| Event | Temp | Pressure | Vibration | Flow | Tool Pos | Energy |
|-------|------|----------|-----------|------|----------|--------|
| 4–6   | 50-58| 1-1.5    | 30-38     | 5    | 5-10     | 2.00-3.00 |

**Rationale:** Sharp jump in **temperature** (2058°C) and **vibration** (038Hz) is characteristic of high-friction mechanical cutting. Tool position increases steadily (510mm), suggesting a blade/tool advancing through material. Constant material flow (5 units/s) indicates feedstock being consumed. Energy consumption rises accordingly due to motor load.

---

### **Phase 3: Assembling Parts (Events 7–8)**
| Event | Temp | Pressure | Vibration | Flow | Tool Pos | Energy |
|-------|------|----------|-----------|------|----------|--------|
| 7–8   | 40   | 2        | 10        | 2    | 15       | 2.00   |

**Rationale:** Readings stabilize at moderate levels — vibration drops significantly (3810Hz) indicating gentler mechanical action, while pressure increases (fixturing/clamping parts together). Tool position holds steady at 15mm (positioning components), and flow rate reduces to 2 units/s (controlled part feeding rather than raw material consumption). This steady-state signature suggests component fitting/joining rather than aggressive material removal.

---

### **Phase 4: Welding (Event 9)**
| Event | Temp | Pressure | Vibration | Flow | Tool Pos | Energy |
|-------|------|----------|-----------|------|----------|--------|
| 9     | 80   | 2        | 50        | 0    | 15       | 5.00   |

**Rationale:** This is the most extreme single-event spike: **highest temperature (80°C)**, **highest vibration (50Hz)**, **highest energy consumption (5.00 kWh)**, yet **zero material flow**. This pattern strongly suggests a high-intensity, short-duration process like welding — heat and energy are concentrated at the joint, no new material is being fed, and vibration reflects arc/mechanical fusion activity. Tool position remains fixed at 15mm (weld point stationary).

---

### **Phase 5: Quality Inspection (Events 10–12)**
| Event | Temp | Pressure | Vibration | Flow | Tool Pos | Energy |
|-------|------|----------|-----------|------|----------|--------|
| 10    | 30   | 1        | 1         | 0    | 15       | 1.00   |
| 11–12 | 25   | 1        | 5         | 1    | 20       | 1.50-1.60 |

**Rationale:** Sudden **cooling** (80°C30°C) and near-zero vibration suggest the machine has stopped aggressive processing and entered a low-energy, low-disturbance state — consistent with sensors/cameras scanning the part. Slight tool movement (1520mm) may represent a probe or inspection arm repositioning. Low, stable energy usage supports a non-manufacturing, monitoring-type operation.

---

### **Phase 6: Packaging / Cycle Completion (Event 13–15)**
| Event | Temp | Pressure | Vibration | Flow | Tool Pos | Energy |
|-------|------|----------|-----------|------|----------|--------|
| 13–15 | 20-21| 1        | 0         | 0    | 0        | 0.40-0.50 |

**Rationale:** Values return to the exact baseline seen in Phase 1 — tool resets to position 0, energy drops to idle levels, vibration and flow cease. This mirrors packaging/completion where the finished part is removed and the machine resets for the next cycle.

---

## Summary Table

| Phase | Events | Activity Label | Key Signature |
|-------|--------|----------------|----------------|
| 1 | 1–3 | **Idle/Startup** | Baseline, zero activity |
| 2 | 4–6 | **Cutting Metal** | Temp & vibration spike, tool advances, steady flow |
| 3 | 7–8 | **Assembling Parts** | Moderate stable readings, reduced vibration, pressure rise |
| 4 | 9 | **Welding** | Extreme temp/vibration/energy spike, zero flow |
| 5 | 10–12 | **Quality Inspection** | Rapid cooling, minimal vibration, low energy, repositioning |
| 6 | 13–15 | **Packaging/Cycle Reset** | Return to baseline idle state |

---

## Key Deduction Logic

1. **Temperature spikes**  mechanical stress/friction (cutting) or thermal joining (welding)
2. **Vibration levels**  intensity of physical process (high = cutting/welding, low = inspection/assembly)
3. **Material flow**  active consumption of raw material (cutting) vs. none (welding/inspection)
4. **Tool position progression**  sequential movement through workspace, marking transitions between operations
5. **Energy consumption**  directly correlates with process intensity, peaking during welding
6. **Return-to-baseline patterns**  demarcate start/end of full production cycles

This phase segmentation demonstrates how **numeric sensor fluctuations alone** can reveal an implicit process model, even without explicit activity labels — a core principle of process mining from unlabeled event data.
