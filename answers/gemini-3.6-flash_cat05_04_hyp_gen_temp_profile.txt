### 1. Temporal Profile Anomalies

* **Receive (`R`) to Approve (`P`) [~25 Hours, STDEV = 1 Hour]:**
  The standard deviation (1 hour) is exceptionally low relative to the average duration (90,000 seconds / ~25 hours). In a human-driven process, claim approval times usually vary based on claim complexity, workload, and documentation. Such a tight distribution suggests an artificial or automated schedule rather than organic case handling.

* **Approve (`P`) to Notify (`N`) [7 Days, STDEV = 2 Days]:**
  An average delay of 7 days between internal approval and customer notification is unusually prolonged. Once a claim is approved, informing the customer should typically take minimal time. A multi-day lag indicates process friction or significant queuing downstream.

* **Assign (`A`) to Close (`C`) [2 Hours, STDEV = 1 Hour]:**
  Closing a claim within an average of 2 hours after assignment is extremely rapid. Given that standard claim execution requires evaluation (`E`) and approval (`P`), a 2-hour window implies that required intermediate steps are either being bypassed or that claims are being prematurely canceled/rejected.

* **Evaluate (`E`) to Notify (`N`) [5 Minutes, STDEV = 1 Minute]:**
  A 5-minute transition from evaluation to customer notification is unnaturally fast if the standard process sequence (`R` $\rightarrow$ `A` $\rightarrow$ `E` $\rightarrow$ `P` $\rightarrow$ `N` $\rightarrow$ `C`) is followed. It indicates that either approval (`P`) is being skipped entirely, recorded retroactively, or triggered automatically without human verification.

---

### 2. Hypotheses for Anomalies

1. **Scheduled Batch Processing (R $\rightarrow$ P):**
   Approvals may be managed by an automated cron job or scheduled system process that executes once every 24 hours. This creates a uniform time interval across claims regardless of individual evaluation times.

2. **Communication Bottlenecks & Outgoing Queue Backlogs (P $\rightarrow$ N):**
   The long delay in notifications could stem from manual batch sending of customer communications (e.g., weekly physical mailings or centralized call-center queue assignments), understaffed customer support departments, or failure of an automated email gateway.

3. **Mass Triage & Fast-Track Rejections (A $\rightarrow$ C):**
   Adjusters may be closing invalid, duplicate, or withdrawn claims immediately upon assignment without performing a full evaluation. Alternatively, claims may be auto-closed due to system errors or incorrect categorization.

4. **Bypassing Approval Gates / Automated Triggers (E $\rightarrow$ N):**
   The notification system might be configured to trigger directly upon submission of the evaluation form (`E`), circumventing the approval step (`P`). Alternatively, small claim amounts might auto-approve without generating a distinct `P` log entry.

### 3. SQL Verification Queries

#### Query 1: Detect Batch-Like Execution Timing Between Receive (`R`) and Approve (`P`)
*Verifies whether approvals cluster around specific hours of the day (indicating scheduled automated jobs) and calculates actual variance.*

```sql
WITH receive_approve AS (
    SELECT 
        e1.claim_id,
        e1.timestamp AS receive_time,
        e2.timestamp AS approve_time,
        EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (e2.timestamp - e1.timestamp)) AS duration_seconds
    FROM claim_events e1
    JOIN claim_events e2 ON e1.claim_id = e2.claim_id
    WHERE e1.activity = 'R' 
      AND e2.activity = 'P'
)
SELECT 
    EXTRACT(HOUR FROM approve_time) AS approval_hour_of_day,
    COUNT(*) AS total_approvals,
    ROUND(AVG(duration_seconds) / 3600.0, 2) AS avg_duration_hours,
    ROUND(STDDEV(duration_seconds) / 3600.0, 2) AS stddev_duration_hours
FROM receive_approve
GROUP BY EXTRACT(HOUR FROM approve_time)
ORDER BY total_approvals DESC;
```

#### Query 2: Identify Excessive Delays Between Approval (`P`) and Notification (`N`) Correlated by Region and Claim Type
*Pinpoints where the 7-day notification delay is concentrated across claim types, regions, and adjusters.*

```sql
WITH approve_notify AS (
    SELECT 
        p.claim_id,
        p.timestamp AS approve_time,
        n.timestamp AS notify_time,
        EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (n.timestamp - p.timestamp)) / 86400.0 AS delay_days
    FROM claim_events p
    JOIN claim_events n ON p.claim_id = n.claim_id
    WHERE p.activity = 'P' 
      AND n.activity = 'N'
)
SELECT 
    c.claim_type,
    a.region,
    COUNT(an.claim_id) AS delayed_claims_count,
    ROUND(AVG(an.delay_days), 2) AS avg_delay_days,
    ROUND(MAX(an.delay_days), 2) AS max_delay_days
FROM approve_notify an
JOIN claims c ON an.claim_id = c.claim_id
LEFT JOIN claim_events ae ON c.claim_id = ae.claim_id AND ae.activity = 'A'
LEFT JOIN adjusters a ON ae.resource = a.name
WHERE an.delay_days > 3 -- Filtering for abnormally delayed notifications
GROUP BY c.claim_type, a.region
ORDER BY avg_delay_days DESC;
```

#### Query 3: Identify Claims Closed (`C`) Quickly After Assignment (`A`) Missing Intermediate Steps
*Finds claims closed within 2 hours of assignment and checks if Evaluation (`E`) or Approval (`P`) were omitted.*

```sql
WITH assign_close AS (
    SELECT 
        a.claim_id,
        a.timestamp AS assign_time,
        c.timestamp AS close_time,
        EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (c.timestamp - a.timestamp)) / 3600.0 AS duration_hours
    FROM claim_events a
    JOIN claim_events c ON a.claim_id = c.claim_id
    WHERE a.activity = 'A' 
      AND c.activity = 'C'
)
SELECT 
    ac.claim_id,
    cl.claim_type,
    cl.claim_amount,
    ROUND(ac.duration_hours, 2) AS hours_to_close,
    ARRAY_AGG(ce.activity ORDER BY ce.timestamp) AS activity_sequence
FROM assign_close ac
JOIN claims cl ON ac.claim_id = cl.claim_id
JOIN claim_events ce ON ac.claim_id = ce.claim_id
WHERE ac.duration_hours <= 2
GROUP BY ac.claim_id, cl.claim_type, cl.claim_amount, ac.duration_hours
HAVING NOT ('E' = ANY(ARRAY_AGG(ce.activity))) 
    OR NOT ('P' = ANY(ARRAY_AGG(ce.activity)))
ORDER BY ac.duration_hours ASC;
```

#### Query 4: Evaluate Direct Rapid Transitions from Evaluate (`E`) to Notify (`N`) and Check Approval Status
*Checks claims where notification occurred within 10 minutes of evaluation and verifies if an Approval (`P`) event existed between them.*

```sql
WITH eval_notify AS (
    SELECT 
        e.claim_id,
        e.timestamp AS eval_time,
        n.timestamp AS notify_time,
        EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (n.timestamp - e.timestamp)) / 60.0 AS duration_minutes
    FROM claim_events e
    JOIN claim_events n ON e.claim_id = n.claim_id
    WHERE e.activity = 'E' 
      AND n.activity = 'N'
)
SELECT 
    en.claim_id,
    c.claim_type,
    c.claim_amount,
    ROUND(en.duration_minutes, 2) AS minutes_e_to_n,
    CASE 
        WHEN EXISTS (
            SELECT 1 
            FROM claim_events p 
            WHERE p.claim_id = en.claim_id 
              AND p.activity = 'P' 
              AND p.timestamp BETWEEN en.eval_time AND en.notify_time
        ) THEN 'Approved Between E and N'
        ELSE 'Approval Skipped / Missing'
    END AS approval_status
FROM eval_notify en
JOIN claims c ON en.claim_id = c.claim_id
WHERE en.duration_minutes <= 10
ORDER BY en.duration_minutes ASC;
```
