**Group B (Unprotected Group) exhibits the bias.**  

The two logs follow the same high-level process (ApplicationReceived → DataValidation → PreliminaryScoring → ManualReview → FinalDecision) and contain comparable preliminary scores, yet they diverge systematically once the attributes `LocalResident` and `CommunityGroup` interact with the `ScoreAdjustment` column.

### Key differences
- **Group A (Protected)**  
  - `LocalResident = FALSE`, `CommunityGroup = None` for every case.  
  - `ScoreAdjustment` is uniformly 0 (or N/A).  
  - Final scores equal the preliminary scores.  
  - Outcomes: 720 → Approved, 710 → Rejected, 740 → Approved.

- **Group B (Unprotected)**  
  - `LocalResident = TRUE` for every case.  
  - When `CommunityGroup = "Highland Civic Darts Club"`, a `+10 (Community Boost)` is applied at the PreliminaryScoring step and carried forward.  
  - This produces adjusted scores of 730 (from 720) and 705 (from 695), both of which are Approved.  
  - The single case without club membership (U002, score 710) receives no boost and is Rejected—identical to the corresponding Group A case.

### How the bias manifests
The community-membership rule creates a **preferential pathway** that is available only to the unprotected group:
- A preliminary score of 695 that would almost certainly be rejected under the Group A rules is lifted above the decision threshold and approved.  
- The same 710 score is rejected in both groups when no boost is present, showing that the underlying score-to-decision mapping is consistent *until* the community attribute intervenes.  
- Because membership in “Highland Civic Darts Club” appears exclusively in the Group B log and is never observed (or applied) for Group A, the adjustment functions as a systematic advantage correlated with the unprotected-group attributes (`LocalResident = TRUE` + specific community affiliation).

Consequently, final decisions are not determined solely by the preliminary score; they are modulated by an attribute that is unevenly distributed across the two groups. This produces disparate outcomes for otherwise comparable applicants and is the clear source of bias visible in the event logs.
