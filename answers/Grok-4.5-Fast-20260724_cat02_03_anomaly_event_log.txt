**Anomalies identified in the event log (relative to standard Procure-to-Pay flow):**

Standard expected sequence (high-level):  
Create Purchase Requisition → Approve Purchase Requisition → Issue Purchase Order → Receive Goods → Vendor Invoice Received → Match Invoice to PO → Release Payment → Close Case.  

Controls typically enforce approval before commitment (PO), goods/services receipt before (or concurrent with) invoice handling, three-way match (PO–receipt–invoice) before payment, and no payment without proper documentation.

### Case 1001
No material anomalies. Sequence follows the expected order.

### Case 1002 — Multiple serious control failures
- **Issue Purchase Order** occurs immediately after Create Purchase Requisition, with **no prior Approve Purchase Requisition**.  
  *Problem*: Bypasses authorization. Creates unauthorized commitment/risk of unapproved spend.
- **Vendor Invoice Received** and **Release Payment** occur *before* **Receive Goods** and *before* **Match Invoice to PO**.  
  *Problem*: Payment without evidence of delivery and without matching → high risk of paying for goods never received or paying incorrect amounts.
- **Approve Purchase Requisition** occurs *after* the PO has already been issued *and after* payment has already been released.  
  *Problem*: Retroactive “approval” is meaningless for control purposes; the spend has already occurred.

### Case 1003 — Payment before documentation and receipt
- **Release Payment** occurs right after the PO, *before* **Vendor Invoice Received**, *before* **Receive Goods**, and *before* **Match Invoice to PO**.  
  *Problem*: Classic “pay first, document later” violation. Creates risk of overpayment, duplicate payment, or payment for non-existent/undelivered goods. The subsequent invoice and receipt cannot properly support a payment that has already left the organization.

### Case 1004 — Receipt before purchase order
- **Receive Goods** occurs *before* **Issue Purchase Order**.  
  *Problem*: Goods arrive without a formal PO. This breaks the audit trail, complicates three-way matching, can create liability/ownership issues, and often indicates informal/unauthorized purchasing (or data-entry error). Matching an invoice to a PO that did not exist at the time of receipt is weak.

**Summary of key risks across the anomalous cases**  
- Unauthorized commitments (missing or late approval).  
- Payments without evidence of receipt or without proper matching (fraud, error, and loss exposure).  
- Broken audit trail and weakened segregation of duties / internal controls.  

These patterns are classic red flags in P2P process mining and internal-audit reviews.
