## Intended practical process

### 1. Receive the application

- `Receive_Application` is mandatory and must be the first activity.
- No credit check, document gathering, or disbursement should happen before an application exists.

Real-world reason: you cannot evaluate or fund a loan that was never submitted.

---

### 2. Run the preliminary credit check

- `Receive_Application` triggers `Preliminary_Credit_Check`.
- The credit check must happen exactly once.
- It must also happen before `Gather_Additional_Documents`.

Real-world reason: this is an early risk screen. It avoids spending time and money collecting documents for applicants who would clearly fail an initial credit assessment.

---

### 3. Gather additional documents

- Document gathering is mandatory.
- It is linked to final authorization: `Gather_Additional_Documents` and `Authorize_Contract_Terms` must either both happen or both be absent.
- In the intended flow, documents are collected before quality assurance and before final approval.

Real-world reason: a complete loan file is needed for underwriting, compliance, and auditability. You cannot responsibly approve a loan without verifying income, identity, or other required documents.

---

### 4. Assemble the loan offer package

- The loan offer package contains the proposed terms, interest rate, conditions, and documents.
- In a realistic process, this package should be completed before quality assurance review so that QA reviews the actual prepared offer.

The model’s `chainsuccession(Quality_Assurance_Review, Assemble_Loan_Offer_Package)` would, if read literally, place QA before package assembly, which conflicts with the activity descriptions. In the intended process, assembly happens before QA.

---

### 5. Perform quality assurance review

- `Quality_Assurance_Review` must occur before `Authorize_Contract_Terms`.
- If a loan offer package is assembled, some QA review must also occur somewhere in the process.

Real-world reason: this is the compliance and quality gate. It ensures the offer is accurate, complete, and aligned with internal policies and regulatory requirements before anyone is allowed to approve it.

---

### 6. Authorize contract terms

- Authorization is the official approval of the loan terms.
- It requires QA to have happened already.
- It requires that additional documents were gathered if they were needed.

Real-world reason: final approval should only happen after due diligence, document verification, and quality review. Authorizing before these steps would create serious regulatory and credit risk.

---

### 7. Transfer funds

- In the intended process, funds are transferred only after the loan offer has been assembled and authorized.
- The model contains `chainresponse(Assemble_Loan_Offer_Package, Transfer_Funds)`, which literally means transfer immediately follows package assembly. In a more realistic model, this should be a normal `response` or `succession` after authorization, not an immediate chain before approval.

Real-world reason: disbursement is the point of no return. Funds should never move until the loan is fully approved and compliant.

---

### 8. Notify the customer

- `Transfer_Funds` must be followed by `Notify_Customer`.
- The model also says that `Authorize_Contract_Terms` cannot be immediately followed by `Notify_Customer`, which makes sense: something must happen in between, namely the funds transfer.
- `Notify_Customer` must not be followed by `Preliminary_Credit_Check`, meaning the customer should not be told the outcome and then have the credit check run later.

Real-world reason: customers should be informed only after the money has actually been transferred. Premature or misleading notifications damage trust and can create legal or service problems.

---

## Why these rules matter in practice

- **Regulatory compliance:** QA before authorization, document collection, and the ban on `Proceed_Without_Compliance` all protect against regulatory violations.
- **Fraud prevention and risk management:** the credit check before document collection and the lack of disbursement before approval reduce the risk of lending to unqualified applicants.
- **Operational best practices:** the process is ordered to avoid wasted work and to ensure that each step has the necessary input from the previous step.
- **Customer satisfaction:** notifying the customer only after funds are transferred avoids confusing or misleading communication.

---

## Consequences of violations

If these rules are ignored, the process breaks down:

- Running the credit check after gathering documents wastes operational effort and increases risk.
- Skipping document gathering can lead to incomplete underwriting and higher default risk.
- Authorizing before QA can allow non-compliant or incorrect loan terms to be approved.
- Transferring funds before authorization could disburse money for a loan that was never properly approved.
- Notifying the customer before the transfer creates false expectations and potential reputational harm.
- Allowing `Proceed_Without_Compliance` to occur would be a serious compliance failure and could expose the institution to fines, legal action, and loss of trust.

So, while the literal DECLARE model contains contradictions, the intended process is a standard, controlled loan lifecycle: **apply  screen credit  gather documents  prepare offer  review quality  authorize  disburse  notify**.
