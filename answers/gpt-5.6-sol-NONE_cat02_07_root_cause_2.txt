## 1. Case-duration analysis

Lead time is measured from **Submit Claim** to **Close Claim**.

| Case | Region | Complexity | Additional-document requests | Lead time | Assessment |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 2001 | A | Low | 0 | **1h 30m** | Fast |
| 2002 | B | Medium | 1 | **25h 55m** | Elevated |
| 2003 | A | High | 2 | **48h 20m** | Long |
| 2004 | B | Low | 0 | **1h 25m** | Fast |
| 2005 | B | High | 3 | **77h 05m** | Longest |

The cases with clear performance issues are:

- **Case 2005:** 77h 05m
- **Case 2003:** 48h 20m
- **Case 2002:** 25h 55m, moderately delayed relative to the low-complexity baseline

Cases **2001** and **2004** establish a normal low-complexity duration of approximately **1.5 hours**.

## 2. Likely root causes

### A. Complexity is the strongest observed driver

There is a clear relationship between complexity, document rework, and total lead time:

- **Low complexity:** about 1.5 hours, no document requests
- **Medium complexity:** about 26 hours, one document request
- **High complexity:** 48–77 hours, two or three document requests

Both high-complexity cases undergo repeated **Request Additional Documents** events. This indicates that delays primarily arise from incomplete evidence, iterative clarification, or more extensive validation requirements.

The longest waits are concentrated before approval:

- **2003:** second document request to approval takes **23 hours**
- **2005:** first-to-second request takes **29.5 hours**, second-to-third takes **22 hours**, and third request to approval takes **19 hours**

Thus, document collection and review—not submission, evaluation, or closure—appear to be the principal bottleneck.

### B. Region B may compound delays, but is not independently causal

Region B contains the longest case, **2005**, and the medium case, **2002**. However, Region B also contains **2004**, which is the fastest case overall.

Therefore:

- Region B itself does **not** make every case slow.
- It may amplify delays for complex claims through local workload, customer responsiveness, document requirements, staffing, or handoff practices.
- A proper regional comparison should control for complexity. There is only one high-complexity case per region, so the evidence is suggestive rather than conclusive.

For high-complexity claims:

- Region A, case 2003: **48h 20m**
- Region B, case 2005: **77h 05m**

This 28h 45m difference may reflect a regional effect, but it is also tied to one extra document-request cycle in case 2005.

### C. Resource associations

#### Adjusters

- **Adjuster_Mike**
  - Low case 2001: fast
  - High case 2003: long, with two document requests
- **Adjuster_Lisa**
  - Low case 2004: fast
  - Medium case 2002: delayed, with one request
  - High case 2005: longest, with three requests

Lisa is associated with the slower medium/high cases, but she also handles a low-complexity case quickly. This suggests that **case mix and repeated documentation cycles** are more plausible causes than individual performance alone.

Still, case 2005 warrants investigation into whether:

- the initial document request was sufficiently complete,
- reviews were performed incrementally rather than as one consolidated review,
- Lisa had excessive workload,
- Region B imposes additional documentation requirements.

#### Managers

- **Manager_Ann** handles low and medium cases; low cases are fast.
- **Manager_Bill** approves both high-complexity cases.

Bill’s cases are much longer, but this is strongly confounded by complexity. Manager Bill may be the designated approver for high-risk claims rather than the cause of delay. Nevertheless, approval queues for high-complexity claims should be examined.

#### Finance

Payment after approval varies:

- 2001, Finance_Alan: 15 minutes
- 2002, Finance_Carl: 45 minutes
- 2003, Finance_Alan: 17 hours
- 2004, Finance_Carl: 30 minutes
- 2005, Finance_Carl: 4 hours

Case 2003 shows a substantial **approval-to-payment delay**, likely influenced by overnight timing: approval occurred at 16:00 and payment at 09:00 the next day. Case 2005 also has a four-hour payment delay. Finance may therefore be a secondary contributor for complex claims, though not the main root cause.

#### CSR resources

CSR activities do not appear to be a meaningful bottleneck. Closure generally occurs within 15–30 minutes after payment, regardless of CSR.

## 3. Explanations and mitigation actions

### Root cause 1: Incomplete or unclear documentation

**Explanation:** Customers may not know all required evidence at submission. High-complexity claims may also expose additional requirements only after review, causing repeated request-and-response cycles.

**Mitigation:**

- Introduce complexity-specific submission checklists.
- Add mandatory-field and document validation at intake.
- Give customers examples of acceptable evidence.
- Use a pre-assessment questionnaire to predict required documents.
- Send one consolidated request instead of multiple sequential requests where possible.

### Root cause 2: Slow external response to document requests

**Explanation:** Much of the elapsed time may be waiting for claimants, providers, repair shops, or other third parties to return documents.

**Mitigation:**

- Use a self-service upload portal.
- Send automated reminders and escalation notifications.
- Track “waiting for customer” separately from internal processing time.
- Establish response targets and clearly communicate deadlines.
- Integrate directly with common external evidence providers where feasible.

### Root cause 3: High-complexity review and approval queues

**Explanation:** High-complexity cases may require specialist review and Manager Bill’s authorization, creating a limited-capacity queue.

**Mitigation:**

- Create a dedicated high-complexity claims team.
- Cross-train additional adjusters and approval managers.
- Set approval service-level targets.
- Prioritize cases that are documentation-complete.
- Use risk-based approval thresholds so only genuinely exceptional cases require senior approval.

### Root cause 4: Possible Region B operating differences

**Explanation:** Region B’s complex case has more document requests and a longer duration. Possible causes include different regulations, third-party response times, customer communication patterns, or workload.

**Mitigation:**

- Compare Regions A and B within the same complexity category using a larger sample.
- Review regional documentation rules and standardize them where possible.
- Monitor workload and queue length by region.
- Share practices from faster regional teams.
- Route overflow work across regions if regulations and operating models allow it.

### Root cause 5: Resource workload or request quality

**Explanation:** Adjuster_Lisa’s complex case required three document requests. This may indicate fragmented requests, but it could equally reflect a harder case or poor customer response.

**Mitigation:**

- Audit the content and timing of document requests.
- Measure repeat-request rates by adjuster, controlled for complexity and region.
- Provide templates and decision-support tools.
- Review adjuster caseload and availability.
- Avoid attributing delays to an individual until case mix and waiting time are controlled.

### Root cause 6: Payment timing and batch processing

**Explanation:** Complex claims experience longer post-approval waits, particularly when approval occurs late in the day.

**Mitigation:**

- Automate payment initiation immediately after approval.
- Add late-day payment runs.
- Establish an approval-to-payment SLA.
- Investigate whether complex/high-value claims require manual finance checks.

## Conclusion

The strongest finding is:

> **Higher complexity leads to more document-request cycles, and those cycles account for most of the extended lead time.**

Case **2005** is the most serious performance issue, followed by **2003**. Region B, Adjuster_Lisa, Manager_Bill, and Finance timing are associated with some slow cases, but the small sample and confounding by complexity prevent firm causal conclusions. The first priorities should be reducing repeated document requests, improving high-complexity case handling, and measuring internal processing time separately from external waiting time.
