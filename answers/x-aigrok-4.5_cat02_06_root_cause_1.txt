**1. Cases with significantly longer resolution times**

Total cycle times (Receive Ticket  Close Ticket):

- **Case 101**: ~2 h 15 min  
- **Case 103**: ~1 h 20 min  
- **Case 102**: ~25 h 10 min  
- **Case 104**: ~24 h 10 min  
- **Case 105**: ~49 h 5 min  

Cases **102, 104 and 105** are outliers (10–40× longer than the fast cases 101/103).

**2. Root-cause patterns**

- **Escalation to Level-2 is the dominant driver**  
  - Present **only** in the long cases 102 and 105.  
  - After “Escalate to Level-2 Agent” there are large idle gaps before the second “Investigate Issue” (2.5 h in 102; ~28 h in 105) and another large gap before “Resolve Ticket”.

- **Long waiting / queueing times even without escalation**  
  - Case 104 (no escalate) still shows:  
    – 40 min receivetriage,  
    – 3.5 h assigninvestigate,  
    – ~19 h investigateresolve (overnight).  
  - Similar overnight gaps appear after investigation in cases 102 and 105.

- **Supporting observations**  
  - Fast cases (101, 103) start investigation within minutes of assignment and finish everything the same morning.  
  - Activity durations themselves (investigate, resolve) are not dramatically longer; the bulk of the cycle-time inflation is idle time between activities.  
  - Overnight gaps suggest either limited working hours, Level-2 backlog, or hand-off friction rather than pure work content.

**3. How the factors inflate cycle time & recommendations**

- Escalation + hand-off latency turns a same-day ticket into a multi-day ticket. Level-2 agents appear to have high queue times (or non-overlapping shifts).  
- Even Level-1 tickets can accrue multi-hour waits before investigation begins, indicating capacity or prioritisation problems.  
- Overnight resolution is normal for non-24×7 support, but it multiplies the impact of every earlier delay.

**Actionable insights**
- Strengthen triage / first-line diagnosis so fewer tickets need Level-2 (or route complex tickets directly to Level-2).  
- Impose SLAs / alerts on “time from Assign/Escalate  start Investigate” (e.g., 30 min for Level-1, 2 h for Level-2).  
- Increase Level-2 capacity during peak intake windows or implement a shared “escalation queue” with visibility.  
- Review shift overlap or introduce limited after-hours coverage (or automated follow-ups) to shrink overnight idle time for tickets that are almost finished.  
- Track the two metrics “% escalated” and “waiting time after escalation” as primary process KPIs; they explain most of the observed variance.

These changes target the waiting periods that dominate the long cases and should bring 102/104/105 into the same-day range of 101/103.
