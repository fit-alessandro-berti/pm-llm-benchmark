## Anomaly assessment

| Relationship | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **R  P: 25 hours, SD 1 hour** | The coefficient of variation is only about 4%, making approval timing unusually rigid. This may indicate an SLA-driven batch job, timestamp rounding, backfilled events, or derived rather than actual approval timestamps. |
| **P  N: 7 days, SD 2 days** | The mean delay is operationally long, and the two-day standard deviation represents substantial absolute variation. At three standard deviations, the expected upper limit would be about 13 days. |
| **A  C: 2 hours, SD 1 hour** | Closure soon after assignment may represent withdrawn, duplicate, automatically rejected, or prematurely closed claims. It is especially suspicious because evaluation normally occurs about 24 hours after receipt. |
| **E  N: 5 minutes, SD 1 minute** | This is likely an automated notification, a non-final notification, or evidence that approval is being skipped or recorded out of order. |
| **E  C: 1 hour, SD 50 minutes** | The 83% coefficient of variation suggests a mixture of process variants rather than one stable process. |
| **Cross-pair inconsistency** | If the same claims and event occurrences underlie all pairs, `E  N = 5 minutes` is incompatible with an intended intervening approval followed by `P  N = 7 days`. Similarly, `A  C = 2 hours` conflicts with `R  E = 24 hours` when assignment occurs about one hour after receipt. |

The cross-pair inconsistencies are not conclusive if the averages use different claim populations or different occurrences of repeated activities. That distinction should be checked explicitly.

## Likely explanations

1. **Different process variants**
   - Withdrawn or duplicate claims may close directly after assignment.
   - Low-value claims may be evaluated and notified automatically.
   - Rejected claims may not require an approval event.
   - Full manual claims may follow the longer process.

2. **Notification backlog or batching**
   - Notifications may be sent by a daily or weekly batch.
   - Weekend approvals may wait until a business day.
   - External mail, SMS, or document-generation systems may be constrained.

3. **Artificial or SLA-driven approval timestamps**
   - Approval timestamps may be generated at a fixed deadline.
   - A scheduler may run at a fixed hour.
   - Approval events may be backfilled from another system.

4. **Ambiguous activity semantics**
   - `N` may include acknowledgements, requests for information, and final decisions.
   - `C` may include temporary or administrative closure.
   - Multiple `P` or `N` events may occur for the same claim.

5. **Event-log extraction problems**
   - The profile may pair the wrong occurrences of repeated events.
   - An all-pairs join could multiply event combinations.
   - Timestamps may represent ingestion time rather than execution time.
   - Regional timestamps stored without timezone information may be misaligned.

6. **Resource or workload effects**
   - Particular adjusters, regions, claim types, or claim-amount bands may have larger backlogs.
   - Specialized claims may wait for scarce evaluators or approvers.

## SQL verification

### 1. Construct deterministic eventual-following durations

The following view uses the first source activity for each claim and the first matching target activity after it. If repeated activities have different business semantics, the pairing rule should be changed to use, for example, the final approval or final notification.

```sql
CREATE OR REPLACE TEMP VIEW profile_pair_durations AS
WITH profile (
    from_activity,
    to_activity,
    model_avg_seconds,
    model_sd_seconds
) AS (
    VALUES
        ('R'::varchar, 'A'::varchar,   3600::numeric,    600::numeric),
        ('R'::varchar, 'E'::varchar,  86400::numeric,  28800::numeric),
        ('R'::varchar, 'P'::varchar,  90000::numeric,   3600::numeric),
        ('A'::varchar, 'C'::varchar,   7200::numeric,   3600::numeric),
        ('E'::varchar, 'N'::varchar,    300::numeric,     60::numeric),
        ('E'::varchar, 'C'::varchar,   3600::numeric,   3000::numeric),
        ('P'::varchar, 'N'::varchar, 604800::numeric, 172800::numeric),
        ('N'::varchar, 'C'::varchar,   1800::numeric,    300::numeric)
),
source_candidates AS (
    SELECT
        p.*,
        e.claim_id,
        e.event_id AS source_event_id,
        e."timestamp" AS source_ts,
        e.resource AS source_resource,
        e.additional_info AS source_info,
        ROW_NUMBER() OVER (
            PARTITION BY p.from_activity, p.to_activity, e.claim_id
            ORDER BY e."timestamp", e.event_id
        ) AS source_number
    FROM profile p
    JOIN claim_events e
      ON e.activity = p.from_activity
)
SELECT
    s.from_activity,
    s.to_activity,
    s.model_avg_seconds,
    s.model_sd_seconds,
    s.claim_id,
    s.source_event_id,
    s.source_ts,
    s.source_resource,
    s.source_info,
    target.event_id AS target_event_id,
    target.target_ts,
    target.target_resource,
    target.target_info,
    EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM target.target_ts - s.source_ts) AS duration_seconds,
    (
        EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM target.target_ts - s.source_ts)
        - s.model_avg_seconds
    ) / NULLIF(s.model_sd_seconds, 0) AS z_score
FROM source_candidates s
JOIN LATERAL (
    SELECT
        e2.event_id,
        e2."timestamp" AS target_ts,
        e2.resource AS target_resource,
        e2.additional_info AS target_info
    FROM claim_events e2
    WHERE e2.claim_id = s.claim_id
      AND e2.activity = s.to_activity
      AND (
          e2."timestamp" > s.source_ts
          OR (
              e2."timestamp" = s.source_ts
              AND e2.event_id > s.source_event_id
          )
      )
    ORDER BY e2."timestamp", e2.event_id
    LIMIT 1
) AS target ON TRUE
WHERE s.source_number = 1;
```

### 2. Recalculate the profile and count statistical deviations

```sql
SELECT
    from_activity,
    to_activity,
    COUNT(*) AS matched_claims,
    model_avg_seconds,
    model_sd_seconds,
    ROUND(AVG(duration_seconds), 1) AS empirical_avg_seconds,
    ROUND(STDDEV_SAMP(duration_seconds), 1) AS empirical_sd_seconds,
    ROUND(
        PERCENTILE_CONT(0.50)
            WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY duration_seconds)::numeric,
        1
    ) AS median_seconds,
    ROUND(
        PERCENTILE_CONT(0.95)
            WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY duration_seconds)::numeric,
        1
    ) AS p95_seconds,
    COUNT(*) FILTER (WHERE ABS(z_score) > 2) AS outside_2_sd,
    COUNT(*) FILTER (WHERE ABS(z_score) > 3) AS outside_3_sd
FROM profile_pair_durations
GROUP BY
    from_activity,
    to_activity,
    model_avg_seconds,
    model_sd_seconds
ORDER BY from_activity, to_activity;
```

Large differences between the stored and empirical statistics could indicate a different pairing rule, stale profile data, or an extraction defect.

### 3. List individual claims outside the expected range

Here `3` is the example ZETA threshold.

```sql
SELECT
    d.claim_id,
    c.customer_id,
    c.claim_type,
    c.claim_amount,
    d.from_activity,
    d.to_activity,
    d.source_ts,
    d.target_ts,
    ROUND(d.duration_seconds / 3600.0, 2) AS duration_hours,
    ROUND(d.z_score, 2) AS z_score,
    d.source_resource,
    d.target_resource,
    d.source_info,
    d.target_info
FROM profile_pair_durations d
JOIN claims c
  ON c.claim_id = d.claim_id
WHERE ABS(d.z_score) > 3
ORDER BY ABS(d.z_score) DESC;
```

Because the profile itself may be abnormal, these results should also be compared with business SLAs rather than relying only on model-derived Z-scores.

### 4. Check for source activities with no later target

This identifies missing activities and cases where the target may have occurred before the source.

```sql
WITH pairs(from_activity, to_activity) AS (
    VALUES
        ('R', 'A'), ('R', 'E'), ('R', 'P'), ('A', 'C'),
        ('E', 'N'), ('E', 'C'), ('P', 'N'), ('N', 'C')
),
sources AS (
    SELECT DISTINCT
        p.from_activity,
        p.to_activity,
        e.claim_id
    FROM pairs p
    JOIN claim_events e
      ON e.activity = p.from_activity
)
SELECT
    p.from_activity,
    p.to_activity,
    COUNT(s.claim_id) AS claims_with_source,
    COUNT(d.claim_id) AS claims_with_later_target,
    COUNT(s.claim_id) - COUNT(d.claim_id) AS missing_or_reversed_target
FROM pairs p
LEFT JOIN sources s
  ON s.from_activity = p.from_activity
 AND s.to_activity = p.to_activity
LEFT JOIN profile_pair_durations d
  ON d.from_activity = s.from_activity
 AND d.to_activity = s.to_activity
 AND d.claim_id = s.claim_id
GROUP BY p.from_activity, p.to_activity
ORDER BY p.from_activity, p.to_activity;
```

### 5. Find rapid closures after assignment with missing intermediate steps

The three-hour threshold is illustrative and should be replaced with the applicable business rule.

```sql
WITH parameters AS (
    SELECT INTERVAL '3 hours' AS maximum_assignment_to_close
),
first_assignment AS (
    SELECT DISTINCT ON (claim_id)
        claim_id,
        event_id AS assignment_event_id,
        "timestamp" AS assignment_ts,
        resource AS assignment_resource
    FROM claim_events
    WHERE activity = 'A'
    ORDER BY claim_id, "timestamp", event_id
),
assignment_to_close AS (
    SELECT
        a.*,
        close_event.event_id AS close_event_id,
        close_event.close_ts,
        close_event.close_resource
    FROM first_assignment a
    JOIN LATERAL (
        SELECT
            e.event_id,
            e."timestamp" AS close_ts,
            e.resource AS close_resource
        FROM claim_events e
        WHERE e.claim_id = a.claim_id
          AND e.activity = 'C'
          AND (
              e."timestamp" > a.assignment_ts
              OR (
                  e."timestamp" = a.assignment_ts
                  AND e.event_id > a.assignment_event_id
              )
          )
        ORDER BY e."timestamp", e.event_id
        LIMIT 1
    ) AS close_event ON TRUE
)
SELECT
    ac.claim_id,
    cl.claim_type,
    cl.claim_amount,
    ac.assignment_ts,
    ac.close_ts,
    ROUND(
        EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM ac.close_ts - ac.assignment_ts) / 60.0,
        1
    ) AS elapsed_minutes,
    ac.assignment_resource,
    ac.close_resource,
    middle.intervening_events,
    middle.evaluation_events,
    middle.approval_events
FROM assignment_to_close ac
JOIN claims cl
  ON cl.claim_id = ac.claim_id
CROSS JOIN parameters p
CROSS JOIN LATERAL (
    SELECT
        COUNT(*) AS intervening_events,
        COUNT(*) FILTER (WHERE e.activity = 'E') AS evaluation_events,
        COUNT(*) FILTER (WHERE e.activity = 'P') AS approval_events
    FROM claim_events e
    WHERE e.claim_id = ac.claim_id
      AND (e."timestamp", e.event_id)
            > (ac.assignment_ts, ac.assignment_event_id)
      AND (e."timestamp", e.event_id)
            < (ac.close_ts, ac.close_event_id)
) AS middle
WHERE ac.close_ts - ac.assignment_ts
          <= p.maximum_assignment_to_close
  AND (
      middle.evaluation_events = 0
      OR middle.approval_events = 0
  )
ORDER BY elapsed_minutes;
```

This separates genuinely compressed processing from closures that omit evaluation or approval.

### 6. Find missing or delayed notifications after approval

```sql
WITH parameters AS (
    SELECT INTERVAL '7 days' AS notification_sla
),
first_approval AS (
    SELECT DISTINCT ON (claim_id)
        claim_id,
        event_id AS approval_event_id,
        "timestamp" AS approval_ts,
        resource AS approval_resource
    FROM claim_events
    WHERE activity = 'P'
    ORDER BY claim_id, "timestamp", event_id
),
approval_notification AS (
    SELECT
        p.*,
        n.notification_event_id,
        n.notification_ts,
        n.notification_resource
    FROM first_approval p
    LEFT JOIN LATERAL (
        SELECT
            e.event_id AS notification_event_id,
            e."timestamp" AS notification_ts,
            e.resource AS notification_resource
        FROM claim_events e
        WHERE e.claim_id = p.claim_id
          AND e.activity = 'N'
          AND (
              e."timestamp" > p.approval_ts
              OR (
                  e."timestamp" = p.approval_ts
                  AND e.event_id > p.approval_event_id
              )
          )
        ORDER BY e."timestamp", e.event_id
        LIMIT 1
    ) n ON TRUE
)
SELECT
    pn.claim_id,
    c.claim_type,
    c.claim_amount,
    pn.approval_ts,
    pn.notification_ts,
    ROUND(
        EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM pn.notification_ts - pn.approval_ts)
            / 86400.0,
        2
    ) AS delay_days,
    pn.approval_resource,
    pn.notification_resource,
    CASE
        WHEN pn.notification_ts IS NULL THEN 'missing notification'
        ELSE 'late notification'
    END AS anomaly
FROM approval_notification pn
JOIN claims c
  ON c.claim_id = pn.claim_id
CROSS JOIN parameters p
WHERE pn.notification_ts IS NULL
   OR pn.notification_ts - pn.approval_ts > p.notification_sla
ORDER BY pn.notification_ts NULLS FIRST, delay_days DESC;
```

### 7. Correlate notification problems with claim type, amount, adjuster, and region

This query assumes that the `resource` on the assignment event contains `adjusters.name`.

```sql
WITH first_assignment AS (
    SELECT DISTINCT ON (claim_id)
        claim_id,
        resource AS assignment_resource
    FROM claim_events
    WHERE activity = 'A'
    ORDER BY claim_id, "timestamp", event_id
),
first_approval AS (
    SELECT DISTINCT ON (claim_id)
        claim_id,
        event_id AS approval_event_id,
        "timestamp" AS approval_ts
    FROM claim_events
    WHERE activity = 'P'
    ORDER BY claim_id, "timestamp", event_id
),
approval_notification AS (
    SELECT
        p.claim_id,
        p.approval_ts,
        n.notification_ts
    FROM first_approval p
    LEFT JOIN LATERAL (
        SELECT e."timestamp" AS notification_ts
        FROM claim_events e
        WHERE e.claim_id = p.claim_id
          AND e.activity = 'N'
          AND (
              e."timestamp" > p.approval_ts
              OR (
                  e."timestamp" = p.approval_ts
                  AND e.event_id > p.approval_event_id
              )
          )
        ORDER BY e."timestamp", e.event_id
        LIMIT 1
    ) n ON TRUE
),
base AS (
    SELECT
        pn.claim_id,
        pn.approval_ts,
        pn.notification_ts,
        c.claim_type,
        CASE
            WHEN c.claim_amount IS NULL THEN 'unknown'
            WHEN c.claim_amount < 1000 THEN '<1k'
            WHEN c.claim_amount < 10000 THEN '1k-10k'
            ELSE '10k+'
        END AS amount_band,
        COALESCE(ad.name, fa.assignment_resource, '<unmapped>')
            AS adjuster_key,
        COALESCE(ad.region, '<unmapped>') AS adjuster_region
    FROM approval_notification pn
    JOIN claims c
      ON c.claim_id = pn.claim_id
    LEFT JOIN first_assignment fa
      ON fa.claim_id = pn.claim_id
    LEFT JOIN adjusters ad
      ON ad.name = fa.assignment_resource
)
SELECT
    claim_type,
    amount_band,
    adjuster_region,
    adjuster_key,
    COUNT(*) AS approved_claims,
    COUNT(*) FILTER (
        WHERE notification_ts IS NULL
    ) AS missing_notifications,
    COUNT(*) FILTER (
        WHERE notification_ts IS NOT NULL
          AND notification_ts - approval_ts > INTERVAL '7 days'
    ) AS late_notifications,
    ROUND(
        100.0 * COUNT(*) FILTER (
            WHERE notification_ts IS NULL
               OR notification_ts - approval_ts > INTERVAL '7 days'
        ) / NULLIF(COUNT(*), 0),
        1
    ) AS late_or_missing_percent,
    ROUND(
        AVG(
            EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM notification_ts - approval_ts)
            / 86400.0
        )::numeric,
        2
    ) AS average_delay_days
FROM base
GROUP BY
    claim_type,
    amount_band,
    adjuster_region,
    adjuster_key
HAVING COUNT(*) >= 10
ORDER BY late_or_missing_percent DESC, approved_claims DESC;
```

### 8. Test for batch schedules

A narrow `R  P` histogram around exactly 1,500 minutes would support a scheduler or timestamp derivation hypothesis.

```sql
SELECT
    ROUND(duration_seconds / 60.0) AS elapsed_minutes,
    COUNT(*) AS claim_count
FROM profile_pair_durations
WHERE from_activity = 'R'
  AND to_activity = 'P'
GROUP BY 1
ORDER BY claim_count DESC
LIMIT 20;
```

Weekly or hourly notification concentrations can be checked with:

```sql
SELECT
    EXTRACT(ISODOW FROM source_ts)::integer AS approval_weekday,
    EXTRACT(ISODOW FROM target_ts)::integer AS notification_weekday,
    EXTRACT(HOUR FROM target_ts)::integer AS notification_hour,
    COUNT(*) AS claim_count,
    ROUND(AVG(duration_seconds) / 86400.0, 2) AS average_delay_days
FROM profile_pair_durations
WHERE from_activity = 'P'
  AND to_activity = 'N'
GROUP BY 1, 2, 3
ORDER BY 1, 2, 3;
```

### 9. Check duplicates, direct skips, and ordering defects

Repeated activities can cause materially different results depending on the pairing rule.

```sql
SELECT
    claim_id,
    activity,
    COUNT(*) AS occurrence_count,
    MIN("timestamp") AS first_occurrence,
    MAX("timestamp") AS last_occurrence
FROM claim_events
GROUP BY claim_id, activity
HAVING COUNT(*) > 1
ORDER BY occurrence_count DESC, claim_id, activity;
```

Direct `A  C` and `E  N` transitions can be measured independently of the eventual-following profile:

```sql
WITH ordered_events AS (
    SELECT
        claim_id,
        activity,
        "timestamp",
        LEAD(activity) OVER event_order AS next_activity,
        LEAD("timestamp") OVER event_order AS next_timestamp
    FROM claim_events
    WINDOW event_order AS (
        PARTITION BY claim_id
        ORDER BY "timestamp", event_id
    )
)
SELECT
    activity AS from_activity,
    next_activity AS to_activity,
    COUNT(*) AS transition_count,
    ROUND(
        AVG(EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM next_timestamp - "timestamp"))::numeric,
        1
    ) AS average_seconds
FROM ordered_events
WHERE (activity = 'A' AND next_activity = 'C')
   OR (activity = 'E' AND next_activity = 'N')
GROUP BY activity, next_activity;
```

Finally, missing and out-of-order mandatory steps can be identified from their first occurrences:

```sql
WITH first_steps AS (
    SELECT
        c.claim_id,
        MIN(e."timestamp") FILTER (WHERE e.activity = 'R') AS r_ts,
        MIN(e."timestamp") FILTER (WHERE e.activity = 'A') AS a_ts,
        MIN(e."timestamp") FILTER (WHERE e.activity = 'E') AS e_ts,
        MIN(e."timestamp") FILTER (WHERE e.activity = 'P') AS p_ts,
        MIN(e."timestamp") FILTER (WHERE e.activity = 'N') AS n_ts,
        MIN(e."timestamp") FILTER (WHERE e.activity = 'C') AS c_ts
    FROM claims c
    LEFT JOIN claim_events e
      ON e.claim_id = c.claim_id
    GROUP BY c.claim_id
)
SELECT
    f.*,
    c.claim_type,
    c.claim_amount
FROM first_steps f
JOIN claims c
  ON c.claim_id = f.claim_id
WHERE f.r_ts IS NULL
   OR f.a_ts IS NULL
   OR f.e_ts IS NULL
   OR f.p_ts IS NULL
   OR f.n_ts IS NULL
   OR f.c_ts IS NULL
   OR f.r_ts > f.a_ts
   OR f.a_ts > f.e_ts
   OR f.e_ts > f.p_ts
   OR f.p_ts > f.n_ts
   OR f.n_ts > f.c_ts
ORDER BY f.claim_id;
```

Missing steps should only be classified as errors after accounting for legitimate rejection, withdrawal, and duplicate-claim branches.

A final schema limitation is that there is no explicit `adjuster_id` on either `claims` or `claim_events`. If `resource` is not exactly the adjuster name or identifier, adjuster and region attribution cannot be verified reliably. A structured claim-to-adjuster relationship would be needed. Likewise, customer geographic segments are not present, and the timezone interpretation of the `TIMESTAMP` values is unspecified.
