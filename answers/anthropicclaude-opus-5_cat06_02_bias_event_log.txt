## 1. Where the Bias Enters the Process

The log contains five cases and only two decision points that can differentiate them: **PreliminaryScoring** (where `ScoreAdjustment` is applied) and **FinalDecision** (where the threshold is applied). Bias is visible at both, and the two mechanisms compound.

## 2. The Decisive Comparison: C003 vs. C004

This pair is the clearest evidence of inequitable treatment:

- **C004**: base score **690** — the *lowest* creditworthiness in the entire log — receives a +10 club bonus, lands at 700, and is **approved**.
- **C003**: base score **715**, a genuinely stronger applicant, receives no adjustment and is **rejected**.

An applicant who is 25 points *less* creditworthy on the underlying measure is approved, while a stronger applicant is denied. The only attributes distinguishing them are `LocalResident` and `CommunityGroup` — neither of which is a measure of ability or willingness to repay.

## 3. Mechanism 1 — The "+10 (Community)" Bonus Is a Proxy Adjustment

Several features make this adjustment problematic beyond its size:

- **It is one-sided.** Non-members receive "0," which is presented as neutral but is functionally a **relative 10-point penalty**. Framing a penalty as the absence of a reward hides it from disparate-treatment review.
- **It is a single-entity whitelist.** Only one organization appears — "Highland Civic Darts Club." There is no evidence of a published, criteria-based list of qualifying civic organizations. A bonus keyed to one named local club is not a general policy; it is membership-based preferential pricing.
- **It has no visible predictive justification.** Nothing in the log establishes that darts club membership correlates with repayment behavior. Absent a documented, statistically validated business necessity, the adjustment is arbitrary.
- **It is perfectly collinear with residency.** Every club member in the log is also `LocalResident = TRUE`. Membership therefore operates as a **geographic and social proxy** — capturing who lives locally, who has time and social access to join local associations, and by extension who is longer-tenured, more socially networked, and plausibly correlated with age, national origin, and neighborhood composition.
- **It is outcome-determinative at the margin.** In C004 the bonus is precisely what carries the file from 690 to 700. This is not a cosmetic tiebreaker.

## 4. Mechanism 2 — Geography Appears to Be Used *Again* at FinalDecision

The most serious structural finding: **the decision is not a monotonic function of the adjusted score.**

- 700 (C004)  Approved
- 715 (C003)  **Rejected**
- 720, 720, 740  Approved

No single threshold explains this. If the cutoff were 700, C003 would pass. If it were 720, C004 would fail. The only consistent reading is a **dual standard**:

- Local residents clear at roughly ** 700**
- Non-residents appear to need something above **715** (C005 clears at 740)

This means `LocalResident` is being consumed *twice*: once indirectly via the community bonus at scoring, and again directly (or via some attribute not written to the log) at the Rules Engine. Non-locals are penalized in the score they carry and then judged against a higher bar with that lower score — a classic compounding effect.

This is the structural signature of **redlining**: geography, not the applicant, determines the standard.

- Five different reviewers (#2, #3, #4, #5, #7) produced perfectly uniform deference — suggesting the reviewers have no authority or no mandate to interrogate the engine's adjustment, rather than that five independent judgments coincided.

The practical effect is **accountability laundering**: the process can claim a "human in the loop," but the human adds no corrective capacity. Worse, because bias originates in a deterministic Scoring Engine rule rather than in individual discretion, it is applied with perfect consistency to every affected applicant — it *scales*, in a way individual reviewer prejudice would not.

- **No threshold is recorded** at FinalDecision, and **no reason code** is attached to C003's rejection. There is no way to tell from the log why 715 failed while 700 passed.
- Consequently the log **cannot explain its own outputs** — which is itself a compliance defect. Any adverse action notice sent to C003 will necessarily state a reason other than the real driver, because the real driver was never logged.

`LocalResident` and `CommunityGroup` are perfectly correlated in this sample. **No case exists** of (a) a non-resident club member or (b) a resident non-member who was rejected. So this log alone cannot separate the residency effect from the membership effect — it only proves that *at least one* of them is driving outcomes independently of score. Pulling a larger sample and deliberately examining those two empty cells should be the first analytical step.

## 10. Remediation

**Immediate**
1. **Suspend the +10 community adjustment** pending validation. Require documented statistical evidence that club membership predicts repayment, plus evidence that no less-discriminatory alternative achieves the same predictive lift.
2. **Enforce a single, published threshold** applied identically to all applicants; instrument the Rules Engine to log the threshold, the exact input score, and a reason code on every decision.
3. **Re-adjudicate C003** and audit the full population for other cases where a below-cutoff local was approved or an above-cutoff non-local was rejected.

**Structural**
4. **Remove residency and affiliation from the decision path**, and test remaining features for proxy leakage (do they still reconstruct postcode or club membership?).
5. **Preserve base score, adjustment, and final score as three separate immutable fields.** Never overwrite the raw score.
6. **Blind reviewers to adjustments** — present the base score and let any override be an affirmative, justified, logged act. A control that never fires is not a control.
7. **Counterfactual testing:** re-run every decision with `CommunityGroup` set to None and `LocalResident` flipped. Any outcome that changes is a flagged case.
8. **Ongoing monitoring** of approval rates and average approved-score by residency and affiliation, with alerting when the favored group's approved scores fall below the disfavored group's rejected scores — the exact inversion visible here.

**Bottom line:** the process encodes a preference for local residents and members of one named club, applies it twice (once as a score bonus, once as a relaxed threshold), routes it through a manual review that never intervenes, and records it in a way that makes the preference invisible to downstream audit. The result is that the least creditworthy applicant in the log was approved and a stronger one was denied.
