### Executive Summary

By analyzing the event sequence, timestamps, resource usage, and process context across cases `A1` and `B2`, the 12 granular low-level events are consolidated into **4 primary high-level manufacturing stages**:

1. **Material Preparation & Conditioning**
2. **Structural Assembly & Weld Inspection**
3. **Surface Treatment & Curing**
4. **Final Quality Assurance & Clearance**

---

### High-Level Process Stage Breakdown & Justification

#### Stage 1: Material Preparation & Conditioning
* **Aggregated Low-Level Events:**
  1. Retrieve raw metal sheet
  2. Scan barcode
  3. Place metal sheet on table
  4. Align metal sheet
  5. Preheat metal sheet
* **Duration Range (Case A1):** 08:00:05 – 08:00:20 (15 seconds)
* **Resources Involved:** Operator A, Robot Arm #2, Heating Unit #1
* **Rationale:**  
  These events represent the physical handling, inventory logging, precise positioning, and thermal preparation required before any permanent modification or joining takes place. They form a coherent pre-processing stage focused on getting the raw material into a state where it is ready to be welded.

---

#### Stage 2: Structural Assembly & Weld Inspection
* **Aggregated Low-Level Events:**
  6. Pick up welding tool
  7. Weld corner A
  8. Weld corner B
  9. Measure weld integrity
* **Duration Range (Case A1):** 08:01:00 – 08:01:20 (20 seconds)
* **Resources Involved:** Operator B, Quality Sensor #1
* **Rationale:**  
  This stage constitutes the core joining/fabrication process. Operator B performs the sequence of picking up the tool and executing the spot welds. Immediate quality validation (measuring weld integrity via Quality Sensor #1) is grouped here because it serves as an **in-line feedback loop** specifically verifying the output of the welding activity before the part advances to surface treatments.

---

#### Stage 3: Surface Treatment & Curing
* **Aggregated Low-Level Events:**
  10. Apply protective coating
  11. Dry coating
* **Duration Range (Case A1):** 08:01:30 – 08:01:45 (15 seconds)
* **Resources Involved:** Coating Unit #3, Heating Unit #2
* **Rationale:**  
  These activities form a distinct chemical/thermal finishing phase. The application of polyurethane protective coating and its subsequent thermal drying cycle are interdependent automated operations aimed at protecting the finished weld and metal surface.

---

#### Stage 4: Final Quality Assurance & Clearance
* **Aggregated Low-Level Events:**
  12. Visual check
* **Duration Range (Case A1):** 08:02:00
* **Resources Involved:** Operator C
* **Rationale:**  
  This acts as the final gatekeeping step. Performed by a dedicated human inspector (Operator C), it validates that all prior operations (assembly, integrity, coating) meet aesthetic and functional criteria before the part exits the process line.

### Rules & Heuristics Used for Aggregation

When applying this transformation to the larger dataset, the following rules can be parameterized and automated:

1. **Resource Continuity:** Consecutive actions performed by the same resource or tightly coupled resources (e.g., Operator B picking tool and welding) indicate a single continuous macro-action.
2. **Temporal Proximity:** Sub-second to few-second intervals between actions indicate sub-steps of a single high-level stage (e.g., retrieving sheet $\rightarrow$ scanning barcode $\rightarrow$ placing on table).
3. **Domain Objectives:**
   * **Inbound & Alignment:** Material Prep
   * **Joining & In-line Sensor Checks:** Assembly
   * **Coating & Heating/Drying Cycles:** Surface Treatment
   * **Human Operator Audits:** Final Inspection/Gatekeeping
