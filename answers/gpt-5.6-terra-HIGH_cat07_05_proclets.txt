The visualization combines three related views of a laboratory-testing workflow.

### (a) Two process/proclet classes connected through channels
On the left is a **Lab visit** process:

1. A doctor initiates a lab visit.
2. The visit performs **Order entry**.
3. A lab assistant **takes a blood sample**.
4. A message is sent through an **Order system** channel to the laboratory-testing process.
5. The lab visit later **receives a result** through the **HIS** (hospital information system).
6. A lab assistant **judges results**:
   - If everything is fine, the assistant **sends the report** and the visit ends.
   - If not, the process goes to **Do tests again**, which sends a request back to the lab-test process and subsequently receives an **updated result**.

On the right is a **Lab test** process:

1. It begins when the lab receives an order.
2. A chemical analyst **performs the test**.
3. The analyst **makes a report**.
4. The report/result is sent back through the HIS channel.
5. The lab test can either **finish**, or receive a retest request and **perform the test again**.
6. After retesting, it **provides an updated report**, then finishes.

The dotted boxes labeled **Order system** and **HIS** represent communication channels. Arrows show the messages exchanged between the two process classes, with multiplicities such as `1..1` and `1..*` indicating how many related messages or instances may be involved.

### (b) Class diagram
The upper-right section shows a simple relationship between the two proclet classes:

- One **Lab visit** requires **one or more Lab tests** (`1..*`).
- Each **Lab test** belongs to exactly one **Lab visit** (`1..1`).

### (c) Example message/performative
The table at the bottom gives an example of a communication event:

- **Time:** 11:00  
- **Channel:** Order system  
- **Sender:** Lab visit – John  
- **Receiver:** Lab test – HGB John  
- **Action:** Create  
- **Content:** “Can you perform a HGB test for John?”  
- **Scope:** Private  
- **Direction:** OUT  

Overall, the diagram illustrates how a patient’s lab visit and the corresponding laboratory tests operate as separate but coordinated processes, communicating through order and hospital-information-system channels.
