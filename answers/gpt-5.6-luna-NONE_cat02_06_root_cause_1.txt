## 2. Potential root causes

### Case 102: escalation and delayed investigation

Important delays include:

- **Assignment to escalation:** 09:00 to 11:30 — 2 h 30 m
- **Escalation to investigation:** 11:30 to 14:00 — 2 h 30 m
- **Investigation to resolution:** Mar 1 14:00 to Mar 2 09:00 — 19 h

The escalation appears to introduce a handoff and queue delay. The long period between investigation and resolution may indicate unresolved technical complexity, waiting for information, or work continuing into the next business day.

### Case 104: delayed investigation and overnight resolution

The main delays are:

- **Assignment to investigation:** 09:30 to 13:00 — 3 h 30 m
- **Investigation to resolution:** Mar 1 13:00 to Mar 2 08:00 — 19 h

There is no escalation, so the delay is more likely related to agent workload, queueing, issue complexity, or the effect of non-working hours. Investigation begins relatively late despite assignment occurring at 09:30.

### Case 105: repeated escalation and long handoff delays

This is the largest outlier:

- **Assignment to investigation:** 09:00 to 09:10 — minimal delay
- **Initial investigation to escalation:** 09:10 to 10:00 — 50 m
- **Escalation to renewed investigation:** Mar 1 10:00 to Mar 2 14:00 — **28 h**
- **Investigation to resolution:** Mar 2 14:00 to Mar 3 09:00 — 19 h

The escalation causes a very large waiting period before Level-2 investigation begins. The case then remains unresolved overnight again. This suggests either a Level-2 queue, insufficient escalation staffing, incomplete transfer of information, or a complex issue requiring multiple teams.

## 3. Main performance patterns

1. **Escalated tickets take much longer.**  
   Cases 102 and 105 both include escalation and have substantially longer cycle times than Cases 101 and 103. Case 105 demonstrates the strongest effect.

2. **Investigation often starts late.**  
   Cases 102 and 104 have several hours between assignment and investigation. This indicates possible agent capacity constraints or a queue after assignment.

3. **Long post-investigation delays dominate total time.**  
   Cases 102, 104, and 105 each have approximately 19 hours between investigation and resolution. This may reflect overnight handoffs, waiting for approvals or customer information, or work being deferred to the next business day.

4. **Cross-day processing is a major contributor.**  
   All severely delayed cases remain open overnight. Business-hour coverage, shift changes, or lack of follow-up outside normal hours may be inflating elapsed resolution time.

5. **Escalation handoffs may be inefficient.**  
   The 28-hour delay in Case 105 between escalation and renewed investigation is especially concerning and suggests a bottleneck in the Level-2 queue.

## Recommendations

- Track and enforce **service-level targets** for assignment, escalation acceptance, investigation start, and resolution.
- Measure **queue time separately from processing time** to distinguish workload issues from technical complexity.
- Improve escalation handoffs with complete diagnostic information, clear ownership, and automatic routing.
- Add Level-2 staffing or an escalation on-call process for urgent or high-priority tickets.
- Introduce alerts when a ticket remains unworked for a defined period after assignment or escalation.
- Review tickets waiting overnight and determine whether they require after-hours coverage or better next-shift prioritization.
- Analyze whether escalation criteria are appropriate and whether Level-1 agents can resolve some cases using better documentation or knowledge-base guidance.
- Monitor the repeated **investigation-to-resolution delay** as a separate bottleneck, since it is present even without escalation, as in Case 104.

Overall, the strongest likely causes are **escalation handoff delays, queueing before investigation, limited coverage across overnight periods, and long waits between investigation and resolution**.
