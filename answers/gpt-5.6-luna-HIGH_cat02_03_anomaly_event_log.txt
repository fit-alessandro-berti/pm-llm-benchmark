### Expected P2P sequence

A typical controlled P2P flow is:

**Create requisition  Approve requisition  Issue PO  Receive goods/services  Receive invoice  Perform three-way match  Release payment  Close case**

The invoice and goods receipt may occur in either order in some organizations, but payment should normally occur only after the required approvals and matching controls have been completed.

## Case analysis

| Case | Anomalous sequence | Why it is problematic |
|---|---|---|
| **1001** | No obvious anomaly | This follows the expected sequence: the requisition is approved before the PO is issued, goods are received, the invoice arrives, the invoice is matched, payment is released, and the case is closed. |
| **1002** | PO issued before requisition approval | The PO is issued at 08:40 on February 1, while approval occurs only on February 3. This means a purchasing commitment was made before the request was formally authorized. It can bypass budgetary approval and procurement controls. |
| **1002** | Payment released before approval, goods receipt, and invoice matching | Payment occurs on February 3 at 07:15, before the requisition is approved, before goods are received, and before the invoice is matched. This is a major control failure because the organization pays without confirming that the purchase was authorized, that the goods were received, or that the invoice agrees with the PO and receipt. |
| **1002** | Invoice received before goods receipt | An invoice can legitimately arrive before goods, so this is not necessarily anomalous by itself. However, payment should normally be held until receipt and matching are complete. In this case, the later match appears to be a post-payment control rather than a preventive control. |
| **1003** | Payment released before invoice receipt | Payment is released on February 8, but the vendor invoice is not received until February 9. This suggests payment was made without an invoice to validate the amount, vendor, tax, or payment terms. |
| **1003** | Payment released before goods receipt and three-way matching | Goods are not received until February 10 and the invoice is not matched until February 11—both after payment. This defeats the purpose of three-way matching and creates risks of overpayment, payment for undelivered goods, duplicate invoices, or fraud. |
| **1004** | Goods received before PO issuance | Goods are received at 09:00 on February 6, but the PO is not issued until 09:15. Normally, the supplier should receive an authorized PO before delivery, and the goods receipt should be recorded against that PO. This may indicate an unauthorized or retrospective purchase, an emergency procurement, or an attempt to regularize a transaction after delivery. |
| **1004** | — | After the PO is issued, the invoice is received, matched, and paid. That portion of the process is broadly consistent with standard P2P controls. |

## Main risks indicated by the log

The anomalous cases suggest several control weaknesses:

- **Unauthorized commitments:** issuing POs before requisition approval.
- **Payments without sufficient evidence:** paying before invoice receipt, goods receipt, or matching.
- **Ineffective three-way matching:** matching after payment rather than before it.
- **Risk of paying for undelivered or incorrect goods:** especially in Case 1003.
- **Retrospective procurement documentation:** receiving goods before a PO exists, as in Case 1004.
- **Fraud and audit risks:** weak approval and payment controls make duplicate, inflated, or fictitious transactions harder to prevent.

Some businesses permit exceptions such as advance payments, invoice-before-receipt processing, or emergency purchases. However, those exceptions should be explicitly authorized and documented. Based solely on the event log, **1001 appears normal, 1002 and 1003 contain significant payment and approval anomalies, and 1004 contains a procurement-ordering anomaly.**
