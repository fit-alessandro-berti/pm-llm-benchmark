Based on the event log, bias appears to be **structured into the scoring and decision logic**, not random or accidental. Two attributes drive the most visible disparities:

- **`CommunityGroup`** – specifically membership in the **Highland Civic Darts Club** – produces an explicit **+10 score adjustment**.
- **`LocalResident`** – being marked `TRUE` appears to lower the effective approval threshold relative to non-residents.

## 1. The Community Group Bonus Is Explicit Favoritism

In `PreliminaryScoring`, applicants with a CommunityGroup receive a **+10 (Community)** adjustment:

| Case | Preliminary Score | CommunityGroup | Score Adjustment | Adjusted Score |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C001 | 710 | Highland Civic Darts Club | +10 | 720 |
| C002 | 720 | None | 0 | 720 |
| C003 | 715 | None | 0 | 715 |
| C004 | 690 | Highland Civic Darts Club | +10 | 700 |
| C005 | 740 | None | 0 | 740 |

So C004’s underlying score of **690** is raised to **700** simply because of club membership. Meanwhile, C003 has a **higher preliminary score of 715** but receives no bonus and is ultimately **rejected**.

> **Bias:** The system awards points for community affiliation, even though community affiliation is not the same as creditworthiness. Applicants who are not members of that specific group are disadvantaged despite having similar or even better preliminary scores.

## 2. Local Residency Appears to Influence the Final Decision

Even after adjusting for community group, the final decisions reveal another pattern:

- All **local residents** in the log are approved:
  - C001: 720 approved
  - C002: 720 approved
  - C004: 700 approved
- Non-residents are split:
  - C003: 715 rejected
  - C005: 740 approved

This suggests that non-residents may need a **higher score** to be approved than local residents. For example, C004 is approved with an adjusted score of **700**, while C003 is rejected with a score of **715**. The most obvious difference is that C004 is a local resident and a club member, while C003 is neither.

> **Bias:** The final decision appears to impose a stricter threshold on non-local applicants. Geographic characteristics — not just financial risk — become a deciding factor.

## 3. The Combined Effect: Affiliation and Geography Can Override Creditworthiness

The clearest unfair comparison is between **C003** and **C004**:

| Case | Preliminary Score | LocalResident | CommunityGroup | Adjusted Score | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C003 | 715 | FALSE | None | 715 | Rejected |
| C004 | 690 | TRUE | Highland Civic Darts Club | 700 | Approved |

C003 has a **higher preliminary score** than C004, but C003 is rejected while C004 is approved. The difference is not creditworthiness; it is **local residency** and **club membership**.

So the system:

- Rewards people who are already integrated into a specific local community.
- Penalizes people who are not local or not part of that community.
- Does not correct for this in manual review — the manual reviews simply carry the already-biased adjusted scores forward.

## 4. How This Affects Fairness and Equity

### Disparate Treatment

Applicants with identical or similar preliminary scores receive different treatment because of group membership and geography. That is a clear fairness problem if those attributes are not legitimate predictors of credit risk.

### Disparate Impact

Community affiliation and local residency are not evenly distributed across populations. People who are new to an area, from different regions, or outside existing social networks may systematically lose out. These attributes can also be proxies for:

- Socioeconomic background
- Race or ethnicity
- Immigrant status
- Housing stability
- Access to social capital

Even if no human reviewer is intentionally biased, the automated rules encode a structural preference for certain groups.

### Similar Creditworthiness, Different Outcomes

If `PreliminaryScore` is meant to approximate creditworthiness, then adding points for community membership or applying a lower threshold for locals means the final decision is **not based on creditworthiness alone**. A riskier applicant from the “right” community can be approved, while a safer applicant outside that community can be rejected.

## 5. Where Bias Enters the Process

1. **ApplicationReceived / DataValidation**  
   The system records and forwards `LocalResident` and `CommunityGroup` as decision-relevant attributes. That makes them available to inject bias downstream.

2. **PreliminaryScoring**  
   The `+10 (Community)` adjustment directly changes scores based on group membership. This is the most obvious place where bias is introduced.

3. **FinalDecision**  
   The decision rule appears to treat local residents more favorably, either through a hidden threshold or through unshown logic. This is less transparent than the score adjustment.

4. **ManualReview**  
   The “ManualReview” step does not appear to catch or correct these biases. It records the already-adjusted score and passes it to the final decision, effectively ratifying the biased outcome.

---

## Conclusion

The bias in this process is not hidden in subjective human judgment. It is built into the data and scoring logic:

- **CommunityGroup membership adds points.**
- **LocalResident status appears to lower the effective approval threshold.**
- Together, these factors allow an applicant with a lower preliminary score to be approved over an applicant with a higher score who lacks those group affiliations.

For fairness and equity, the process should be redesigned so that decisions are based on legitimate, validated risk factors, not on whether someone belongs to a particular club or lives in a particular area — unless there is strong, transparent evidence that those factors are directly relevant to the decision. As presented, the system disadvantages people who lack local ties or community affiliations, even when their underlying creditworthiness is similar.
