This pseudo-BPMN describes a customer order handling process that branches based on the nature of the request, performs validations and checks, determines feasibility for custom work, and includes an approval loop with rework. Below is a detailed breakdown of the business logic, the purpose of each element, and how the paths interact.

---

### 1. Overall Process Purpose
The process manages incoming customer requests, distinguishes between standard (catalog) and custom (bespoke) orders, performs necessary validations and feasibility checks, obtains managerial approval when required, generates a final invoice, and sends a confirmation. It also handles rejection of infeasible custom requests and includes a rework loop when an approval is denied.

---

### 2. StepbyStep Walkthrough

#### **Start Event  Task A: “Receive Customer Request”**
- **Purpose:** The process is triggered when a customer submits a request (e.g., via a web form, email, or sales representative). Task A captures the initial data and logs the request into the system.

#### **Gateway (XOR): “Check Request Type”**
- **Meaning:** An exclusive gateway that evaluates the request against predefined criteria (e.g., product type, configuration, or customer specification). It splits the flow into two mutually exclusive paths:
  - **Standard** – the request matches an existing product/service with fixed parameters.
  - **Custom** – the request requires bespoke design, engineering, or special terms.

---

### 3. Standard Path

#### **Task B1: “Perform Standard Validation”**
- **Purpose:** Verifies that the request is complete and conforms to standard business rules (e.g., correct product code, valid quantity, pricing consistency). This is a quick sanity check before deeper processing.

#### **Gateway (AND): “Run Parallel Checks”**
- **Meaning:** An ANDsplit gateway that launches two independent tasks simultaneously. Both must finish before the process can continue.

#### **Task C1: “Credit Check” & Task C2: “Inventory Check”**
- **Purpose:**  
  - *Credit Check* assesses the customer’s creditworthiness or outstanding balance to mitigate financial risk.  
  - *Inventory Check* confirms that the requested items are in stock or can be procured within the required timeframe.  
- These checks run in parallel to save time; neither depends on the other.

#### **All Parallel Checks Completed (Join)**
- **Meaning:** An ANDjoin gateway that synchronises the two parallel branches. The process waits until both C1 and C2 have finished (and any required results are available) before proceeding.

#### **Task D: “Calculate Delivery Date”**
- **Purpose:** Using the validated standard data and the results of the credit/inventory checks, the system (or a planner) computes a reliable delivery date. This date may depend on stock availability, shipping methods, and the customer’s credit status.

---

### 4. Custom Path

#### **Task B2: “Perform Custom Feasibility Analysis”**
- **Purpose:** A detailed technical and commercial assessment of the custom request. Engineers or product specialists evaluate whether the requested customisation is technically possible, what it would cost, and whether it aligns with company capabilities.

#### **Gateway (XOR): “Is Customization Feasible?”**
- **Meaning:** A decision point based on the feasibility analysis. Two outcomes:
  - **Yes** – the customisation can be offered; the process continues to prepare a quotation.
  - **No** – the request cannot be fulfilled; the process moves to rejection.

#### **Task E1: “Prepare Custom Quotation”** (if feasible)
- **Purpose:** A detailed proposal is created, including pricing, technical specifications, lead times, and any special terms. This quotation is later used for approval and, if accepted, for invoicing.

#### **Task E2: “Send Rejection Notice”  End Event** (if not feasible)
- **Purpose:** The customer is informed that their custom request cannot be met, with an explanation. The process terminates for this request.

---

### 5. Convergence and Approval Handling

After the standard path completes **Task D** and the custom (feasible) path completes **Task E1**, both paths merge into a common sequence. The pseudoBPMN indicates this with the label *“After Standard or Custom Path Tasks Completed”*.

#### **Gateway (XOR): “Is Approval Needed?”**
- **Meaning:** A business rule determines whether the order requires managerial approval. Typical triggers include:
  - Order value exceeds a threshold.
  - Special pricing or discounts are applied.
  - High credit risk or unusual payment terms.
  - Custom orders that need signoff on feasibility/cost.
- **If No approval needed:** The flow proceeds directly to **Task G**.
- **If Yes:** The process moves to **Task F**.

#### **Task F: “Obtain Manager Approval”**
- **Purpose:** The request (with all supporting data – validation results, delivery date, or custom quotation) is routed to a manager for review and decision.

#### **Gateway (XOR): “Is Approval Granted?”**
- **Meaning:** The manager’s decision splits the flow:
  - **Yes:** The order is approved; proceed to **Task G**.
  - **No:** The approval is denied; the process enters a rework loop.

#### **Task H: “Re-evaluate Conditions”** (if approval denied)
- **Purpose:** The denial triggers a reexamination of the order conditions. This could involve renegotiating price, adjusting delivery terms, modifying the custom design, or reassessing credit limits. The task is a manual or semiautomated step where the sales team or planner updates the request.

#### **Loop Back**
- **Meaning:** After reevaluation, the process loops back to an earlier task, depending on the original path:
  - **Standard path:** loops back to **Task D** (“Calculate Delivery Date”). The updated conditions (e.g., new payment terms, changed quantity) may affect the delivery date, so it is recalculated.
  - **Custom path:** loops back to **Task E1** (“Prepare Custom Quotation”). The custom quotation is revised based on the reevaluated conditions.
- After the loop, the flow again reaches the *“Is Approval Needed?”* gateway. The cycle repeats until approval is granted (or, in a real process, a timeout or escalation might occur, though not shown here).

---

### 6. Finalisation

#### **Task G: “Generate Final Invoice”**
- **Purpose:** Once all checks are passed and any required approval is obtained, a final invoice is produced. For standard orders, it is based on the validated data and delivery date; for custom orders, it reflects the approved quotation.

#### **Task I: “Send Confirmation to Customer”**
- **Purpose:** The customer receives an official confirmation (email, document, or system notification) containing the invoice, delivery date (for standard), or the agreed custom terms. This closes the loop with the customer.

#### **End Event**
- The process instance is completed.

---

### 7. Summary of Gateway Types and Their Roles

| Gateway | Type | Role |
|---------|------|------|
| Check Request Type | XOR (exclusive) | Splits flow into Standard or Custom based on request attributes. |
| Run Parallel Checks | AND (parallel) | Forks into two simultaneous tasks (Credit Check, Inventory Check). |
| All Parallel Checks Completed | AND (join) | Synchronises the parallel branches; waits for both to finish. |
| Is Customization Feasible? | XOR | Decides whether a custom request can be fulfilled or must be rejected. |
| Is Approval Needed? | XOR | Determines if managerial approval is required before invoicing. |
| Is Approval Granted? | XOR | Routes to final invoice or to rework based on the manager’s decision. |

---

### 8. How Tasks Differ: Standard vs. Custom

- **Standard requests** undergo a structured validation (B1), followed by mandatory parallel credit and inventory checks (C1, C2), and then a delivery date calculation (D). The focus is on confirming that the standard product can be delivered reliably and within financial risk limits.
- **Custom requests** skip the parallel checks entirely. Instead, they require a feasibility study (B2). If feasible, a custom quotation (E1) is prepared, which encapsulates all commercial and technical terms. The process assumes that the feasibility analysis already addresses technical and resource availability, so separate inventory/credit checks are not performed at this stage (they might be embedded in the feasibility analysis or handled later during approval).

---

### 9. Conditions That Trigger Approvals and Rework

- **Approval is triggered** when business rules flag the order as needing oversight. Common conditions (not explicitly listed in the diagram but typical) include:
  - Order total exceeds a predefined limit.
  - A custom quotation involves nonstandard pricing or high engineering effort.
  - The credit check reveals a borderline or highrisk customer.
  - The delivery date is unusually short or requires special logistics.
- **Rework (Task H) is triggered** only when an approval is denied. The process then loops back to recalculate the delivery date (standard) or revise the custom quotation, allowing the team to adjust the offer and resubmit for approval. This creates an iterative cycle that continues until the manager grants approval, ensuring that all orders meet company policies before an invoice is issued.

---

This process design ensures that standard orders are processed efficiently with automated parallel checks, while custom orders receive a thorough feasibility assessment. The approval gate with a rework loop provides managerial control over exceptional cases, balancing automation with human judgment.
