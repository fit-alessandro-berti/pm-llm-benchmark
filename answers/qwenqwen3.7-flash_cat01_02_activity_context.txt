### Analysis and Activity Renaming Strategy

The original event log contains several generic activity names (`Initiate Request`, `Verify Documents`, `Call Customer`) that mask significant variations in process behavior, intent, and outcome. By analyzing the **Additional Context** and the **position of the activity** within the case flow, we can disambiguate these activities.

**Key Observations:**
1.  **`Initiate Request`**: While the name is the same, the context reveals two distinct entry points: online submissions (C001, C002) versus walk-ins (C003). Renaming these highlights channel-specific workflows.
2.  **`Verify Documents`**: This activity recurs with different semantic meanings based on iteration.
    *   *First occurrence*: Always represents the **initial check**, which often triggers exceptions (missing docs, discrepancies, or customer absence).
    *   *Second occurrence*: Represents a **re-verification** triggered by a prior remediation step (e.g., after document upload or account adjustment).
3.  **`Call Customer`**: This activity has four distinct purposes across the log:
    *   Requesting missing information (C001).
    *   Clarifying a discrepancy (C002, 1st call).
    *   Confirming a resolution (C002, 2nd call).
    *   Recalling a customer who stepped out (C003).
    *   Generic naming obscures the root cause analysis; specific names reveal the nature of the intervention.
4.  **`Adjust Account` & `Approve Request`**: These appear multiple times but maintain consistent meanings (internal correction and final sign-off, respectively). They do not require disambiguation.
