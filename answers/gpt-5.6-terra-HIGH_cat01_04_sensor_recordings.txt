## Rationale by activity

### 1. Idle / Machine Ready — Events 1–3 and 13–15
These events form the baseline operating state:

- Temperature remains near room temperature.
- Pressure is low and stable.
- Vibration and material flow are zero.
- The tool is at its home position.
- Energy consumption is minimal.

The repeated pattern at the end of the log indicates that the machine has completed a cycle and reset for another production run.

### 2. Cutting Metal / Material Machining — Events 4–6
This phase has the clearest signs of active machining:

- Temperature increases quickly from about 20°C to 58°C.
- Vibration rises from 0 to 38 Hz, consistent with tool contact or rotation.
- Material flow is high and steady at 5 units/s.
- Tool position advances from 5 mm to 10 mm.
- Energy use increases from baseline to 2–3 kWh.

Together, these features indicate that a tool is actively processing material, plausibly cutting, drilling, or milling.

### 3. Assembling Parts / Positioning and Clamping — Events 7–8
After the machining phase, the machine enters a more stable, moderate-load condition:

- Temperature falls to 40°C.
- Vibration drops substantially to 10 Hz.
- Material flow decreases from 5 to 2 units/s.
- Pressure increases to 2 bar.
- The tool reaches 15 mm and remains fixed there.

This combination is consistent with placing, aligning, pressing, or clamping components before a joining operation.

### 4. Welding / Heat Joining — Event 9
Event 9 is a short but highly distinctive peak:

- Temperature jumps to 80°C.
- Vibration reaches 50 Hz.
- Energy use reaches the maximum value of 5 kWh.
- Material flow falls to zero.
- Tool position remains fixed at 15 mm.

High heat and energy, while the part is held stationary and no new material is being fed, strongly indicate welding or another heat-based joining process.

### 5. Quality Inspection / Post-Weld Check — Event 10
Immediately after the inferred weld, the process enters a low-activity phase:

- Temperature declines sharply.
- Vibration is almost absent.
- There is no material flow.
- Energy consumption is low.
- The tool remains in the same position.

This is reasonably labeled as a brief inspection, confirmation, cooling, or post-process check. With only one event, it is difficult to distinguish these possibilities exactly, but it is clearly a transition away from active welding.

### 6. Packaging / Finished-Part Transfer — Events 11–12
The final active phase shows:

- Low and stable temperature.
- Low vibration.
- Small material flow of 1 unit/s.
- Tool position moves to 20 mm and remains there.
- Moderate-low energy use.

This likely represents moving the completed component out of the work area, feeding packaging material, or performing a final handling operation before the machine resets.
