### **Process Mining Strategy for Instance-Spanning Constraint Optimization**

As the Senior Process Analyst, I recognize that traditional process mining often focuses on within-case path analysis. However, this scenario is defined by **inter-case dependencies**. To drive meaningful improvement, we must extend our analysis to quantify how orders interact via shared resources, batching logic, priorities, and regulatory limits. Below is the comprehensive strategy.

### **1. Identifying Instance-Spanning Constraints and Their Impact**

To formally identify and quantify these constraints, we will move beyond standard conformance checking and employ advanced process mining techniques tailored for inter-case dependencies.

#### **Techniques & Identification Methods**
*   **Cross-Case Correlation Analysis:** We will join events across cases based on temporal overlaps and resource usage. By analyzing the timestamp differences between a case entering a queue and the state of the resource/collection, we can attribute delays to specific constraints.
*   **Resource Contention Heatmaps:** Generate visualizations showing the overlap of resource usage (e.g., Cold-Packing stations) versus incoming demand to identify saturation points.
*   **Batch Dependency Mapping:** Create a virtual "Batch" entity derived from the log. We will link individual order events to their parent batch and analyze the lifecycle of the batch versus the lifecycle of its constituent orders.
*   **Constraint Conformance Checking:** Define formal rules (e.g., `IF Hazardous THEN Count(Packing/QC, Hazardous, Simultaneous) <= 10`) and run automated checks to detect violations or near-misses (e.g., systems operating at 9/10 capacity).

#### **Impact Quantification & Metrics**
For each constraint, we will track specific metrics derived from the log:

| Constraint | Key Metrics | Impact Measurement |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Shared Cold-Packing** | • **Station Utilization:** Ratio of busy time to available time for C-Stations.<br>• **Queue Depth Distribution:** Number of orders waiting for C-Stations.<br>• **Wait-Time Contribution:** % of total cycle time spent waiting for C-Station availability. | High utilization (>85%) combined with long tail wait times indicates a bottleneck. We calculate the **"Cold-Packing Penalty"** as the average additional delay incurred by orders requiring cold packing compared to similar non-cold orders. |
| **Shipping Batches** | • **Batch Formation Latency:** Time from first order ready to batch dispatch.<br>• **Batch Holding Time:** Duration orders sit in "Ready to Ship" state awaiting batch completion.<br>• **Batch Efficiency:** Ratio of orders shipped per batch vs. theoretical max. | Measures the trade-off between route optimization and flow speed. High holding times indicate batching is throttling throughput. |
| **Priority Handling** | • **Interruption Frequency:** Count of Express orders preempting Standard orders.<br>• **Preemption Overhead:** Time lost due to context switching/resumption of Standard orders.<br>• **Standard Order Delay Attribution:** Increase in Standard order cycle time correlated with Express spikes. | Quantifies the disruption cost. We measure the **"Priority Tax"** paid by Standard orders in terms of increased variance and mean delay. |
| **HazMat Limits** | • **Regulatory Utilization:** Frequency of 8, 9, or 10 simultaneous HazMat orders.<br>• **Throughput Drop-off:** Reduction in packing rate when HazMat count reaches 10.<br>• **Violation Events:** Any breaches of the limit (critical safety KPI). | Even if violations are zero, operating near the limit (9/10) causes significant queuing. We measure the **"HazMat Cap Effect"** as the reduction in overall facility throughput during high HazMat volume periods. |

#### **Differentiating Within-Instance vs. Between-Instance Waiting**
To isolate instance-spanning delays, we will decompose the total waiting time (`W_total`) for each activity:
1.  **Calculate Expected Duration:** Use statistical modeling (e.g., quantile regression) based on historical data to estimate the expected processing time for an activity given its attributes.
2.  **Compute Residual Wait:** `W_total - Expected_Duration`.
3.  **Root Cause Attribution:**
    *   **Resource Contention:** If the residual wait correlates strongly with the utilization of the assigned resource by *other* cases, it is attributed to **Shared Resources**.
    *   **Batching:** If the residual wait correlates with the number of pending orders in the same region/batch, it is attributed to **Batching**.
    *   **Priority:** If the residual wait coincides temporally with Express preemptions on the same resource, it is attributed to **Priority Handling**.
    *   **HazMat:** If the wait occurs when the global HazMat count is at the limit, it is attributed to **Regulatory Limits**.

### **2. Analyzing Constraint Interactions**

Optimizing constraints in isolation can lead to suboptimal outcomes or unintended consequences. We must analyze their interactions:

*   **Priority × Cold-Packing Interaction:**
    *   *Scenario:* An Express order requires a Cold-Packing station. A Standard order occupies it. The Express order preempts the Standard order.
    *   *Risk:* Preemption may cause the Standard order to miss its own SLA or require re-inspection if the interruption disrupts packing integrity. Furthermore, if multiple Express orders arrive rapidly, the Standard order could be thrashed (repeatedly paused), effectively removing it from the queue.
    *   *Analysis:* We need to measure the **Preemption Cascade Effect**. Does preemption actually reduce the Express order's completion time significantly, or does the overhead negate the gain?

*   **Batching × HazMat Interaction:**
    *   *Scenario:* A batch destined for "North" contains 12 orders, 6 of which are HazMat. When the batch completes picking, all 6 HazMat orders arrive at Packing simultaneously.
    *   *Risk:* The HazMat limit allows only 10 simultaneous. If the facility is already processing 5 HazMat orders elsewhere, these 6 cannot enter Packing immediately. The entire batch (including non-HazMat orders) may stall, creating a **Clustered Bottleneck**.
    *   *Analysis:* We will analyze **Batch Composition Variance**. Batches with high HazMat ratios likely experience higher variance in flow through Packing/QC due to the hard cap.

*   **Priority × Batching Interaction:**
    *   *Scenario:* An Express order arrives late in a batch formation window.
    *   *Risk:* Rigid batching rules may force the Express order to wait for the batch to close, violating the spirit of "Express" service.
    *   *Analysis:* Evaluate **Express Bypass Feasibility**. Data may show that allowing Express orders to form micro-batches or skip batching entirely yields better ROI than strict route optimization.

**Crucial Insight:** Understanding these interactions reveals that **Batching acts as a magnifier for HazMat constraints**, and **Priority handling introduces instability to resource sharing**. Strategies must be holistic; e.g., relaxing batching for HazMat-heavy regions may be necessary to respect regulatory caps.

### **3. Developing Constraint-Aware Optimization Strategies**

Based on the analysis, I propose three distinct strategies designed to mitigate constraints while leveraging data insights.

#### **Strategy 1: Adaptive Batching with HazMat-Aware Release Logic**
*   **Constraints Addressed:** Shipping Batches, HazMat Limits, Priority.
*   **Proposed Changes:**
    *   **Dynamic Batch Triggers:** Instead of fixed time windows, implement a hybrid trigger. Batches form when either a time threshold is met OR when an Express order arrives (creating a "Priority Micro-Batch").
    *   **HazMat Staggered Release:** Modify the workflow so that when a batch containing HazMat items reaches Packing, the system enforces a **Staggered Release**. Even if the batch is complete, HazMat items are released into Packing/QC in controlled waves (e.g., groups of 5) to prevent hitting the global limit of 10, ensuring non-HazMat orders in the same batch can still flow.
    *   **Predictive Batching:** Use ML to predict arrival rates. If a surge of HazMat orders to a region is predicted, the system automatically splits the batch into smaller sub-batches to smooth downstream flow.
*   **Expected Outcomes:**
    *   Reduced end-to-end time for Express orders (faster batch formation).
    *   Elimination of clustered bottlenecks at Packing/QC.
    *   Maintained route optimization benefits for Standard orders while preventing regulatory chokepoints.

#### **Strategy 2: Intelligent Preemption & Weighted Scheduling for Cold-Packing**
*   **Constraints Addressed:** Shared Cold-Packing, Priority Handling.
*   **Proposed Changes:**
    *   **Weighted Scheduling Algorithm:** Replace simple FCFS with a scoring function for the C-Station queue. Score = `(Remaining_Time / Deadline_Proximity) * Priority_Weight`. This prioritizes orders that are urgent and quick to finish over those that are less urgent or would take a long time.
    *   **Smart Preemption Policy:** Implement **"Resumable Preemption"**. Preemption is allowed only if the Standard order has completed >50% of packing (minimizing rework risk) AND the estimated completion time of the Express order is < X minutes. If the Express order is complex, it queues behind the Standard order instead of preempting.
    *   **Buffer Zone Assignment:** Designate specific C-Stations for "Short-Duration" tasks and "Long-Duration" tasks based on historical data, reducing swap-over time.
*   **Expected Outcomes:**
    *   Lower variance in cycle times.
    *   Reduced "Priority Tax" on Standard orders by avoiding unnecessary preemptions.
    *   Higher throughput of Express orders without sacrificing quality or fairness.

#### **Strategy 3: HazMat Decoupling & Parallel QC Pathways**
*   **Constraints Addressed:** HazMat Limits, Quality Check.
*   **Proposed Changes:**
    *   **Decouple Packing from QC for HazMat:** Currently, HazMat orders block both Packing and QC. We propose a **Physical or Logical Decoupling**: Introduce a secure, regulated staging area immediately after Packing for HazMat items. Once packed, the order moves to Staging, freeing the Packing station for the next HazMat order (up to the limit of 10 active in Packing). QC can then pull from Staging as QC resources become available.
    *   **Dedicated HazMat QC Shifts:** Analyze log data to see if HazMat QC takes longer. If so, allocate specific QC staff trained in HazMat to dedicated shifts, reducing the cognitive load and inspection time, thereby increasing effective capacity.
*   **Expected Outcomes:**
    *   Increases effective throughput at Packing by allowing faster turnover of HazMat orders (they don't hold the station while waiting for QC).
    *   Smooths the load on QC resources.
    *   Reduces the risk of violations by providing a buffer that manages the flow into the constrained QC stage.

*   **Global Performance:**

*   **Drift Detection:** If the log starts deviating from the simulation model (e.g., new product types change HazMat profiles), trigger a re-analysis.
*   **Strategy Tuning:** Use real-time data to adjust strategy parameters dynamically. For example, if HazMat volumes drop, the system can relax the staggered release logic to increase throughput.

By implementing this data-driven, constraint-aware strategy, the fulfillment center will transition from reactive firefighting to proactive optimization, ensuring delivery targets are met even under the pressure of complex inter-case dependencies.
