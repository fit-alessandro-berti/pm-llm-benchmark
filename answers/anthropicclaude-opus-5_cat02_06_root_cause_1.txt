## 3. Root Causes

**A. Escalation handoff dead time (the single biggest driver).**
Both escalated cases (102, 105) are in the slow group. The cost is not the escalation decision itself but the *waiting for Level-2 to pick the ticket up*: 2 h 30 m in case 102 and **28 hours** in case 105. There is evidently no SLA, no shared queue, and no capacity guarantee on the Level-2 side — tickets simply sit until an L2 agent happens to look.

**B. Rework / mis-routing at triage.**
Case 105 shows the pattern `Investigate  Escalate  Investigate`: Level-1 spent 50 minutes investigating a ticket it could never solve, then handed it to L2 who had to start the investigation from scratch. Case 102 escalated *before* any investigation (`Assign L1  Escalate`), meaning the L1 assignment was pure overhead — 2 h 30 m lost to routing a ticket to the wrong queue. Triage is not discriminating complexity correctly.

**C. Level-1 queueing before investigation.**
Case 104 was never escalated yet still took 24 h. Its killer gap is `Assign  Investigate = 3 h 30 m` (vs. 15–40 min for the fast cases). Note the correlation with arrival order: 101 (08:00) and 103 (08:10) sailed through, while 104 (08:20) and 105 (08:25) — the tail of the morning arrival burst — accumulated waits at triage and assignment. This is classic morning-peak congestion against fixed agent capacity.

**D. The working-day boundary / shift cut-off.**
Every slow case has an `Investigate  Resolve` gap of exactly ~19 hours, and every one of these resolutions lands at 08:00–09:00 the next morning. The rule is visible: **if investigation does not start before roughly 13:00–14:00, the ticket will not be resolved that day.** The delay is not effort — it is calendar. This effect compounds with A, B and C: any upstream wait of a few hours pushes the ticket past the cut-off and instantly converts it into a 19-hour delay. Case 105 crossed the boundary twice, which is why it took two days.

**E. Resolve  Close is never the problem** (15–30 min in all cases). No action needed there.

## 4. How These Factors Compound

The delays are multiplicative, not additive. A 3-hour L1 queue (C) or a 2.5-hour escalation wait (A) is annoying on its own, but its real damage is that it pushes the investigation past the daily cut-off (D), adding ~19 hours. Case 105 illustrates the worst chain: morning congestion  wasted L1 investigation (B)  escalation with no pickup SLA (A)  missed cut-off (D)  second missed cut-off (D) = 49 hours for what is probably a few hours of actual work. Flow efficiency (touch time ÷ cycle time) is roughly 60–80 % for cases 101/103 but below 10 % for 102, 104 and 105.

## 5. Recommendations

1. **Put an SLA and a pull-based shared queue on escalations** — e.g., L2 must acknowledge within 30 minutes, with automatic alerting when a ticket has been in `Escalate` state without a following `Investigate`. This alone removes ~28 h from case 105.
2. **Route by complexity at triage, not by default to L1.** Use a skills/complexity checklist (or a classifier trained on historical escalation outcomes) so tickets that will end up at L2 go there directly. Track "escalation rate after L1 assignment" as a triage-quality KPI; cases 102 and 105 would both have skipped a wasted hop.
3. **Enforce a "start-of-investigation cut-off" rule.** Prioritise dispatching so that no ticket sits unassigned past ~11:00, and treat any ticket still un-investigated by early afternoon as at-risk. Alternatively introduce late-shift or follow-the-sun coverage for L2 so the overnight gap disappears.
4. **Smooth the morning arrival peak** — add triage/assignment capacity in the 08:00–10:00 window, or auto-triage low-complexity tickets, so late-arriving tickets (104, 105) are not systematically penalised.
5. **Measure waiting time explicitly**, per handoff, not just end-to-end cycle time. Dashboards should show `AssignInvestigate`, `EscalateInvestigate` and `InvestigateResolve` gaps, since these three transitions account for essentially all the variance in this log.
6. **Auto-close after resolution** (or close on the same interaction) — a small win (15–30 min) but free.

**Caveat:** with only five cases, these are hypotheses supported by strong but limited evidence. Confirming them requires a larger log, plus resource/ticket-category attributes to distinguish "genuinely hard tickets" from "tickets stuck in queues."
