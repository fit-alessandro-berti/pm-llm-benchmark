# 20 Thought-Provoking Questions on the Smartphone Supply Chain Process

## Process Design & Flow Rationale

1. Why does the process sequence Market Research and R&D Design as strictly linear steps rather than iterative or parallel activities—what would be gained or lost by allowing feedback loops between them?

2. What criteria determined the decision to secure components from four geographically dispersed regions (Taiwan, Japan, South Korea, Germany/USA) rather than consolidating suppliers, and how does this choice balance cost, quality, and risk?

3. Why is the "Ship Components to Assembly Plant" step positioned only after all four component-sourcing tasks converge at the parallel join—could staggered or partial shipments improve efficiency?

4. What is the underlying logic for choosing Malaysia as the centralized assembly location, and how sensitive is the entire downstream process to disruptions at this single site?

## Risk Management

5. Given the reliance on Taiwan for microprocessors, what contingency plans exist if geopolitical tensions or natural disasters disrupt this single point of failure?

6. How would the process flow change if two or more component-sourcing regions experienced simultaneous delays—does the current parallel gateway design adequately absorb such multi-node risk?

7. What early-warning indicators could be embedded into the "Secure Components" tasks to detect supply risks before they cascade into assembly delays?

8. How resilient is the single-plant assembly model (Malaysia) to black-swan events like pandemics, and what would a multi-plant redundancy model look like in this BPMN?

## Decision-Making & Exclusive Gateways

9. What specific thresholds or KPIs trigger the exclusive gateway decision between "Quality Checks & Diagnostics" (pass) versus "Rework or Scrap" (fail), and who owns this decision?

10. How is the "Rework or Scrap" loop-back to assembly time-boxed—what happens if repeated failures create an infinite loop that delays the entire shipment cycle?

11. What data feeds the "Monitor Inventory & Sales Forecast" step, and how quickly can the exclusive gateway (Adjust Orders vs. Proceed) react to sudden demand shocks (e.g., viral product demand or economic downturns)?

12. Why is the demand-adjustment decision positioned at the very end of the process rather than earlier, closer to production planning—would earlier signal integration reduce waste?

## Process Improvement Opportunities

13. Could the "Air Freight vs. Cargo Ship" parallel split be replaced with a dynamic, cost-and-demand-sensitive algorithm rather than a fixed dual-path structure?

14. What would be the impact of introducing a "Quality Checks" gateway *before* full assembly (e.g., component-level inspection) rather than only after assembly, in terms of cost and rework rates?

15. How might automation or AI-driven demand forecasting reshape the "Monitor Inventory & Sales Forecast" task, and would this shift the exclusive gateway logic entirely?

## Stakeholder Communication

16. Which stakeholders (suppliers, logistics partners, regional distributors, retailers) require real-time visibility into this process, and where are the current communication gaps in the BPMN?

17. How is information about quality failures or reworks communicated back to the original component suppliers to prevent recurring defects?

18. What role do regional distribution centers play in feeding demand signals back to the "Adjust Orders to Suppliers" decision, and is this feedback loop fast enough to be actionable?

## Performance Measurement

19. What lead-time, cost, and quality KPIs should be tracked at each parallel gateway (component sourcing, freight split, distribution split) to benchmark process health over time?

20. How would you measure the trade-off between speed (air freight, expedited processing) and cost/sustainability (cargo shipping, bulk distribution) across the entire end-to-end process, and what metrics would best capture this balance?
