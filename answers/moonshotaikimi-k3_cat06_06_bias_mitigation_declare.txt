## Rationale for the Added Constraints

**1. Quarantining sensitive attributes (`responded_existence`, `response`, `chainresponse`)**
- `CheckApplicantRace/Gender/Age  BiasMitigationCheck`: whenever a protected attribute is inspected, a bias-mitigation check is obligated in the trace (and must *follow* the inspection). The `chainresponse` on race goes further — the bias check must happen *immediately after*, so sensitive information can never be acted on "raw."

**2. Blocking biased decision paths (`nonsuccession`, `nonchainsuccession`, `absence`)**
- `CheckApplicant*  Reject` (`nonsuccession`): a trace that inspects a protected attribute can never subsequently reject the applicant — an absolute barrier against attribute-driven adverse decisions.
- `CheckApplicant*  Approve` (`nonchainsuccession`): approvals can't immediately follow attribute checks either, forcing a neutral step (the bias check) in between, so neither favorable nor adverse outcomes can be directly attributable to sensitive data.
- `absence(AutoReject_ProtectedAttribute)` and `StartApplication  Reject`: bans automated rejections based on protected classes and snap rejections without any review.

**3. Human-in-the-loop for sensitive demographics (`coexistence`, `precedence`, `chainprecedence`)**
- `Approve_Minority` / `Reject_Minority` / `RequestAdditionalInfo_Minority` coexist with and are preceded by `ManualReview`: decisions (and even extra scrutiny) affecting protected-group applicants always require human oversight, with `chainprecedence(ManualReview, Reject_Minority)` demanding an immediate human sign-off right before a minority rejection.

**4. Decision gating and consistency (`existence`, `altprecedence`, `succession`, `exactly_one`, `noncoexistence`)**
- `BiasMitigationCheck` is mandatory in every trace, gates all approvals (`precedence`), is the last step before `FinalDecision` (`chainprecedence`/`succession`), and each rejection requires a *fresh* bias check (`altprecedence`), preventing "one check, many biased rejections."
- `exactly_one(FinalDecision)` and `noncoexistence(Approve, Reject)` ensure a single, consistent outcome per case, eliminating contradictory decisions that could mask discriminatory treatment.

**Caveat:** constraints like `nonsuccession(CheckApplicantRace, Reject)` are deliberately strict guardrails; in a real deployment you might relax them (e.g., replace with `nonchainsuccession` plus precedence constraints) to keep the model satisfiable while still forcing every adverse decision through documented, reviewable fairness checks.
