A typical P2P control-oriented sequence is:

1. Create purchase requisition  
2. Approve purchase requisition  
3. Issue purchase order (PO)  
4. Receive goods/services  
5. Receive vendor invoice  
6. Perform PO/invoice/receipt match (often a three-way match)  
7. Release payment  
8. Close the case  

Some variation is normal—for example, an invoice may arrive before the goods are physically received—but payment should ordinarily not be released until required approvals and matching controls have been completed.

| Case | Assessment | Unusual sequence / anomaly | Why it is problematic |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1001** | Largely normal | No material anomaly. Invoice is received after goods receipt and is matched before payment. | This follows the expected controlled P2P flow. The payment is released only after goods receipt and invoice matching. |
| **1002** | Anomalous | **PO issued before requisition approval**: PO is issued on Feb. 1, while approval occurs on Feb. 3. | A PO normally represents an authorized commitment to a supplier. Issuing it before approval can bypass purchasing authority, budget checks, and segregation-of-duties controls. |
| **1002** | Highly anomalous | **Payment released before requisition approval, goods receipt, and invoice matching.** Payment occurs Feb. 3 at 07:15; approval follows at 08:00, goods are received Feb. 4, and matching occurs Feb. 5. | This is the most significant anomaly. The organization paid before confirming that the purchase was authorized, the goods were received, or the invoice agreed with the PO and receipt. This creates risks of overpayment, payment for undelivered goods, duplicate/fraudulent invoices, and weak audit compliance. |
| **1003** | Highly anomalous | **Payment released before invoice receipt, goods receipt, and invoice matching.** Payment is on Feb. 8; invoice arrives Feb. 9, goods are received Feb. 10, and matching occurs Feb. 11. | Payment without an invoice, evidence of delivery, or a completed match is inconsistent with normal P2P controls. Although advance payments can exist in exceptional contractual situations, they should be explicitly approved and documented. Otherwise, the sequence may indicate control failure or a potential improper payment. |
| **1004** | Anomalous | **Goods received before the PO is issued.** Goods are received Feb. 6 at 09:00; the PO is issued at 09:15. | Receiving goods before a PO may indicate a retrospective PO, “after-the-fact” ordering, emergency purchasing, or inaccurate timestamps. It bypasses the intended pre-authorization of spending and makes it harder to ensure that the vendor, pricing, quantity, and terms were approved before delivery. |
| **1004** | Otherwise mostly consistent after the anomaly | Invoice is received and matched after both goods receipt and PO issuance; payment follows matching. | The later invoice-to-PO match and payment sequence is reasonable, but it does not eliminate the concern that goods were accepted before the PO existed. |

### Summary

- **Case 1001** is the expected standard flow.
- **Case 1002** has both an authorization failure (PO before approval) and a severe payment-control failure (payment before approval, receipt, and matching).
- **Case 1003** has an even clearer premature-payment pattern: payment precedes invoice receipt, goods receipt, and matching.
- **Case 1004** suggests a retrospective or late-issued PO because the goods were received before the PO was created.

The strongest control concerns are the premature payments in **1002** and **1003**, since they expose the organization directly to financial loss and indicate that core authorization and three-way-match controls may have been bypassed.
