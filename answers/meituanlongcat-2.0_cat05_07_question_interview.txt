Here is a list of targeted, open-ended questions designed to clarify and deepen your understanding of the property onboarding process. 

**Decision Criteria & Property Manager Assignment**
1. You mentioned that assigning a property manager depends on their workload, geographic specialization, and experience. Can you walk me through the specific decision criteria or thresholds used to balance these factors? For example, how do you define a "full" workload, and how do you handle a situation where there are no available managers with the exact required property-type experience?
2. When the property coordinator initially reviews the landlord's documentation, what are the specific "fatal flaws" or disqualifying factors that would cause the company to decline bringing a property into the portfolio before it ever reaches the manager assignment stage?
3. In the tenant screening phase, you mentioned negotiating lease terms and offering promotional discounts when multiple tenants are interested. How is the final tenant selected if competing applicants are closely matched—do you prioritize higher rent, longer lease terms, or projected tenant longevity? Who has the authority to approve a non-standard lease clause or agree to a promotional discount?

**Roles, Responsibilities & Rental Pricing**
4. Once the property manager and marketing team are involved, how do you determine the final rental price? Who has the ultimate authority if the property manager's on-the-ground knowledge of the landlord's needs conflicts with the digital marketing team's algorithmic pricing models based on market trends?
5. During the inspection phase, the property manager works with the landlord to address issues. Who is responsible for sourcing and hiring the contractors for these repairs—the property manager or the landlord? More importantly, who bears the financial responsibility for bringing the property up to code if the landlord's budget is exhausted?
6. At the end of the process, a compliance officer, financial analyst, and IT department conduct audits. If one of these audits uncovers a major discrepancy—say the IT department finds a data integration error, or the financial analyst deems the signed lease unprofitable—who takes the lead on resolving the issue, and what is the typical impact on the timeline?

**Timing, Sequencing & Bottlenecks**
7. Looking at the entire lifecycle from landlord contact to move-in, what are the most common bottlenecks or delays that push the process past its expected timeline? How long does the documentation update phase typically last if a landlord has missing or outdated compliance documents?
8. The process involves a lot of back-and-forth (e.g., scheduling the photographer and inspector simultaneously, then waiting for repairs). Are there any steps in this sequence that currently happen linearly but you feel could be run in parallel to speed up the overall onboarding time?
9. Once a suitable tenant is chosen and the lease is signed, how much time typically elapses between lease signing and the actual move-in date? Are there quality checks or secondary steps that happen in this gap (e.g., a walkthrough) that aren't currently described?

**Exception Handling & Edge Cases**
10. You briefly mentioned that exceptions occur, such as properties requiring special insurance or landlords insisting on custom lease clauses. Can you describe the escalation path for these exceptions? Who has the authority to approve a custom lease clause, and how does the team ensure it doesn't compromise the compliance officer's fair housing review?
11. What happens if the certified inspector finds habitability issues that the landlord is entirely unwilling or unable to fix? At what point in the process does the company walk away from the onboarding, and who makes that final call?
12. How does the process change if the property is highly specialized, like luxury units or student housing? Do different property types require a completely different set of compliance checks, or do they simply trigger a different group of specialized managers?

**Missing Details & Initial Triage**
13. Before the property coordinator even begins reviewing documentation, is there an initial triage step? How does the company quickly assess whether a property is even a good fit for their portfolio (e.g., zip code, property class, estimated ROI) before investing the coordinator's time in document verification?
14. How does the company handle landlord communication and expectation-setting during the "waiting periods" of the process, such as when the property is being repaired or when the marketing team is optimizing pricing? What happens if the landlord becomes unresponsive during these phases?
