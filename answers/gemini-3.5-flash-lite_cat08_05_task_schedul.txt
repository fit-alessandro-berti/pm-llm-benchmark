### 1. Analyzing Historical Scheduling Performance and Dynamics

To transform Precision Parts Inc.’s raw Manufacturing Execution System (MES) event logs into actionable operational insights, we must apply **Process Mining** through an event log structure where *Cases* represent `Job IDs`, *Activities* map to lifecycle milestones (`Queue Entry`, `Setup Start`, `Setup End`, `Task Start`, `Task End`, `Job Released`), and *Resources* equate to `Machine ID` and `Operator ID`. 

#### Process Mining Techniques and Metrics

*   **Job Flow Times, Lead Times, and Makespan Distributions:**
    *   *Technique:* **Fuzzy Mining** and **Directly-Follows Graphs (DFGs)** to visualize alternative job routings and rework loops. **Case Duration Analysis** to calculate lead times from `Job Released` to `Task End` (Final Inspection).
    *   *Metrics:* Mean, median, and 90th percentile of total lead time; makespan distributions grouped by product family, order priority, and complexity.
*   **Task Waiting Times (Queue Times):**
    *   *Technique:* **Performance Social Network Analysis** and bottleneck discovery algorithms (e.g., measuring token accumulation in Petri net models). 
    *   *Metrics:* Time delta between `Queue Entry` and `Setup Start` per work center. Highlighting queues at critical paths (e.g., CNC milling vs. manual grinding).
*   **Resource Utilization (Machine and Operator):**
    *   *Technique:* **Resource Utilization Dashboards** built via case/event filtering over time windows. 
    *   *Metrics:* 
        *   *Productive Time:* Duration of `Task Start` to `Task End`.
        *   *Idle Time:* Periods where resources are available but no tasks are allocated.
        *   *Setup Time:* Duration of `Setup Start` to `Setup End`.
*   **Sequence-Dependent Setup Times (SDST):**
    *   *Technique:* **Attribute-Based Event Correlation**. We correlate the `Setup Start` event of Job $N$ with the `Task End` event attributes of Job $N-1$ on the same `Machine ID`. 
    *   *Metrics:* Setup duration matrices mapped against product attribute changes (e.g., tool changeovers, material grade switches, fixture modifications). 
*   **Schedule Adherence and Tardiness:**
    *   *Technique:* **Conformance Checking** against planned routings/due dates and **Performance Drift Analysis**.
    *   *Metrics:* Tardiness ($\max(0, \text{Completion Time} - \text{Due Date})$), lateness (signed difference), and percentage of orders delivered on time (OTD).
*   **Impact of Disruptions:**
    *   *Technique:* **Sub-process Performance Analysis** and **Variant Filtering** comparing jobs executed during periods of unplanned `Breakdown Start` events versus stable periods.
    *   *Metrics:* Lead time inflation factor, secondary queue buildups, and downstream starvation duration following a bottleneck breakdown.

### 2. Diagnosing Scheduling Pathologies

By processing the historical logs, several systemic pathologies emerge across the Precision Parts Inc. shop floor:

```
[Upstream Bottleneck: CUT-01] 
          (Starvation)
         
[Under-utilized Downstream: MILL-03] > [WIP Accumulation] > [Missed Due Dates]
         
          (Sequence-Dependent Setup Bloat)
[Frequent Rework / Hot-Job Interruptions]
```

#### Key Pathologies & Process Mining Evidence

1.  **Critical Resource Bottlenecks:** 
    *   *Pathology:* Specialized machines (e.g., primary 5-axis CNC mills or Heat Treatment ovens) act as severe capacity constraints.
    *   *Evidence:* **Process Discovery & Bottleneck Analysis** shows high time-in-queue (accounting for up to 60% of total lead time) specifically before these machines, while downstream inspection stations show high idle times.
2.  **Suboptimal Sequencing (SDST Bloat):**
    *   *Pathology:* Local First-Come-First-Served (FCFS) rules ignore setup affinities, resulting in excessive, redundant changeovers.
    *   *Evidence:* **Variant Analysis** and setup-time logging reveal that machines process jobs in an arbitrary order, causing setup times to routinely exceed processing times for small batch jobs.
3.  **Inversion of Priorities ("Priority Inversion"):**
    *   *Pathology:* High-priority or urgent ("hot") jobs get stuck behind long queues because local dispatching doesn't dynamically re-route or pre-empt work in progress.
    *   *Evidence:* **Performance Filtering** shows that "High Priority" orders exhibit a wider lead-time distribution and higher average tardiness than medium-priority orders due to being blocked by intermediate queues.
4.  **Upstream Starvation and Downstream Surges (Bullwhip Effect in WIP):**
    *   *Pathology:* Poor coordination creates alternating periods of starvation and bunching.
    *   *Evidence:* **Token Replay / Performance Animation** demonstrates that when a bottleneck machine breaks down, WIP spikes exponentially upstream; upon recovery, a shockwave of work floods downstream machines, causing micro-bottlenecks.

### 3. Root Cause Analysis of Scheduling Ineffectiveness

#### Root Causes
*   **Static Local Rules vs. Dynamic Global Reality:** FCFS and EDD (Earliest Due Date) applied locally fail to account for downstream machine states or non-linear job routings. A job prioritized by EDD at Station A might be routed to a completely jammed Station B, whereas an alternative routing exists.
*   **Information Asymmetry:** Planners lack real-time visibility; schedules are generated using static, deterministic process times rather than stochastic realities (actual operator speeds, tool wear, and historical setup variance).
*   **Lack of Explicit SDST Modeling:** Standard MES logic treats setup times as fixed constants rather than functions of preceding job attributes ($f(\text{Job}_{i-1}, \text{Job}_i)$).
*   **Reactive Firefighting:** Unplanned breakdowns and rush orders are handled by ad-hoc manual intervention (supervisors jumping the queue), which shatters the baseline schedule and degrades overall shop performance.

#### Differentiating Scheduling Logic vs. Capacity/Variability
Process mining isolates these factors through **Conformance Checking** and **Discrete-Event Simulation baselining**:
*   *Capacity Limits:* If a resource's utilization rate calculated from process logs approaches 98–100% across all variants, the root cause is **physical capacity shortage**, not scheduling logic. Scheduling can only sequence, not create capacity.
*   *Scheduling Logic Inefficiencies:* If capacity utilization is healthy (e.g., 75–80%), but lead times are inflated due to high queue times and excessive setup variance, the root cause is **suboptimal scheduling logic and lack of sequencing coordination**.

### 4. Developing Advanced Data-Driven Scheduling Strategies

#### Strategy 1: Dynamic Composite Dispatching Rule (DCDR) with Sequence Awareness
*   **Core Logic:** Replace static local rules with a dynamic priority index calculated for every job waiting in a queue whenever a machine becomes free. The index balances due-date urgency, remaining processing time, downstream load, and estimated SDST.
*   **Formula Example:** 
    $$\text{Priority Index}_j = w_1 \left(\frac{\text{Slack}_j}{\text{Remaining Ops}}\right) + w_2 (\text{Urgency Score}) - w_3 (\text{Est. Setup Time}_{i \rightarrow j})$$
    Where $\text{Slack}_j = (\text{Due Date} - \text{Current Time}) - \text{Remaining Processing Time}$.
*   **Process Mining Integration:** Weights ($w_1, w_2, w_3$) are optimized using machine learning models trained on historical event logs to identify which factors historically correlated with on-time delivery under varying shop loads.
*   **Impact:** Reduces tardiness for near-due orders while drastically cutting setup waste by penalizing job switches that require massive changeovers.

#### Strategy 2: Predictive Stochastic Scheduling & Risk Buffering
*   **Core Logic:** Instead of using fixed, deterministic planned durations, the scheduling engine uses empiric probability distributions (Weibull or Log-Normal distributions) mined from MES logs for task execution and setup durations, segmented by machine, operator skill level, and job complexity.
*   **Process Mining Integration:** Historical logs supply the parameters (mean, variance) for task durations. Predictive maintenance logs supply failure probabilities for bottleneck machines.
*   **Impact:** Generates robust, probabilistic schedules with integrated time buffers before critical operations, sharply reducing lead-time unpredictability and customer schedule slippage.

#### Strategy 3: Setup-Optimized Family Batching and Traveling Salesperson (TSP) Sequencing
*   **Core Logic:** For bottleneck work centers (e.g., Cutting and Heat Treatment), jobs are not scheduled strictly by due date. Instead, the scheduler groups jobs into setups/families (e.g., same material grade, identical tooling) and sequences them using heuristic optimization (such as a constrained TSP algorithm) to minimize total daily setup time.
*   **Process Mining Integration:** The transition cost matrix (SDST matrix) is auto-populated directly from historical setup event correlations (Setup Duration vs. Job Attribute changes).
*   **Impact:** Maximizes throughput at bottleneck machines, drops WIP by clearing families of work concurrently, and dramatically increases overall productive machine utilization.

### 5. Simulation, Evaluation, and Continuous Improvement

#### Discrete-Event Simulation (DES) Framework
To prove the efficacy of the proposed strategies before shop-floor deployment, we construct a DES model (e.g., using Python/SimPy or AnyLogic):

*   **Parameterization via Process Mining:**
    *   *Routing Probabilities & Task Durations:* Derived directly from event log process discovery (empirical distributions rather than theoretical guesses).
    *   *SDST Matrices:* Imbedded into the machine state-change logic.
    *   *Breakdown Frequencies/Repair Times:* Modeled using Time-Between-Failures (MTBF) and Time-To-Repair (MTTR) extracted from MES resource event logs.
*   **Scenario Testing:**
    *   *Scenario A (Baseline):* Current FCFS/EDD rules under normal load.
    *   *Scenario B (Stress Test):* High load (+25% job release rate) combined with stochastic machine breakdowns.
    *   *Scenario C (Hot-Job Injection):* Simulating the sudden insertion of 5 high-priority urgent orders per shift.
    *   *Evaluation Metrics:* Makespan, Mean Tardiness, Max Tardiness, Average WIP, and Resource Utilization.

#### Continuous Monitoring and Drift Detection Framework
Once the chosen strategies are deployed, the MES event logs feed a **Continuous Process Mining and Conformance Checking Pipeline**:

```
[MES Event Logs] > [Real-Time Conformance Checking] > [Drift Detection]
                                                                
                                                    (Alert if Divergence > Threshold)
                                                                
                                                    [Recalibrate Scheduling Weights]
```

1.  **Online Conformance Checking:** Continuously compare the *executed* process model (mined daily) against the *prescribed* scheduling model.
2.  **Concept Drift Detection:** Automatically monitor KPI shifts (e.g., if actual setup times begin creeping past historical distributions due to tooling wear or new operators).
3.  **Automated Feedback Loop:** When drift or emerging bottlenecks are detected (e.g., a newly overloaded work center due to product mix changes), the DCDR weight parameters are automatically retrained via machine learning on recent event logs, ensuring the scheduling system continuously adapts to shop-floor realities without manual intervention.
