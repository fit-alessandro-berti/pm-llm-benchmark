# Customer Support Ticket Resolution – Performance Analysis

## 1. Case Duration Comparison

| Case ID | Start | End | Total Duration | Escalated? |
|---------|-------|-----|-----------------|------------|
| 101 | 03-01 08:00 | 03-01 10:15 | **2h 15m** | No |
| 103 | 03-01 08:10 | 03-01 09:30 | **1h 20m** | No |
| 104 | 03-01 08:20 | 03-02 08:30 | **24h 10m** | No |
| 102 | 03-01 08:05 | 03-02 09:15 | **25h 10m** | Yes |
| 105 | 03-01 08:25 | 03-03 09:30 | **49h 05m** | Yes |

**Cases 102, 104, and 105 are clear outliers**, taking roughly 10–20x longer than the "normal" cases (101, 103). Case 105 is the worst performer, taking over two full days.

## 2. Root Cause Analysis

### a) Escalation to Level-2 Agent is a major delay driver
- Both cases involving escalation (102 and 105) are among the slowest.
- Case 102: Gap between **Assign  Escalate** = 2.5 hrs, then **Escalate  Investigate** = 2.5 hrs. The escalation handoff itself introduces idle time before work resumes.
- Case 105: After escalation at 10:00 (Day 1), the next "Investigate Issue" doesn't occur until 14:00 the **following day** — a ~28-hour gap. This suggests the Level-2 team was not immediately available or the ticket sat in a queue.

**Insight:** Escalation is not itself the problem — it's the *handoff latency* (waiting for Level-2 agent pickup) that consumes the majority of extra time.

### b) Large idle gaps before the Investigation step
- Case 104 (no escalation) still took 24+ hours due to a **19-hour gap** between "Investigate Issue" (03-01 13:00) and "Resolve Ticket" (03-02 08:00).
- Case 102 also shows a large "Investigate  Resolve" gap (14:00 Day1  09:00 Day2, ~19 hrs).

**Insight:** Even without escalation, the *investigation-to-resolution* phase is where tickets stall — likely due to agents pausing work at end of day/shift and only resuming the next morning. This points to a **lack of after-hours coverage or handover procedure**, causing tickets to "sleep" overnight.

### c) Overnight/off-hours effect
- Notice the pattern: delays of ~19–28 hours consistently start in the afternoon and resolve the next morning (e.g., 13:00–14:00  08:00–09:00 next day).
- This strongly suggests these tickets crossed a **shift boundary or end-of-business-day**, and no evening/night staff picked them up.

### d) Fast cases share a common pattern
- Cases 101 and 103 had **no escalation** and **all activities completed within the same business day**, with short (10–45 min) gaps between steps — indicating continuous agent engagement without shift-related interruptions.

## 3. Recommendations

1. **Reduce escalation handoff latency**
   - Implement automatic routing/paging to Level-2 agents immediately upon escalation instead of manual queue pickup.
   - Set SLA targets (e.g., Level-2 must acknowledge within 30 min).

2. **Address overnight/shift-change bottlenecks**
   - Introduce a "follow-the-sun" or on-call rotation so tickets don't sit idle for 12+ hours awaiting the next business day.
   - Flag any ticket idle for more than 1–2 hours during business hours for supervisor review.

3. **Monitor Investigate  Resolve duration specifically**
   - This transition is the single largest contributor to cycle time in delayed cases. Consider time-boxing investigation phases and requiring status updates every few hours for open investigations.

4. **Use case 101/103 as the benchmark**
   - Since both fast cases avoided escalation and had no idle periods, this reflects the ideal process flow. Deviations (escalation + overnight gaps) should trigger monitoring alerts.

5. **Root-cause escalation triggers**
   - Investigate *why* 102 and 105 needed escalation — if certain ticket types/issues systematically require Level-2 expertise, consider training Level-1 agents on those issues or triaging correctly the first time to avoid the escalation delay entirely.

**Summary:** The performance bottlenecks are driven primarily by (1) delays in Level-2 escalation handoffs and (2) large idle gaps during the investigation phase that align with end-of-day/overnight periods, indicating insufficient after-hours ticket handling rather than issues with the process steps themselves.
