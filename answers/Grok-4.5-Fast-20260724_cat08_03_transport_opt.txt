**1. Process Discovery and Conformance Checking**

**Data preprocessing and integration**  
The multi-source event data must be unified into a single case-centric event log.  

- Define the primary case notion as **Vehicle-Day** (e.g., `V12-20241205`), which captures one vehicle’s complete operational day from shift start to end. Secondary case notions (Package ID or Driver-Day) can be derived for focused analyses.  
- Map heterogeneous event types to a common activity vocabulary: “Start Shift”, “Route Assigned”, “Depart Depot”, “Travel”, “Arrive Customer”, “Service (Success/Failed)”, “Depart Customer”, “Unscheduled Stop / Maintenance”, “Arrive Depot”, “End Shift”.  
- Enrich each event with attributes: timestamp, Vehicle ID, Driver ID, Package ID (when present), GPS coordinates, speed, planned vs. actual indicators, and free-text notes.  
- Construct lifecycle transitions: use GPS speed/status to detect “Moving” vs. “Idle”, scanner events for customer interactions, and maintenance logs for repair intervals.  
- Handle temporal alignment by converting all timestamps to a common timezone and resolving minor clock skew via interpolation or nearest-neighbor matching of GPS and scanner records.  

**Challenges**  
- Incomplete or noisy GPS (gaps, multipath errors in urban canyons).  
- Missing scanner events (driver forgot to scan).  
- Ambiguous idle periods (traffic jam vs. break vs. parking search).  
- Different granularities (high-frequency GPS vs. sparse scanner events).  
- Linking packages correctly when multiple packages share a stop.  
Standard techniques—event correlation rules, heuristic filtering of short GPS noise, and activity clustering—mitigate most of these issues.

**Process discovery**  
Apply discovery algorithms (Inductive Miner, Heuristics Miner, or Fuzzy Miner for high-variability logistics data) to the cleaned Vehicle-Day log. The resulting process map visualizes the actual end-to-end flow: depot departure → sequence of customer stops interspersed with travel arcs → possible failed-delivery loops or unscheduled maintenance detours → depot return. Frequency and performance annotations on arcs and activities immediately highlight dominant paths, rare deviations, and average durations.

**Conformance checking**  
Align the discovered model (or a normative model built from the dispatch system’s planned routes) against every case using token-based replay or alignment-based conformance. Key deviation types to quantify:  
- Sequence deviations (stops performed out of planned order).  
- Unplanned activities (extra stops, maintenance interventions, long idle periods not in the plan).  
- Timing deviations (actual travel or service times significantly outside planned windows).  
- Missing activities (planned stops never executed).  
Fitness and precision metrics, plus deviation frequency heat-maps, reveal systemic non-compliance.

**2. Performance Analysis and Bottleneck Identification**

**Relevant KPIs and calculation from the event log**  
- **On-Time Delivery Rate**: fraction of “Delivery Success” events whose timestamp falls inside the customer time window recorded in the dispatch data.  
- **Average Time per Delivery Stop**: mean duration between “Arrive Customer” and “Depart Customer”.  
- **Travel Time vs. Service Time ratio**: sum of inter-stop travel durations divided by total service durations per case.  
- **Fuel proxy / Vehicle Utilization**: distance derived from successive GPS points combined with ignition-on time; utilization = productive moving + service time / total shift duration.  
- **Traffic Delay Frequency & Duration**: periods where speed < threshold (e.g., 10 km/h) for > X minutes while between customers.  
- **Failed Delivery Rate**: “Delivery Failed” events / total attempts; re-delivery cost can be estimated by subsequent same-package events.  
- **Overtime Rate**: cases where “End Shift” exceeds contractual hours.  

All KPIs are computed by filtering, aggregating, and joining the enriched event log (standard process-mining performance spectrum or dashboard queries).

**Bottleneck detection techniques**  
- Performance spectrum analysis and dotted charts ordered by time-of-day or route ID reveal waiting or slow-moving intervals.  
- Bottleneck miners highlight activities or transitions with high mean/median duration and high variance.  
- Spatial enrichment of GPS points identifies recurring traffic hotspots.  
- Multi-dimensional filtering (by driver, vehicle type, day-of-week, weather proxy if available) isolates whether bottlenecks are route-specific, driver-specific, or temporal.  
Impact is quantified by the total waiting time attributable to each bottleneck and its correlation with downstream late deliveries or overtime.

**3. Root Cause Analysis for Inefficiencies**

Potential root causes and supporting process-mining analyses:  
- **Suboptimal static routing** → variant analysis comparing planned sequence fitness against actual sequences; high-frequency re-sequencing indicates poor initial planning.  
- **Inaccurate travel-time estimates** → compare planned travel arcs with actual GPS-derived durations stratified by time-of-day and corridor.  
- **Traffic congestion patterns** → correlate low-speed GPS segments with external traffic data or recurring time windows; delay heat-maps by hour and location.  
- **High service-time variability** → statistical distribution of dwell times per customer type or stop density; outliers often linked to parking difficulty or complex hand-overs.  
- **Unscheduled maintenance** → frequency and duration of “Unscheduled Stop / Engine Warning” events; survival analysis relating vehicle age/mileage (from cumulative GPS) to breakdown probability.  
- **Driver differences** → comparative process models and KPI dashboards per driver (after normalizing for route difficulty); skill or behavior clusters emerge.  
- **Failed deliveries** → process variants that contain “Delivery Failed” followed by same-day or next-day re-attempts; correlation with time-window tightness and customer notification status.  

These analyses validate or refute hypotheses by measuring effect sizes (time/cost impact) and statistical significance across the six-month log.

**4. Data-Driven Optimization Strategies**

**Strategy A – Dynamic re-routing engine informed by historical process variants**  
- Targets: sequence deviations and traffic-induced delays.  
- Root cause: static plans that ignore real-time conditions and historically observed congestion corridors.  
- Support: conformance and performance spectra identify high-impact corridors and typical delay magnitudes; these become cost coefficients in a real-time optimizer.  
- Expected impact: +8–15 % On-Time Delivery Rate, reduced Travel Time / Service Time ratio, lower fuel consumption through shorter effective routes.

**Strategy B – Predictive maintenance triggered by usage patterns**  
- Targets: unscheduled stops and vehicle downtime mid-shift.  
- Root cause: reactive maintenance after warning lights appear.  
- Support: process mining of maintenance events correlated with cumulative distance, idle time, and prior warning frequency yields remaining-useful-life models.  
- Expected impact: fewer mid-route breakdowns, higher Vehicle Utilization, reduced overtime and failed deliveries caused by vehicle swaps.

**Strategy C – Time-window and customer-communication redesign plus targeted driver coaching**  
- Targets: failed-delivery loops and high service-time variance.  
- Root causes: tight or poorly communicated windows and inconsistent driver practices at the stop.  
- Support: failed-delivery process variants and dwell-time distributions identify customer segments and driver clusters that drive most re-attempts; coaching focuses on the worst quartile of drivers while windows are widened or confirmed via SMS for high-risk stops.  
- Expected impact: lower Failed Delivery Rate (and associated re-delivery cost), reduced Average Time per Stop, improved On-Time rate.

**5. Operational Constraints and Continuous Monitoring**

All strategies respect hard constraints already present in the dispatch data: driver working-hour limits (including legal breaks), vehicle capacity and weight, and customer time windows. Dynamic routing treats these as hard constraints in the optimization model; predictive maintenance schedules interventions outside peak operating hours; coaching and window adjustments never force drivers beyond contractual limits.

**Continuous monitoring plan**  
Deploy a live process-mining dashboard refreshed daily (or near-real-time for GPS streams):  
- Process map with current fitness and most frequent deviations.  
- KPI trend lines (On-Time Delivery, Failed Delivery Rate, Travel/Service ratio, Overtime, Utilization) with statistical process-control limits.  
- Bottleneck heat-map (spatial + temporal).  
- Driver and vehicle scorecards normalized for workload.  
- Alerting on emerging high-variance variants or sudden drops in fitness.  

Periodic re-discovery (monthly) detects concept drift—new congestion patterns, seasonal demand shifts, or behavioral changes—so that the optimization models can be retrained. This closed-loop approach ensures improvements remain sustainable and new inefficiencies are detected early.
