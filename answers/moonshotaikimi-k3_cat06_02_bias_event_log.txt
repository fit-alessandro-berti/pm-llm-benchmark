## Where Bias Enters the Process

1. **Policy design (upstream of the log):** The rule granting "+10 (Community)" exists at all. It rewards membership in one specific named organization — the Highland Civic Darts Club — not community involvement generally. This is an institutional choice that bakes favoritism into the scoring model.

2. **PreliminaryScoring (Scoring Engine):** The +10 adjustment is applied automatically and exclusively to club members (C001, C004). No equivalent adjustment exists for any other attribute.

3. **ManualReview (Underwriter):** This step should function as a fairness control but doesn't. Reviewers see the score labeled "720 (Adjusted)" / "700 (Adjusted)" and appear to rubber-stamp it. The adjustment is never questioned, and review durations are roughly uniform across cases — no extra scrutiny, but also no corrective scrutiny.

4. **FinalDecision (Rules Engine):** The decisions cannot be explained by a single score threshold. C004 was approved at 700 (adjusted) while C003 was rejected at 715. This implies the rules engine either weighs LocalResident directly or applies a **lower effective cutoff for residents** — residents are approved at ~700+, while non-residents fall somewhere above a 715 bar (C005 needed 740).

## Attributes That Favor Certain Groups

- **CommunityGroup = Highland Civic Darts Club:** Worth +10 points — enough to move marginal applicants across the approval line (C004: 690  700). - **LocalResident = TRUE:** Associated with a more lenient effective threshold. Approval rates in the log: **residents 3/3 (100%) vs. non-residents 1/2 (50%)**.

Notably, neither attribute measures repayment ability. They measure social embeddedness and geography.

## Fairness and Equity Implications

- **Disparate treatment:** The +10 bonus is explicit differential treatment on a non-credit attribute. Even if framed neutrally as "community engagement," restricting it to one organization produces **disparate impact** on anyone outside that club's demographic profile.
- **Score validity is undermined:** Once adjustments are folded in, the score no longer represents credit risk — it represents credit risk *plus* social affiliation. Presenting it downstream as "720 (Adjusted)" obscures this from reviewers and decision-makers.
- **Cliff effects:** The bonus matters most at the margin (the 690–720 band), which is exactly where it flipped two decisions (C001, C004).
- **Gatekeeping feedback loop:** If membership visibly improves approval odds, the club becomes a de facto credit gatekeeper, and historical exclusion from the club translates into financial exclusion.

## Implications for Unaffiliated and Non-Local Applicants

An applicant like C003 — non-resident, unaffiliated — faces a structurally higher bar even with equal or superior creditworthiness. They are:
- Denied the +10 boost available to insiders,
- Subject to what appears to be a stricter decision threshold,
- Likely unaware that any of these adjustments exist, since the log shows no transparency mechanism.

The process effectively operates a **two-tier system**: one standard for locally connected applicants, a harder one for outsiders. Over time this penalizes newcomers, mobile populations, and anyone outside the dominant local social networks — groups that often overlap with protected classes.

1. **Remove or universalize the affiliation adjustment** — if community engagement is valued, credit all verifiable forms equally, not one named club.
2. **Separate the credit score from discretionary adjustments** and display the raw score at every stage, including FinalDecision.
3. **Audit the decision threshold by residency status** and run disparate-impact testing on approval rates.
4. **Make ManualReview a genuine checkpoint** — reviewers should explicitly evaluate whether adjustments, not just scores, are justified.
5. **Monitor outcome equity as a process metric** (approval rates segmented by residency and affiliation), since the bias here lives in the data attributes, not in process deviations — every case followed the identical activity sequence.
