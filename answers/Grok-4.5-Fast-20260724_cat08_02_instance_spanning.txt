**1. Identifying Instance-Spanning Constraints and Their Impact**

Process mining on the event log (with case ID = Order ID, activity, timestamps typed as START/COMPLETE, resource, and case attributes such as Order Type, Destination Region, Requires Cold Packing, Hazardous Material) enables formal discovery and quantification of instance-spanning constraints.

- **Shared Cold-Packing stations**: Filter cases where Requires Cold Packing = TRUE. Discover the packing subprocess and compute resource occupancy timelines for the limited Cold-Packing stations (C1–C5). Use interval algebra or a resource-centric event log projection to detect overlapping START–COMPLETE intervals across different cases on the same station. Quantify contention via average/median waiting time (time from ready-for-packing to actual START on a cold station), queue length distributions, and utilization. Impact metric: percentage of cold-order end-to-end time spent waiting for a cold station, and the resulting contribution to late deliveries.

- **Shipping Batches**: Identify the “Shipping Label Generation” activity and the Batch attribute (or reconstruct batches by grouping cases with the same Destination Region whose label-generation timestamps are nearly simultaneous). Compute inter-case waiting: for each order, the time from its own Quality Check COMPLETE until the batch’s label-generation event. Metrics: batch formation time, number of orders delayed solely by waiting for co-region orders, and the fraction of total cycle time attributable to batch synchronization.

- **Priority Handling (Express)**: Tag Express cases. Reconstruct preemption events by detecting when a standard order’s activity interval is interrupted (START without matching COMPLETE, or a long gap followed by a later COMPLETE after an Express order occupies the same resource). Metrics: number and duration of preemptions suffered by standard orders, extra waiting time injected into standard cases, and the net cycle-time benefit realized by Express cases.

- **Hazardous Material limit**: Count the number of concurrent cases that have Hazardous Material = TRUE and are simultaneously in Packing or Quality Check (by sliding-window concurrency analysis on the projected log). Measure the frequency and duration of periods when the concurrency reaches or is forced below the regulatory ceiling of 10. Impact metrics: throughput reduction (orders delayed or held upstream), additional waiting time caused by the concurrency throttle, and any compliance violations observed.

**Differentiating within-instance vs. between-instance waiting**  
Within-instance waiting is the residual sojourn time after subtracting pure activity duration (COMPLETE − START of the same activity) and after removing known resource contention or batch delays. Between-instance waiting is isolated by:  
- resource-availability timelines (waiting for a free cold station or for a preempted resource to be released),  
- batch-completion timestamps,  
- concurrency counters that enforce the hazardous limit.  
Techniques such as performance spectra, dotted charts colored by case attributes, and causal net / multi-instance Petri-net discovery make the distinction visible and quantifiable.

**2. Analyzing Constraint Interactions**

The four constraints are not independent:

- An Express order that also Requires Cold Packing jumps the cold-station queue, lengthening waits for ordinary cold orders and potentially starving the limited stations for longer periods.  
- Batching by region can concentrate multiple Hazardous orders into the same time window; if more than ten hazardous cases become ready for Packing/Quality Check simultaneously, the regulatory throttle forces some to wait even though the batch itself is ready.  
- Preemption of a standard order that occupies a cold station frees the station for an Express cold order but may later re-introduce the preempted order into a congested hazardous queue.  
- Dynamic batch-size decisions interact with cold-station availability: holding an order for a larger batch may keep a cold station occupied longer than necessary, reducing throughput for other cold orders.

Understanding these cross-effects is essential. Optimizing one constraint in isolation (e.g., always prioritizing Express) can degrade overall KPIs by amplifying another constraint. Multi-perspective process mining (resource + data + concurrency views) and correlation analysis between the derived waiting-time attributes reveal the dominant interaction patterns that any viable strategy must respect.

**3. Developing Constraint-Aware Optimization Strategies**

**Strategy A – Predictive Dynamic Allocation of Cold-Packing Stations**  
Primary constraints addressed: Shared Cold-Packing + Priority Handling.  
Changes: Maintain a real-time prediction model (trained on historical event-log features: arrival rate of cold orders, current queue lengths, Express probability) that forecasts cold-station demand for the next 30–60 min. Reserve a configurable number of stations for imminent Express cold orders; remaining stations are allocated by a weighted priority that balances Express urgency, remaining SLA slack, and predicted hazardous concurrency.  
Data leverage: Process-mining-derived waiting-time distributions and resource-utilization heat maps feed the predictor; online event streams update the model.  
Expected outcomes: Reduced cold-station waiting time (especially for Express), lower preemption frequency for standard cold orders, higher overall cold-order throughput while still respecting priority rules.

**Strategy B – Adaptive, Constraint-Aware Batch Formation**  
Primary constraints addressed: Shipping Batches + Hazardous Material Limits.  
Changes: Replace static regional batch thresholds with a dynamic trigger that considers (1) number of ready orders per region, (2) current global hazardous concurrency, and (3) predicted arrival of additional hazardous orders destined for the same region. A batch is released when either a size/time threshold is met *or* releasing it would prevent the hazardous concurrency from approaching the limit of 10. Small “express batches” can be formed for high-priority regions.  
Data leverage: Historical batch-size vs. cycle-time curves and concurrency time-series extracted from the log determine optimal trigger parameters.  
Expected outcomes: Shorter average batch-waiting times, fewer situations in which hazardous limits force extra holds, improved regional delivery reliability without violating safety rules.

**Strategy C – Multi-Constraint Scheduling Policy with Soft Capacity Buffers**  
Primary constraints addressed: all four, with emphasis on interactions.  
Changes: Introduce a central dispatcher that, at each decision point (order ready for Packing or Quality Check), solves a lightweight multi-objective assignment: maximize priority-weighted progress subject to (a) cold-station availability, (b) remaining hazardous slots (keep a safety buffer of 1–2 under the hard limit of 10), and (c) projected batch-completion windows. Minor process redesign: allow “pre-packing” of non-hazardous, non-cold items into temporary buffers so that the constrained resources are occupied only for the final, constrained steps.  
Data leverage: Discovered multi-instance process models and performance spectra supply the cost functions and constraint bounds.  
Expected outcomes: Simultaneous reduction in end-to-end cycle time, higher throughput under peak load, maintained regulatory compliance, and fewer cascade delays caused by interacting constraints.

**4. Simulation and Validation**

Construct a discrete-event simulation model whose control flow is taken from the process-mining discovery result (e.g., a Data Petri net or BPMN model annotated with the discovered decision logic). Explicitly encode:

- Finite cold-packing resources with preemption semantics for Express orders.  
- Batch formation queues keyed by Destination Region, with configurable release policies.  
- A global concurrency counter for hazardous cases that blocks further Packing/Quality Check starts when the limit is reached.  
- Stochastic activity durations, arrival processes, and attribute distributions fitted to the three-month event log (via process-mining performance analysis).

Run baseline (as-is) and scenario (what-if) experiments under identical random seeds. Key evaluation dimensions:  
- end-to-end cycle-time distributions and on-time delivery rate,  
- cold-station queue length and waiting-time statistics,  
- batch-waiting times and average batch size,  
- frequency and duration of hazardous-concurrency saturation,  
- number of preemptions and their impact on standard orders,  
- overall throughput and resource utilization.  

Sensitivity analysis on the policy parameters (reservation fractions, batch triggers, buffer sizes) quantifies robustness before any live deployment.

**5. Monitoring Post-Implementation**

Deploy a real-time process-mining dashboard (streaming discovery + performance analysis) fed by the live event stream:

- **Cold-packing view**: live queue length, average waiting time, utilization per station, Express vs. standard split.  
- **Batch view**: current open batches per region, formation time, number of orders waiting solely for co-region partners.  
- **Hazardous concurrency gauge**: instantaneous count of hazardous cases in Packing/Quality Check, time spent at or near the limit of 10, any near-violations.  
- **Priority impact panel**: preemption frequency, extra delay injected into standard orders, SLA attainment by Order Type.  
- **Aggregate KPIs**: end-to-end cycle time, throughput, on-time delivery percentage, and a composite “constraint friction” index that aggregates the four waiting-time components.

Alert thresholds (e.g., cold-queue > 5 for > 15 min, hazardous concurrency ≥ 9, batch-wait > historical 90th percentile) trigger immediate investigation. Periodic comparative process mining (as-is vs. post-change logs) quantifies whether the instance-spanning waiting times have decreased while compliance and throughput targets are maintained or improved.
