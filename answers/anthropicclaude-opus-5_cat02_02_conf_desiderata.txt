## 1. Normative (Hard-Rule) Violations

### Case 1 — No violations
Trace: `Application Received  Credit Check  Risk Assessment  Loan Decision (Approved)  Inform Applicant  Finalize Documentation  Close Case`

Every mandatory ordering constraint holds:
- Credit Check (09:30) < Risk Assessment (10:00) 
- Risk Assessment (10:00) < Loan Decision (11:00) 
- Inform Applicant (13:00) > Loan Decision (11:00) 
- Approved  Finalize Documentation (13:30) < Close Case (14:00) 
- No skipped or duplicated mandatory steps 

### Case 2 — Three distinct hard-rule violations
Trace: `Application Received  Risk Assessment  Credit Check  Loan Decision (Rejected)  Finalize Documentation  Close Case`

1. **Order reversal — Risk Assessment (08:20) precedes Credit Check (09:00).**
   This breaks the constraint *Credit Check  Risk Assessment*. Substantively, the risk assessment was performed without the credit information that is supposed to feed it, so the decision basis is questionable. Note that even though a Credit Check *does* eventually appear in the log, it appears *after* the assessment, so the constraint is not "repaired" by its later occurrence.

2. **Skipped mandatory activity — Inform Applicant is missing entirely.**
   The process goes from Loan Decision straight to Finalize Documentation and Close Case. The rule *Inform Applicant must occur after Loan Decision* is unsatisfied, and the "no skipping" prohibition is violated. This is arguably the most serious deviation: a rejected applicant was never notified of the outcome, which is likely a regulatory/consumer-protection exposure, not just a process-quality issue.

3. **Forbidden activity for the decision outcome — Finalize Documentation after a Rejection.**
   The rules explicitly state that on rejection the case must be closed *without* finalization. Executing Finalize Documentation at 10:00 is a contradictory action (documentation for a loan that does not exist). This also raises a data-integrity concern: were approval documents generated for a rejected application?

**Net effect:** Case 2 is non-conformant on ordering, completeness, **and** decision logic — i.e., all three prohibition categories listed in the norm.

### Case 3 — No violations
Trace: `Application Received  Credit Check  Risk Assessment  Loan Decision (Approved)  Inform Applicant  Finalize Documentation  Close Case`

The control-flow is identical to the ideal path. All ordering constraints and the approval-branch rule (Finalize Documentation 01-17 09:00 < Close Case 01-17 10:00) are satisfied. **Slow  non-compliant**: the delays in this case touch only the desiderata layer.

## 3. Plausible Interpretations and Justifications

### Case 2 — likely causes (none of which excuse the deviations)
- **Parallel/concurrent work with imprecise timestamping.** If risk analysts and the credit bureau interface run in different systems, the credit check may have been *initiated* first but only *recorded* at completion (09:00), while the risk assessment was logged at start (08:20). The "violation" could therefore be partly an artefact of start-vs-complete timestamp semantics. This is worth checking before treating it as willful non-compliance.
- **Fast-track / pre-screen rejection.** An obviously ineligible application (e.g., income far below threshold) may have been assessed immediately, with the credit check pulled afterwards only to document the file. This is a rational shortcut but still violates the norm — the fix is to define an explicit, sanctioned "pre-screen rejection" variant rather than to bend the standard path.
- **Automated template generation mistaken for "Finalize Documentation."** The rejection may have triggered an automatic letter/dossier-generation routine that the log maps onto the *Finalize Documentation* activity label. If so, the root cause is a **label/mapping problem**, and the remedy is to introduce a distinct activity (e.g., "Generate Rejection Notice") so that legitimate rejection paperwork is not indistinguishable from loan finalization.
- **Missing Inform Applicant.** Two benign explanations: (i) the applicant was informed verbally/by phone and the event was never recorded (a *recording* gap rather than a *behavioral* gap), or (ii) the notification was folded into the automated document step. Either way the log, as the system of record, cannot demonstrate that the applicant was informed — which is itself a defect.

### Case 3 — likely causes (fully benign)
- **Weekend and holiday effects.** 2024-01-12 was a Friday; the Credit Check landed on Sunday 01-14, and 01-15 was a Monday public holiday in some jurisdictions. A 49 h calendar delay may correspond to well under 24 h of *working* time. Similarly, informing the applicant on Tuesday 01-16 after a Sunday decision is only marginally late in business-hours terms.
- **Recommendation:** measure D1/D2 in **business hours against a calendar**, not raw elapsed time. Under that measure Case 3 might be fully compliant with the desiderata as well.
- **Third-party dependency.** Credit bureau turnaround, missing applicant documents, or manual underwriting review can legitimately stretch the credit-check window; the process owner does not fully control it.

### Case 1 and the missing Follow-up Call
The case completed in five hours. A follow-up call before closure adds little value when the whole lifecycle fits inside one business day and the applicant was informed only 60 minutes before closure. **Interpretation:** the omission is rational, not negligent — which illustrates why the follow-up call is correctly modelled as a desideratum rather than a rule.

## Recommended Actions

**To eliminate normative violations (mandatory controls):**
1. **Hard-block** Risk Assessment in the workflow engine until a completed Credit Check result exists for the case.
2. **Hard-block** Finalize Documentation when the recorded Loan Decision is "Rejected"; introduce a separate "Rejection Notice" activity for legitimate rejection paperwork.
3. **Make Close Case unreachable** until an Inform Applicant event is recorded, for both outcomes.
4. **Audit Case 2 retrospectively**: verify whether the applicant was actually notified, whether any approval documentation was erroneously issued, and whether the rejection decision is defensible given that the credit check post-dated the risk assessment. Consider re-decisioning the case.
5. Clarify **timestamp semantics** (start vs. complete) so that concurrency is not misdiagnosed as order reversal.

**To improve desiderata performance (steering, not blocking):**
6. Add non-blocking SLA timers with escalation alerts at 24 h (credit check) and 48 h (notification); measure in business hours.
7. Surface the optional Follow-up Call as a suggested task in the worklist before Close Case, with a documented "skip reason" for short-cycle cases like Case 1.
8. Report desiderata misses in a **separate KPI view** from compliance breaches, so that Case 3-type performance noise never dilutes attention to Case 2-type control failures.
