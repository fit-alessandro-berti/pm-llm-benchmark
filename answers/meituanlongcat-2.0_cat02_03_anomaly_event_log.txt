The standard Procure-to-Pay (P2P) process follows a tightly controlled sequence to prevent fraud, duplicate payments, and unauthorized spending. Key internal controls include:

- Segregation of duties: no single person should be able to raise a requisition, approve it, and pay the invoice.
- Approvals before commitments: purchase orders must be authorized before they are issued.
- Goods received verification: goods should only be accepted if backed by a valid PO.
- Three-way match: invoices are matched to the PO and goods receipt **before** any payment is released.
- Timely closing: cases are closed only after all financial transactions are complete.

Below are the anomalies detected in the event log, along with explanations of why they are problematic.

### Case 1002 – “Approval after the fact, payment without controls”

| Timestamp | Activity |
|-----------|----------|
| 08:30 | Create Purchase Requisition |
| 08:40 | Issue Purchase Order |
| 02-03 07:00 | Vendor Invoice Received |
| 02-03 07:15 | Release Payment |
| 02-03 08:00 | Approve Purchase Requisition |
| 02-04 09:00 | Receive Goods |
| 02-05 08:00 | Match Invoice to PO |
| 02-06 16:00 | Close Case |

**Anomalies:**
1. **PO issued before requisition approval** – The purchase order was created at 08:40, but the requisition was approved at 08:00 the next day. This violates the fundamental control that no purchase order may be issued without prior authorization, opening the door to unauthorized or fraudulent purchases.
2. **Payment released before goods receipt, before invoice matching, and before approval** – Payment was made at 07:15 on 0203, **immediately after receiving the invoice**, yet:
   - Goods were not received until 0204.
   - Approved requisition did not exist until 0203 08:00.
   - Invoice matching happened later on 0205.
3. **Postmatching** – The invoice was only matched **after** payment was sent, leaving the business exposed to overpayment (e.g., goods shortshipped or never delivered) without any verification.

**Why it’s problematic:** This sequence fails all three classic threeway match controls. The vendor could be paid for goods that never arrive, or paid an incorrect amount, with no prior approval to hold anyone accountable. It indicates a severe breakdown in procurement policy, often a red flag for fraudulent collusion or a lax controllership environment.

### Case 1003 – “Payment before any supporting document”

| Timestamp | Activity |
|-----------|----------|
| 02-02 09:00 | Create PR |
| 02-02 09:10 | Approve PR |
| 02-02 10:00 | Issue PO |
| 02-08 11:00 | Release Payment |
| 02-09 12:00 | Vendor Invoice Received |
| 02-10 13:00 | Receive Goods |
| 02-11 14:00 | Match Invoice to PO |
| 02-12 10:00 | Close Case |

**Anomaly:**
- **Payment released on 0208, before any invoice had been received (0209), before goods were received (0210), and certainly before matching (0211).** In effect, the company paid a vendor without an invoice.

**Why it’s problematic:** This is a classic “prepayment” or “advance payment” with zero documentation. At the moment of payment, no verification existed that the vendor had performed any obligation. Such payments are extremely vulnerable to fraud, intentional overpayment, or outright loss of funds. Accounting standards and strong internal controls require an invoice and, typically, proof of delivery before a payment is authorized. The subsequent receipt of an invoice on 0209 and matching on 0211 cannot retroactively justify the payment; the control failure has already occurred.

### Case 1004 – “Goods received before purchase order”

| Timestamp | Activity |
|-----------|----------|
| 02-05 10:00 | Create PR |
| 02-05 10:15 | Approve PR |
| 02-06 09:00 | Receive Goods |
| 02-06 09:15 | Issue Purchase Order |
| 02-07 08:00 | Vendor Invoice Received |
| 02-07 08:30 | Match Invoice to PO |
| 02-07 09:00 | Release Payment |
| 02-08 11:00 | Close Case |

**Anomaly:**
- **Goods were received on 0206 at 09:00, but the purchase order was issued only 15 minutes later at 09:15.** In a properly controlled goodsreceipt system, acceptance of delivery requires a valid PO number, so receiving goods before a PO exists is illogical.

**Why it’s problematic:** Receiving goods without a PO bypasses the key control that only preagreed items and quantities should be accepted. It suggests “maverick” buying—perhaps the materials were needed urgently and someone informally ordered them, then hastily created a paperwork trail afterward. This exposes the company to:
- Accepting unordered or incorrect goods.
- Prices or terms that were not prenegotiated.
- Difficulty performing a valid threematch, because the receipt event predates the PO it should reference.
While the rest of the case looks wellordered (invoice, match, payment), the initial sequence break weakens the entire audit trail and indicates that procurement policies were simply ignored at the warehouse/unloading point.
