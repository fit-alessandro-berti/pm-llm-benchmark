A typical controlled Procure-to-Pay flow is:

**Create requisition  Approve requisition  Issue PO  Receive goods  Receive vendor invoice  Match invoice against PO/goods receipt  Release payment  Close case**

Invoice receipt and goods receipt may occur in either order, but payment should normally follow invoice validation and, for goods purchases, a successful two- or three-way match.

| Case | Assessment | Anomalies and concerns |
|---|---|---|
| **1001** | **Normal** | The case follows the expected sequence. The requisition is approved before the PO is issued, goods are received before invoice matching, and payment is released only after matching. |
| **1002** | **Highly anomalous** | The **PO is issued before requisition approval**, indicating unauthorized or retrospective approval. **Payment is released immediately after invoice receipt**, before requisition approval, goods receipt, and invoice matching. This bypasses key controls and creates risk of paying for unauthorized, undelivered, incorrectly priced, or fraudulent purchases. The later match cannot function as a preventive payment control. |
| **1003** | **Highly anomalous** | **Payment occurs before the vendor invoice is received**, before goods are received, and before invoice matching. Unless this is explicitly governed as an approved advance-payment scenario, there is no documented liability or confirmation of delivery when funds are released. This creates substantial fraud, duplicate-payment, overpayment, and non-delivery risk. |
| **1004** | **Anomalous** | **Goods are received before the PO is issued.** This suggests a retrospective or “after-the-fact” PO, sometimes called a confirming PO. The organization may have committed to the purchase before formal authorization and commercial terms were established. Although invoice matching and payment subsequently occur in order, the procurement control was bypassed at the purchasing stage. |

### Key control violations

1. **Unauthorized commitment — Cases 1002 and 1004**
   - A PO should normally exist, based on an approved requisition, before goods are ordered or received.
   - Violations may indicate approval bypass, off-contract purchasing, or retrospective record creation.

2. **Premature payment — Cases 1002 and 1003**
   - Payment should generally occur only after an invoice has been received and validated.
   - For goods purchases, payment ordinarily follows confirmation of receipt and a three-way match among the **PO, goods receipt, and invoice**.

3. **Post-payment matching — Cases 1002 and 1003**
   - Matching after payment is detective rather than preventive.
   - Any quantity, price, tax, or vendor discrepancy is found only after cash has already left the organization.

Thus, **1001 is the only clearly conforming case**, while **1002 and 1003 contain severe payment-control failures**, and **1004 indicates retrospective PO creation or unauthorized purchasing**. Some variations could be legitimate under documented exceptions—such as approved prepayments or emergency procurement—but the log contains no activities showing such authorization.
