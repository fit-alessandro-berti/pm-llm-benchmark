Group B exhibits bias. Group A is the neutral / fair baseline.

In Group A - labeled the Protected Group - the process is consistent and merit-based:
`ApplicationReceived -> BasicScreening -> SkillTest -> PersonalityAssessment -> CulturalFitCheck -> FinalInterview -> HiringDecision`

At `CulturalFitCheck` the attribute `ScoreAdjustment = 0` for all cases. The resulting `CulturalFit` score is used as-is, and the `HiringDecision` appears to be based on those objective scores. 

> P001: Skill 85 / Personality 70 / Fit 65 = **Hired**
> P002: Skill 78 / Personality 75 / Fit 60 = **Not Hired**
> P003: Skill 82 / Personality 68 / Fit 65 = **Hired**

The hiring rule inferred is: Fit >= 65 required.

In Group B - the Unprotected Group - the control-flow is identical, but the **data perspective** of the process is different, which introduces discrimination.

### 1. How the bias manifests

**Location:** The bias is injected at the `CulturalFitCheck` activity, performed by Resource `HR Analyst`. There a new business rule is applied:

```
IF ProfessionalAssociation == "LocalBusinessLeadersClub"
THEN ScoreAdjustment = +5 AND CulturalFit = CulturalFit + 5
```

Evidence from the log:

* **U001:** base Fit 60 -> 60+5 = **65** Adjusted -> Hired
* **U003:** base Fit 58 -> 58+5 = **63** Adjusted -> Hired
* **U002:** no association, Fit 60 -> 0 -> Not Hired

Without the boost, both U001 and U003 would have failed the 65 threshold. In fact, comparing similarly qualified individuals shows direct violation of individual fairness:

* **P002 [protected] vs U001 [unprotected]:** Both have Skill ~78-80, Personality ~72-75, raw Fit 60. P002 is rejected, U001 is hired solely because of the club membership.
* **P002 vs U003:** U003 is *less* qualified - Skill 75 vs 78, Fit 58 vs 60 - yet is hired over P002 because of the +5.

This is a classic case of **proxy / indirect discrimination**:

1. `LocalResident` = FALSE for all protected cases, TRUE for all unprotected cases.
2. `ProfessionalAssociation = LocalBusinessLeadersClub` only appears for `LocalResident = TRUE`. In practice, non-locals / newcomers / migrants cannot join a LocalBusinessLeadersClub. 
3. Therefore a seemingly neutral attribute - club membership - is a perfect proxy for the protected attribute - residency status.

In process mining terms, this is **decision-rule discrimination**. If you run a decision-miner on the two logs you will discover two different rules for `CulturalFitCheck`. The log attribute `ScoreAdjustment` has distribution 0 in Group A and {0, +5} in Group B, highly correlated with protected status.

Group fairness metrics hide the problem if you only look at outcome rates: Both groups have 2/3 = 66.7% hired. But **conditional** on qualification, fairness fails:

* **Demographic parity** is artificially achieved by lowering the bar for Group B.
* **Equal Opportunity / Equalized Odds fails:** Among candidates who have raw Fit = 60, hiring probability is 0% for protected, 50% for unprotected.
* **Individual fairness fails:** Similar individuals are treated dissimilarly.

### 2. Implications for fairness and equity

**a) Disparate treatment and disparate impact:** Even if the +5 is not intentionally meant to exclude non-locals, it has disparate impact. In many jurisdictions - US Title VII, EU Directive 2000/78 - residency, nationality or social network can be a protected characteristic. Using club membership that is inaccessible to the protected group is unlawful indirect discrimination.

**b) Violation of meritocracy:** The process is no longer based on job-relevant criteria of Skill, Personality and CulturalFit. An extraneous, non-job-relevant factor determines outcome.

**c) Equity and feedback loop:** Local residents get jobs because they are in the local club, which gives them more local experience and network, which makes them more likely to stay in the club - a self-reinforcing loop. The protected group, regardless of merit, is systematically excluded from social mobility and integration.

**d) Process opacity:** Because the boost is encoded as a `ScoreAdjustment` and propagated to `FinalInterview` and `HiringDecision` as `65 (Adjusted)`, downstream decision-makers may not even realize bias occurred. The decision looks justified. Event log analysis is crucial to make this hidden rule visible.

**Mitigation from a process mining perspective:**

1. Remove `ProfessionalAssociation` and `ScoreAdjustment` from the decision model or audit it for job-relevance.
2. Blind the `CulturalFitCheck` to proxies.
3. Use discrimination-aware process discovery and conformance checking: compare decision rules learned from Group A vs Group B.
4. Apply fairness constraints: require same threshold for all groups for equal raw scores.

In short, Group A shows a fair, uniform procedure. Group B encodes a biased procedure that gives systematic advantage to the already-privileged local network, violating both individual and group notions of fairness.
