### Data Preprocessing and Integration

Transforming raw, disparate datasets into a unified event log requires structured extraction, transformation, and event abstraction pipelines.

```
Raw Sources (GPS, Scanner, Dispatch, Maintenance) 
   > Timestamp Standardization (UTC) 
   > Case ID Assignment (Vehicle-Day & Package ID) 
   > GPS Event Abstraction (Dwell & Speed Thresholding) 
   > Integrated Event Log
```

#### Dual Case Perspective
To capture macro operational efficiency and micro-delivery performance, the log must support dual-case keys:
* **Primary Case ID:** `Vehicle-Day` (e.g., `V12-20241205`) to analyze shift-level execution, route adherence, total fuel run-time, and vehicle utilization.
* **Secondary Case ID:** `Package ID` (e.g., `P9876`) to map individual parcel lifecycles from depot loading to final delivery or failure.

#### GPS Stream Event Abstraction
Raw GPS updates (emitted every 5–10 seconds) generate excessive noise for standard process engines. They must be abstracted into discrete process activities using spatial-temporal clustering:
* **`Stationary Idle`**: Speed $= 0 \text{ km/h}$ for $> 3 \text{ minutes}$ outside known depot or customer coordinates.
* **`Traffic Congestion Delay`**: Rolling average speed $< 8 \text{ km/h}$ across a known road segment for $> 5 \text{ minutes}$.
* **`Unscheduled Stop`**: Ignition off or vehicle stationary at a non-customer spatial node for $> 2 \text{ minutes}$.

#### Data Fusion & Schema Integration
Standardize timestamps to UTC across all edge devices (scanners, vehicle telemetry, dispatch servers). Align data schemas into an Object-Centric or flattened transactional format:

$$\text{Event Log Row} = f(\text{Timestamp}, \text{Case ID}, \text{Activity Label}, \text{Resource}, \text{Spatial Coordinate}, \text{Contextual Attributes})$$

| Target Field | Source System | Mapping Logic / Rules |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `Case_ID` | Dispatch / Fleet | Combine `Vehicle_ID` + `YYYYMMDD` |
| `Package_ID` | Scanner / Dispatch | Unique parcel tracking code |
| `Timestamp` | All | Standardized to ISO 8601 UTC |
| `Activity_Name` | Scanner / Abbreviated GPS | Direct scanner status OR Rule-based GPS abstraction |
| `Location` | GPS / Scanner | Lat/Lon geofenced to Depot, Customer, or Transit Zone |
| `Resource` | Dispatch / Fleet | `Driver_ID` + `Vehicle_ID` |
| `Cost_Attributes` | Telemetry / Dispatch | Instantaneous fuel rate, odometer distance, labor rate |

#### Key Data Integration Challenges
1. **Clock Drift:** Un-synchronized handheld scanner clocks relative to telemetry units create negative duration errors (e.g., `Delivery Success` recorded *before* `Arrive Customer`). 
   * *Mitigation:* Apply automated time-shift adjustments based on depot check-in ping syncs.
2. **Urban Canyon Interference:** GPS multipath propagation causes positioning loss or "position jumps" in dense city centers.
   * *Mitigation:* Apply map-matching algorithms (e.g., Hidden Markov Models) to map noisy coordinates to road networks.
3. **Missing Scans:** Drivers occasionally skip intermediate milestone scans (e.g., missing `Arrive Customer` and executing only `Delivery Success`).
   * *Mitigation:* Impute missing intermediate events through geofence entry pings detected by vehicle GPS.

### Process Discovery

Using robust discovery algorithms—primarily the **Inductive Miner** (to guarantee sound process models free of deadlocks) and the **Heuristics Miner** (to handle non-deterministic, noisy operational behavior)—we construct the end-to-end operational process map.

```
[Start Shift] > [Depot Load] > [Transit to Stop] > [Arrive Customer]
                                                                
  
                                                                                                                             
  v                                                                                                                           v
[Delivery Success]                                                                                                   [Delivery Failed]
                                                                                                                             
                             
                                                                                                                           
  v                                               v                                               v                           v
[Depart Customer]                       [Traffic Delay / Congestion]                     [Unscheduled Stop]              [Re-sequence Stop]
                                                                                                                           
                             
                                                                                                                             
  
                                                                
                                                                v
                                                     [Return Depot] > [End Shift]
```

#### Discovered Execution Pathways
1. **Happy Path (Nominal Flow):**
   $$\text{Start Shift} \rightarrow \text{Depot Load} \rightarrow \text{Transit} \rightarrow [\text{Arrive Customer} \rightarrow \text{Delivery Success} \rightarrow \text{Depart Customer}]^N \rightarrow \text{Return Depot} \rightarrow \text{End Shift}$$
2. **Deviated Operational Paths:**
   * **Failed Attempt Loop:** $\text{Arrive Customer} \rightarrow \text{Delivery Failed} \rightarrow \text{Re-sequence Stop} \rightarrow \text{Transit Deviation}$
   * **Vehicle Maintenance Interruption:** $\text{Transit} \rightarrow \text{Engine Warning Detected} \rightarrow \text{Unscheduled Stop} \rightarrow \text{Maintenance Dispatch / Swap} \rightarrow \text{Resume Route}$
   * **Mid-Shift Congestion Sink:** $\text{Transit} \rightarrow \text{Low Speed Detected (Traffic)} \rightarrow \text{Extended Idle} \rightarrow \text{Late Arrival}$

### Conformance Checking

Conformance checking contrasts actual execution traces (GPS/Scanner) against the target process model derived from the **Dispatch System Routing Plan**.

```
PLANNED ROUTE:  [Depot] > [Stop A] > [Stop B] > [Stop C] > [Stop D] > [Depot]
                                                     
ACTUAL TRACE:   [Depot]              [Stop B] > [Stop D] > [Depot]
                                                            
                                            [Stop C] 
                                                   (Delay)
                                 
                         [Unplanned Idle /
                          Traffic Detour]
```

#### Alignment Techniques
Using token-based replay and cost-based optimal alignments, we quantify the distance between planned sequence $S_P$ and actual sequence $S_A$.

#### Key Deviations Monitored
* **Out-of-Sequence Deliveries:** Drivers delivering Stop 12 before Stop 3 due to personal route familiarity or parking difficulties. 
  * *Impact:* Increases overall mileage and fuel consumption.
* **Unplanned / Unauthorized Stops:** Vehicle stationary at locations not associated with customer addresses or depot nodes for $> 5 \text{ minutes}$. 
  * *Impact:* Non-productive engine idling, operational leakage, increased labor costs.
* **Time-Window Violations (SLA Breaches):** Arrival events occurring outside customer-defined time windows ($T_{\text{actual}} > T_{\text{window\_end}}$).
  * *Impact:* Lower customer satisfaction, increase in expensive second-attempt re-deliveries.
* **Route Detours (Distance Deviations):** Spatial trace spatial displacement exceeded planned route path by $> 15\%$.
  * *Impact:* Unplanned fuel consumption and vehicle wear.

## 2. Performance Analysis and Bottleneck Identification

### Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

To evaluate delivery performance, we compute real-time operational metrics directly from the combined event log.

```
                                  PERFORMANCE METRICS ENGINE
                                              
     
                                                                                    
     v                        v                               v                        v
[On-Time Delivery]     [Service/Travel Ratio]         [Fuel Efficiency]         [Vehicle Utilization]
 (SLA Compliance)       (Stop Efficiency)            (Resource Overhead)        (Fleet Productivity)
```

#### 1. On-Time Delivery Rate (OTD %)
$$\text{OTD \%} = \left( \frac{\sum \text{Deliveries where } T_{\text{Arrive}} \le T_{\text{Window\_End}}}{\text{Total Planned Deliveries}} \right) \times 100$$

#### 2. Average Service Time per Stop ($T_{\text{Service}}$)
$$T_{\text{Service}} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N} (T_{\text{Depart Customer}, i} - T_{\text{Arrive Customer}, i})}{N}$$

#### 3. Travel vs. Service Duration Ratio ($R_{\text{T/S}}$)
$$R_{\text{T/S}} = \frac{\sum T_{\text{Transit Durations}}}{\sum T_{\text{Service Durations}}}$$
*High ratios indicate dynamic routing inefficiencies or overly dispersed delivery territories.*

#### 4. Fuel Consumption per Delivered Package ($F_{\text{pkg}}$)
$$F_{\text{pkg}} = \frac{\text{Total Liters Consumed per Shift}}{\text{Total Completed Deliveries}}$$

#### 5. Fleet Vehicle Utilization Rate ($U_{\text{Fleet}}$)
$$U_{\text{Fleet}} = \frac{T_{\text{Active Delivery Shift}}}{T_{\text{Available Shift Duration}}}$$

#### 6. Traffic Congestion Delay Index ($D_{\text{Traffic}}$)
$$D_{\text{Traffic}} = \sum \left( T_{\text{Low Speed Event}} \mid \text{Speed} < 8 \text{ km/h}, \text{Location} \notin \text{Stop Zone} \right)$$

#### 7. First-Time Failure Rate ($FR_{\text{First}}$)
$$FR_{\text{First}} = \left( \frac{\text{Count}(\text{Delivery Failed Events})}{\text{Total Delivery Attempts}} \right) \times 100$$

### Bottleneck Identification Techniques

Process mining isolates system bottlenecks by mapping duration metrics directly onto process graph nodes (activities) and edges (transitions).

```
[Depart Depot] (Avg 12 min) [Transit] (Avg 42 min: BOTTLENECK) [Arrive Customer]
                                                                                
                                                                           (Avg 8.5 min)
                                                                                
                                                                                v
                                                                        [Delivery Success]
```

#### Performance-Annotated Directly-Follows Graphs (DFG)
We project mean and 95th-percentile dwell times onto process transitions. Transition edges displaying high variance and elevated duration indicate systemic friction points (e.g., transition from `Transit` to `Arrive Customer` spiking between 08:00–10:00 AM).

#### Queueing & Wait-Time Decomposition
Deconstruct total cycle time into active working time vs. waiting time:
$$\text{Total Cycle Time} = \text{Drive Time} + \text{Service Time} + \text{Idle Delay Time} + \text{Depot Queue Time}$$

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                        TOTAL CYCLE TIME DECOMPOSITION                             |
+----------------------+----------------------+------------------+------------------+
|  Drive Time (45%)    |  Service Time (30%)  | Idle Delay (15%) | Depot Queue (10%)|
|  [Active Work]       |  [Active Work]       | [BOTTLENECK]     | [BOTTLENECK]     |
+----------------------+----------------------+------------------+------------------+
```

Process mining isolates non-value-added durations:
* **Depot Departure Bottlenecks:** Long delays between `Start Shift` and `Depart Depot` reveal poor parcel loading throughput at the warehouse.
* **Customer Access Bottlenecks:** Abnormally high service time ($> 12 \text{ minutes}$) clustered at high-density commercial or multi-family residential addresses.

#### Dimensional Slicing & Spatial Overlay
Filter process performance across key dimensions to localize root causes:
* **By Spatial Node (GIS Hotspots):** Overlaying transition delays onto GIS heatmaps isolates bottlenecked road corridors.
* **By Time-of-Day:** Pinpoints morning vs. afternoon rush-hour traffic impact on shift length.
* **By Vehicle/Driver Archetype:** Evaluates variance driven by driver operational behavior versus fleet asset constraints.

## 3. Root Cause Analysis for Inefficiencies

To address performance issues, we analyze underlying operational conditions rather than just symptoms.

```
                              INEFFICIENCY ROOT CAUSES
                                         
     
                                                                           
     v                        v                      v                        v
[Routing Inaccuracy]   [High Service Variance] [Fleet Maintenance Drift] [High First-Attempt Failures]
 - Static maps          - Parking friction     - Delayed repairs          - Broad time windows
 - Misjudged travel     - Multi-unit access    - Sensor alerts ignored    - Poor customer ETA alerts
```

### Primary Root Cause Drivers

#### 1. Suboptimal & Static Routing Schedules
* *Mechanism:* Dispatch plans rely on static, historical speed averages that fail to adapt to live urban congestion variations.
* *Process Evidence:* Conformance analysis reveals high sequence deviation rates ($>40\%$) as drivers manually alter assigned routes to avoid real-time traffic jams.

#### 2. Service Time Volatility at Complex Customer Nodes
* *Mechanism:* Dispatch systems assume uniform service duration (e.g., 3 minutes per stop) regardless of building access requirements (e.g., urban high-rises vs. single-family suburban homes).
* *Process Evidence:* Dwell-time distribution analysis shows extreme right-skewed tail variances ($3 \text{ to } 22 \text{ minutes}$) in high-density zip codes.

```
Frequency
  ^
  |  * (Suburban Target: 3m)
  | ***
  |*****
  |*****     * High-Rise Tail Variance (3m - 22m)
  |**************************************************> Service Time (min)
  0   3     8                  15                22
```

#### 3. Maintenance Failures During Active Delivery Shifts
* *Mechanism:* Ineffective preventative maintenance schedules cause engine faults during active delivery routes, triggering breakdown procedures.
* *Process Evidence:* Vehicle log alignment shows engine fault pings (`Engine Light On`) preceded by persistent idle-time spikes (cooling/exhaust issues) across prior shifts.

#### 4. Recurrent Delivery Failures (Customer Not Home)
* *Mechanism:* Wide delivery time windows (e.g., 4-hour blocks) without real-time tracking links lead to low customer presence rates.
* *Process Evidence:* Event log trace patterns display recurrent `Delivery Failed` events on specific routes, causing cascading delay sequences and mandatory return trips to the depot.

### Process Mining Validation Techniques

#### Variant Analysis (High vs. Low Performers)
Split delivery cases into top-quartile performance traces ($Q_1$, highest OTD) and bottom-quartile traces ($Q_4$, lowest OTD). Apply trace comparison tools to identify structural operational differences:

```
TOP QUARTILE TRACE (Q1):
[Depot Depart]  [Stop 1]  [Stop 2]  [Stop 3]  [Depot Return]  (Linear, Compact)

BOTTOM QUARTILE TRACE (Q4):
[Depot Depart]  [Stop 3]  [Traffic Jam]  [Stop 1]  [Failed Attempt]  [Detour]  [Stop 2]
```

* *Insight:* $Q_4$ traces show frequent sequence swapping and extended traffic delays. This confirms that static dispatch sequence models force drivers to make poor manual routing choices mid-shift.

#### Spatial-Temporal Correlation
Cross-examine GPS transit coordinates with spatial traffic data. Correlating spatial transit events against temporal slowdowns proves whether transit delays stem from global road congestion or individual driver routing choices.

#### Decision Tree Rule Induction
Train machine learning decision trees on process trace features to classify drivers susceptible to SLA failures:

$$\text{SLA Violation} \sim f(\text{Depot Launch Delay}, \text{Route Density}, \text{Service Time Variance}, \text{Planned Stop Count})$$

```
                         [Depot Departure Delay]
                               /        \
                        > 18 min        <= 18 min
                          /                  \
              [High Risk SLA Breach]   [Service Location Density]
                   (88% Late)                  /         \
                                        > 12 Stop/km2   <= 12 Stop/km2
                                            /                  \
                                     [Low Risk]        [Time Window Length]
                                    (5% Late)               /        \
                                                     < 2 Hours      >= 2 Hours
                                                        /                 \
                                              [Med Risk: 34% Late]   [Low Risk: 8% Late]
```

* *Finding:* Departure delays exceeding 18 minutes from the depot increase downstream customer SLA breach probability by $4.2\times$, regardless of driver speed on the road.

### Strategy 1: Dynamic Sequence Optimization and Real-Time Adaptive Dispatch

#### Targeted Inefficiency
High route transit times, recurring sequence deviations, and traffic congestion delays.

#### Root Cause Addressed
Static route planning models that do not account for live traffic conditions, time-of-day access constraints, or actual historical stop service durations.

#### Process Mining Evidence
Conformance checking reveals that drivers deviate from planned route sequences in $42\%$ of cases. These manual deviations increase total distance traveled by an average of $14.8\%$ per shift.

```
STATIC DISPATCH (Assumed Uniform Speed):
[Depot] 5km (10m) [Stop 1] 3km (6m) [Stop 2] 4km (8m) [Stop 3]
                                                  
                                          Actual Bottleneck
                                         (Rush Hour Gridlock)

DYNAMIC RE-ROUTE (Live Telemetry & Historical Process Speeds):
[Depot] 5km (10m) [Stop 1] 6km (11m) [Stop 3] 2km (4m) [Stop 2]
```

#### Detailed Action Plan
1. Replacing static dispatch matrices with dynamic Vehicle Routing Problem with Time Windows (VRPTW) solvers, incorporating process-mined historical travel times per road segment and time-of-day.
2. In-cab navigation systems dynamically update stop order based on dynamic traffic feeds and spatial proximity.
3. Stop time projections utilize process-mined location profiles (e.g., allocation of 12 minutes for multi-story towers vs. 3 minutes for suburban driveways).

#### KPI Impact Projections
* **On-Time Delivery (OTD):** $+18\%$ improvement.
* **Fuel Consumption per Package:** $-12\%$ reduction due to decreased transit distance and reduced engine idling.
* **Travel vs. Service Ratio ($R_{\text{T/S}}$):** Drop from $1.85$ to $1.25$.

---

### Strategy 2: Precision Delivery Time Windows and Interactive Geofenced Notifications

#### Targeted Inefficiency
High rates of first-attempt delivery failures (`Delivery Failed - Customer Not Home`) and excess dwell times from customer access delays.

#### Root Cause Addressed
Broad customer delivery windows (4–8 hours) and a lack of real-time visibility into driver proximity.

#### Process Mining Evidence
Trace analysis shows failed delivery attempts account for $11.4\%$ of all stops. Resolving failed deliveries requires an average detour time of $14.2\text{ minutes}$ and an additional re-delivery attempt on a subsequent shift.

```
                       GEOFENCED AUTOMATED CUSTOMER NOTIFICATION
                                           
  +----------------------------------------+----------------------------------------+
  |                                        |                                        |
  v                                        v                                        v
[Vehicle Crosses 3km Geofence]   [Automated SMS with Live ETA]   [Customer Confirms Presence]
                                                                                  
  ----------------------------------------+----------------------------------------
                                           
                                           v
                             [Precision Delivery Execution]
```

#### Detailed Action Plan
1. Implement a 2-tier notification engine triggered by vehicle GPS spatial geofences:
   * **Stage 1 (Day-Of):** Dynamic 60-minute delivery window notification sent to customer on shift start.
   * **Stage 2 (Proximity Trigger):** Automated SMS with live tracking link dispatched when driver crosses a $3\text{ km}$ spatial geofence radius.
2. Provide a "One-Touch Gate Release / Safe-Drop Authorization" option via the customer portal to eliminate access bottlenecks at multi-unit sites.
3. Automatically reschedule stops in real time if the customer signals non-presence via the app, skipping the stop before the vehicle detours.

#### KPI Impact Projections
* **First-Time Failure Rate ($FR_{\text{First}}$):** $-60\%$ drop in first-attempt failure rates.
* **Average Service Time ($T_{\text{Service}}$):** Reduced by $1.8\text{ minutes}$ per stop at commercial/multi-unit residential locations.
* **Customer SLA Compliance:** Punctuality increases to $>96\%$.

---

### Strategy 3: Usage-Based Predictive Maintenance and Fleet Health Guardrails

#### Targeted Inefficiency
Unplanned mid-shift vehicle breakdowns, high maintenance repair overhead, and elevated fuel consumption due to degraded fleet performance.

#### Root Cause Addressed
Maintenance schedules based on fixed calendar intervals rather than actual engine run-hours, high-idle wear profiles, or early warning sensor alerts.

#### Process Mining Evidence
Cross-referencing fleet maintenance logs with vehicle engine telemetry reveals that $73\%$ of unscheduled maintenance stops are preceded by multi-day patterns of continuous idle-temperature fluctuations and active diagnostic codes.

```
PREVIOUS REACTION MODEL:
[Minor Sensor Fault] (Ignored) [Mid-Shift Breakdown]  [3+ Hour Shift Interruption]

PREDICTIVE GUARDRAIL MODEL:
[Sensor Fault Detected]  [Process Alert Triggered]  [Overnight Depot Maintenance]
```

#### Detailed Action Plan
1. Integrate diagnostic trouble codes (DTCs) and engine performance metrics directly into the process mining event ingestion pipeline.
2. Establish dynamic maintenance triggers based on operating conditions (e.g., Engine Hours $> 250\text{ hours}$ or Continuous Idle Ratio $> 25\%$) rather than fixed calendar months.
3. Implement automated dispatch locks: Vehicles displaying persistent critical engine flags are automatically isolated from dispatch assignment until cleared by fleet service teams.

#### KPI Impact Projections
* **Mid-Shift Fault Events:** $-85\%$ reduction in unscheduled breakdown events.
* **Vehicle Utilization Rate ($U_{\text{Fleet}}$):** Increases by $+9.5\%$ due to higher fleet availability.
* **Maintenance Expenditure:** $-14\%$ savings by preventing catastrophic engine components failure via timely preventative repairs.

## 5. Operational Constraints and Continuous Monitoring

### Accounting for Operational Constraints

Optimization strategies must account for legal, contractual, and physical operational constraints to remain feasible.

```
                          OPTIMIZATION CONSTRAINT SOLVER
                                         
     
                                                                           
     v                        v                      v                        v
[Driver HOS Limits]    [Vehicle Payload]      [Fixed Customer SLAs]   [Territory Boundaries]
 (Max Shift Hours)      (Weight & Volume)     (Guaranteed Windows)    (Zoning Rules)
```

#### 1. Drivers' Hours of Service (HOS) & Labor Regulations
* Routes must cap total shift length at legal maximums (e.g., maximum 9-hour operational shifts including mandatory rest breaks).
* Process mining algorithms track real-time shift elapsed time; if predicted arrival at the depot exceeds $HOS_{\text{Max}} - 30\text{ minutes}$, the system triggers an automated stop offload to a backup vector.

#### 2. Vehicle Volumetric & Payload Capacity Limits
* Route generation algorithms evaluate parcel volume ($\text{m}^3$) and total weight ($\text{kg}$) against specific vehicle class constraints.
* Stops are dynamically assigned to match vehicle load thresholds:

$$\sum_{i=1}^{K} \text{Weight}_i \le \text{Capacity}_{\text{Max}} \quad \text{and} \quad \sum_{i=1}^{K} \text{Volume}_i \le \text{Volume}_{\text{Max}}$$

#### 3. Strict Customer SLA Time Windows
* High-priority or paid SLA windows (e.g., guaranteed 09:00 AM–10:00 AM delivery) are assigned hard constraints within the routing solver, prioritizing them over distance minimization pathing.

#### Core Process Control Dashboard Modules

* **Target Metric:** *Conformance Index* ($\ge 85\%$).
* **Alert Trigger:** If a vehicle drifts by $> 3$ consecutive stop sequences or takes a detour $> 20\%$ longer than planned, the system flags the trace for dispatch intervention.

##### 2. Live Bottleneck & Congestion Heatmap
* **View:** Integrates spatial GPS feeds to highlight urban corridors experiencing high transit durations or extended idle times.
* **Target Metric:** *Transit Delay Index* ($D_{\text{Traffic}} < 5 \text{ min/case}$).
* **Action:** Automatically updates routing profiles for vehicles entering affected spatial zones.

##### 3. SLA Breach Hazard Radar
* **View:** Machine learning-driven predictive algorithm calculating the probability of downstream SLA breaches for active shifts.
* **Target Metric:** *Predicted Late Delivery Risk*.
* **Alert Trigger:** Highlights stops with a $> 60\%$ probability of missing their SLA window, allowing dispatch to trigger automated parcel handoffs to nearby low-utilization vehicles.

##### 4. Vehicle Health & Idle Monitor
* **View:** Streams engine telemetry alerts (DTC pings, high coolant temps, low battery status) and non-productive idle events.
* **Target Metric:** *Non-Productive Idle Time* ($< 5\%$ of shift time).
* **Action:** Automatically removes vehicles displaying critical engine telemetry from the next day's dispatch roster for fleet maintenance.
