**Merged Event Log (Chronological Order)**

Events are ordered by **primary timestamp** (preferring Log A’s timestamps where available, as it is designated the primary timeline).  

For matched events: both timestamps are retained, all attributes from both logs are combined, event names are normalized (preferring Log A’s more readable form), and original names are preserved for traceability.  

Tolerance applied: ≤ 5 seconds (consistent with the prompt’s allowance for “a few seconds” of offset, plus semantic/name similarity and supporting notes).

### Reasoning & Matching Decisions

1. **Matching criteria used**  
   - Identical `order_id` (Order#1234) for every record.  
   - Temporal proximity (differences of 1–5 s). The prompt’s example of “less than 2 seconds” was treated as guidance rather than a hard cutoff; the broader context of “slightly offset by a few seconds or milliseconds” plus semantic equivalence justified the inclusive 5-second window.  
   - Clear name/activity similarity (Order Received ↔ OrderReceived, Order Validated ↔ OrderValidation, Payment Processed ↔ PaymentCheck, Item Shipped ↔ Shipping).  

2. **Why the Payment pair was matched despite a 5 s gap**  
   - Only payment-related events in either log.  
   - Log B’s notes explicitly reference a “Payment gateway delay,” providing a domain-plausible explanation for the offset.  
   - Leaving them separate would artificially invent two payment steps that do not exist in the process.  

3. **Unmatched events**  
   - **Quality Check** (B only) – no temporal or semantic counterpart in A.  
   - **Item Delivered** (A only) – no counterpart in B; Log B’s last recorded activity is the shipping hand-off.  

4. **Attribute integration & conflict resolution**  
   - All attributes from both logs are retained (enrichment).  
   - When timestamps differ, both are stored; Log A’s value is designated primary.  
   - No conflicting values existed for user_id, resource_id, or notes.  
   - Event names were normalized for readability while originals were kept for auditability.  

5. **Ordering**  
   Events are sorted strictly by the chosen primary timestamp, producing a coherent end-to-end process timeline that interleaves the unique Quality Check step between payment and shipping.  

The resulting log is a single, enriched, chronologically ordered record that preserves provenance, maximizes information from both source systems, and remains transparent about every matching decision.
