### Process Mining Consultancy Report: Speedy Parcels Optimization

**To:** Management Team, Speedy Parcels  
**From:** Lead Process Mining Consultant  
**Subject:** Comprehensive Analysis and Optimization Framework for Last-Mile Delivery Operations  

Based on the six-month event log data integrating GPS, scanner, dispatch, and maintenance records, the following approach outlines how process mining will uncover root causes of lateness and cost overruns, followed by actionable optimization strategies.

---

### 1. Process Discovery and Conformance Checking

#### Data Preprocessing and Integration
To perform process mining, raw heterogeneous data must be transformed into a structured **Event Log**.

*   **Case ID Definition:** The current `Vehicle-Day` format is too coarse for granular analysis. We will redefine the **Case ID** as `Route_Instance_ID` (linked to Dispatch System). This allows us to analyze the lifecycle of each planned route. Events from scanners and GPS will be mapped to these route instances. Package-level analysis can be handled as sub-processes or resource attributes within the route case.
*   **Timestamp Alignment & Granularity:**
    *   *Scanner Events:* Provide precise activity start/end times (e.g., `Arrive Customer` to `Depart Customer`). These are treated as atomic activities.
    *   *GPS Events:* Streaming data must be aggregated. We will segment GPS streams based on scanner events. For example, GPS data between `Arrive Customer` and `Depart Customer` defines the **Service Time**. GPS data between `Depart Customer_n` and `Arrive Customer_n+1` defines **Travel Time**.
    *   *Status Mapping:* GPS `Speed=0` combined with location proximity to a customer coordinate validates a "Stop." GPS `Ignition=Off` during a shift indicates unauthorized breaks.
*   **Challenges:**
    *   *Clock Skew:* Synchronization errors between handheld scanners and GPS units could misalign travel times. *Mitigation:* Apply statistical synchronization algorithms using overlapping events (e.g., matching the moment the scanner clicks "Arrive" with the GPS track slowing to 0 km/h).
    *   *Missing Context:* Scanner events may lack location context if GPS is intermittent. *Mitigation:* Impute missing GPS coordinates using nearest neighbor interpolation or map-matching algorithms.

#### Process Discovery
We will apply discovery algorithms (e.g., **Inductive Miner** or **Heuristics Miner**) to visualize the actual process.
*   **Output:** A Petri net or BPMN diagram showing the stochastic nature of operations.
*   **Expected Visualization:**
    *   Main path: `Depart Depot`  `[Loop: Travel  Arrive  Scan  Deliver  Depart]`  `Return Depot`.
    *   **Splits/Divergences:** Nodes for `Delivery Failed`, `Unscheduled Stop`, `Overtime Extension`.
    *   **Loops:** Explicit modeling of `Re-delivery Attempts` where a failed package loops back into the active route or is scheduled for the next day.
    *   **Parallelism:** Representation of multiple drivers operating simultaneously if cases are grouped by fleet rather than route.

#### Conformance Checking
We will compare the discovered model against the **Planned Model** derived from the Dispatch System.
*   **Technique:** Alignment-based conformance checking (e.g., using the Alignments algorithm) to compute a fitness score.
*   **Deviation Types to Detect:**
    *   *Sequence Deviations:* Drivers skipping stops or visiting them out of order to save time.
    *   *Timing Deviations:* Significant drift between planned arrival windows and actual scanner timestamps.
    *   *Unplanned Activities:* Occurrences of `Unscheduled Stop` or extended idle times not present in the plan.
    *   *Completion Deviations:* Routes marked complete in GPS but lacking scanner scans for assigned packages (potential fraud or missed deliveries).

### 2. Performance Analysis and Bottleneck Identification

#### Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)
Derived directly from the enriched event log:

| KPI | Calculation Logic | Business Value |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **On-Time Delivery Rate (OTDR)** | % of `Delivery Success` events occurring within the customer's requested time window. | Direct measure of customer satisfaction. |
| **Avg. Service Time per Stop** | Mean duration between `Arrive Customer` and `Depart Customer`. | Identifies efficiency at drop-off points. |
| **Travel-to-Service Ratio** | Total Travel Time / Total Service Time per route. | High ratio indicates poor routing or traffic issues. |
| **Failed Delivery Rate** | % of `Delivery Failed` events vs. Total Attempts. | Measures accuracy and re-delivery cost risk. |
| **Fuel Waste Index** | Aggregate GPS `Idle Time` + `Aggressive Driving` (high acceleration/deceleration) hours per km. | Proxy for fuel cost inefficiency. |
| **Vehicle Utilization** | Active Delivery Time / Total Shift Time. | Assesses productivity of assets. |
| **Rework Cost Indicator** | Frequency of `Delivery Failed` leading to `Re-attempt` events within 24h. | Quantifies cost of process failure. |

#### Bottleneck Identification Techniques
*   **Waiting Time Analysis:** Filter events to calculate waiting times in specific states. We expect high waiting times in `Travel` state due to traffic.
*   **Resource Usage Analysis:** Correlate KPIs with dimensions:
    *   *Drivers:* Identify top/bottom performers based on OTDR and Service Time consistency.
    *   *Routes/Segments:* Heatmap bottlenecks by geographic cluster. Identify specific intersections or zones causing `Low Speed Detected` events.
    *   *Time-of-Day:* Overlay delays with rush hour patterns.
*   **Quantification:** Use **Process Variation Analysis** to determine how much variance in OTDR is explained by traffic vs. driver behavior vs. route complexity. This quantifies the impact of each bottleneck on the bottom line.

### 3. Root Cause Analysis for Inefficiencies

Beyond symptoms, we will validate hypotheses using specific mining techniques:

*   **Suboptimal Routing & Static Planning:**
    *   *Analysis:* **Variant Analysis**. Cluster routes by performance. If high-performing variants consistently deviate from the planned sequence to avoid certain streets, the static planner lacks dynamic awareness.
    *   *Root Cause:* Dispatch uses historical averages that don't reflect real-time congestion or road closures.
*   **Traffic Congestion Patterns:**
    *   *Analysis:* **Cross-Attribute Filtering**. Correlate `Low Speed` GPS events with `Late Arrival` scanner events. Generate a heatmap of delay duration by location and time.
    *   *Root Cause:* Recurring bottlenecks at specific urban nodes are not accounted for in time estimations.
*   **High Service Time Variability:**
    *   *Analysis:* **Dwell Time Distribution**. Analyze the histogram of `Depart - Arrive` times. Look for long-tail outliers.
    *   *Root Cause:* Inconsistent customer readiness, difficult parking/access, or lack of standard operating procedures (SOPs) for scanning/loading.
*   **Vehicle Breakdowns & Maintenance:**
    *   *Analysis:* **Association Rule Mining**. Check correlations between `Unscheduled Stop` events and vehicle attributes (Age, Mileage, Type) or preceding GPS anomalies (e.g., speed drops, vibration spikes if available).
    *   *Root Cause:* Reactive maintenance culture; vehicles fail unexpectedly during shifts, causing delays and overtime.
*   **Failed Deliveries:**
    *   *Analysis:* **Pattern Matching**. Analyze notes in failed events. If "Customer Not Home" dominates, check if failures cluster around specific time windows or driver routes.
    *   *Root Cause:* Time windows are too broad, or customers are not receiving adequate notification/preparation.

### 4. Data-Driven Optimization Strategies

#### Strategy 1: Dynamic Routing with Traffic-Aware Time Windows
*   **Target Inefficiency:** Late deliveries, High Travel Time, Fuel Waste.
*   **Root Cause:** Static routing ignores real-time traffic; time windows are unrealistic.
*   **PM Insights:** Variant analysis reveals drivers manually detouring around specific streets. Heatmaps identify chronic congestion zones. GPS data provides accurate travel time distributions per segment.
*   **Proposal:** Integrate process mining insights into the routing engine.
    *   Replace fixed travel times with **probabilistic travel times** derived from historical GPS data.
    *   Implement **dynamic re-routing alerts**: If a driver falls behind schedule (detected via real-time scanner/GPS comparison against the expected path), the system suggests an optimized alternative path or advises rescheduling non-critical stops.
*   **Expected Impact:** +15% OTDR, -10% Fuel Consumption, Reduced Overtime.

#### Strategy 2: Predictive Maintenance & Fleet Health Management
*   **Target Inefficiency:** Unscheduled stops, High Maintenance Costs, Rework.
*   **Root Cause:** Breakdowns occur unpredictably; maintenance is reactive.
*   **PM Insights:** Analysis of `Unscheduled Stop` events shows patterns linked to vehicle mileage, age, or specific component warnings logged in notes. Correlation between high-idle-time driving styles and increased maintenance frequency.
*   **Proposal:**
    *   Develop a **Predictive Maintenance Model** using features extracted from the event log (e.g., duty cycles, harsh braking events from GPS, mileage accumulation rates).
    *   Shift from calendar-based to **usage-based maintenance**. Vehicles showing stress patterns are serviced proactively during off-peak hours.
    *   Flag "High-Risk" vehicles for immediate inspection before assigning critical routes.
*   **Expected Impact:** -25% Unscheduled Downtime, -15% Maintenance Costs, Improved Vehicle Availability.

#### Strategy 3: Service Time Standardization & Failed Delivery Reduction
*   **Target Inefficiency:** Variable Service Times, High Failed Delivery Rate.
*   **Root Cause:** Lack of SOPs, poor customer communication, inefficient parking/search.
*   **PM Insights:** Dwell time analysis shows significant variance across drivers and locations. Failed delivery notes indicate "Not Home" as primary cause.
*   **Proposal:**
    *   **Standardize Service Protocols:** Based on the median service time of top performers, define realistic SOPs for scanning, loading, and customer interaction. Train drivers on these standards.
    *   **Smart Time Windows:** Use process mining to analyze customer acceptance rates. Narrow time windows only where data supports high reliability; otherwise, use broader windows with SMS updates.
    *   **Proactive Notifications:** Trigger automated customer messages when the driver is 10 minutes away (derived from GPS location vs. customer location) to reduce "Not Home" failures.
    *   **Parking Optimization:** Identify locations with high `Arrive` to `Scan` gaps (likely searching for parking) and work with city management or provide drivers with parking guidance.
*   **Expected Impact:** -30% Failed Delivery Rate, +10% Stops per Hour, Improved Customer Experience.

### 5. Operational Constraints and Continuous Monitoring

#### Accounting for Constraints
Optimization proposals must respect hard operational constraints, which process mining helps characterize:
*   **Driver Working Hours:** The event log captures `Start Shift` and `End Shift`. We will enforce legal driving/rest limits in any re-scheduling algorithms. Process mining can highlight routes that consistently push drivers close to these limits, signaling a need for rebalancing.
*   **Vehicle Capacity:** Dispatch data includes capacity constraints. We will monitor `Load Factor` vs. `Stops Completed` to ensure capacity is utilized efficiently without compromising safety or speed.
*   **Customer Time Windows:** These are hard constraints in the dispatch system. Our dynamic routing strategy will treat these as priority filters; deviations are only permitted if they minimize overall penalty costs while respecting windows.

#### Continuous Monitoring Plan
Post-implementation, we will deploy a **Process Mining Dashboard** for ongoing governance:
*   **Real-Time Conformance Monitor:** Live stream of events comparing actual execution against the updated plan. Alerts trigger for significant deviations (e.g., route deviation >5 mins, unplanned stop >15 mins).
*   **KPI Trend Dashboards:** Track OTDR, Fuel Index, and Failed Rate over time, segmented by driver, region, and vehicle type.
*   **Drift Detection:** Automated alerts when process characteristics change (e.g., a new road closure causes a sudden spike in travel times in a specific zone, or a new driver joins with a different performance profile).
*   **Feedback Loop:** Monthly review sessions where process mining reports guide decisions on route adjustments, training needs, and maintenance schedules. This ensures optimizations are sustainable and adapts to seasonal variations or network changes.
