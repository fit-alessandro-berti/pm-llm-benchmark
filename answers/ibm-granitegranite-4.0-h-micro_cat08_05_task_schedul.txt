### Analyzing Historical Scheduling Performance and Dynamics

**Process Mining Application:**
To analyze the scheduling performance and dynamics of Precision Parts Inc., we begin by importing the MES event logs into a process mining tool capable of handling complex manufacturing workflows. The logs include timestamps, event types (e.g., job release, task start/end, setup start/end), resource assignments, operator IDs, priorities, due dates, and notes on disruptions. 

**Quantifying Key Metrics:**

1. **Job Flow Times and Lead Times:**
   - Employ process mining to reconstruct the actual flow of jobs through the shop floor. This involves mapping the sequence of events for each job, identifying start and end timestamps for each task, and calculating the actual flow time (time from job release to completion).
   - Use the mining algorithm to generate distributions of job flow times, which helps in understanding the variability and identifying outliers. Key metrics include average, median, and 95th percentile job flow times.

2. **Task Waiting Times:**
   - Calculate queue times for each work center/machine by analyzing timestamps between when a task is released and when it actually starts. Process mining allows us to visualize the queues and their lengths over time.
   - Metrics include average wait time per task, longest queue duration, and variability in queue times across machines.

3. **Resource Utilization:**
   - Assess productive time (actual task duration), idle time (time resources are available but idle), and setup time (duration of setup activities). Process mining's event logging captures these transitions, enabling a detailed breakdown.
   - Utilization rates are derived by comparing productive time to available time (total time between job arrivals and completions). This highlights underutilized resources or those running at full capacity.

4. **Sequence-Dependent Setup Times:**
   - Analyze the sequence of jobs processed on each machine to identify patterns in setup times. By clustering jobs based on their properties (e.g., material type, complexity), we can infer the impact of preceding jobs on setup durations.
   - Use statistical clustering and sequence analysis to quantify setup times and identify any consistent increases in setup time following specific job sequences.

5. **Schedule Adherence and Tardiness:**
   - Measure deviations from due dates using the timestamps of task completions and job due dates. Tardiness is calculated as the difference between completion time and due date, with negative values indicating early completion.
   - Tardiness rates per job, per machine, and overall shop floor provide a clear picture of schedule adherence. Frequency and magnitude of delays are quantified to identify systemic issues.

6. **Impact of Disruptions:**
   - Identify and quantify the impact of unplanned events like machine breakdowns and priority changes. Process mining allows us to trace how these disruptions cascade through the schedule, affecting downstream jobs and overall lead times.
   - Metrics include recovery time after disruptions, the frequency of such events, and their correlation with delays or changes in WIP levels.

### Diagnosing Scheduling Pathologies

**Identifying Pathologies:**

1. **Bottleneck Resources:**
   - Bottlenecks are identified by analyzing resource utilization metrics. Machines or operators consistently running at or near full capacity with high waiting times are flagged as bottlenecks. Process mining helps quantify the extent of these bottlenecks by showing their contribution to overall throughput loss.

2. **Poor Task Prioritization:**
   - By comparing on-time vs. late jobs, we can assess how effectively the scheduling logic prioritizes tasks. Jobs that frequently miss deadlines often have lower priority or are scheduled after higher-priority jobs, indicating a potential bias in the dispatching rules.

3. **Suboptimal Sequencing:**
   - Analyzing setup times reveals instances where suboptimal sequencing leads to increased total setup times. For example, if certain jobs consistently cause setup delays, this analysis highlights the need for resequencing or batching strategies.

4. **Starvation of Downstream Resources:**
   - By mapping the flow of resources and identifying periods where downstream machines are underutilized or starved, we can pinpoint scheduling decisions that contribute to such inefficiencies. Process mining reveals the extent and impact of these bottlenecks on overall throughput.

5. **Bullwhip Effect in WIP Levels:**
   - WIP fluctuations are analyzed to identify the bullwhip effect, where small variations in scheduling lead to disproportionately large changes in WIP levels. Process mining provides insights into the variability drivers, helping to stabilize WIP through better scheduling.

**Process Mining Techniques:**
- Bottleneck analysis using resource utilization metrics.
- Variant analysis comparing on-time vs. late jobs to identify scheduling logic discrepancies.
- Analysis of resource contention periods to understand how upstream scheduling decisions affect downstream resources.

### Root Cause Analysis of Scheduling Ineffectiveness

**Root Causes Identified:**

1. **Static Dispatching Rules:**
   - The current use of simplistic rules (First-Come-First-Served, Earliest Due Date) fails to account for dynamic factors like setup times, job priority, and machine load. Process mining reveals where these rules fail, such as when a low-priority job outpaces a high-priority one due to incorrect prioritization.

2. **Lack of Real-Time Visibility:**
   - Insufficient real-time data leads to poor decision-making. Process mining highlights the gap between scheduled and actual job progress, emphasizing the need for enhanced shop floor visibility.

3. **Inaccurate Duration Estimations:**
   - Historical data shows a mismatch between planned and actual task durations, suggesting inaccuracies in duration estimations. Process mining can quantify this discrepancy by comparing planned vs. actual durations, identifying factors influencing estimation errors.

4. **Handling Sequence-Dependent Setups:**
   - The current scheduling logic does not adequately account for sequence-dependent setups. Process mining uncovers patterns in setup times that indicate inefficiencies, such as repetitive setup sequences that could be optimized through intelligent batching.

5. **Inadequate Response to Disruptions:**
   - The ability of the shop floor to adapt to unplanned events (e.g., breakdowns, priority changes) is analyzed. Process mining shows how disruptions ripple through the schedule, underscoring the need for more adaptive scheduling strategies.

**Differentiating Causes:**
Process mining differentiates between scheduling logic issues and resource capacity limitations by:
- Quantifying the impact of each factor on schedule adherence and resource utilization.
- Comparing the frequency and impact of disruptions to baseline scheduling performance.
- Providing a detailed timeline of events leading to delays, allowing us to isolate root causes.
