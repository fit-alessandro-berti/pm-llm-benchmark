## 1. Normative-rule conformance

| Case | Status | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Compliant** | All mandatory activities occur in the required order. The approved branch correctly includes Finalize Documentation before Close Case. |
| **2** | **Noncompliant** | Multiple normative violations occur: Risk Assessment precedes Credit Check; Inform Applicant is missing; and Finalize Documentation is performed after a rejection. |
| **3** | **Compliant** | The required sequence is preserved. Because the loan is approved, documentation is finalized before closure. The timing delays do not violate the strict rules. |

### Case 1
The trace is:

Application Received  Credit Check  Risk Assessment  Approved Decision  Inform Applicant  Finalize Documentation  Close Case.

This follows the approved-loan path exactly. There are no normative violations.

### Case 2
This case has three hard violations:

1. **Risk Assessment occurs before Credit Check**  
   - Risk Assessment: 08:20  
   - Credit Check: 09:00  
   The later Credit Check does not repair the ordering violation.

2. **Inform Applicant is skipped**  
   The process moves from the rejected decision to documentation and closure without recording the mandatory notification.

3. **Documentation is finalized after rejection**  
   Under the rejected branch, the case must be closed without Finalize Documentation. This is explicitly prohibited.

Thus, Case 2 is the only case with clear normative nonconformance.

### Case 3
The ordering and branch logic are correct:

Application Received  Credit Check  Risk Assessment  Approved Decision  Inform Applicant  Finalize Documentation  Close Case.

Although some activities are delayed, delays alone are not hard violations under the stated rules.

### Case 1
The timing recommendations are met:

- Credit Check occurred 30 minutes after receipt.
- The applicant was informed 2 hours after the decision.

However, no Follow-up Call is shown. If the log is complete, this is a minor desideratum deviation, not a serious violation.

### Case 2
The Credit Check occurs within 24 hours, but its position after Risk Assessment is a separate normative problem.

No Inform Applicant event exists. This is more serious than merely missing the 48-hour recommendation: it violates the mandatory process sequence itself. A Follow-up Call is also not recorded.

### Case 3
This case has several soft deviations:

- Credit Check took approximately **49 hours**, exceeding the recommended 24 hours.
- Applicant notification took approximately **51 hours**, exceeding the recommended 48 hours.
- No Follow-up Call occurred.

These are suboptimal behaviors, but they do not invalidate the case’s normative conformance.

> If the explicit “No Follow-up Call” notation was intended to apply only to Case 3 and the logs for Cases 1 and 2 are incomplete, then only Case 3 can be definitively classified as missing the call. Based on a complete-log assumption, all three cases lack it.

## 3. Possible interpretations or justifications

These explanations may clarify the behavior, but they do not automatically remove recorded violations.

- **Case 1:** The process was completed quickly and the applicant had already been contacted, so staff may have considered an additional follow-up call unnecessary. This is acceptable as a soft deviation because the call is optional.

- **Case 2:** Possible data-quality or semantic explanations include:
  - The 08:20 “Risk Assessment” may actually have been preliminary triage rather than the formal assessment.
  - The Credit Check may have been completed earlier but recorded at 09:00.
  - The missing applicant notification may have occurred but not been logged.
  - “Finalize Documentation” may have referred to rejection or archival paperwork rather than approval documentation.

  Such explanations would require evidence, correction, or relabeling. With the events interpreted literally, Case 2 remains normatively noncompliant.

- **Case 3:** The delays could reflect weekend processing, external credit-bureau delays, a complex application, staffing constraints, or difficulty contacting the applicant. These are plausible operational reasons because the timing targets are recommendations rather than strict deadlines. The missing follow-up call may similarly reflect resource constraints or a judgment that prior communication was sufficient.

Overall, **Case 2 contains serious hard-rule violations**, while **Case 3 mainly demonstrates delayed and suboptimal—but normatively valid—processing**.
