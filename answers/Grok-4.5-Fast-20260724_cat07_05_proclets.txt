**This visualization illustrates the Proclets framework** (lightweight interacting processes) applied to a healthcare example involving laboratory visits and tests. Proclets model complex, fragmented real-life processes as collections of smaller, interacting process fragments rather than as one large monolithic workflow. They emphasize interaction (via messages called *performatives* sent over *channels*) and support one-to-many relationships between process instances.

The figure has three parts:

### (a) Two proclet classes connected through two channels
This is a process model (using YAWL-style notation) showing the life-cycles of two types of proclets:

- **Lab visit** (left side): The process for a patient lab visit. It starts with an order entry (by a doctor), taking a blood sample (by a lab assistant), judging results, possibly doing tests again, and eventually sending a report. There is a decision point (“Everything fine”) that can lead to finishing or looping back for more work. Ports (shown as circles or triangles attached to tasks) allow sending/receiving messages.

- **Lab test** (right side): The process for an individual lab test. It involves performing the chemical test, making a report, possibly performing the test again, providing an updated report, and finishing the lab test. Roles like “chemical analyst” are indicated.

The two classes interact via two **channels** (dashed boxes):
- **Order system**: Used to create/initiate lab test instances from a lab visit (e.g., ordering one or more tests after taking a blood sample).
- **HIS** (Hospital Information System): Used to exchange results and updates between lab tests and the parent lab visit (e.g., receiving results, receiving updated results, providing reports).

Ports have attributes for **cardinality** (how many recipients a message goes to, e.g., 1 or *) and **multiplicity** (how many times a port is used during a proclet’s lifetime, e.g., 1 or *). Arrows show the direction of performatives. This allows one lab visit to spawn and interact with multiple lab tests.

### (b) Class diagram containing the two proclet classes
A simple UML-style class diagram showing the relationship between the classes:
- A **Lab visit** *requires* one or more (1..*) **Lab test** instances.
- This captures the one-to-many relationship: one blood sample/lab visit can trigger multiple specific tests.

### (c) Example of a performative
A table showing a concrete message (performative) exchanged between proclets:
- **Time**: 11:00
- **Channel**: Order system
- **Sender**: Lab visit – John
- **Receivers**: Lab test – HGB John
- **Action**: Create
- **Content**: “Can you perform a HGB test for John?”
- **Scope**: Private
- **Direction**: OUT

This is an example of a creation message sent from a specific lab visit instance to instantiate a specific lab test (hemoglobin test for patient John).

**Overall purpose**: The figure demonstrates how Proclets “divide, interact, and conquer” complex processes. Instead of forcing everything into one rigid end-to-end model, related but independent life-cycles (lab visits and individual tests) run concurrently, communicate via well-defined channels and performatives, and handle varying cardinalities naturally. This is particularly useful for healthcare processes that involve multiple intertwined cases at different levels of granularity.
