The visualization is a **BPMN-style process diagram** showing an order-to-delivery workflow across four organizational lanes:

- **Sales**
- **Production**
- **Procurement**
- **Warehouse**

### Process flow

1. **Sales**
   - The process starts on **Monday morning**.
   - Sales checks the order for completeness and determines whether the customer already exists in the database.
   - If not, the customer is added to the database; otherwise, the process continues directly.
   - Sales then sends the order details to Production and Procurement.
   - After receiving confirmation that the product has been sent, Sales drafts an invoice and completes the process.

2. **Production**
   - Production receives the order details.
   - It plans production and waits for required materials.
   - Once planning and material availability are synchronized, the product is manufactured.
   - A **product complete** message is sent to the Warehouse.
   - If materials are faulty, a message is sent to Procurement and replacement materials are awaited.

3. **Procurement**
   - Procurement receives the order details and runs a **Material Compensation Subprocess** to order materials.
   - Materials are checked after arrival.
   - If satisfactory, they are sent to Production.
   - If there is a problem or cancellation, the flow can trigger material return or terminate the procurement process.

4. **Warehouse**
   - The Warehouse receives the **product complete** notification.
   - It sends the finished product.
   - A **product sent** confirmation is returned to Sales, enabling invoice creation.

Solid arrows represent internal sequence flows, while dashed lines represent messages exchanged between departments. Gateways, events, a database object, and a compensation subprocess model decisions, synchronization, communication, and exception handling.
