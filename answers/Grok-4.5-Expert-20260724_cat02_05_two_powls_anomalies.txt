**Model 1 is closer to a normative Hire-to-Retire process.**

### Standard/normative expectations
A typical Hire-to-Retire sequence requires:
- Post_Job_Ad before Screen_Candidates,
- Screen_Candidates before Conduct_Interviews,
- Conduct_Interviews before Make_Hiring_Decision (interviews must inform the decision),
- Make_Hiring_Decision before Onboard_Employee,
- Onboard_Employee before Add_to_Payroll,
- Add_to_Payroll before Close_Case,
with every activity mandatory, executed once, and no unnatural repetition or omission of employment-critical steps.

### Anomalies in POWL Model 1
The model is a single StrictPartialOrder containing all seven activities. The forced edges are:
- Post → Screen,
- Screen → Decide and Screen → Interview,
- Decide → Onboard → Payroll → Close.

**Anomaly (order violation between Interview and Decide):**  
Interview and Decide are unordered relative to each other. Both may therefore execute concurrently, or Decide may complete before Interview. This violates the logical requirement that interviews must precede (and inform) the hiring decision.  

Because the structure is a StrictPartialOrder, every activity is still mandatory and occurs exactly once; the only deviation is the missing precedence Interview ≺ Decide. All other ordering constraints and the absence of loops/skips match the normative flow.

### Anomalies in POWL Model 2
The model is a StrictPartialOrder whose nodes include two operators:
- `loop_onboarding = LOOP(Onboard, skip)` (silent transition),
- `xor_payroll = XOR(Payroll, skip)`.

Forced edges are Post → Screen, Post → Interview, Interview → Decide, Decide → loop_onboarding → xor_payroll → Close. Screen has no further successors.

**Anomalies:**
1. **Screening placement.** Screen is only constrained by Post; it may execute concurrently with, or even after, Interview/Decide. Screening is therefore not forced before interviews.
2. **Repeatable onboarding.** The LOOP operator permits Onboard to be executed once and then repeated any number of times (Onboard (skip · Onboard)*). Onboarding is not a one-time activity.
3. **Optional payroll (most severe).** The XOR with a silent transition allows the process to skip Add_to_Payroll entirely after onboarding.  

Omitting payroll after a positive hiring decision fundamentally breaks the “hire” semantics of a Hire-to-Retire process: an employee is onboarded yet never enters the payroll system. The extra loop further departs from normal practice.

### Why Model 1 aligns more closely
- Model 1’s single anomaly is a missing precedence between two activities that are otherwise correctly embedded in an otherwise normative chain; every step remains mandatory and occurs once.
- Model 2 introduces control-flow operators that permit (a) omission of a core employment activity (payroll) and (b) non-standard repetition of onboarding, both of which violate the essential integrity of the process more severely than a partial-order interleaving.

Consequently, despite the shared presence of deviations, Model 1 stays nearer the expected sequential logic and mandatory character of Hire-to-Retire.
