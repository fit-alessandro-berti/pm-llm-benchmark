## Finding

**Group B exhibits the clearest procedural bias.** Its hiring process gives members of the **LocalBusinessLeadersClub** an explicit **+5 cultural-fit adjustment**, while Group A receives no comparable adjustment.

## How the bias manifests

- **Unequal scoring rule:**  
  - Group A: all `ScoreAdjustment` values are 0.  
  - Group B: U001 and U003 receive +5 solely because of club membership.
- **Material effect on outcomes:**  
  - U003’s cultural-fit score increases from **58 to 63**, and U003 is hired despite having relatively low underlying scores (Skill 75, Personality 65, raw Cultural Fit 58).
  - By comparison, P002 is not hired with stronger underlying scores (Skill 78, Personality 75, Cultural Fit 60).
- **Potential proxy discrimination:** Group B is also characterized as local residents, while Group A is not. If club membership is more accessible to—or correlated with—the unprotected/local group, the boost functions as a proxy advantage that protected candidates are less likely to obtain.
- **Network-based favoritism:** Professional association membership may reflect social connections or access rather than job-related ability. Calling the adjustment “cultural fit” can obscure that preferential treatment.

## Fairness implications

The observed hiring rates are both **2 of 3 (66.7%)**, so this small sample does not show a difference in overall selection rates. However, equal aggregate outcomes do **not** establish fairness. The decision procedure is unequal: similarly situated candidates are not assessed under the same rules, and U003 appears to benefit decisively from the club-based adjustment.

This raises concerns about:

- **Procedural fairness:** candidates are evaluated differently based on affiliation.
- **Equity of opportunity:** candidates without access to the club cannot receive the boost.
- **Consistency and transparency:** the score adjustment may be difficult to justify as job-related.
- **Disparate impact or treatment:** especially if association membership tracks protected status, residence, socioeconomic position, race, or other protected characteristics.

## Conclusion

**Group B is biased because it explicitly privileges candidates affiliated with a particular professional network.** Group A’s lack of such an adjustment is more procedurally neutral, although the logs alone cannot prove that every other component—such as cultural-fit scoring—is unbiased. A fairer process would remove the association boost, use validated job-related criteria, apply identical scoring rules to all candidates, and audit matched candidates and selection rates using a larger dataset.
