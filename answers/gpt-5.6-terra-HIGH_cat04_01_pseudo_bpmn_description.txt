## Process Overview

This process manages a customer request from initial receipt through validation or feasibility analysis, possible approval, invoicing, and customer confirmation.

The request follows one of two main routes depending on whether it is a **standard** request or a **custom** request:

- A **standard request** undergoes standard validation and parallel operational checks, then receives a calculated delivery date.
- A **custom request** undergoes a feasibility analysis. If feasible, a custom quotation is prepared; if not, the customer is notified and the process ends.

Once a valid standard or custom request has reached the appropriate preparation stage, the process determines whether manager approval is required. The request may then be approved, rejected for rework, or sent directly to invoicing if approval is unnecessary.

---

## Step-by-Step Explanation

### 1. Start Event

The process begins when a customer request is received.

### 2. Task A — “Receive Customer Request”

This task records and acknowledges the incoming request. Typical activities might include:

- Capturing customer details;
- Recording requested products, services, quantities, and dates;
- Identifying whether the request is standard or requires customization;
- Creating an internal case, order opportunity, or request record.

After the request has been received, the process evaluates its type.

---

## Request Type Decision

### XOR Gateway — “Check Request Type”

This is an **exclusive gateway (XOR)**. It means that exactly one outgoing route should be selected, based on the request classification:

- **Standard request** route; or
- **Custom request** route.

The request should not normally follow both routes at the same time.

---

# Standard Request Path

### 3. Task B1 — “Perform Standard Validation”

If the request is classified as standard, the organization validates it against normal business rules.

This may include checking:

- Whether required information is complete;
- Whether the requested product or service is available in the standard catalog;
- Whether pricing, quantities, or terms comply with normal policies;
- Whether the customer account is valid;
- Whether there are obvious fulfillment restrictions.

The purpose is to ensure that the request is suitable for the routine processing path before operational checks are performed.

---

### 4. AND Gateway — “Run Parallel Checks”

After standard validation, the process enters an **AND gateway**, also called a **parallel gateway**.

Unlike the earlier XOR gateway, this gateway activates **all** outgoing paths. Both checks begin independently:

- **Task C1 — “Credit Check”**
- **Task C2 — “Inventory Check”**

The intent is to reduce total processing time by performing checks concurrently rather than sequentially.

#### Task C1 — “Credit Check”

The credit check assesses the financial risk associated with accepting the request. It may verify:

- Available customer credit;
- Outstanding invoices or overdue balances;
- Credit limits;
- Payment history;
- Whether prepayment is required.

#### Task C2 — “Inventory Check”

The inventory check determines whether the requested goods, materials, or service capacity are available. It may assess:

- Current stock levels;
- Reserved stock;
- Supplier lead times;
- Production or service capacity;
- Availability at the relevant warehouse or location.

---

### 5. Parallel Join — “All Parallel Checks Completed”

The two parallel paths converge at a corresponding **AND join**.

This join waits until **both** the credit check and inventory check have completed. The process cannot proceed until results from both branches are available.

This is different from an XOR merge: the parallel join is used because two branches were deliberately activated and both must finish before continuing.

---

### 6. Task D — “Calculate Delivery Date”

Once credit and inventory have been reviewed, the process calculates a delivery or fulfillment date.

This date would typically be based on:

- Stock availability;
- Production lead times;
- Shipping or logistics schedules;
- Customer location;
- Required customer delivery date;
- Capacity constraints;
- Any credit-related conditions that affect release of the order.

Task D represents the standard route’s final preparation activity before the request moves into the shared approval/invoicing portion of the process.

---

# Custom Request Path

### 3. Task B2 — “Perform Custom Feasibility Analysis”

If the request is classified as custom, it does not follow the standard validation and parallel-check route. Instead, it undergoes a more specialized feasibility analysis.

This task may involve evaluating:

- Technical feasibility;
- Design or engineering effort;
- Availability of special materials;
- Production capability;
- Required lead times;
- Compliance or regulatory requirements;
- Cost and profitability;
- Capacity and resource implications.

The purpose is to determine whether the organization can realistically fulfill the requested customization.

---

### 4. XOR Gateway — “Is Customization Feasible?”

This is another **exclusive gateway**. Based on the feasibility analysis, one of two outcomes is selected:

- **Yes — customization is feasible**
- **No — customization is not feasible**

Only one of these outcomes is allowed for an individual request.

---

### If Feasible: Task E1 — “Prepare Custom Quotation”

If the customization can be delivered, the organization prepares a quotation tailored to the customer’s request.

The quotation may include:

- Customized product or service specifications;
- Special pricing;
- Estimated delivery date;
- Engineering or setup fees;
- Required approvals or assumptions;
- Validity period;
- Terms and conditions.

This task is the custom equivalent of the standard route’s operational preparation stage. Once the quotation is prepared, the custom request can proceed to the common approval decision.

---

### If Not Feasible: Task E2 — “Send Rejection Notice”

If the customization is not feasible, the organization informs the customer that the request cannot be fulfilled as requested.

The notice may explain:

- That the requested customization cannot be supported;
- Technical, capacity, material, cost, or compliance reasons;
- Possible alternative standard products or services, if applicable.

This path goes directly to an **End Event**, meaning no quotation, approval, invoice, or confirmation is generated for the rejected request.

---

# Convergence of Standard and Custom Routes

After either:

- **Task D — Calculate Delivery Date** on the standard route, or
- **Task E1 — Prepare Custom Quotation** on the feasible custom route,

the process enters the shared downstream section.

The pseudo-BPMN describes this as:

> “After Standard or Custom Path Tasks Completed”

Because the standard/custom decision is an XOR decision, only one of those routes was taken. Therefore, this convergence should conceptually be an **XOR merge**, not an AND join.

An XOR merge does not wait for both routes; it simply allows the active route to continue. This is appropriate because a request cannot simultaneously be both standard and custom under the stated model.

---

## Approval Decision

### XOR Gateway — “Is Approval Needed?”

At this point, the process determines whether a manager or other authorized person must review the request.

Approval could be required based on rules such as:

- High order value;
- Low margin;
- Non-standard pricing;
- Exceptional discounts;
- Credit-risk concerns;
- Unusual delivery commitments;
- Custom product or service requests;
- Contractual or regulatory obligations;
- Significant operational or capacity impact.

This is an XOR gateway with two outcomes.

---

### If No Approval Is Needed

The process moves directly to:

### Task G — “Generate Final Invoice”

No managerial review is required, so the organization finalizes billing documentation.

The invoice may be based on the standard request details or the approved custom quotation details, depending on the route taken.

---

### If Approval Is Needed

The request enters the approval sub-process.

### Task F — “Obtain Manager Approval”

A manager reviews the request and decides whether it should proceed.

The manager may review:

- Customer and commercial terms;
- Pricing and discounts;
- Delivery date;
- Credit status;
- Profitability;
- Risks and exceptions;
- Feasibility assumptions;
- Custom quotation details.

---

### XOR Gateway — “Is Approval Granted?”

After review, this exclusive gateway selects one of two possible outcomes:

- **Yes — approval is granted**
- **No — approval is not granted**

---

### If Approval Is Granted

The process continues to:

### Task G — “Generate Final Invoice”

Once approval is granted, the invoice can be created using the final approved terms.

This is a shared task used by both:

- Requests that required approval and received it; and
- Requests that did not require approval.

---

### If Approval Is Not Granted

The process moves to:

### Task H — “Re-evaluate Conditions”

This task represents rework. The request is not necessarily cancelled; instead, the relevant conditions must be reviewed and corrected before another approval attempt.

Examples of rework may include:

- Revising the delivery date;
- Changing quantities;
- Adjusting pricing or discounts;
- Resolving credit concerns;
- Updating assumptions;
- Revising terms and conditions;
- Changing the custom design or scope;
- Recalculating operational feasibility.

After re-evaluation, the process loops back differently depending on the original request type.

#### For a Custom Request

The process loops back to:

- **Task E1 — Prepare Custom Quotation**

This makes sense because a custom request may need a revised quotation after changes to scope, price, lead time, technical requirements, or other commercial conditions.

After the quotation is updated, it returns to the approval decision.

#### For a Standard Request

The process loops back to:

- **Task D — Calculate Delivery Date**

For a standard request, the main variable likely requiring revision is the delivery commitment, potentially due to inventory, capacity, credit, or approval feedback.

After recalculating the delivery date, the request returns to the approval decision.

In a practical BPMN implementation, the model should preserve a flag or case attribute identifying whether the request is **standard** or **custom**, so the rework route can reliably return to the correct activity.

---

# Finalization

### Task G — “Generate Final Invoice”

The invoice is generated when either:

1. Approval was not required; or
2. Approval was required and granted.

This task formalizes the final commercial commitment. It may include:

- Final prices;
- Taxes;
- Payment terms;
- Delivery charges;
- Product or service details;
- Customer billing information;
- Reference to the approved quotation, where applicable.

---

### Task I — “Send Confirmation to Customer”

After invoicing, the organization sends a confirmation to the customer.

The confirmation may include:

- Request or order confirmation number;
- Confirmed product or service details;
- Final pricing;
- Delivery date;
- Invoice information;
- Payment instructions;
- Terms and conditions;
- Next steps.

This task closes the customer-facing processing portion of the request.

---

### End Event

The process ends after the confirmation has been sent.

There is also an earlier end point for custom requests that are determined to be infeasible and for which a rejection notice has been sent.

---

## Summary of Gateway Meanings

| Gateway | Type | Meaning in This Process |
|---|---|---|
| Check Request Type | XOR | Selects either the standard route or the custom route. |
| Run Parallel Checks | AND split | Starts both credit and inventory checks at the same time. |
| All Parallel Checks Completed | AND join | Waits until both credit and inventory checks finish. |
| Is Customization Feasible? | XOR | Selects either quotation preparation or rejection. |
| Standard/Custom convergence | XOR merge | Combines the mutually exclusive standard and feasible-custom routes before approval assessment. |
| Is Approval Needed? | XOR | Determines whether the request requires manager review. |
| Is Approval Granted? | XOR | Determines whether to proceed to invoicing or enter rework. |

---

## Key Differences Between Standard and Custom Requests

| Aspect | Standard Request | Custom Request |
|---|---|---|
| Initial processing | Standard validation | Custom feasibility analysis |
| Operational checks | Credit and inventory checks run in parallel | May involve technical, engineering, cost, and capacity analysis |
| Main preparation activity | Calculate delivery date | Prepare custom quotation |
| Potential early termination | Not explicitly shown in the model | Ends if customization is infeasible |
| Rework destination after rejected approval | Recalculate delivery date at Task D | Revise custom quotation at Task E1 |

---

## When Approval and Rework Are Triggered

### Approval is triggered when:
The business rules classify the request as requiring manager review, such as for high-value, exceptional, risky, discounted, custom, or non-standard requests.

### Rework is triggered when:
Approval is required but the manager does not approve the current conditions.

The rework step does not automatically reject the request permanently. Instead, it sends it back for revision:

- Back to **Task D** for standard requests; or
- Back to **Task E1** for custom requests.

The request can then go through the approval decision again, potentially repeating the approval/rework cycle until it is approved, no longer requires approval, or is handled through an additional cancellation or rejection process not shown in the pseudo-BPMN.
