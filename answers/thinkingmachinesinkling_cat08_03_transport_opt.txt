*   **Proposal:** A three-pronged intervention:
    1.  **Dynamic Service Buffers:** Adjust planned stop durations per customer/location based on historical process-mined dwell times (e.g., allocate 12 min for Apartment Complex B, 3 min for Residential House C).
    2.  **Real-Time Customer Notification:** Integrate scanner/GPS progress with an automated SMS/app system. When the driver departs the previous stop, send a "30-minute arrival" alert, reducing "Customer Not Home" failures.
    3.  **Failure-Reduction Protocol:** For addresses with >20% historical failure rate, require a pre-arrival confirmation call or shift delivery to a secured locker/partner point.
*   **Expected KPI Impact:** Reduce **Failed Delivery Rate**; reduce **Average Time per Delivery Stop** (lower variance improves schedule adherence); improve **OTD Rate**; reduce fuel and labor costs associated with re-delivery attempts.

### Strategy D: Performance-Based Territory Design and Driver Coaching
*   **Target Inefficiency:** Driver-dependent inefficiencies, uneven workload distribution, and excessive overtime.
*   **Root Cause Addressed:** Driver skill differences and poorly defined territories lead to some drivers facing disproportionate traffic/service-time burdens.
*   **Process Mining Insight:** Resource comparison shows `Driver D105` has consistently longer travel arcs and more failures despite similar routes. Geographic clustering of delays reveals that certain drivers are consistently assigned to high-bottleneck zones without adequate time allocation.
*   **Proposal:**
    1.  **Territory Optimization:** Redesign delivery zones using process-mined geographic clusters of stop density and travel-time connectivity—not just postal codes. Ensure zones have balanced aggregate service-time and travel-time loads.
    2.  **Targeted Coaching:** Generate individual driver dashboards showing specific inefficiencies (e.g., excessive idle at depot, frequent sequence deviations that do not improve outcomes, long parking-to-door times inferred from GPS clusters near customer locations).
*   **Expected KPI Impact:** Improve consistency of **Travel/Service Time Ratio** across drivers; reduce **Overtime Rate** through balanced loads; increase overall fleet **OTD Rate**.

## 5. Considering Operational Constraints and Continuous Monitoring

### 5.1 Integrating Constraints into Optimization
Any optimization must respect hard and soft operational boundaries visible in the event log:

*   **Driver Working Hours:** The log captures `Start Shift` and `End Shift`. Dynamic routing and territory redesign must include a real-time constraint engine: the sum of remaining planned travel and service times (using process-mined averages for those arcs) plus mandatory breaks must not exceed the legal maximum shift length. If a dynamic reroute risks overtime, the system should flag the dispatcher to split the remaining load or return to depot.
*   **Vehicle Capacities:** The dispatch data includes assigned packages per vehicle (`35 Stops Planned`). Real-time re-sequencing must check remaining package volume/weight (linked to `Package ID` scans) against vehicle capacity. A vehicle nearing capacity should not be dynamically assigned an additional pick-up or large-volume delivery unless it returns to a micro-hub.
*   **Customer Time Windows:** These are hard or soft constraints embedded in the dispatch system. The dynamic routing algorithm must treat hard windows (e.g., business delivery 09:00–11:00) as non-violable boundaries. Process mining can identify which windows are genuinely "hard" by analyzing failure/deviation patterns—if drivers consistently miss a particular window, it may be unrealistic given travel patterns, signaling a need for window renegotiation with the customer.
*   **Maintenance Windows:** Predictive maintenance (Strategy B) must integrate with the dispatch scheduler. A vehicle flagged for maintenance must be automatically excluded from route assignment for the next shift, with its load redistributed according to capacity constraints.

### 5.2 Continuous Monitoring Plan: Dashboards and Metrics
Post-implementation, process mining should shift from retrospective analysis to **continuous operational intelligence**.

**Monitoring Architecture:**

| Monitoring Layer | Key Metrics / Process Views | Purpose & Alert Triggers |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Process Conformance View** | Live fitness score of actual routes vs. updated planned model; frequency of new deviation types (e.g., a new unscheduled stop type). | **Alert:** Fitness drops below 0.85; emergence of a new loop/deviation not seen in the baseline 6-month log. |
| **Performance KPI Dashboard** | Daily/Weekly trends: OTD Rate, Failed Delivery Rate, Average Shift Duration, Unscheduled Stop Rate, Fuel Cost/Package, Overtime % per Driver. | **Alert:** OTD falls by >2% week-over-week; Unscheduled Stop Rate exceeds 1 per 10 shifts. |
| **Bottleneck Heatmap** | Geo-spatial overlay of `Travel` delays and `Traffic Delay` durations; time-series of dwell times per zone. | **Alert:** A zone’s average travel time exceeds the process-mined 80th percentile for 3 consecutive days, indicating a persistent new bottleneck (e.g., road construction). |
| **Resource Performance View** | Per-driver/per-vehicle comparison tables: Travel/Service ratio, sequence deviation rate, failure rate, overtime hours. | **Alert:** A driver’s case duration variance exceeds 1.5 standard deviations from the fleet mean, triggering coaching or route review. |
| **Predictive Health Monitor** | Real-time scoring of vehicles based on GPS-derived stress indicators (idle ratio, deceleration frequency). | **Alert:** A vehicle scores above the pre-failure pattern threshold; dispatch is notified to substitute the vehicle for the next shift. |

**Sustainability Mechanism:**
Conduct a **monthly Process Mining Review** comparing the current month’s event log against the pre-implementation baseline (the original 6-month log). Use statistical testing (e.g., t-tests on average shift duration, chi-square on failure rates) to validate that improvements in KPIs are statistically significant and not seasonal anomalies. If dynamic routing is implemented via A/B testing (pilot on 20% of vehicles), use variant comparison to prove that the pilot group achieves significantly better conformance and shorter travel arcs before fleet-wide rollout.

By embedding these monitoring layers into Speedy Parcels’ operational control tower, the organization transforms process mining from a one-time diagnostic tool into a **continuous feedback loop**—ensuring that routing, maintenance, and driver management strategies adapt as urban traffic patterns, customer behaviors, and fleet conditions evolve.
