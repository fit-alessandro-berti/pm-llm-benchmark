### How the bias manifests

The key biased factor is the **CommunityGroup attribute + ScoreAdjustment**:

- Every Group A applicant has `CommunityGroup = None` and receives **no score adjustment**.
- Two Group B applicants belong to `Highland Civic Darts Club` and receive **+10 points**.
- U002 also has `LocalResident = TRUE`, but because it has no community group, it receives **no boost**. This shows the advantage is tied specifically to community-group membership, not simply to residency.

So the system gives extra points to applicants who are members of a community group — and in this dataset, **only unprotected Group B applicants can be members of that community group**. Protected Group A applicants are structurally excluded from the boost.

### Systemic impact on final decisions

This can directly change who gets approved.

Example:

- **P002** (Protected Group): preliminary score **710**, no adjustment, final score **710**  **Rejected**.
- **U003** (Unprotected Group): preliminary score **695**, +10 adjustment, final score **705**  **Approved**.

So an unprotected applicant with a **lower preliminary score** is approved after receiving a boost, while a protected applicant with a **higher preliminary score** is rejected because they receive no equivalent boost.

This is a **disparate-impact bias**: even if the decision rule is applied consistently after scoring, the scoring process itself is not group-fair. The `LocalResident` and `CommunityGroup` attributes are highly correlated with the protected/unprotected group split, and they are used to give an advantage only to the unprotected group. If this adjustment is not justified by a valid, non-discriminatory business reason, it is a biased scoring practice that can systematically harm the protected group.
