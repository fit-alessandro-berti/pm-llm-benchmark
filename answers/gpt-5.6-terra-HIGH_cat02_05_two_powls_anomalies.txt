A normative successful-hire path would normally look like:

\[
\text{Post} \rightarrow \text{Screen} \rightarrow \text{Interview} \rightarrow \text{Decision}
\rightarrow \text{Onboard} \rightarrow \text{Payroll} \rightarrow \text{Close}.
\]

More completely, the hiring decision should usually branch:

- **Hire:** onboard employee, add to payroll, close the recruitment/onboarding case.
- **Reject / no hire:** close the case without onboarding or payroll.

## Model 1

### Behaviour implied by the model

The model enforces:

- `Post_Job_Ad  Screen_Candidates`
- After screening, both `Conduct_Interviews` and `Make_Hiring_Decision` are enabled.
- `Make_Hiring_Decision  Onboard_Employee  Add_to_Payroll  Close_Case`.

However, there is no dependency:

\[
\text{Conduct\_Interviews} \rightarrow \text{Make\_Hiring\_Decision}.
\]

Thus, after screening, the decision and interviews may occur in either order or concurrently.

### Anomalies

1. **A hiring decision may be made before interviews are conducted.**  
   This is the main anomaly. The model permits:

   \[
   \text{Post} \rightarrow \text{Screen} \rightarrow \text{Decision}
   \rightarrow \text{Onboard} \rightarrow \text{Payroll} \rightarrow \text{Close},
   \]

   with `Conduct_Interviews` occurring only afterwards.

   This contradicts the usual purpose of interviews: they should inform the hiring decision.

2. **The case may be closed before the interview occurs.**  
   Since `Conduct_Interviews` has no precedence relationship with onboarding, payroll, or closure, it could occur after `Close_Case` in a valid execution ordering. Semantically, conducting an interview after closing the hiring case is inconsistent.

3. **No explicit rejection path.**  
   Every case necessarily proceeds from decision to onboarding and payroll. If the model is intended to represent both successful and unsuccessful candidates, it should contain a choice after the decision, such as:

   \[
   X(\text{Onboard} \rightarrow \text{Payroll},\; \text{Close as rejected}).
   \]

   If the model is explicitly intended to represent only successful hires, this is less problematic.

### Severity

The missing `Interview  Decision` relation is significant because it can produce an uninformed hiring decision. Still, the process contains all required activities, and payroll is mandatory before closure. A relatively small repair—adding:

```python
model1.order.add_edge(Interview, Decide)
```

would restore the main normative ordering for successful hires.

## Model 2

### Behaviour implied by the model

The model enforces:

\[
\text{Post} \rightarrow \text{Interview} \rightarrow \text{Decision}
\rightarrow \text{Loop(Onboard)} \rightarrow X(\text{Payroll}, \tau)
\rightarrow \text{Close}.
\]

It also requires `Post  Screen`, but screening is otherwise disconnected from the decision path.

The onboarding loop is:

```python
loop_onboarding = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP, children=[Onboard, skip])
```

Under loop semantics, `Onboard` is executed at least once and may be repeated an arbitrary number of times. The silent transition is the redo part of the loop; it does **not** make onboarding optional.

The payroll XOR is:

```python
xor_payroll = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[Payroll, skip])
```

This makes payroll optional.

### Anomalies

1. **Interviews can occur before screening.**  
   Both `Screen_Candidates` and `Conduct_Interviews` are enabled after posting, with no order between them. Therefore, the model permits:

   \[
   \text{Post} \rightarrow \text{Interview} \rightarrow \text{Decision},
   \]

   while screening happens later.

   In a standard recruitment workflow, screening normally identifies suitable candidates before interviews are arranged.

2. **Screening is not required before the hiring decision.**  
   There is no path from `Screen_Candidates` to `Make_Hiring_Decision`. Hence a decision can be made, onboarding can happen, payroll can be completed, and the case can close before screening is performed.

   This is a major integrity problem: screening becomes an unrelated activity rather than a decision input.

3. **Onboarding can be repeated indefinitely.**  
   The loop permits:

   \[
   \text{Onboard} \rightarrow \text{Onboard} \rightarrow \text{Onboard} \rightarrow \cdots
   \]

   Rework may occasionally be realistic, but repeating the entire onboarding process without a specific rework condition is not a normative design. It can lead to duplicate accounts, duplicate orientation tasks, or repeated employee setup.

4. **Payroll is optional after onboarding.**  
   The XOR permits:

   \[
   \text{Decision} \rightarrow \text{Onboard} \rightarrow \text{Close},
   \]

   without `Add_to_Payroll`.

   This is especially severe. An employee who has been hired and onboarded but is not added to payroll cannot be paid and may violate legal, contractual, tax, or compliance requirements.

5. **No explicit rejection path.**  
   Like Model 1, Model 2 always executes onboarding at least once. The silent alternative applies only to payroll, not to onboarding. Therefore, it does not correctly model a rejected candidate.

### Severity

Model 2 has one positive feature: it enforces:

\[
\text{Interview} \rightarrow \text{Decision}.
\]

However, this advantage is outweighed by several major deviations:

- screening is not a prerequisite for interviewing or deciding;
- onboarding may be repeated unnecessarily;
- payroll may be skipped after hiring and onboarding;
- closure can occur despite incomplete employee setup.

---

## Which model is closer to the normative process?

**Model 1 is closer to a normative Hire-to-Retire process.**

Its primary problem is the missing precedence relation between interviews and the hiring decision. This is serious, but the overall backbone remains close to the expected flow:

\[
\text{Post} \rightarrow \text{Screen} \rightarrow
(\text{Interview and Decision incorrectly unordered})
\rightarrow \text{Onboard} \rightarrow \text{Payroll} \rightarrow \text{Close}.
\]

All core successful-hire activities are present and mandatory, especially payroll before closure.

By contrast, Model 2 introduces multiple structural issues:

- it detaches screening from the actual hiring decision;
- it permits interviews before screening;
- it allows repeated onboarding;
- it permits an employee to be onboarded but omitted from payroll.

The optional payroll step is particularly damaging because payroll enrollment is normally an essential consequence of a successful hire.

A minimal correction to Model 1 would be to add:

\[
\text{Conduct\_Interviews} \rightarrow \text{Make\_Hiring\_Decision}.
\]

A fuller correction would also add an explicit post-decision choice between a hire path and a rejection/closure path.
