**High-Level Process Steps (Aggregated from Low-Level Events)**

Based on consistent patterns across the sample cases (A1, B2), the low-level events group cleanly into **five coherent high-level manufacturing stages**. These groupings respect temporal clustering, resource transitions, and logical process phases.

### 1. Material Preparation
**Low-level events included:**
- Retrieve raw metal sheet
- Scan barcode
- Place metal sheet on table
- Align metal sheet
- Preheat metal sheet

**Rationale:**  
These form the initial setup phase. They are tightly clustered at the start of each case, primarily involve Operator A transitioning to automated equipment (Robot Arm #2 and Heating Unit #1), and all serve the single purpose of identifying, positioning, and thermally conditioning the raw material before any transformative work begins. This is a classic “material readiness” stage.

### 2. Welding
**Low-level events included:**
- Pick up welding tool
- Weld corner A
- Weld corner B

**Rationale:**  
These constitute the core joining/assembly activity. They are performed consecutively by the same resource (Operator B), and the tool pickup is an integral preparatory action for the subsequent welding operations. This is a distinct physical transformation phase that follows material preparation.

### 3. Weld Integrity Inspection
**Low-level events included:**
- Measure weld integrity

**Rationale:**  
This is an intermediate quality gate that occurs immediately after welding and uses a dedicated sensor (Quality Sensor #1). It is functionally and temporally distinct from the final visual check, allowing detection of welding-specific defects before the product proceeds to coating. Keeping it separate preserves important process-mining insights (e.g., intermediate vs. end-of-line quality performance).

### 4. Protective Coating
**Low-level events included:**
- Apply protective coating
- Dry coating

**Rationale:**  
These two events form a standard paired finishing sequence (application + curing). They are executed by specialized equipment (Coating Unit #3 and Heating Unit #2) after welding has been verified, and they serve the single purpose of applying and setting a protective surface layer.

### 5. Final Visual Inspection
**Low-level events included:**
- Visual check

**Rationale:**  
This is the terminal quality assurance step performed by a different operator (Operator C) after all manufacturing and intermediate quality activities are complete. It represents overall visual acceptance of the finished item and is temporally and functionally separate from the earlier weld-specific sensor measurement.

```
Material Preparation → Welding → Weld Integrity Inspection → Protective Coating → Final Visual Inspection
```
