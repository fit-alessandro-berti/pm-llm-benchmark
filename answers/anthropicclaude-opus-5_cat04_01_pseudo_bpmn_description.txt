## 1. What the process is, in one sentence

This is a **customer request-to-confirmation process** (a classic "quote/order handling" process): a request arrives, it is classified as *standard* or *custom*, it is validated or assessed for feasibility, an approval may be required, and if everything is in order a final invoice is produced and the customer is confirmed. If the request cannot be fulfilled, the process terminates early with a rejection.

Conceptually it has four phases:

| Phase | Purpose | Tasks |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Intake & classification** | Capture the request and decide how to treat it | Start Event, Task A, XOR "Check Request Type" |
| **2. Path-specific processing** | Verify a standard request, or assess and price a custom one | B1  (C1, C2)  D **or** B2  E1 / E2 |
| **3. Governance (approval)** | Apply a control step where risk/value warrants it | XOR "Is Approval Needed?", Task F, XOR "Is Approval Granted?", Task H |
| **4. Fulfilment of the commercial document** | Produce the invoice and inform the customer | Task G, Task I, End Event |

---

## 2. Step-by-step explanation

**Start Event** – A trigger from outside the organisation: a customer submits a request (portal form, e-mail, phone, EDI). It is a *message-type* trigger in practice.

**Task A – "Receive Customer Request"**
Registers the request in the system: customer identity, requested product/service, quantities, dates, special requirements. Output: a request record with a **request type attribute**, which is the data the very next gateway needs.

**Gateway (XOR) – "Check Request Type"**
An **exclusive (data-based) decision split**: exactly *one* outgoing branch is taken. The condition is the request classification:

* `Type = Standard`  catalogue item, known configuration, standard price  routine handling.
* `Type = Custom`  non-catalogue / engineered / modified item  requires engineering or technical judgment.

XOR here means the two branches are **mutually exclusive and never run together**.

---

### 2a. The Standard branch

**Task B1 – "Perform Standard Validation"**
Rule-based, largely automatable checks: is the customer record complete, is the product code valid, are quantities within limits, are the requested terms standard? No engineering judgment is needed.

**Gateway (AND) – "Run Parallel Checks"**
A **parallel split**: *both* outgoing branches are activated **simultaneously**. This is a deliberate design choice for speed — the two checks are independent and neither needs the other's result.

* **Task C1 – "Credit Check"** – Finance-side question: *can the customer pay?* (credit limit, open receivables, payment history, possibly an external credit bureau).
* **Task C2 – "Inventory Check"** – Supply-side question: *do we have or can we get the goods?* (on-hand stock, reservations, replenishment lead time).

**AND Join – "All Parallel Checks Completed"**
A **synchronising merge**: the token waits until *both* C1 and C2 are finished before the flow continues. This is the defining difference from XOR: nothing proceeds until every parallel branch has arrived. The overall duration of this block equals the *slower* of the two checks, not their sum.

**Task D – "Calculate Delivery Date"**
Uses the inventory/availability result (and implicitly the credit result) to promise a date: available-to-promise logic, production or picking lead time, transport time, calendar/holidays. Output: a committed delivery date — this is the *deliverable of the standard path* and, importantly, the **re-entry point for standard-path rework**.

---

### 2b. The Custom branch

**Task B2 – "Perform Custom Feasibility Analysis"**
A knowledge-intensive task, typically by engineering/technical sales: can the customisation actually be built, with what materials, effort, cost and lead time, and does it conflict with standards, capacity or regulations? Output: a feasibility verdict plus cost/effort estimates.

**Gateway (XOR) – "Is Customization Feasible?"**
An exclusive decision based on B2's verdict:

* **Yes  Task E1 "Prepare Custom Quotation"** – Convert the engineering assessment into a commercial offer: bespoke pricing, margin, validity period, terms, custom lead time. E1 is the *deliverable of the custom path* and the **re-entry point for custom-path rework**.
* **No  Task E2 "Send Rejection Notice"  End Event** – A **terminating (negative) outcome**. The customer is informed that the request cannot be met; no invoice, no confirmation. This is the only path that bypasses phases 3 and 4, and it is why the model has (at least) two end states.

---

### 2c. Convergence of the two paths

**"After Standard or Custom Path Tasks Completed"**
This is an **XOR (uncontrolled/exclusive) merge**: whichever single branch was taken (ending in Task D or Task E1) flows into the shared remainder of the process. Because the upstream split was XOR, only one token ever arrives here, so **no synchronisation is needed** — an AND join here would deadlock, waiting forever for a branch that was never activated. From this point on, standard and custom requests are treated identically.

---

### 2d. The approval / governance phase

**Gateway (XOR) – "Is Approval Needed?"**
A **policy-driven decision**, not a quality decision. Typical triggering conditions (not stated in the model, but this is what such a gateway encodes):

* order value above a monetary threshold;
* discount or margin outside policy;
* customer credit result marginal or credit limit exceeded;
* non-standard payment or delivery terms;
* custom path in general, or custom items above a certain complexity/cost;
* promised delivery date outside standard lead time.

Because it is XOR, the request either goes to approval **or** goes straight to invoicing — never both.

* **No  Task G** – Straight-through processing; the fast lane.
* **Yes  Task F "Obtain Manager Approval"** – A human decision task assigned to an authorised role (sales manager, finance, or per an authority matrix). It is a **control**, providing accountability and an audit trail.

**Gateway (XOR) – "Is Approval Granted?"**
Evaluates F's outcome:

* **Yes  Task G "Generate Final Invoice"**.
* **No  Task H "Re-evaluate Conditions"** – *Rework*, not rejection. Someone revisits the commercial or technical parameters that caused the refusal: reduce discount, change payment terms, require prepayment, adjust quantities, change the promised date, re-scope the customisation.

**The loop back**
After H, the flow returns to the point where the offending parameters were created:

* **Custom path  back to Task E1** ("Prepare Custom Quotation") — the quotation is re-priced/re-issued.
* **Standard path  back to Task D** ("Calculate Delivery Date") — availability/date commitment is recalculated.

The revised result then flows again through "Is Approval Needed?"  possibly F again. This is therefore a **rework loop with the potential to iterate multiple times**, and its exit condition is either (a) approval granted, or (b) approval no longer needed after the conditions change. Note that the loop **remembers which branch was originally taken** — the model needs the original request type as a routing variable (or, better, two separate loop arcs modelled explicitly per branch).

---

### 2e. Closing phase

**Task G – "Generate Final Invoice"** – Creates the binding commercial document (invoice or order confirmation with invoice) from the validated data: prices, taxes, delivery date, terms. Reached from three directions: no approval needed, approval granted, or approval granted after rework.

**Task I – "Send Confirmation to Customer"** – Sends the invoice/confirmation, closing the loop with the customer and handing over to downstream processes (fulfilment, accounts receivable).

**End Event** – The **positive** end state. The model has two end states in total: *rejected as infeasible* (after E2) and *confirmed* (after I).

## 3. Gateway cheat-sheet

| Gateway | Type | Split/Join | Semantics |
|---|---|---|---|
| Check Request Type | XOR | Split | Exactly one of Standard / Custom |
| Run Parallel Checks | AND | Split | Both C1 and C2 start concurrently |
| All Parallel Checks Completed | AND | Join | Waits for **both** before Task D |
| Is Customization Feasible? | XOR | Split | Quote (E1) or reject and end (E2) |
| After Standard or Custom | XOR | Merge | Passes the single active token through; no waiting |
| Is Approval Needed? | XOR | Split | Approval route (F) or straight to G |
| Is Approval Granted? | XOR | Split | Invoice (G) or rework (H  loop) |

Key contrast: **XOR = "one of", decision or simple merge; AND = "all of", concurrency and synchronisation.** The AND join is the only place in the process where the flow *waits*; the XOR merge never waits.

## 4. Standard vs. Custom: how the work differs

| Aspect | Standard request | Custom request |
|---|---|---|
| First activity | B1: rule-based validation | B2: engineering feasibility analysis |
| Nature of work | Deterministic, checklist/automatable | Judgment-based, expert knowledge |
| Concurrency | Yes — credit and inventory checks run in parallel | No parallelism modelled |
| Checks performed | Credit **and** inventory | Technical feasibility (credit/inventory not modelled — a likely gap) |
| Possible early termination | None modelled (even a failed credit or stock check has no explicit reject path — a gap) | Yes — infeasible  rejection notice  End |
| Key output artefact | Committed delivery date (Task D) | Custom quotation (Task E1) |
| Rework re-entry point | Task D | Task E1 |
| Typical duration/cost | Short, low-touch | Longer, involves specialists |
| Likelihood of approval being needed | Lower (standard terms) | Higher (bespoke pricing, non-standard terms) |
| Roles involved | Customer service, finance, warehouse | Engineering/technical sales, sales, finance |

---

## 5. When approvals and rework are triggered — summary of the logic

1. **Rejection** is triggered only by *technical infeasibility* on the custom path (gateway "Is Customization Feasible?" = No).
2. **Approval** is triggered by *policy conditions* evaluated after the path-specific work is complete — value, discount, credit, terms, delivery deviation. It is independent of request type, although custom requests will trigger it more often.
3. **Rework (Task H)** is triggered only by an *approval refusal*. It is a corrective loop, not an exit: the process re-does the pricing (E1) or the date commitment (D) and re-submits.
4. **Straight-through processing** occurs when no approval is needed: A  (path)  G  I  End.

## 6. Modelling observations / weaknesses worth flagging

If this pseudo-BPMN is to be turned into an executable model, these points need resolving:

1. **Failure outcomes on the standard path are missing.** What happens if the credit check fails or the item is unavailable? The model unconditionally proceeds to "Calculate Delivery Date". You would normally add an XOR gateway after the AND join with paths for *credit hold / backorder / reject*.
2. **The loop back is under-specified.** "Loop back to E1 or D" is a conditional routing decision that must be modelled explicitly — either an XOR gateway after H reading the request-type variable, or two distinct H tasks per branch.
3. **No loop-termination safeguard.** The rework loop could in principle cycle indefinitely. Add a maximum iteration count, an escalation to a higher authority, or an explicit "withdraw/reject offer" exit after N failed attempts.
4. **Task G appears twice.** In BPMN it should be a *single* activity with two incoming sequence flows (or an XOR merge before it), otherwise you duplicate logic and metrics.
5. **Custom path lacks credit/inventory checks.** Bespoke work usually carries *more* commercial risk, so omitting a credit check there is likely an oversight rather than a design intent.
6. **Task E2 does not lead through Task I.** That is defensible (rejection is its own communication), but it means "customer is always informed" is realised by two different tasks — worth consolidating for consistency and measurement.
7. **No roles/lanes, no data objects, no timers.** Adding lanes (Customer Service, Finance, Engineering, Manager), data objects (Request, Quotation, Invoice) and timer/escalation events on Task F (approval SLA) would make the model operational rather than merely illustrative.
8. **Terminology check:** "Generate Final Invoice" before the customer has accepted a *quotation* is commercially unusual on the custom path — normally quotation  customer acceptance  order  invoice. A missing "Customer Accepts Quotation" event may be an intentional simplification or a genuine modelling error.
