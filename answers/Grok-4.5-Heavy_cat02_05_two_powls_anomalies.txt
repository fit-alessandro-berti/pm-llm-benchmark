**Model 1 more closely aligns with a normative Hire-to-Retire process.**

### Standard / Normative Process
A typical Hire-to-Retire (or hire-to-pay / recruitment-to-onboarding) workflow has a clear logical backbone:
- Post job advertisement  
- Screen candidates  
- Conduct interviews  
- Make hiring decision  
- Onboard employee  
- Add to payroll  
- Close case  

Screening and interviewing are prerequisites to a sound decision; onboarding and payroll are mandatory, one-time (or non-arbitrarily-repeatable) steps that complete a successful hire; activities should not float freely after the case is closed or after later stages have already occurred. Both given models force a “hire” path after the decision (no explicit reject branch), so that shared trait is treated as baseline rather than a differentiator.

### Analysis of POWL Model 1
The model is a pure strict partial order:
- Post → Screen  
- Screen → Decide and Screen → Interview  
- Decide → Onboard → Payroll → Close  

Interview has no outgoing edges and is unordered with respect to Decide (and everything after it).

**Anomalies**
- **Severe – decision can precede or run concurrently with interviews.** After screening, Decide and Interview are concurrent. Valid linear extensions therefore include Decide before (or overlapping with) Conduct_Interviews. This violates core hiring logic: a hiring decision should rest on interview outcomes.
- **Severe – Interview is “dangling.”** Because Interview has no successors, it may legally execute after Decide, after Onboard, after Payroll, or even after Close. The activity is mandatory but completely detached from its intended place in the decision process, harming process integrity.

**Positive aspects.**  
The backbone Post → Screen → Decide → Onboard → Payroll → Close is fully present, correctly ordered, and forced. Every activity executes exactly once; nothing essential can be skipped. Screening correctly precedes the decision path.

### Analysis of POWL Model 2
Structure:
- Post → Screen and Post → Interview (so Screen ∥ Interview)  
- Interview → Decide  
- Decide → LOOP(Onboard, silent skip) → XOR(Payroll, silent skip) → Close  

Screen has no further constraints.

**Anomalies**
- **Severe – screening is essentially unconstrained after Post.** Screen may interleave anywhere after the job is posted, including after Interview, after Decide, after Onboarding, or even after Close. Valid executions therefore include a full hire (decision + onboarding + close) without prior screening.
- **Severe – payroll is optional.** The XOR with a silent transition allows the process to reach Close without ever adding the employee to payroll. For a path that has already decided to hire and performed onboarding, this omits an essential employment-setup step and breaks completeness of a successful hire.
- **Medium-to-severe – onboarding is repeatable.** The loop operator *(Onboard, silent)* requires Onboard to execute at least once and then permits zero or more additional executions via the silent redo path. Normative Hire-to-Retire treats onboarding as a one-time activity per hire case; arbitrary repetition is anomalous.
- Concurrent Screen and Interview after Post (no forced Screen before Interview) is a milder deviation; the Interview → Decide ordering itself is correct and is a strength relative to Model 1.

### Which Model Is Closer to Normative, and Why
Model 1 is closer overall.

- Model 1 localizes its problems to the placement of a single activity (Interview). The essential prerequisite chain (screening before decision), the post-decision sequence, and the mandatoriness of every step remain intact. Interview is still required; it is merely poorly ordered relative to Decide and the later stages.
- Model 2 introduces multiple independent, high-severity deviations: a free-floating foundational activity (Screen), a skippable essential post-hire activity (Payroll), and an unnatural loop that permits repeated onboarding. These more fundamentally compromise both **correctness** (prerequisite ordering for a valid hiring decision) and **integrity** (completeness of the steps required to finish a successful hire and close the case properly).

Although Model 2 correctly forces Interview before Decide—an important point that Model 1 lacks—this single strength is outweighed by the broader structural damage caused by the unconstrained Screen, the optional Payroll, and the repeatable Onboard. In terms of process correctness and integrity, preserving a complete, forced backbone (Model 1) is preferable to a model that allows essential steps to be omitted or performed out of any sensible order (Model 2).

Thus Model 1, despite its clear Interview-related anomalies, remains the closer approximation of a normative Hire-to-Retire workflow.
