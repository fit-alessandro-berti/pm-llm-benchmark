# High-Level Process Step Aggregation

## Analysis Approach

I examined the event sequences for both cases (A1, B2) and identified that the events follow a consistent pattern with natural breakpoints based on:
- **Functional cohesion** (events serving a common sub-goal)
- **Resource type transitions** (e.g., Operator  Robot  Heating Unit signals a phase change)
- **Temporal clustering** (tight timestamp groupings vs. gaps)
- **Object state changes** (raw material  aligned material  welded material  coated material  inspected product)

This produces **four high-level process steps**, each mapping cleanly to a manufacturing macro-phase.

---

## Proposed High-Level Activities

### 1. **Material Preparation**
**Low-level events grouped:**
- Retrieve raw metal sheet
- Scan barcode
- Place metal sheet on table
- Align metal sheet
- Preheat metal sheet

**Resources involved:** Operator A, Robot Arm #2, Heating Unit #1

**Rationale:**
These events all concern getting the raw material into a ready state for processing. They involve identification (barcode scan), physical staging (retrieval, placement), and conditioning (alignment, preheating). The step ends when the material is physically and thermally ready for joining — indicated by the resource switch from Operator A/Robot Arm to Operator B in the next phase. All events occur within a tight ~15-20 second window and act on the same physical object (the metal sheet) without altering its fundamental identity (it's still "a sheet," not yet "welded").

---

### 2. **Welding / Assembly**
**Low-level events grouped:**
- Pick up welding tool
- Weld corner A
- Weld corner B

**Resources involved:** Operator B

**Rationale:**
This is a clear functional unit: tool acquisition immediately followed by the joining operation itself. The same operator performs all steps, and the actions transform the material state (separate/aligned sheet  welded structure). This is the core value-adding transformation step in the process — where the actual assembly of the product occurs. Grouping is justified by single-resource continuity and direct dependency (you can't weld without first picking up the tool).

---

### 3. **Weld Quality Verification & Coating**
**Low-level events grouped:**
- Measure weld integrity
- Apply protective coating
- Dry coating

**Resources involved:** Quality Sensor #1, Coating Unit #3, Heating Unit #2

**Rationale:**
While this could arguably be split into two steps (verification vs. coating), I grouped them together because:
- The integrity measurement acts as a **gate check** immediately preceding the coating decision (in this log, coating is applied only after a passing/acceptable integrity score).
- All three events are **automated machine/sensor operations** (no human operator), suggesting a continuous automated sub-process on the production line.
- They act on the *same weld* as a single continuous treatment pipeline: assess  protect  cure.

*(Note: If business logic reveals that coating occurs regardless of integrity score, or if rejected welds are reworked, this should be split into "Quality Inspection" and "Surface Treatment" as separate steps — see alternative structure below.)*

---

### 4. **Final Inspection**
**Low-level events grouped:**
- Visual check

**Resources involved:** Operator C

**Rationale:**
This is a distinct terminal event performed by a human operator (not a sensor), representing a final human-in-the-loop quality gate before the product exits this stage of the process. It's separated from Step 3 because it involves a different resource type (human vs. machine) and appears to be a holistic, final-product-level check rather than a component-level (weld-specific) check.

---

## Alternative Structure (if stricter QA/Production separation is desired)

If your process modeling standards require strict separation of **production actions** from **quality actions**, consider this 5-step alternative:

| Step | Activities | Resource Type |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Material Preparation | Retrieve, Scan, Place, Align, Preheat | Operator/Robot/Heating |
| 2. Welding/Assembly | Pick up tool, Weld A, Weld B | Operator |
| 3. **In-Process Quality Check** | Measure weld integrity | Sensor |
| 4. **Surface Treatment** | Apply coating, Dry coating | Coating/Heating Units |
| 5. **Final Quality Inspection** | Visual check | Operator |

This separates automated sensor-based QA (Step 3) from human visual QA (Step 5), and isolates coating as its own value-adding treatment phase distinct from quality control.

## Recommendations for Validation on Full Log

1. **Check for branching:** Does "Measure weld integrity" ever lead to a rework loop (e.g., re-welding) instead of coating? If so, this confirms the QA/Coating split is needed.
2. **Check resource consistency:** Do other cases ever skip steps (e.g., no preheating)? This may indicate conditional sub-processes worth flagging as variants.
3. **Check timestamp gaps:** Large gaps between activities (e.g., >5 min) in the full log may indicate additional macro-phases (e.g., "Queue/Wait Time") not visible in this sample.
4. **Cross-case consistency:** Since A1 and B2 show near-identical patterns, this strongly supports that the proposed 4-step (or 5-step) template generalizes well — but should be tested against outlier cases (rejected parts, rework, etc.) in the full log.
