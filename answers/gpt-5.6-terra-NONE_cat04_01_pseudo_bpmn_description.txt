## Process Overview

This process handles a customer request from initial receipt through validation or feasibility analysis, possible approval, invoicing, and customer confirmation.

The main decision that shapes the workflow is whether the request is **standard** or **custom**:

- **Standard requests** follow a predefined validation process and include parallel operational checks.
- **Custom requests** require a feasibility assessment and may be rejected early if the requested customization cannot be delivered.

Once a request has progressed sufficiently on either path, the process determines whether managerial approval is required. If approval is granted—or not needed—the final invoice is generated and the customer receives confirmation. If approval is denied, the relevant work is re-evaluated and repeated.

---

## Step-by-Step Process Explanation

### 1. Start Event

The process begins when a customer submits a request.

This event triggers the workflow and creates the need to assess the nature, feasibility, commercial conditions, and fulfillment requirements of the request.

---

### 2. Task A — Receive Customer Request

**Purpose:**  
The organization receives and records the customer's request.

Typical activities could include:

- Capturing customer details
- Recording products, services, quantities, delivery requirements, or customization needs
- Assigning a request or quotation reference number
- Checking that enough information has been supplied to begin processing

The request then moves to a classification decision.

---

## Request Classification

### 3. XOR Gateway — Check Request Type

**Purpose:**  
This exclusive gateway determines whether the request is a **standard** request or a **custom** request.

An **XOR gateway** means that only one outgoing route is selected for a given request. The request cannot be both standard and custom at the same time under this model.

Possible outcomes:

- **Type = Standard**  standard validation path
- **Type = Custom**  custom feasibility path

---

# Standard Request Path

## 4. Task B1 — Perform Standard Validation

**Purpose:**  
For standard requests, the organization validates the request against known business rules, product availability conditions, customer eligibility rules, pricing rules, or standard service criteria.

Because the request is standard, the organization can use predefined validation logic rather than performing a detailed bespoke analysis.

Examples of standard validation may include:

- Confirming the requested item or service is part of the normal catalog
- Checking customer account status
- Verifying order quantities and minimum requirements
- Applying standard pricing or contractual terms
- Checking basic delivery restrictions

After standard validation, the process launches multiple checks at the same time.

---

## 5. AND Gateway — Run Parallel Checks

**Purpose:**  
The parallel gateway starts two independent activities simultaneously:

- **Task C1: Credit Check**
- **Task C2: Inventory Check**

An **AND gateway** means all outgoing branches are activated. Unlike the previous XOR gateway, this is not a choice between alternatives; both checks are required.

### Task C1 — Credit Check

**Purpose:**  
Assess whether the customer is financially eligible to proceed.

This could involve:

- Reviewing credit limits
- Checking unpaid balances
- Checking payment history
- Identifying whether prepayment or special terms are necessary

### Task C2 — Inventory Check

**Purpose:**  
Verify whether the requested standard products or materials are available, or whether they can be supplied within the required timeframe.

This could involve:

- Checking current stock
- Checking incoming supply
- Identifying shortages
- Confirming production capacity where relevant

---

## 6. Parallel Join — All Parallel Checks Completed

The process waits until both the credit check and inventory check have finished.

This is the matching synchronization point for the earlier AND gateway. It ensures that delivery planning is not performed until the organization has both:

1. Financial information about the customer, and  
2. Availability information about the requested items.

---

## 7. Task D — Calculate Delivery Date

**Purpose:**  
Determine a realistic delivery or completion date for the standard request.

The calculation may use:

- Inventory availability
- Warehouse processing time
- Production lead time
- Transportation schedules
- Customer location
- Service capacity
- Any outcomes from the credit and inventory checks

Task D represents the main completion point of the standard processing path before the request proceeds toward approval and invoicing.

---

# Custom Request Path

## 8. Task B2 — Perform Custom Feasibility Analysis

**Purpose:**  
For custom requests, the organization evaluates whether the requested product, service, configuration, or modification can realistically be delivered.

This differs from standard validation because custom work may require special design, engineering, sourcing, production, or service considerations.

Typical feasibility factors may include:

- Technical capability
- Engineering requirements
- Availability of specialized materials
- Production capacity
- Legal, regulatory, or safety constraints
- Cost and profitability
- Delivery time implications
- Customer-specific specifications

---

## 9. XOR Gateway — Is Customization Feasible?

This exclusive gateway determines whether the requested customization can be fulfilled.

Possible outcomes:

- **Yes**  prepare a custom quotation
- **No**  reject the request and end the process

Only one path is selected.

### If Yes: Task E1 — Prepare Custom Quotation

**Purpose:**  
Create a quotation tailored to the custom request.

The quotation may include:

- Customized product or service specifications
- Pricing
- One-time engineering or setup costs
- Delivery date or estimated lead time
- Assumptions and exclusions
- Terms and conditions
- Required approvals or dependencies

This is the custom-path equivalent of reaching a commercially actionable proposal.

### If No: Task E2 — Send Rejection Notice

**Purpose:**  
Notify the customer that the requested customization cannot be provided.

The rejection notice may explain, where appropriate:

- That the customization is technically infeasible
- That required materials or capacity are unavailable
- That the request does not meet regulatory or policy requirements
- Alternative standard options, if available

After the rejection notice is sent, the process reaches an **End Event**. No invoice, approval, or customer confirmation is generated for this rejected request.

---

# Convergence of Standard and Custom Paths

## 10. After Standard or Custom Path Tasks Completed

The successful standard and custom paths converge after their respective preparation activities:

- The **standard path** arrives after **Task D: Calculate Delivery Date**
- The **custom path** arrives after **Task E1: Prepare Custom Quotation**

At this point, both types of requests have reached a stage where the organization has enough information to decide whether management approval is necessary.

The rejected custom path does not converge here because it has already ended.

---

## 11. XOR Gateway — Is Approval Needed?

**Purpose:**  
Determine whether the request requires manager approval before final invoicing.

This is an XOR gateway because only one of two outcomes applies:

- **Approval is required**
- **Approval is not required**

Approval rules may be triggered by factors such as:

- High order value
- Large discounts
- Low-profit margins
- Extended payment terms
- Credit-risk concerns
- Non-standard commercial terms
- Custom work involving special risk or cost
- Long delivery commitments
- Exceptions to normal policy

### If No Approval Is Needed

The request goes directly to:

- **Task G: Generate Final Invoice**

This indicates the request meets standard rules, delegated authority thresholds, or automatic approval criteria.

---

# Approval Path

## 12. Task F — Obtain Manager Approval

**Purpose:**  
Submit the request, quotation, delivery commitment, pricing, and supporting information for managerial review.

The manager may evaluate:

- Commercial viability
- Customer creditworthiness
- Pricing and margin
- Operational capability
- Delivery commitments
- Risk exposure
- Compliance with internal policies
- Whether exceptions should be accepted

---

## 13. XOR Gateway — Is Approval Granted?

This gateway represents the manager’s decision.

Possible outcomes:

- **Yes**  proceed to final invoicing
- **No**  re-evaluate the request conditions and perform rework

### If Approval Is Granted

The workflow proceeds to:

- **Task G: Generate Final Invoice**

Approval confirms that the organization accepts the commercial and operational conditions of the request.

### If Approval Is Not Granted

The process goes to:

- **Task H: Re-evaluate Conditions**

---

## 14. Task H — Re-evaluate Conditions

**Purpose:**  
Review and revise the conditions that caused approval to be denied.

The exact rework depends on whether the request is standard or custom.

Possible rework actions include:

- Adjusting price or discount
- Revising delivery commitments
- Changing payment terms
- Reducing quantity
- Changing specifications
- Revising profitability assumptions
- Addressing credit concerns
- Updating resource or inventory assumptions

The rework task does not itself complete the request. Instead, it routes the process back to the appropriate earlier task.

---

# Rework Loops

## For Custom Requests: Loop Back to Task E1

If approval is denied for a **custom request**, Task H loops back to:

- **Task E1: Prepare Custom Quotation**

This is appropriate because custom requests often need their quotation, scope, technical assumptions, pricing, or delivery terms to be revised.

For example:

1. A custom quotation is prepared.
2. Management rejects it because the price does not cover engineering costs.
3. Conditions are re-evaluated.
4. The custom quotation is revised.
5. The revised request may then return through the approval decision flow.

In a fully detailed BPMN diagram, the revised quotation would normally be routed back into the approval decision cycle after rework.

---

## For Standard Requests: Loop Back to Task D

If approval is denied for a **standard request**, Task H loops back to:

- **Task D: Calculate Delivery Date**

This suggests that the main variable requiring revision for a standard request is the delivery commitment, likely due to capacity, inventory, urgency, service level, or commercial implications associated with the promised date.

For example:

1. Delivery is calculated based on current assumptions.
2. Approval is denied because the promised date requires expensive expedited shipping.
3. Conditions are re-evaluated.
4. A more realistic delivery date is calculated.
5. The revised request is assessed again for approval if needed.

In practice, re-evaluation might also affect pricing, terms, or quantities, but the pseudo-model identifies delivery-date calculation as the main standard-path rework point.

---

# Finalization Steps

## 15. Task G — Generate Final Invoice

**Purpose:**  
Generate the final invoice after either:

- Approval has been granted, or
- No approval was required.

The invoice reflects the final agreed conditions, such as:

- Products or services
- Customized specifications, if applicable
- Quantity
- Price
- Taxes
- Payment terms
- Delivery details
- Reference to the approved quotation or customer request

Task G is shared by both standard and custom requests. This is one of the major convergence points in the process.

---

## 16. Task I — Send Confirmation to Customer

**Purpose:**  
Notify the customer that the request has been accepted and processed.

The confirmation may include:

- Confirmation of the order or request
- Final invoice information
- Delivery or completion date
- Product or service details
- Payment instructions
- Any final terms and conditions
- Contact information for questions or changes

This task is performed after the invoice is generated, so the customer receives a finalized communication rather than only a preliminary quotation.

---

## 17. End Event

The process ends after customer confirmation has been sent.

At this point, the request has either:

1. Been successfully processed, approved if necessary, invoiced, and confirmed; or  
2. Been rejected earlier because a custom request was not feasible.

---

# Summary of Gateway Meanings

| Gateway | Type | Meaning in This Process |
|---|---|---|
| Check Request Type | XOR | Select either the standard path or the custom path. |
| Run Parallel Checks | AND | Start both credit and inventory checks simultaneously. |
| All Parallel Checks Completed | AND Join | Wait until both credit and inventory checks are complete. |
| Is Customization Feasible? | XOR | Decide whether the custom request can proceed or must be rejected. |
| Is Approval Needed? | XOR | Decide whether management approval is required. |
| Is Approval Granted? | XOR | Decide whether to proceed to invoicing or return for rework. |

---

# Main Differences Between Standard and Custom Requests

| Aspect | Standard Request | Custom Request |
|---|---|---|
| Initial analysis | Standard validation | Detailed feasibility analysis |
| Key operational checks | Credit and inventory checks in parallel | Technical, commercial, and operational feasibility review |
| Main output before approval | Calculated delivery date | Custom quotation |
| Early rejection possibility | Not explicitly shown | Yes, if customization is infeasible |
| Rework point after denied approval | Recalculate delivery date at Task D | Revise custom quotation at Task E1 |
| Typical complexity | Lower and more repeatable | Higher and more tailored |

---

# Conditions That Trigger Approval or Rework

## Approval Is Triggered When

Approval is needed when the request falls outside predefined authority or risk limits. Examples include:

- Request value exceeds an approval threshold
- Discount exceeds permitted limits
- Margins are below target
- Customer has elevated credit risk
- Delivery terms are unusually demanding
- The request contains non-standard commercial terms
- The request is highly customized or operationally complex
- The organization must accept unusual technical, financial, or contractual risk

## Rework Is Triggered When

Rework occurs only when:

1. Approval was required, and  
2. The manager did not grant approval.

The process then returns to the relevant preparation task:

- **Standard request:** revise conditions and recalculate delivery date.
- **Custom request:** revise the quotation and its terms, scope, price, or assumptions.

The request can then be reconsidered until it is approved, no longer requires approval, or is otherwise withdrawn or rejected under business rules not explicitly shown in the pseudo-model.
