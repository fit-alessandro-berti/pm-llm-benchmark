To achieve this in DuckDB, we can use window functions (specifically `LEAD`) to inspect sequences of activities and their corresponding timestamps within each case. 

Here is the SQL query that implements the logic:

```sql
WITH ordered_events AS (
    SELECT 
        case_id,
        activity,
        timestamp,
        -- Look ahead to the next two activities in the same case
        LEAD(activity, 1) OVER (PARTITION BY case_id ORDER BY timestamp, activity) AS next_activity_1,
        LEAD(activity, 2) OVER (PARTITION BY case_id ORDER BY timestamp, activity) AS next_activity_2,
        -- Look ahead to the timestamp of 'Confirm Order' (which is 2 steps ahead in the sequence)
        LEAD(timestamp, 2) OVER (PARTITION BY case_id ORDER BY timestamp, activity) AS confirm_timestamp,
        -- Specifically grab the timestamp of 'Create Order' for the time difference calculation
        -- (which is 1 step ahead in the sequence)
        LEAD(timestamp, 1) OVER (PARTITION BY case_id ORDER BY timestamp, activity) AS create_timestamp
    FROM event_log
),
target_cases AS (
    -- Identify cases that match the sequence and the > 5 days duration condition
    SELECT DISTINCT case_id
    FROM ordered_events
    WHERE activity = 'Approve Purchase Requisition'
      AND next_activity_1 = 'Create Order'
      AND next_activity_2 = 'Confirm Order'
      -- Calculate the time elapsed between 'Create Order' and 'Confirm Order'
      AND confirm_timestamp - create_timestamp > INTERVAL 5 DAYS
)
-- Return all original events for cases that are NOT in the target_cases list
SELECT 
    e.case_id,
    e.activity,
    e.timestamp
FROM event_log e
WHERE e.case_id NOT IN (SELECT case_id FROM target_cases)
ORDER BY e.case_id, e.timestamp;
```

### Explanation of the Query:
1. **`ordered_events` CTE**: Uses the `LEAD` window function partitioned by `case_id` and ordered by `timestamp` to look at consecutive events. This allows us to inspect a 3-event sliding window (`activity`, `next_activity_1`, and `next_activity_2`) and their respective timestamps in a single row.
2. **`target_cases` CTE**: Filters the windowed events to find cases where:
   - The exact sequence `'Approve Purchase Requisition'` $\rightarrow$ `'Create Order'` $\rightarrow$ `'Confirm Order'` occurs.
   - The time difference between `'Create Order'` (`create_timestamp`) and `'Confirm Order'` (`confirm_timestamp`) is strictly greater than 5 days (`> INTERVAL 5 DAYS`).
3. **Final `SELECT`**: Queries the original `event_log` table and excludes (`NOT IN`) any `case_id` identified in the `target_cases` CTE, returning all other events ordered by case and time.
