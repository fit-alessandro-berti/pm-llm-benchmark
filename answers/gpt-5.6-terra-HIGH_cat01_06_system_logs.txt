### Case-grouping and activity-mapping logic

Cases were defined primarily by the business artifact being handled:

- **`DOC-DOCUMENT1-001`**: Creation and completion of `Document1.docx`.
- **`DOC-QUARTERLY-001`**: Work on `Quarterly_Report.docx`, including an initial access, interruption, later drafting, saving, and closure.
- **`EMAIL-ANNUAL-MEETING-001`**: Reading and responding to the identifiable Annual Meeting email.
- **`PDF-REPORT-DRAFT-001`**: Reviewing and annotating `Report_Draft.pdf`.
- **`XLS-BUDGET-2024-001`**: Updating and saving `Budget_2024.xlsx`.

Low-level technical actions were translated into business-oriented activities. For example, `TYPING` became activities such as **Draft introductory content**, **Update Q1 budget figures**, or **Draft email response**, using the available typed-text context. `SCROLL` was interpreted as a review activity, while `HIGHLIGHT` became an annotation activity.

`SWITCH` events were assigned to the case of the destination application/artifact because they represent the start or resumption of work on that target item. This produces separate but connected artifact-centric cases, allowing a process-mining tool to analyze document work, email handling, spreadsheet updates, and review tasks independently.
