## 1. Normative-rule compliance

### Case 1 — Compliant

The case follows the required approved-loan path:

> Application Received  Credit Check  Risk Assessment  Approved Decision  Inform Applicant  Finalize Documentation  Close Case

No required activity is skipped or performed out of order.

### Case 2 — Multiple normative violations

Case 2 violates the strict rules in several ways:

1. **Risk Assessment occurred before Credit Check**
   - Risk Assessment: 08:20
   - Credit Check: 09:00  
   This reverses the mandatory ordering.

2. **Applicant was not informed**
   - There is no `Inform Applicant` event after the loan decision.
   - This is a skipped mandatory activity, not merely a timing issue.

3. **Documentation was finalized after rejection**
   - Decision: Rejected at 09:30
   - Finalize Documentation: 10:00  
   The rejected path must proceed to informing the applicant and closing the case without finalizing documentation.

4. **The case was closed using the wrong decision path**
   - Although closure itself is appropriate after rejection, it was preceded by a prohibited documentation-finalization activity and omitted the required applicant notification.

Therefore, **Case 2 is normatively noncompliant**.

### Case 3 — Normatively compliant

Despite the delays, Case 3 follows the required approved-loan sequence:

> Application Received  Credit Check  Risk Assessment  Approved Decision  Inform Applicant  Finalize Documentation  Close Case

The timing problems concern recommendations only, so they do not constitute strict violations.

## 2. Desiderata compliance

Assuming the time limits are measured in calendar hours and an absent event means it did not occur:

| Case | Credit Check within 24 hours? | Applicant informed within 48 hours? | Follow-up Call before closure? | Assessment |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | Yes — 30 minutes | Yes — 2 hours | No event recorded | Meets timing recommendations, but misses the recommended follow-up |
| 2 | Yes — 1 hour after receipt | No applicant notification recorded | No event recorded | Misses notification/follow-up recommendations, in addition to its serious normative violations |
| 3 | No — approximately 49 hours | No — approximately 51 hours | Explicitly absent | Multiple desiderata deviations, but no normative violation |

### Case-specific observations

- **Case 1:** The only apparent desideratum failure is the missing Follow-up Call. This is suboptimal but not a serious compliance issue.
- **Case 2:** No Follow-up Call is recorded. More importantly, the applicant was not informed at all; that is already a normative violation and should not be treated merely as a late-notification recommendation failure.
- **Case 3:** The Credit Check exceeded 24 hours, notification exceeded 48 hours, and no Follow-up Call occurred. These are all suboptimal behaviors, but the mandatory process sequence remains valid.

## 3. Possible interpretations or justifications

These explanations may help investigate the log, but they do not erase strict violations unless supported by corrected evidence:

- **Case 1:** The `Inform Applicant` interaction may have informally served the alignment purpose of a Follow-up Call. However, if the follow-up is intended as a distinct activity, the recommendation was still not followed.
- **Case 2:**
  - The reversed Credit Check and Risk Assessment timestamps could reflect a logging or timestamp error.
  - `Finalize Documentation` might have meant administrative closure paperwork rather than approved-loan documentation. If so, the activity label should be corrected or distinguished.
  - The applicant may have been informed through an unlogged channel. Until that event is documented, the trace remains noncompliant.
  - If the decision was incorrectly labeled “Rejected” and was actually approved, that could explain finalization—but the recorded data must be corrected before the trace can be considered compliant.
- **Case 3:**
  - The delays may have resulted from weekends, staffing constraints, external credit-data availability, or additional review.
  - If the 24- and 48-hour recommendations refer to **business hours** rather than calendar hours, the assessment may differ. The policy should define this explicitly.
  - A Follow-up Call may have been combined with the applicant notification or omitted from the log. As recorded, however, it remains an unmet recommendation.

## Overall conclusion

- **Normative violation:** Case 2 only.
- **Normatively compliant:** Cases 1 and 3.
- **Desiderata deviations:** Case 1 misses the follow-up; Case 3 misses both timing targets and the follow-up; Case 2 lacks a follow-up and, more seriously, fails mandatory notification and decision-path requirements.
