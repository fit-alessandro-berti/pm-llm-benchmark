### **Advanced Process Mining-Driven Scheduling Optimization for Precision Parts Inc.**

**Executive Summary:**
Precision Parts Inc. operates in a complex, stochastic environment where traditional static scheduling fails due to high mix, sequence dependencies, and dynamic disruptions. As a Senior Operations Analyst, I propose a closed-loop optimization framework leveraging **Process Mining (PM)** to transform raw MES event logs into actionable intelligence. This approach moves beyond descriptive analytics to diagnostic, predictive, and prescriptive capabilities, enabling the design of robust, adaptive scheduling strategies that directly address tardiness, WIP inflation, and resource inefficiency.

---

### **1. Analyzing Historical Scheduling Performance and Dynamics**

To reconstruct the operational reality, I will employ **Event Log Parsing and Case Reconstruction**. Each `Case ID` (Job ID) will link discrete events (`Queue Entry`, `Setup`, `Task`, `Queue Exit`) to form complete job trajectories. Using the `Timestamp` fields, I will compute temporal metrics, while `Resource` and `Notes` fields will enable structural and behavioral analysis.

**Key Techniques and Metrics:**

*   **Job Flow and Lead Time Distributions:**
    *   *Technique:* **Duration Analysis** and **Percentile Profiling**.
    *   *Metrics:* Compute `Lead Time = Job End Timestamp - Job Released Timestamp`. Generate cumulative distribution functions (CDFs) for lead times segmented by `Order Priority` and `Routing Complexity`. Identify outliers (jobs with lead times > P90) to isolate chronic delays.
    *   *Insight:* Quantify the gap between promised and actual lead times.

*   **Task Waiting Times and Queue Dynamics:**
    *   *Technique:* **Waiting Time Decomposition**.
    *   *Metrics:* For every task, calculate `Queue Time = Task Start - Queue Entry`. Aggregate by `Activity/Task` and `Resource`. Compute `Cycle Time Efficiency = Actual Processing / Total Flow Time`.
    *   *Insight:* Identify work centers where jobs spend disproportionate time waiting versus being processed, indicating queue management failures or bottlenecks.

*   **Resource Utilization and Productivity:**
    *   *Technique:* **Resource Profile Analysis**.
    *   *Metrics:* Decompose resource state into `Productive`, `Idle`, `Setup`, `Blocked`, and `Maintenance`. Calculate `Utilization = Productive / Available Time` and `Effectiveness = Productive / (Productive + Setup + Idle)`. Analyze operator-machine pairings to detect skill mismatches.
    *   *Insight:* Differentiate between true capacity constraints (high productive utilization) and scheduling-induced idle time (high idle/block time despite available work).

*   **Sequence-Dependent Setup Times:**
    *   *Technique:* **Transition Frequency and Duration Matrix**.
    *   *Methodology:* Using the `Previous job` and `Setup Required` fields, extract `Setup Duration = Setup End - Setup Start`. Construct an $N \times N$ matrix where cells represent the average setup time from Job $i$ to Job $j$. Apply **Clustering Algorithms** (e.g., K-Means) on job attributes (material, geometry) to discover clusters with low inter-cluster setup times.
    *   *Insight:* Quantify the "Setup Tax." Identify high-cost transitions that should be avoided and opportunities for batching similar jobs.

*   **Schedule Adherence and Tardiness:**
    *   *Technique:* **Conformance Checking** and **Slack Analysis**.
    *   *Metrics:* Calculate `Tardiness = Max(0, Job End - Due Date)` and `Lateness = Job End - Due Date`. Compute `Flowtime Slack = Due Date - Planned Lead Time`. Analyze deviations between `Planned Duration` and `Actual Duration` per task type.
    *   *Insight:* Determine if tardiness stems from unrealistic planning, execution delays, or external factors.

*   **Impact of Disruptions:**
    *   *Technique:* **Event Type Filtering and Regression Analysis**.
    *   *Methodology:* Tag events associated with `Breakdown` and `Priority Change`. Measure `Disruption Duration` and track downstream ripple effects (e.g., increase in queue size at downstream machines post-breakdown). Run multivariate regression to quantify how breakdown frequency and hot-job insertion magnitude correlate with overall tardiness and WIP growth.
    *   *Insight:* Establish the elasticity of the schedule to disruptions.

### **2. Diagnosing Scheduling Pathologies**

Based on the analysis, I expect to uncover several critical pathologies inherent to the current FCFS/EDD local rules:

*   **Bottleneck Mismanagement:**
    *   *Pathology:* Critical resources (e.g., CNC Milling, Heat Treatment) may show >90% utilization, yet total throughput is suboptimal.
    *   *PM Evidence:* **Bottleneck Analysis** will reveal these resources have the longest average queue times and highest setup durations. However, **Throughput Contribution Analysis** may show that non-bottleneck resources are starving the bottleneck due to poor synchronization, or the bottleneck is frequently switching jobs unnecessarily, increasing setup waste.

*   **Suboptimal Prioritization and Starvation:**
    *   *Pathology:* High-priority "Hot Jobs" are delayed by low-priority jobs under FCFS. Conversely, strict EDD might starve long-duration jobs, causing them to become perpetually late.
    *   *PM Evidence:* **Variant Analysis** comparing on-time vs. late high-priority jobs will likely show that late jobs were preceded by low-priority jobs with shorter remaining processing times, indicating the dispatcher favored short tasks over urgency. **Resource Contention Heatmaps** will show high-priority jobs accumulating in queues while resources process queued low-priority work.

*   **Excessive Sequence-Dependent Setup Waste:**
    *   *Pathology:* Jobs are sequenced based on arrival rather than setup compatibility, leading to frequent high-cost transitions.
    *   *PM Evidence:* Analysis of the **Setup Matrix** will reveal that the current schedule incurs X hours of setup time per week, whereas an optimized sequence could reduce this by Y%. **Process Tree** analysis may show frequent "zig-zag" patterns in material types across machines, confirming lack of sequencing optimization.

*   **WIP Amplification (Bullwhip Effect):**
    *   *Pathology:* Variability in processing times and setups causes queues to grow exponentially downstream.
    *   *PM Evidence:* **Correlation Analysis** between arrival rate variability and queue length growth. **Flow Variance Metrics** will show that variability increases at each subsequent operation, indicating that upstream delays are not absorbed but propagated, leading to excessive WIP accumulation.

*   **Starvation of Flexible Resources:**
    *   *Pathology:* Specialized machines are overloaded while flexible alternatives sit idle.
    *   *PM Evidence:* **Resource Pool Analysis** will highlight instances where multiple machine IDs could perform a task, but the scheduler assigned jobs only to specialized machines, leaving others with <40% utilization while queues built up elsewhere.

### **3. Root Cause Analysis of Scheduling Ineffectiveness**

Process mining enables us to differentiate between **Capacity Issues**, **Logic Issues**, and **Data Accuracy Issues**.

*   **Static vs. Dynamic Mismatch:**
    *   *Root Cause:* Local rules react to immediate conditions without foresight. PM reveals that jobs released during peak load suffer significantly higher delays than those released during off-peak, even with similar characteristics, proving the system cannot handle dynamic load fluctuations.
    *   *PM Differentiation:* By segmenting performance by time-of-day and load level, we can prove that the rule failure is load-dependent, necessitating load-aware scheduling.

*   **Lack of Global Visibility:**
    *   *Root Cause:* Dispatchers act locally. PM reveals "Decoupled Flows" where Machine A completes a job but Machine B is already backed up, yet Machine A continues pulling work from its input queue.
    *   *PM Differentiation:* **Conformance Checking** against a pull-based policy (e.g., CONWIP) would show massive violations, proving that the current push-based behavior is a root cause of WIP.

*   **Inaccurate Estimations:**
    *   *Root Cause:* Planning relies on standard times that do not reflect reality. PM compares `Planned Duration` vs. `Actual Duration`.
    *   *PM Differentiation:* If variance is high and biased (e.g., actuals consistently exceed planned), the root cause is estimation error. If variance is random, the root cause is process instability. This distinction dictates whether to improve estimation models or stabilize operations first.

*   **Setup Ignorance in Planning:**
    *   *Root Cause:* Schedules assume zero or constant setup times.
    *   *PM Differentiation:* **Setup Contribution Analysis** will demonstrate that for bottleneck machines, setup time accounts for >30% of total load. Since the scheduler ignores this, it creates infeasible schedules that inevitably fail, causing reactive rescheduling and chaos.

*   **Poor Disruption Response:**
    *   *Root Cause:* No predefined recovery protocol; ad-hoc reactions.
    *   *PM Differentiation:* Analysis of `Breakdown` events will show inconsistent recovery behaviors. Some breakdowns lead to permanent schedule shifts; others recover quickly. The lack of a pattern indicates no standardized response strategy.

### **4. Developing Advanced Data-Driven Scheduling Strategies**

I propose three synergistic strategies informed by PM insights:

#### **Strategy 1: Adaptive Multi-Criteria Dispatching (AMCD)**
*   **Core Logic:** Replace FCFS/EDD with a dynamic scoring function calculated at every decision point (queue entry or resource availability).
    *   $$Score_j = w_1 \cdot CR_j + w_2 \cdot \frac{1}{\hat{T}_{rem,j}} + w_3 \cdot \text{SeqCost}(LastJob, j) + w_4 \cdot \text{DownstreamLoad}_j$$
    *   Where $CR$ is Critical Ratio, $\hat{T}_{rem}$ is estimated remaining processing time, $\text{SeqCost}$ is the predicted setup cost based on the previous job, and $\text{DownstreamLoad}$ penalizes sending jobs to congested next stations.
*   **PM Insights Used:**
    *   Weights ($w_i$) are optimized using **Regression Analysis** on historical data to maximize the probability of on-time completion.
    *   $\text{SeqCost}$ is populated by the **Setup Matrix** mined from logs.
    *   $\text{DownstreamLoad}$ uses real-time queue depths derived from live event streams.
*   **Addressed Pathologies:** Prioritization errors, starvation, setup waste, WIP amplification.
*   **Expected Impact:** Reduced tardiness by balancing urgency with flow efficiency; reduced setup times by favoring compatible sequences; smoother WIP flow.

#### **Strategy 2: Predictive Stochastic Scheduling**
*   **Core Logic:** Move from deterministic Gantt charts to **Probabilistic Schedules**. Use Monte Carlo simulations to generate schedules with confidence intervals.
    *   Task durations are modeled using distributions fitted to historical `Actual Duration` data (e.g., Gamma or Weibull), conditioned on factors like `Operator ID` and `Material Type` identified via **Feature Importance Analysis**.
    *   Schedules include **Dynamic Buffers**: Instead of fixed due dates, jobs are scheduled with "Release Windows" and "Protection Buffers" placed at bottleneck resources.
*   **PM Insights Used:**
    *   Duration distributions and factor impacts from **Duration Analysis**.
    *   Breakdown frequencies and MTBF/MTTR from **Reliability Analysis** of `Breakdown` events.
    *   Bottleneck locations from **Throughput Analysis**.
*   **Addressed Pathologies:** Unpredictable lead times, disruption vulnerability, unrealistic planning.
*   **Expected Impact:** More accurate customer promises (e.g., "90% probability of delivery by X"); increased schedule robustness; proactive buffer management absorbs variability.

#### **Strategy 3: Setup-Aware Sequencing at Bottlenecks**
*   **Core Logic:** Implement a **Traveling Salesperson Problem (TSP)** solver or **Genetic Algorithm** specifically for bottleneck machines.
    *   Jobs are grouped into batches based on similarity metrics derived from **Clustering Analysis** of job attributes.
    *   Within batches, jobs are sequenced to minimize total setup time.
    *   Non-bottleneck machines synchronize release rates to match the optimized bottleneck throughput.
*   **PM Insights Used:**
    *   **Setup Matrix** defines the distance metric for the TSP.
    *   **Cluster Analysis** identifies job families suitable for batching.
    *   **Bottleneck Identification** targets the algorithm where it matters most.
*   **Addressed Pathologies:** Excessive setup times, bottleneck congestion, inefficient resource use.
*   **Expected Impact:** Significant reduction in setup waste (potentially 20-40%); increased effective capacity at bottlenecks; reduced makespan for batch completions.
