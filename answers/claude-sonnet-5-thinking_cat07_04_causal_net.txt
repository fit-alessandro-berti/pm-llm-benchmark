# Workflow Process Diagram (Petri Net / Workflow Net)

This visualization depicts a **business process model** for handling requests, using a workflow net notation with routing constructs.

## Process Flow

**Nodes (Activities):**
- **a** - Register request (start)
- **b** - Examine thoroughly
- **c** - Examine casually
- **d** - Check ticket
- **e** - Decide
- **f** - Reinitiate request
- **g** - Pay compensation
- **h** - Reject request
- **z** - End

## Process Logic

1. **Start (a  OR-split)**: After registering a request, the process splits into an OR-split pattern leading to examining thoroughly (b), examining casually (c), and/or checking ticket (d) — meaning one or more of these paths may execute.

2. **Convergence at "decide" (e)**: All examination paths (b, c, d) converge via an **OR-join** into the decision activity.

3. **Decision point (e  XOR-split)**: After deciding, the process branches exclusively (XOR) into one of two paths:
   - **Reinitiate request (f)** — loops back to the OR-split before b/c/d, allowing the examination process to repeat
   - **Proceed to outcome** — leads to either paying compensation (g) or rejecting the request (h)

4. **Final XOR-split (after e)**: Chooses between **pay compensation (g)** or **reject request (h)**

5. **XOR-join at "end" (z)**: Both outcome paths converge to end the process.

## Legend (Bottom-Right Box)

The inset explains the **gateway notation** used in the diagram:
- **Splits**: XOR-split, AND-split, OR-split (diverging control flow)
- **Joins**: XOR-join, AND-join, OR-join (converging control flow)

Each is distinguished by how the black dots (tokens/connectors) combine at the branching/merging points, indicating whether paths are mutually exclusive (XOR), all required (AND), or optional/inclusive (OR).

## Summary

This diagram represents a **claim/request handling workflow**, commonly used in business process modeling (similar to examples in workflow mining or Petri net literature), showing how a request is examined (with flexible routing), decided upon, and either compensated, rejected, or sent back for re-examination.
