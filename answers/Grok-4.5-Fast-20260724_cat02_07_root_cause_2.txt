**Long-duration cases**

Case durations (from first “Submit Claim” to final “Close Claim”):

- **2001** (Low complexity, Region A): ~1.5 hours  
- **2002** (Medium, Region B): ~26 hours  
- **2003** (High, Region A): ~48 hours  
- **2004** (Low, Region B): ~1.4 hours  
- **2005** (High, Region B): ~77 hours  

The clear outliers are **2002, 2003 and 2005**. The two Low-complexity cases finish the same day with a straight path (Submit → Evaluate → Approve → Pay → Close). The longer cases all contain one or more “Request Additional Documents” activities and therefore span multiple calendar days.

**Attribute correlations**

| Case | Complexity | Region | Dominant Adjuster | # of “Request Additional Documents” | Duration |
|------|------------|--------|-------------------|--------------------------------------|----------|
| 2001 | Low        | A      | Mike              | 0                                    | Short    |
| 2004 | Low        | B      | Lisa              | 0                                    | Short    |
| 2002 | Medium     | B      | Lisa              | 1                                    | Medium   |
| 2003 | High       | A      | Mike              | 2                                    | Long     |
| 2005 | High       | B      | Lisa              | 3                                    | Very long|

- **Complexity** is the strongest predictor. High-complexity claims trigger repeated document requests; Medium complexity triggers at least one request. Low-complexity claims never request extra documents in this log.
- **Resource (adjuster)** matters once complexity is high. Both Mike (Region A) and Lisa (Region B) issue multiple requests on High claims, but Lisa’s High claim (2005) accumulates three requests and the longest overall cycle time.
- **Region** shows a secondary effect: Region-B High claim (2005) is slower than Region-A High claim (2003). The Medium claim also occurs in Region B. No Low-complexity claim in either region is slow, so region alone is not causal.
- Managers and Finance resources appear only after the document-request loops finish; they do not drive the long lead times.

**Root-cause explanations**

1. High (and to a lesser extent Medium) complexity claims inherently need more information. The current process handles this by successive “Request Additional Documents” steps rather than a single, comprehensive request or parallel gathering. Each extra request adds waiting time (often overnight or multi-day).
2. The same adjuster stays on the case and re-requests documents, suggesting either incomplete initial evaluation, unclear customer communication, or missing checklists for High-complexity claims.
3. Region B appears slightly slower on non-Low claims, possibly because of local workload, different documentation standards, or resource availability (Lisa handles every Region-B claim in the log).

**Mitigation suggestions**

- Introduce a complexity-based triage at “Evaluate Claim”: High-complexity cases should trigger a single, exhaustive document checklist (or a dedicated “Gather All Required Documents” activity) instead of iterative requests.
- Add a time-box or escalation rule: if a second document request is needed, automatically involve a senior adjuster or manager.
- Provide adjusters with standardized High-complexity templates and decision support so the first request is complete.
- Monitor region-level workload and, if Region B consistently shows longer cycles on Medium/High claims, rebalance cases or cross-train resources.
- Track the metric “number of document-request loops per case” and set a target of ≤1 for High-complexity claims; review any case that exceeds it.

These changes address the observed pattern that longer lead times are driven primarily by repeated document requests on higher-complexity claims, with secondary contributions from the handling resource and region.
