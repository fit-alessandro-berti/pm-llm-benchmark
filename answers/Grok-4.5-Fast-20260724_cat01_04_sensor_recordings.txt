**Labeled process from the event log**

The raw sensor time series shows clear sequential phases on a single machine (M1). Groups were formed by simultaneous changes in multiple signals (temperature, vibration, material flow, tool position, energy) rather than any single threshold. Transitions are sharp and consistent with the physical signatures of common manufacturing steps.

### 1. Idle / Standby (Events 1–3 and 13–15)
- **Readings**: Temperature ≈ 20–21 °C, pressure = 1 bar, vibration = 0 Hz, material flow = 0, tool position = 0 mm, energy ≈ 0.4–0.5 kWh.  
- **Rationale**: All sensors are at their lowest steady values; the machine is powered but performing no work. The identical pattern reappears after the cycle completes, confirming a reset to the idle state.

### 2. Cutting Metal (Events 4–6)
- **Readings**: Temperature rises rapidly (50 → 58 °C), vibration jumps to 30–38 Hz, material flow stabilizes at 5 units/s, tool position advances (5 → 10 mm), energy climbs to 2–3 kWh; pressure stays moderate.  
- **Rationale**: High vibration + continuous material feed + progressive tool movement + elevated energy and temperature are classic signatures of a cutting or machining operation. The tool is actively engaged with the workpiece while material is being removed.

### 3. Assembling Parts (Events 7–8)
- **Readings**: Temperature drops to a steady 40 °C, pressure rises to 2 bar, vibration falls to a moderate 10 Hz, material flow decreases to 2 units/s, tool position holds at 15 mm, energy remains ≈ 2 kWh.  
- **Rationale**: After the aggressive cutting phase the process stabilizes. Lower vibration, reduced but non-zero flow, higher holding pressure and a fixed tool position are consistent with parts being positioned, clamped or joined under controlled force.

### 4. Welding (Event 9)
- **Readings**: Temperature spikes to 80 °C, vibration peaks at 50 Hz, material flow drops to 0, tool position remains 15 mm, energy jumps to 5 kWh.  
- **Rationale**: A short, intense burst of heat and vibration with zero material flow and maximum energy draw is characteristic of a welding (or similar thermal joining) operation performed at a fixed location.

### 5. Cool-down / Transition (Event 10)
- **Readings**: Temperature falls sharply to 30 °C, vibration nearly disappears (1 Hz), flow remains 0, tool still at 15 mm, energy drops to 1 kWh.  
- **Rationale**: Immediate post-weld cooling; residual heat dissipates while the tool stays in place before the next low-intensity activity begins.

### 6. Quality Inspection (Events 11–12)
- **Readings**: Temperature settles near ambient (25 °C), pressure = 1 bar, low vibration (5 Hz), small material flow (1 unit/s), tool advances to 20 mm, energy ≈ 1.5–1.6 kWh.  
- **Rationale**: Gentle tool movement to a new position, very low vibration and only a trickle of material movement match a non-destructive inspection or measurement step (probe contact, light material handling for checking dimensions/finish). Packaging is less likely because the tool is still actively repositioned and a small flow is present.

### Overall process cycle
Idle → Cutting Metal → Assembling Parts → Welding → Cool-down → Quality Inspection → Idle  

The sequence is linear, repeats cleanly, and the sensor transitions align with the physical expectations of each labeled activity. This constitutes a high-level process model discovered purely from the unlabeled numeric event log.
