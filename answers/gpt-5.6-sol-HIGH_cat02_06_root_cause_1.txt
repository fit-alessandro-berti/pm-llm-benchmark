## 1. Cases with longer cycle times

Using elapsed time from **Receive Ticket** to **Close Ticket**:

| Case | Total cycle time | Largest delay | Assessment |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 103 | 1h 20m | 30m: Investigate  Resolve | Fastest |
| 101 | 2h 15m | 1h: Investigate  Resolve | Normal/fast |
| 104 | 24h 10m | 19h: Investigate  Resolve | Long |
| 102 | 25h 10m | 19h: Investigate  Resolve | Long; escalated |
| 105 | 49h 05m | 28h: Escalation  Level-2 investigation | Severe outlier; escalated |

The overall average is **20h 24m**, although this is heavily increased by the three slow cases. Cases **102, 104, and 105** form a delayed group compared with cases 101 and 103. **Case 105 is the clearest performance outlier**, taking approximately twice as long as cases 102 and 104.

## 2. Likely root causes

### A. Long delay after investigation

A recurring **19-hour gap between investigation and resolution** appears in every slow case:

- **102:** Mar 1, 14:00  Mar 2, 09:00
- **104:** Mar 1, 13:00  Mar 2, 08:00
- **105:** Mar 2, 14:00  Mar 3, 09:00

This strongly suggests a shared bottleneck after investigation, such as:

- Resolution work being deferred until the next morning
- Limited afternoon or overnight staffing
- Waiting for approval, technical input, or another team
- Resolutions being processed in batches
- Investigations finishing after a same-day processing cutoff

Case 104 shows that this issue is **not limited to escalated tickets**.

### B. Escalation and Level-2 handoff delays

Both escalated cases are slow:

- **Case 102:** 25h 10m
- **Case 105:** 49h 05m

Case 105 has a particularly severe delay:

- Escalated at Mar 1, 10:00
- Level-2 investigation did not begin until Mar 2, 14:00
- **Handoff delay: 28 hours**

Case 102 also experienced:

- 2h 30m from Level-1 assignment to escalation
- Another 2h 30m from escalation to investigation

Escalation therefore appears to add queueing, ownership transfer, or specialist-capacity delays. However, escalation is not the only cause because non-escalated case 104 also took more than 24 hours.

### C. Delayed start of investigation

Case 104 had a substantial delay before investigation:

- Assigned at 09:30
- Investigation began at 13:00
- **Wait: 3h 30m**

By comparison:

- Case 101: 40m
- Case 103: 15m
- Case 105: 10m for the initial Level-1 investigation

This may indicate an agent backlog, uneven workload distribution, or inadequate prioritization.

### D. Triage and closure are not the primary bottlenecks

Initial triage and assignment usually take minutes rather than hours. Case 104’s 40-minute wait for triage is somewhat longer, but it contributes little compared with the later 19-hour delay.

Likewise, closing takes only **15–30 minutes** in every case. The performance problem occurs before resolution, not during administrative closure.

## 3. How these factors increase cycle time

- **Escalations create additional queues and handoffs.** Tickets may lose clear ownership while waiting for Level-2 capacity.
- **Late-day investigations appear to roll over to the next day.** This creates a predictable overnight delay of about 19 hours.
- **Delayed investigation consumes available same-day resolution time.** In case 104, the 3h 30m wait pushed investigation into the afternoon, possibly causing it to miss a resolution cutoff.
- **Case 105 combines both problems:** a 28-hour Level-2 pickup delay followed by another 19-hour post-investigation delay.

## Recommendations

1. **Prioritize the investigation-to-resolution bottleneck**
   - Set transition-time targets between investigation completion and resolution.
   - Trigger alerts when a ticket remains unresolved for several hours after investigation.
   - Determine whether approvals, dependencies, or batch processing cause the recurring 19-hour gap.

2. **Improve escalation handling**
   - Route tickets directly to Level-2 during triage when escalation is predictable.
   - Define clear ownership during handoffs.
   - Use “warm handoffs,” including diagnostics and investigation notes.
   - Review Level-2 staffing and queue capacity, particularly around late morning and afternoon.

3. **Reduce agent queue time**
   - Monitor assignment-to-investigation waiting time.
   - Rebalance workloads or use automated skill-based assignment.
   - Establish same-day investigation targets for tickets received in the morning.

4. **Address overnight rollover**
   - Add late-shift coverage where justified.
   - Prioritize afternoon investigations for same-day resolution.
   - Automate standard resolutions or approval steps where possible.

5. **Capture more diagnostic data**
   - Add agent/team, priority, severity, queue-entry time, waiting status, business-hours calendar, and “waiting for customer/external party” fields.
   - This would distinguish active investigation from queueing and confirm whether overnight elapsed time represents avoidable delay.

Overall, the strongest evidence points to a **post-investigation resolution bottleneck**, compounded by **slow Level-2 escalation handoffs**, especially in case 105.
