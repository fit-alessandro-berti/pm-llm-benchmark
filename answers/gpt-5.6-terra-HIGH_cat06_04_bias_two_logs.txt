## Finding

**Group B’s process exhibits preferential bias relative to Group A.** The bias appears through a score-boost mechanism that is available to some **LocalResident = TRUE** applicants who belong to the **Highland Civic Darts Club**, while no comparable adjustment appears in Group A.

## Evidence in the logs

| Case | Group | Initial score | Adjustment | Adjusted score | Final decision |
|---|---|---:|---|---:|---|
| P001 | A / protected | 720 | 0 | 720 | Approved |
| P002 | A / protected | 710 | 0 | 710 | Rejected |
| P003 | A / protected | 740 | 0 | 740 | Approved |
| U001 | B / unprotected | 720 | +10 community boost | 730 | Approved |
| U002 | B / unprotected | 710 | 0 | 710 | Rejected |
| U003 | B / unprotected | 695 | +10 community boost | 705 | Approved |

### Differential treatment

- Every Group A applicant is recorded as `LocalResident = FALSE`, has `CommunityGroup = None`, and receives **no score increase**.
- Group B applicants are `LocalResident = TRUE`; those affiliated with the Highland Civic Darts Club receive a **+10 “Community Boost.”**
- In particular, **U003 begins with a score of 695**, lower than protected-group applicant **P002’s 710**, but receives a +10 adjustment and is ultimately approved. P002 receives no adjustment and is rejected.

This does not by itself prove that the boost alone caused U003’s approval—there may be unrecorded underwriting factors—but it clearly documents an unequal scoring rule that can advantage Group B applicants.

## How the bias manifests

1. **Community-group-based advantage**  
   Membership in a particular local community organization directly increases an applicant’s score. That benefit is not available to applicants without that affiliation.

2. **Local-residency correlation / proxy risk**  
   In these logs, LocalResident status and the community affiliation are aligned:
   - Group A: non-local, no affiliated group, no boost.
   - Group B: local, with some applicants eligible for the boost.

   Therefore, even if the system does not explicitly label someone “protected” or “unprotected,” `LocalResident` and `CommunityGroup` can function as proxies for group membership. A policy that rewards local civic-club affiliation may systematically exclude or disadvantage non-local applicants, including the protected group.

3. **Decision impact near a cutoff**  
   A 10-point increase can be material for applicants near an approval threshold. It can move applicants from below a cutoff to above it, or make them appear stronger during manual review. U003 illustrates the concern: a lower preliminary score becomes an adjusted score that is followed by approval.

## Important limitation

The small sample does **not** show a different overall approval rate: both groups have 2 approvals out of 3 cases. So the logs do not establish a statistically reliable group-level approval-rate disparity.

However, they do show a **disparate treatment mechanism**: Group B applicants with a particular local-community affiliation receive a benefit that Group A applicants, as represented here, never receive. If Group A members are disproportionately unable to qualify for that affiliation or residency-based boost, the process can produce systematic disadvantage over a larger population.

## Conclusion

The concerning bias is **favoritism toward eligible Group B applicants**, implemented through the `CommunityGroup`-based +10 score adjustment and potentially reinforced by `LocalResident` status. The protected Group A receives no equivalent opportunity for adjustment. To assess and mitigate this, the organization should test whether the community boost is job/credit/business-relevant, evaluate whether it acts as a proxy for protected status or residency, and compare approval outcomes before and after adjustment across similarly situated applicants.
