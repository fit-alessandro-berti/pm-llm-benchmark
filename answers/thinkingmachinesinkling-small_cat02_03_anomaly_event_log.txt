Based on standard **Procure-to-Pay (P2P)** best practices, the canonical control flow is:

**Create PR  Approve PR  Issue PO  Receive Goods (GR)  Receive Vendor Invoice  3-Way Match (PO/GR/Invoice)  Release Payment  Close Case**

This sequence ensures **authorization** (approval before commitment), **verification** (goods received before payment), and **matching** (invoice verified against PO and receipt before cash outflow). Below is the case-by-case analysis.

---

### Case 1001 — **Normal / Compliant**
| Step | Observed Order | Assessment |
|------|----------------|------------|
| PR  Approve  PO  GR  Invoice  Match  Pay  Close | Follows standard sequence | **No major anomaly.** The 3-day gap between goods/invoice (Feb 2) and matching/payment (Feb 5–6) is slightly delayed but acceptable for processing/approval cycles. Controls are intact.

---

### Case 1002 — **Critical Control Failure / High Risk**
**Observed sequence:**  
`Create PR`  **`Issue PO`** (no approval)  `Vendor Invoice Received`  **`Release Payment`**  `Approve PR` (retroactive)  `Receive Goods`  `Match` (after payment)  `Close`

**Specific anomalies:**
1. **Purchase Order issued before PR approval** (08:40 Feb 1 vs. 08:00 Feb 3). The organization committed to a vendor without an approved requisition.
2. **Payment released before approval** (07:15 Feb 3), before goods receipt (Feb 4), and before invoice matching.
3. **Goods received after payment and after invoice** — the company paid for items that had not yet been delivered.
4. **Approval is retroactive** — occurring 30 minutes *after* payment and nearly 2 days after the PO was issued.
5. **3-way match performed after cash outflow** (Feb 5) — eliminating the primary fraud/accuracy check.

**Why it’s problematic:**  
This represents a breakdown of **authorization**, **verification**, and **segregation of duties**. It opens the door to unauthorized/vendor fraud (e.g., “ghost” vendors, inflated invoices), paying for non-delivered goods, and unapproved spending. Auditors and finance controls would flag this as a severe violation of internal controls.

---

### Case 1003 — **Payment Before Verification (Pre-Payment / Unverified Cash Outflow)**
**Observed sequence:**  
`Create PR`  `Approve PR`  `Issue PO`  **`Release Payment`** (Feb 8)  `Vendor Invoice Received` (Feb 9)  `Receive Goods` (Feb 10)  `Match` (Feb 11)  `Close`

**Specific anomalies:**
1. **Payment released before the vendor invoice was received** (Feb 8 vs. Feb 9) — payment with no invoice to verify amount, terms, or vendor legitimacy.
2. **Goods received after both payment and invoice** (Feb 10) — the company paid for goods that had not been delivered or inspected.
3. **Matching occurs only after everything else** (Feb 11) — no 3-way reconciliation before cash was released.

**Why it’s problematic:**  
Even if this were intended as a “prepayment” or “advance,” standard P2P requires that payment be tied to verified delivery and matched documentation. Paying before receipt risks **non-delivery**, **overpayment**, or **duplicate payments**. Without an invoice at the time of release, there is no documentary basis for the exact amount paid. This weakens audit trails and working-capital controls.

---

### Case 1004 — **Retroactive / Post-Receipt Purchase Order**
**Observed sequence:**  
`Create PR`  `Approve PR`  **`Receive Goods`** (Feb 6 09:00)  **`Issue Purchase Order`** (Feb 6 09:15)  `Vendor Invoice`  `Match`  `Release Payment`  `Close`

**Specific anomalies:**
1. **Goods received before the PO was issued** (09:00 vs. 09:15). The organization accepted physical delivery without an active purchase order.
2. **PO is created retroactively** — 15 minutes after goods arrived — suggesting the order was backdated or created to “fit” an unplanned delivery.

**Why it’s problematic:**  
A PO is the contractual and authorization mechanism that defines price, quantity, vendor, and terms. Receiving goods first undermines competitive bidding, price validation, and vendor vetting. It is a classic indicator of **m off-book purchasing**, **emergency/unauthorized buys**, or **collusion** (e.g., goods delivered by a preferred vendor at non-standard terms). Financial controls typically require a PO before goods acceptance to prevent “shadow” procurement.
