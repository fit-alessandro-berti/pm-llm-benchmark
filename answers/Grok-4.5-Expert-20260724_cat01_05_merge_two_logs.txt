### Reasoning for Matches, Mismatches, and Attribute Integration

**Matching criteria applied**  
- Same `order_id` (all events share Order#1234).  
- Temporal proximity (target tolerance ~2 s; relaxed to a few seconds when name semantics also align, given the problem statement that timestamps may be offset by a few seconds/milliseconds and that Log A timestamps may reflect receipt rather than occurrence).  
- Semantic equivalence of event names despite differing conventions (camel-case vs. spaced, abbreviations).  
- When matched, every attribute from both logs is retained; no attributes are dropped. Conflicting timestamps are both recorded rather than arbitrarily overwritten.

**Individual decisions**

1. **Order Received ↔ OrderReceived**  
   Name equivalence is obvious. Absolute difference = 2 s. Matched. Attributes from B (user_id, resource_id, notes) enrich the A record. Primary timestamp taken from A.

2. **Order Validated ↔ OrderValidation**  
   Name equivalence is obvious. Absolute difference = 1 s. Matched with high confidence. Full attribute union performed.

3. **Payment Processed ↔ PaymentCheck**  
   Name equivalence is strong (both clearly refer to the payment step). Absolute difference = 5 s (slightly outside the strict <2 s example). Matched at medium confidence because:  
   - the sequential position in the process is identical,  
   - B’s note explicitly mentions a “Payment gateway delay”, which supplies a plausible explanation for the offset,  
   - no competing payment-related event exists in either log.  
   Full attribute union performed; primary timestamp from A.

4. **Quality Check (B only)**  
   No counterpart in Log A by name or by proximity to any A event. Left as a standalone B-origin record (adds the quality-inspection step that System A never recorded).

5. **Item Shipped ↔ Shipping**  
   Name equivalence is clear. Absolute difference = 2 s. Matched with high confidence. Attributes from B enrich the record.

6. **Item Delivered (A only)**  
   No counterpart in Log B. Left as a standalone A-origin record.

**Unresolved / non-overlapping events**  
Only the Quality Check and Item Delivered events could not be paired; both are retained with an explicit origin flag so downstream analysis can distinguish system-specific observations.

**Final ordering**  
Events are sorted by the chosen primary timestamp (A when available). The resulting sequence preserves the natural process flow while incorporating the richer metadata from System B and the extra Quality Check step that only B observed.
