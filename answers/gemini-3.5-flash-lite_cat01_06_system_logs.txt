### Explanation of Methodology

1. **Case Identification Logic:**
   - Instead of treating the entire session as a single monolithic case or splitting strictly by application, cases were defined around **business artifacts/documents** (e.g., specific files, emails, or tasks). 
   - For example, `Document1.docx` represents an independent work item (Case_NewDocument) that the user started, temporarily left to check email or update a budget (`Budget_2024.xlsx`), and later returned to before finalizing and closing. Similarly, `Quarterly_Report.docx` represents its own discrete case lifecycle (`Case_QuarterlyReport`).

2. **Activity Naming Strategy:**
   - Raw, low-level operating system actions (`TYPING`, `CLICK`, `SCROLL`, `FOCUS`, `SAVE`, `CLOSE`) are too granular and noisy for process mining. They were elevated into standardized, human-readable business activities.
   - For instance, consecutive typing and clicking inside an email client were generalized into coherent workflow steps like *Open Email*, *Review Email*, *Reply to Email*, and *Send Email*. 
   - Document-centric actions were mapped to standardized lifecycle steps such as *Open [Document]*, *Draft Content*, *Save Document*, and *Close Document*.
