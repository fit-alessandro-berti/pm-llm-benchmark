# The Loan Approval Process Behind the DECLARE Model

## 1. Reading the model as a business narrative

A DECLARE model doesn't draw a flowchart; it fences off the space of acceptable behaviour with rules. The rules above sketch a **document-driven, control-heavy retail lending process** in which:

* every case must begin with an intake event and must include a documentation step,
* creditworthiness is assessed **once and only once**,
* nothing gets packaged, priced or disbursed until a quality/compliance gate has been passed,
* money movement and customer communication are tightly paired, and
* a "shortcut" activity representing compliance circumvention is categorically banned.

### The intended happy path

| # | Activity | Why it sits here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Receive_Application** | `init` + `existence`: every case, without exception, starts with a formally logged intake. |
| 2 | **Preliminary_Credit_Check** | `response(Receive_Application  Preliminary_Credit_Check)` + `exactly_one`: one authoritative bureau pull / scorecard run per case. |
| 3 | **Gather_Additional_Documents** | `succession(Preliminary_Credit_Check  Gather_Additional_Documents)`: documentation is *targeted* by what the credit check revealed, and can never be collected before it. |
| 4 | **Notify_Customer** (decision-in-principle) | `altprecedence(Notify_Customer, Transfer_Funds)`: the customer must be told something before money moves. |
| 5 | **Quality_Assurance_Review** | `altsuccession(Gather_Additional_Documents  Quality_Assurance_Review)`: the file is checked for completeness/compliance once documents are in. |
| 6 | **Assemble_Loan_Offer_Package** | `chainsuccession(Quality_Assurance_Review  Assemble_Loan_Offer_Package)`: packaging happens *immediately* after — and only after — QA sign-off, so no unreviewed version can be slipped in. |
| 7 | **Transfer_Funds** | `chainresponse(Assemble_Loan_Offer_Package  Transfer_Funds)`: disbursement follows the approved package directly, with no intervening edits. |
| 8 | **Notify_Customer** (confirmation) | `altresponse(Transfer_Funds  Notify_Customer)`: no disbursement is ever left unannounced. |
| 9 | **Authorize_Contract_Terms** | `coexistence` with documents + `precedence(Quality_Assurance_Review, Authorize_Contract_Terms)`: the contract is finalised, and only after QA. |
| — | ~~Proceed_Without_Compliance~~ | `absence`: never, in any case, under any circumstance. |

Steps 4 and 9 sit in unusual places; that is a direct consequence of the constraint set rather than of good banking practice, and I return to it in §4.

## 2. What each constraint actually enforces, in practical terms

**Mandatory and forbidden behaviour**

* `existence(Receive_Application)` and `existence(Gather_Additional_Documents)` — you cannot have a loan file with no intake record and no supporting evidence. This is the audit trail: a regulator asking "where did this exposure come from and on what basis?" must always find an answer.
* `absence(Proceed_Without_Compliance)` — a hard prohibition. In real logs this is usually a "manual override", "policy exception granted", or "waive checks" event. Making it an absence constraint means it is not a rare exception but a **defect**: a single occurrence invalidates the case.
* `exactly_one(Preliminary_Credit_Check)` — exactly one credit assessment. Zero would mean underwriting blind; two or more would mean "score shopping" (re-running the check until a favourable result appears), which distorts risk pricing and, in many jurisdictions, harms the applicant's bureau record.
* `init(Receive_Application)` — no back-door origination. Work cannot begin on a prospect before the application is formally logged, which prevents pre-selling and undocumented "relationship" deals.

**Ordering (the backbone of due diligence)**

* `response(Receive_Application  Preliminary_Credit_Check)` — no application is allowed to die silently in an inbox; every intake must eventually reach an assessment.
* `succession(Preliminary_Credit_Check  Gather_Additional_Documents)` — bidirectional: the check always leads to document collection, and document collection never occurs without a prior check. Operationally, this stops staff from burdening customers with paperwork before the bank knows what it needs, and stops "document-first" files where evidence is assembled to justify a decision already made.
* `altsuccession(Gather_Additional_Documents  Quality_Assurance_Review)` — the *alternating* flavour means each documentation episode gets its own review: you cannot collect documents twice and review once. Every new batch of evidence is re-verified.
* `precedence(Quality_Assurance_Review, Authorize_Contract_Terms)` — four-eyes principle: terms can only be authorised on a file that has been independently reviewed.
* `responded_existence(Assemble_Loan_Offer_Package  Quality_Assurance_Review)` — a weaker safety net that guarantees that if an offer package exists anywhere in the case, a QA review exists too.
* `coexistence(Gather_Additional_Documents, Authorize_Contract_Terms)` — all-or-nothing: a case that collects documents must reach a formal authorisation decision (no abandoned half-processed files), and an authorisation can never rest on an undocumented file.

**Chain (immediacy) constraints — anti-tampering controls**

* `chainsuccession(Quality_Assurance_Review  Assemble_Loan_Offer_Package)` — the reviewed artefact and the packaged artefact must be the *same* artefact. Nothing may be inserted between review and packaging, which is precisely the window in which rates, fees or covenants could be quietly altered.
* `chainresponse(Assemble_Loan_Offer_Package  Transfer_Funds)` — the money that leaves matches the package that was just assembled; no re-pricing or re-keying between packaging and payment.
* `chainprecedence(Authorize_Contract_Terms, Preliminary_Credit_Check)` — as written, this ties the credit check immediately to the authorisation event (see §4; the plausible business intent is "authorisation must be immediately preceded by the underwriting evidence").

**Alternation constraints around money and communication**

* `altresponse(Transfer_Funds  Notify_Customer)` + `altprecedence(Notify_Customer, Transfer_Funds)` together create a strict **alternating dialogue**: notification  disbursement  notification  (possible further disbursement)  … Two consecutive disbursements without an intervening notification are impossible. For tranche-based lending this is exactly right: each drawdown is announced and confirmed, so the customer can spot an unauthorised transfer immediately.

**Negative constraints**

* `nonsuccession(Notify_Customer  Preliminary_Credit_Check)` — once the customer has been informed of an outcome, you may not go back and re-run the credit assessment. This prevents "decision laundering": telling the customer no (or yes), then retro-fitting the underwriting evidence.
* `nonchainsuccession(Authorize_Contract_Terms  Notify_Customer)` — authorisation may not be *immediately* followed by notification. In practice this forces a step in between: recording the decision, generating documents, archiving the signed contract, or a cooling-off/second-check event. It blocks the reflex "approve and instantly tell the customer" behaviour that skips post-authorisation controls.
* `noncoexistence(Transfer_Funds, Receive_Application)` — as written, the strongest rule in the model (see §4). Its plausible intent is "a case in which funds have moved may not also contain a fresh application event", i.e. **no re-origination on a live disbursed file**, which is a classic fraud pattern (recycling one approved file into multiple payouts).

## 3. Why a bank would impose these rules

* **Regulatory compliance.** Consumer-credit regimes (affordability/responsible-lending rules, KYC/AML, GDPR-style data minimisation) require demonstrable evidence that an assessment took place, that it was based on documents, and that the customer was informed. `existence`, `exactly_one`, `succession` and the notification constraints are the process-level encoding of those obligations.
* **Fraud prevention.** Most lending fraud is *sequence* fraud, not content fraud: assembling a package after QA saw a different one, disbursing twice on one notification, re-running a credit check after a rejection, or spawning a second application on an already-funded file. The chain and negative constraints attack exactly these windows.
* **Risk management.** `exactly_one(Preliminary_Credit_Check)` fixes a single point of truth for the risk grade; `precedence(QA, Authorize)` guarantees the risk file was independently validated before the bank commits contractually.
* **Operational best practice / cost.** Ordering the credit check before document gathering avoids collecting paperwork for applications that will be declined — cheaper for the bank, less intrusive for the customer.
* **Customer experience and trust.** The alternating notify/transfer pattern means the customer is never surprised by money appearing or not appearing. `coexistence(Gather_Additional_Documents, Authorize_Contract_Terms)` means "if we asked you for paperwork, you will get a decision" — no silent abandonment.
* **Auditability.** Every constraint is machine-checkable against the event log, so conformance checking can flag deviations automatically rather than through sampling.

## 4. Where the model, read literally, breaks — and what that tells us

It is worth being precise here, because a formally inconsistent model can be *more* dangerous than a loose one: it will reject every real trace, and teams then start ignoring the checker.

**(a) The model as written is unsatisfiable.**
`existence(Receive_Application)` and `init(Receive_Application)` make intake mandatory. `noncoexistence(Transfer_Funds, Receive_Application)` then makes **Transfer_Funds impossible** in every valid trace. The prohibition cascades:

* No `Transfer_Funds`  by `chainresponse(Assemble_Loan_Offer_Package  Transfer_Funds)`, no `Assemble_Loan_Offer_Package`.
* No `Assemble_Loan_Offer_Package`  by `chainsuccession(Quality_Assurance_Review  Assemble_Loan_Offer_Package)`, no `Quality_Assurance_Review`.
* But `existence(Gather_Additional_Documents)` + `altsuccession(Gather  QA)` *requires* a QA review, and `coexistence(Gather, Authorize)` + `precedence(QA, Authorize)` requires one too.

Contradiction: **no trace satisfies the model**. A loan process that can never disburse funds is not a loan process.
*Likely intended rule:* `nonsuccession(Transfer_Funds  Receive_Application)` ("no new application after disbursement on the same case") or `noncoexistence(Transfer_Funds, Proceed_Without_Compliance)`.

**(b) `chainprecedence(Authorize_Contract_Terms, Preliminary_Credit_Check)` is circular under either reading.**
If it means "the credit check must be immediately preceded by authorisation", it inverts the whole flow (authorise  check  documents  QA  authorise …), which given `exactly_one(Preliminary_Credit_Check)` is impossible. If it means "authorisation must be immediately preceded by the credit check", it collides with `precedence(QA, Authorize)` plus `succession(PCC  Gather)` plus `altsuccession(Gather  QA)`: QA must fall *after* the credit check yet *before* an authorisation that must immediately follow the credit check.
*Likely intended rule:* `chainprecedence(Authorize_Contract_Terms, Quality_Assurance_Review)` — "authorisation is immediately preceded by QA sign-off", which is the classic four-eyes control.

**(c) A genuine control gap: nothing forces authorisation before disbursement.**
Even ignoring (a) and (b), the only constraints on `Authorize_Contract_Terms` are that QA precedes it and that it coexists with document gathering. `Transfer_Funds`, meanwhile, is chained to `Assemble_Loan_Offer_Package`. Therefore a fully "compliant" trace exists in which **money leaves the bank before the contract terms are ever authorised** — the single worst outcome the model was presumably designed to prevent.
*Missing rule:* `precedence(Authorize_Contract_Terms, Transfer_Funds)`, ideally `chainprecedence`.

**(d) The notification ordering is inverted for a first drawdown.**
`altprecedence(Notify_Customer, Transfer_Funds)` requires a notification *before* the very first transfer. Charitably this is the "decision in principle / disbursement scheduled" message; but combined with `nonsuccession(Notify_Customer  Preliminary_Credit_Check)` it means the credit check is irrevocably locked before any customer contact — fine — while also implying the customer is contacted before the QA/packaging block even starts.

**A repaired model** (swap the `noncoexistence` for a `nonsuccession`, re-target the `chainprecedence` to QA, add `precedence(Authorize_Contract_Terms, Transfer_Funds)`) yields the intuitive trace:

`Receive_Application  Preliminary_Credit_Check  Gather_Additional_Documents  Quality_Assurance_Review  Assemble_Loan_Offer_Package  Authorize_Contract_Terms  Notify_Customer(decision)  Transfer_Funds  Notify_Customer(confirmation)`

…with the chain constraints re-anchored so that QApackageauthorisedisburse form the tamper-proof core.

## 5. What happens when the rules are broken

| Violation | Immediate operational effect | Downstream consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Documents gathered before the credit check** (`succession` broken) | Staff request evidence they may not need; customers submit sensitive data for applications that will be declined | Data-minimisation breach, wasted handling cost, poor NPS, possible complaint that the decline was pre-determined |
| **Credit check run twice** (`exactly_one` broken) | Two conflicting risk grades in one file | Score shopping; mispriced exposure; multiple hard searches damaging the applicant's bureau file; provisioning and capital calculations based on an unverifiable grade |
| **Offer package assembled without/before QA** (`chainsuccession`, `responded_existence` broken) | Unreviewed terms reach the customer | Mis-selling, unenforceable or non-compliant clauses, mandatory remediation and redress programmes, regulatory censure |
| **Terms authorised without QA** (`precedence` broken) | Single-signature approval | Four-eyes control failure — the archetypal internal-fraud enabler; audit finding; potentially void contract |
| **Funds transferred before authorisation** (gap (c) above) | Unauthorised exposure on the balance sheet | Loss with no contractual recourse; AML exposure if the payee was never validated; misstated financials |
| **Two transfers with no notification between** (`altresponse`/`altprecedence` broken) | Duplicate or unnoticed drawdown | Duplicate payout fraud; the customer cannot detect the error until reconciliation; liability for unauthorised transactions typically falls on the bank |
| **Credit check re-run after notifying the customer** (`nonsuccession` broken) | Evidence created after the decision | Looks — and may legally count — as fabrication of the decision rationale |
| **Authorisation immediately followed by notification** (`nonchainsuccession` broken) | Post-authorisation controls (archiving, funding checks, sanctions re-screen) skipped | Customer informed of terms that later have to be retracted; reputational and legal fallout |
| **Documents gathered but no authorisation event** (`coexistence` broken) | Case abandoned mid-flight | Customer left in limbo holding a hard search and submitted ID; complaint and regulatory attention on treating-customers-fairly grounds |
| **Any `Proceed_Without_Compliance`** (`absence` broken) | Deliberate control bypass | Not a process deviation but a compliance incident: mandatory reporting, individual accountability, potential enforcement action |
| **Application not initiated by intake** (`init` broken) | Work begun off-book | No KYC baseline, no consent record, no audit trail — the file cannot be defended at all |

## 6. Bottom line

Read charitably, the model describes a disciplined lending pipeline: **log the application  assess once  collect evidence  review  package under review  authorise  tell the customer  pay  tell the customer again**, with hard chains around the review-package-pay core to prevent tampering, alternation around money-and-message to prevent duplicate payouts, and an absolute ban on compliance bypass.

Read literally, the model is a useful cautionary example: a single over-strong negative constraint (`noncoexistence(Transfer_Funds, Receive_Application)`) and one mis-targeted chain constraint make it unsatisfiable, while the one control that actually matters most — *authorise before you pay* — is not encoded at all. Before deploying such a model for conformance checking, it should be run through a satisfiability check and a "worst compliant trace" analysis: not just "does every real trace fit?", but "what is the worst behaviour the model still permits?"
