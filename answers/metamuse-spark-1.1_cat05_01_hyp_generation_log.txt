### PROMPT TO COPY TO TARGET LLM

You are a Senior Process Mining and Forensic Analyst expert in Order-to-Cash processes.

You have access to a PostgreSQL database with the following schema:

**1. Table: `order_event_log`**
*   `case_id` INTEGER - Order identifier
*   `event_id` INTEGER - Primary key
*   `activity` VARCHAR - Name of the process activity
*   `timestamp` TIMESTAMP - When the activity occurred
*   `resource` VARCHAR - Who performed the activity
*   `additional_info` VARCHAR - Semi-structured context like `key=value`

**2. Table: `orders`**
*   `case_id` INTEGER
*   `customer_id` INTEGER
*   `order_type` VARCHAR - e.g., standard, priority
*   `order_value` DECIMAL

**3. Table: `resources`**
*   `resource_id` VARCHAR - joins to `order_event_log.resource`
*   `role` VARCHAR - e.g., SalesRep, CreditOfficer, WarehouseClerk
*   `department` VARCHAR - e.g., Sales, Finance, Logistics

**Assumed Normal Process Flow is:**
1. Register Order
2. Perform Credit Check
3. Validate Stock
4. Confirm Shipment
5. Ship Goods
6. Issue Invoice
7. Receive Payment

**Example data from `order_event_log`:**

**Your Task:**

You have been asked to audit the full event log for process inefficiencies, compliance risks, and undesirable behaviors.

1.  **Identify Anomalies:** Based on control-flow, temporal, resource, and data perspectives, what anomalies or deviations from expected behavior can you observe or suspect?
2.  **Hypothesize Causes:** For each anomaly you identify, hypothesize plausible root causes. Consider system errors, policy violations, human error, lack of training, intentional circumvention, high workload, etc.
3.  **Propose Investigation with SQL:** For each hypothesis, propose specific, executable PostgreSQL queries against `order_event_log`, `orders`, and `resources` that would allow an analyst to validate, quantify, and further investigate the issue across the entire database. Explain what each query is intended to check.

Do not limit your analysis to only the example rows shown. Assume your queries will run on thousands of cases. Structure your answer clearly.
