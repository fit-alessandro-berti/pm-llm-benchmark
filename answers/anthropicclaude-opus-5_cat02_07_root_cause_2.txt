## 3. Root-cause analysis by attribute

### Complexity — the dominant driver
| Complexity | Cases | Avg duration | Avg # doc requests |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low | 2001, 2004 | **1 h 28 m** | 0 |
| Medium | 2002 | **25 h 55 m** | 1 |
| High | 2003, 2005 | **62 h 43 m** | 2.5 |

Duration scales almost linearly with the number of "Request Additional Documents" events: 0  1.5 h, 1  26 h, 2  48 h, 3  77 h. **Each additional request adds roughly 22–25 hours (one calendar day cycle).** This is classic *rework / iterative information gathering*: the adjuster asks for documents piecemeal instead of once, and each round-trip costs a full day of waiting.

### Resource
- **Adjuster_Lisa (Region B)** generates the longest inter-request gaps (2005: 29.5 h and 22 h between consecutive requests) and the slowest EvaluateRequest step in 2002 (4 h 15 m). She is simultaneously the only adjuster in Region B and is handling Low, Medium and High cases — a likely overload/queueing effect.
- **Manager_Bill** appears *only* on High-complexity claims and adds **19–23 h** of waiting before approval, whereas **Manager_Ann** approves in **25–30 minutes** on straight-through cases. Part of this is confounded with complexity (documents must arrive first), but Bill is clearly a single point of dependency for the high-complexity queue with no visible SLA.
- **Finance:** Finance_Carl pays in 30 m–45 m normally but took 4 h in case 2005; Finance_Alan took 17 h in case 2003 purely because approval landed at 16:00. Payment is a minor contributor but shows an end-of-day cutoff.
- **CSRs (Jane, Paul, Mary)** are consistently fast — not a cause.

## 4. Explanations

1. **Iterative, un-batched information requests.** High-complexity claims are evaluated without a complete documentation checklist, so the adjuster discovers missing items one at a time. Each discovery triggers a new request and a new day-long wait for the claimant/third party.
2. **Specialist bottleneck for approvals.** High-complexity claims are routed exclusively to Manager_Bill, creating a single-server queue with no backup and no approval SLA.
3. **Regional under-capacity.** One adjuster per region means a High case (which consumes multiple touches over days) blocks capacity for other claims; Region B's Lisa handles all three of its cases.
4. **Business-hours / end-of-day truncation.** Events created after ~16:00 are not picked up until the next morning, so every loop iteration effectively rounds up to a full calendar day. With 3 loops that alone explains ~3 days.

## 5. Recommendations

**Eliminate the rework loop (biggest win)**
- Introduce a **complexity-specific documentation checklist** validated at Submit/Evaluate; request *all* missing items in a single consolidated request.
- Add an automated **completeness check at intake** (portal validation, mandatory attachments) so High-complexity claims arrive complete.
- Deploy a **self-service document upload portal with reminders** to shorten claimant response time; measure and report "customer wait" separately from "insurer wait" so the two are not confused.
- Set a hard rule: **maximum one additional-document request**; a second request requires supervisor review (turns rework into an exception, not the norm).

**Fix the approval bottleneck**
- Cross-train/authorize a **second high-complexity approver** (e.g., certify Manager_Ann or a deputy) and load-balance the queue.
- Define **SLAs**: approval within 4 working hours of documents being complete; payment within 2 hours of approval; alert dashboards on breaches.

**Capacity and routing**
- Add adjuster capacity or a **shared cross-region pool** for High-complexity claims so a single adjuster (Lisa/Mike) is not a chokepoint; consider a dedicated "complex claims" team with specialist skills.
- Route Low-complexity claims to a **fast-track / straight-through path** (already achieving 1.5 h) and protect it from complex-case queueing.

**Reduce calendar effects**
- Trigger requests and approvals **earlier in the day** (work-scheduling rule: no new request initiated after 15:00 without same-day dispatch), or enable extended/staggered shifts and automated payment runs so approvals late in the day are not parked overnight.

**Monitoring**
- Track KPIs: cases per complexity band, number of document requests per case, average wait per transition, per-resource waiting time, and per-region throughput — this makes the "each extra request = one lost day" relationship visible and manageable.
