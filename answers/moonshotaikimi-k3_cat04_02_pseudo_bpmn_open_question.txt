# Redesigning the Process: Automation, Prediction, and Adaptive Resourcing

Below is a structured redesign proposal, followed by task-level changes, new process constructs, and an honest assessment of the trade-offs.

## 1. Guiding Principles

Three levers, applied selectively:

- **Automate the deterministic** (validation, invoicing, notifications)  straight-through processing (STP).
- **Predict the probabilistic** (request type, cycle time, approval outcome)  route *before* humans confirm what the data already suggests.
- **Humanize the exceptional**  people handle exceptions, high-value custom work, and judgment calls, supported by AI rather than replaced by it.

A key strategic insight: the binary Standard/Custom split is itself a bottleneck. The goal should be to **shrink the custom lane** (via configuration tools and modularization) and **dissolve the rigid boundary** (via probabilistic triage).

## 4. New Gateways and Subprocesses

1. **Predictive Triage Subprocess (P):** Scores every request on customization probability, complexity, and risk. Requests the *customer* labeled "standard" but that historically convert to custom get flagged early — directly addressing proactive routing. Low-confidence scores default to human review (never auto-reject on model output).
2. **Customer Clarification Subprocess:** Uses BPMN message events to pause the process while awaiting customer input, instead of letting requests rot in a queue consuming WIP and skewing cycle-time metrics.
3. **Alternative-Offer Subprocess (E2'):** Converts rejections into recoverable revenue and goodwill.
4. **Mid-Flight Change Event Subprocess:** A non-interrupting event subprocess handling order modifications/cancellations — essential for "flexibility in non-standard requests," which the original linear model cannot accommodate.
5. **Dynamic Resource Orchestration (not BPMN-visible, but architecturally central):**
   - Priority-scored work queues (score = customer tier × SLA risk × value).
   - Skill-based routing of engineering reviews; load balancing across reviewers.
   - **Predictive escalation:** if the cycle-time model predicts an SLA breach, resources are reallocated *before* the breach, not after.
   - Volume forecasting per lane to pre-position staffing for expected custom-request surges.
6. **Ad hoc/case-management lane:** Truly novel custom work may be better modeled as a *case* (CMMN-style) with guardrails and milestones rather than a rigid sequence — acknowledging that not all work is repeatable enough for prescriptive flow.

## 5. Impact Analysis

**Overall performance**
- Standard lane becomes near-real-time STP: intake-to-confirmation in minutes rather than days for the majority of volume.
- Parallel checks gain timer-based escalation, so the join no longer blocks on the slowest branch indefinitely.
- Removing the approval bottleneck and shrinking the re-evaluation loop attacks the two largest sources of P90 (tail) latency — which matters more to customers than median speed.
- Throughput rises with flat headcount; human effort concentrates on high-value custom engineering.

**Customer satisfaction**
- Faster quotes, accurate delivery promises (confidence intervals build trust), proactive status updates, and fewer flat rejections.
- Risks: automated decisions can feel opaque; customers flagged by models shouldn't experience unexplained friction. Mitigate with clear communication and an easy path to a human.

**Operational complexity (the honest cost)**
- You trade process complexity for *system* complexity: ML models need data pipelines, drift monitoring, retraining, explainability (especially for credit and pricing — potentially regulated), and champion/challenger governance.
- Integration surface grows (ERP, CRM, CPQ, credit bureaus); fallback manual modes are mandatory for outages.
- Exception taxonomies must be deliberately designed — "automate by exception" fails if everything becomes an exception.
- Staff need reskilling, and automation bias (over-trusting model outputs) requires calibration and audit sampling.
- Dynamic routing makes the process harder to document, test, and certify for compliance.

## 6. Sequencing and Measurement

**Crawl  walk  run:**
1. **Phase 1:** Instrument everything; automate deterministic tasks (intake, validation, invoicing); API-enable parallel checks. Low risk, immediate ROI, and generates the training data for later phases.
2. **Phase 2:** Risk-based approvals, CPQ for the custom lane, alternative-offer subprocess.
3. **Phase 3:** Predictive triage, predictive ETAs, predictive resource allocation — deployed first as *decision support* (recommendations humans confirm), graduating to automation as accuracy is proven.

**KPIs:** P50/P90 cycle time, STP rate, first-pass yield, approval-loop frequency and iteration count, quote turnaround, delivery-promise accuracy, CSAT/NPS, cost per order. Feed all of it into process mining to continuously compare the modeled process against reality — the redesigned process should be treated as a living system, not a fixed diagram.
