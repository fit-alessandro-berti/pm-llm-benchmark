**This visualization is a Causal Net (C-net), a modeling language from process mining (introduced by Wil van der Aalst) that focuses on activities and their causal dependencies.**

It represents a classic “Request for Compensation” (RFC) process—e.g., a customer requesting compensation from an airline for a delayed/cancelled flight. Unlike Petri nets (which use places/tokens) or BPMN (which uses explicit gateway diamonds), a C-net connects activities directly with arcs and uses **black dots** to indicate *input/output bindings*. These bindings define the allowed sets of predecessors and successors for each activity and thereby encode the routing logic (choices, concurrency, loops).

### Activities
| Label | Activity              | Role |
|-------|-----------------------|------|
| **a** | register request      | Start activity |
| **b** | examine thoroughly    | Detailed investigation of the request |
| **c** | examine casually      | Lighter investigation of the request |
| **d** | check ticket          | Administrative verification that a valid ticket exists |
| **e** | decide                | Decision point after examination + check |
| **f** | reinitiate request    | Loop back for more work / additional information |
| **g** | pay compensation      | Positive outcome |
| **h** | reject request        | Negative outcome |
| **z** | end                   | End activity |

### Overall Process Flow
1. The process always begins with **a (register request)**.
2. After registration, exactly one of the two examinations must occur **together with** the ticket check:
   - Either **b + d** (examine thoroughly *and* check ticket), or
   - **c + d** (examine casually *and* check ticket).  
   The examination and the ticket check may run concurrently or in either order.
3. Once both the chosen examination and the ticket check are finished, **e (decide)** occurs.
4. After the decision there is an exclusive choice:
   - **g (pay compensation)** → **z (end)**
   - **h (reject request)** → **z (end)**
   - **f (reinitiate request)** → the process loops back and the examination + ticket-check combination must be performed again, after which another decision is made.
5. The process terminates only when **z** is reached (after a final pay or reject).

### How the Diagram Encodes This (Bindings & Legend)
The black filled circles are the visual representation of the **bindings**:
- On the *output* side of an activity they group the activities that must be enabled together.
- On the *input* side they group the activities that must have completed before the current activity can fire.

Typical bindings for this model (matching the arcs and dots in the figure) are:

- **a** (start): empty input; output bindings `{b, d}` or `{c, d}`  
  → always pair the ticket check with exactly one examination type.
- **b / c / d**: each has a simple input from a (or from the loop via f) and outputs toward e.
- **e**: input bindings `{b, d}` or `{c, d}`; output bindings `{f}`, `{g}` or `{h}`  
  → pure exclusive choice among reinitiate / pay / reject.
- **f**: receives the obligation from e and re-creates the same output obligations that a had (`{b, d}` or `{c, d}`), closing the loop.
- **g / h**: simple pass-through to **z**.
- **z**: accepts input from either g or h; empty output.

The small grey legend in the lower-right corner shows the conventional visual patterns used for the three classic gateway types when they appear as bindings:

- **XOR-split / XOR-join** – exclusive choice (exactly one branch).
- **AND-split / AND-join** – all branches (true concurrency).
- **OR-split / OR-join** – inclusive choice (any non-empty subset of branches).

In the main diagram you can recognise these patterns around a (the combined XOR between b/c + AND with d), around e (the join of the examination+check pair and the subsequent XOR split), and at the final merge into z.

### Why Causal Nets Are Used
C-nets are deliberately tailored for process discovery. By making the possible combinations of predecessors and successors explicit via bindings, they avoid many of the artificial constructs (invisible transitions, extra places, etc.) that appear when the same behaviour is modelled as a Petri net or BPMN diagram. The resulting model is compact, focuses purely on causal relations, and still precisely defines which sequences of activities are valid.

In short, the figure shows a flexible yet well-defined request-handling workflow that supports concurrent examination and ticket checking, an exclusive choice of examination thoroughness, a re-work loop, and a final exclusive outcome of payment or rejection—all expressed through activity boxes, dependency arcs, and the black-dot binding notation explained in the legend.
