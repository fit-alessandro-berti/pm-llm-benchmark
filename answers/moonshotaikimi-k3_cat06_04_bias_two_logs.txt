# Analysis: Which Log Exhibits Bias?

**The Group B (Unprotected Group) log exhibits bias.** Group A's process applies a uniform, score-only decision rule. Group B's process embeds preferential treatment triggered by the `LocalResident` and `CommunityGroup` attributes, encoded through the `ScoreAdjustment` column.

---

## 1. The Mechanism: Where the Bias Enters

| Attribute | Role in the Bias |
|---|---|
| **LocalResident** | Acts as a *gate*: only residents (all of Group B) are eligible for adjustments. Group A (all `FALSE`) can never receive a boost — the possibility itself is excluded by this attribute. |
| **CommunityGroup** | Acts as the *trigger*: within Group B, only members of the "Highland Civic Darts Club" receive the **+10 Community Boost**. U002 — a local resident with no membership — gets nothing. |
| **ScoreAdjustment** | The *delivery mechanism*. In Group A it is uniformly `0`. In Group B it is applied automatically by the Scoring Engine at `PreliminaryScoring` — i.e., the bias is **systemic, not discretionary** — and then propagates: the underwriter at `ManualReview` sees the inflated "730 (Adjusted)" / "705 (Adjusted)" figures (an anchoring effect), and the `FinalDecision` inherits them. No downstream step corrects it. |

## 3. Why This Bias Is Easy to Miss

- **Aggregate parity masks it.** Both groups have identical approval rates (2/3  66.7%). A demographic-parity check would declare the process fair, while individual-level fairness is clearly violated.
- **Within-group discrimination is hidden.** U002 and U003 differ *only* in club membership; the member is approved, the non-member rejected, despite U002's higher raw score.
- **Score comparability is destroyed.** A "705" in Group B does not mean the same thing as a "705" would in Group A, so the logs can no longer be compared on merit terms.

## 4. Systematic Consequences

1. **Disparate treatment by proxy:** Because the boost is conditioned on residency and club membership, any protected characteristic correlated with those attributes (e.g., long-established local communities vs. newcomers, demographic composition of the club) inherits the advantage or penalty — producing **disparate impact** even if the rule is facially neutral.
2. **Cumulative/intersectional advantage:** The benefit stacks — one must be local *and* a club member — so non-residents (all of Group A) and unaffiliated residents (U002) are doubly excluded.
3. **Process entrenchment:** Because the adjustment originates in the automated Scoring Engine and is merely *displayed* at manual review, human oversight ratifies rather than corrects it, making the bias repeatable and scalable.

## 5. Caveat and Recommendation

If the "Community Boost" is a deliberate policy (e.g., a community-reinvestment incentive), it constitutes **explicit differential treatment** — its legality and legitimacy must be justified as such, since it demonstrably overrides merit ordering. To remediate: remove or uniformly apply the adjustment, decouple eligibility from `LocalResident`/`CommunityGroup`, report raw and adjusted scores separately at review, and audit decisions with **counterfactual testing** (e.g., "would U003 have been approved without membership?" — the logs strongly suggest no), since group-level approval rates alone fail to detect this bias.
