**High-Level Process Steps**

Based on temporal proximity, resource involvement (operators vs. machines/sensors), logical process flow, and domain semantics in metal-sheet manufacturing, the low-level events consistently map to the following five higher-level activities (identical pattern across cases A1 and B2):

1. **Material Preparation**  
   Low-level events: Retrieve raw metal sheet → Scan barcode → Place metal sheet on table → Align metal sheet → Preheat metal sheet  
   **Rationale**: These form the initial setup phase. They occur in rapid succession at the start of each case, primarily involve Operator A (human handling + identification) plus positioning/heating equipment, and prepare a single raw sheet for subsequent value-adding work. Barcode scanning provides traceability; alignment and preheating are enabling conditions for welding.

2. **Welding Assembly**  
   Low-level events: Pick up welding tool → Weld corner A → Weld corner B  
   **Rationale**: Core joining/assembly operations performed by Operator B with the same tool. They form a contiguous sequence focused on structural fabrication of the part; tool pickup is a direct prerequisite for the welds.

3. **Weld Integrity Inspection**  
   Low-level events: Measure weld integrity  
   **Rationale**: Immediate post-assembly quality-assurance check performed by a dedicated sensor. It evaluates the just-completed welds (IntegrityScore) and acts as a distinct decision gate before surface finishing proceeds.

4. **Protective Coating**  
   Low-level events: Apply protective coating → Dry coating  
   **Rationale**: Surface-finishing phase executed by specialized coating and heating units. Application and curing are sequential, process-linked steps that protect the welded part; they share a common functional goal distinct from structural assembly or inspection.

5. **Final Visual Inspection**  
   Low-level events: Visual check  
   **Rationale**: Terminal human quality-control step performed by Operator C. It confirms overall product acceptability after all transformation and finishing activities are complete.

**Resulting Aggregated Process Flow (per case)**  
Material Preparation → Welding Assembly → Weld Integrity Inspection → Protective Coating → Final Visual Inspection

This abstraction collapses 12 granular events into 5 coherent manufacturing stages, improving readability for process mining, bottleneck analysis, or workflow visualization while preserving the original causal and temporal order.
