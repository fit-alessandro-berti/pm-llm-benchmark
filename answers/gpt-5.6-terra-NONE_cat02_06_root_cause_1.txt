## 2. Likely root causes of the delays

### Case 102: Escalation and overnight resolution delay

Key waiting periods:

| Transition | Delay |
|---|---:|
| Assign Level-1  Escalate Level-2 | 2h 30m |
| Escalate Level-2  Investigate | 2h 30m |
| Investigate  Resolve | 19h |

Case 102 includes a **Level-2 escalation**, unlike the fast cases. The largest delay occurs after investigation begins: investigation is recorded at 14:00, but resolution does not occur until 09:00 the following day.

Potential causes:
- Level-2 team may have limited availability or a queue.
- The issue may require more technical work, information, or cross-team dependencies.
- The ticket may have waited overnight without an after-hours support process.
- Investigation may not have been actively worked continuously; the timestamp gap may indicate a handoff, queue wait, or unresolved dependency.

### Case 104: Delayed start of investigation and overnight resolution

Key waiting periods:

| Transition | Delay |
|---|---:|
| Receive  Triage | 40m |
| Assign Level-1  Investigate | 3h 30m |
| Investigate  Resolve | 19h |
| Resolve  Close | 30m |

Case 104 has **no escalation**, so its delay appears to be caused primarily by internal waiting rather than increased technical routing.

Its main problems are:

1. **Long queue time before investigation:**  
   The ticket is assigned at 09:30 but investigation does not start until 13:00, a delay of 3.5 hours. This suggests possible Level-1 workload imbalance, insufficient capacity, lack of prioritization, or delayed agent pickup.

2. **Very long investigation-to-resolution period:**  
   After investigation starts at 13:00, resolution occurs only at 08:00 the next day. This 19-hour gap suggests work was paused overnight, waiting on information, or not completed within the same shift.

This case is important because it shows that escalations are not the only source of poor cycle time. Even the standard Level-1 path can become slow when ticket queues or handoffs delay active work.

### Case 105: Escalation plus very long Level-2 waiting time

Key waiting periods:

| Transition | Delay |
|---|---:|
| Investigate  Escalate | 50m |
| Escalate Level-2  Investigate | 28h |
| Level-2 Investigate  Resolve | 19h |
| Resolve  Close | 30m |

Case 105 is the longest case by a substantial margin. The most notable issue is the **28-hour delay between escalation and Level-2 investigation**.

This strongly suggests:
- A significant Level-2 support queue or backlog.
- Insufficient specialized-team capacity.
- Escalations possibly being processed only during limited business hours.
- Weak escalation prioritization or unclear ownership after handoff.
- Delays in transfer of context, diagnostic information, or ticket ownership.

Even after Level-2 investigation begins, the ticket takes another 19 hours to resolve, which may reflect complexity, overnight delay, or dependency on another team/system.

## 3. Impact on cycle times and recommendations

### Main observed patterns

#### A. Escalations correlate with longer cycle times
Both escalated tickets are slow:

- Case 102: **25h 10m**
- Case 105: **49h 05m**

Escalation itself may be necessary for complex issues, but the log suggests that the biggest problem is not merely escalation—it is the **waiting time after escalation**, especially in Case 105.

**Recommendation:**
- Measure and manage an explicit SLA for:
  - escalation acceptance,
  - Level-2 assignment,
  - Level-2 investigation start,
  - resolution after Level-2 intake.
- Implement automated routing, alerts, and ownership assignment immediately upon escalation.
- Provide Level-1 agents with clear escalation criteria and required diagnostic templates so Level-2 receives complete context and does not need to wait for additional information.
- Review Level-2 staffing, workload, and on-call coverage, especially if escalations frequently wait overnight.

#### B. Long delays before investigation indicate queueing or capacity issues
Case 104 waits **3h 30m** after assignment before investigation. Case 102 also has several multi-hour waits before Level-2 investigation.

These delays increase end-to-end ticket time even when the actual hands-on work may be limited.

**Recommendation:**
- Track “assignment-to-first-action” time as a key operational metric.
- Balance tickets across agents based on workload and skill.
- Use queue aging alerts for tickets not investigated within a target threshold.
- Introduce triage-based priority rules so high-impact tickets are investigated faster.
- Ensure assignments represent active ownership rather than simply placing a ticket in an agent’s queue.

#### C. Overnight gaps dominate the delayed cases
Cases 102, 104, and 105 each have approximately **19-hour gaps** between investigation and resolution, while Case 105 also has a 28-hour post-escalation wait.

This implies that the process may effectively pause outside business hours, or that tickets are left without clear next actions before the end of a shift.

**Recommendation:**
- Identify whether these overnight gaps are expected business-hour effects or avoidable backlog.
- For urgent or high-priority tickets, use on-call coverage or follow-the-sun support.
- Require agents to document pending actions and next owners before shift end.
- Add service targets based on business hours where appropriate, while still monitoring elapsed time for customer impact.
- Improve customer/self-service information collection early in the process to reduce delays caused by waiting for clarification.

#### D. Closing is not a major bottleneck
Closing delays are small across all cases:

- 101: 15 minutes
- 102: 15 minutes
- 103: 15 minutes
- 104: 30 minutes
- 105: 30 minutes

Therefore, process-improvement efforts should focus on **investigation start, escalation handling, and resolution**, not the closure step.

---

## Overall conclusion

The delayed cases are **102, 104, and especially 105**. The strongest performance issues are:

1. **Long Level-2 escalation queues**, particularly in Case 105.
2. **Delays before an assigned agent begins investigation**, as seen in Case 104.
3. **Large overnight or inactive periods between investigation and resolution** across all delayed cases.
4. Potentially limited specialist capacity, weak handoff ownership, or unresolved dependencies during investigation.

The most impactful improvements would be to reduce post-escalation waiting time, enforce faster first investigation after assignment, actively manage aging tickets, and improve coverage and handoff practices across shifts.
