# FollowUp Interview Questions: Property Onboarding Process

## 1. Process Entry & Triggers

1. You mentioned the process begins when a landlord contacts the regional office. What are all the ways an onboarding can be initiated — do inbound referrals, broker partnerships, or portfolio acquisitions (multiple properties at once) enter the process differently?
2. Is there any qualification or screening of the *landlord* or the property before the coordinator invests time in document review? For example, do you ever decline properties, and if so, on what grounds?
3. How does the process differ when an existing landlord adds a second, third, or tenth property versus a brandnew landlord relationship?
4. Does a property that is currently tenanted (an occupied unit transferring management) follow the same path, or is there a separate flow?

## 2. Documentation Review Stage

5. Walk me through what "verified" means for documentation — is there a formal checklist, and who has the authority to declare documentation complete?
6. When documents are missing or outdated, how is the request for updates communicated, and how long do you typically wait before following up or closing the case?
7. Is there a point at which an incomplete application is abandoned or archived? Who decides that, and does the landlord get notified?
8. Can the process move forward in parallel while a minor document is still outstanding, or is verification a hard gate before anything else happens?
9. Roughly what proportion of submissions arrive complete on the first attempt, and what are the most common gaps?

## 3. Data Entry & System of Record

10. Who enters the property details into the central database, and is there any review or second check on the accuracy of that data?
11. Where does the "expected rental price range" come from at this early stage — the landlord's expectation, a coordinator's judgment, or a system estimate? How is a disagreement handled?
12. Is the property record created as a single entity even when it contains many units, or is each unit onboarded separately?

## 4. Property Manager Assignment

13. You listed four factors for assigning a property manager — workload, geography, propertytype experience, and landlordrequirement track record. How are these weighted when they conflict, and is there a formal rule or is it judgmentbased?
14. Who actually makes the assignment decision — the coordinator, a regional supervisor, an automated system, or a combination?
15. What happens if no suitable manager is available (all at capacity, or no one with the right specialization)? Is onboarding paused, is the property queued, or is it assigned anyway?
16. How is "current workload" measured — number of properties, number of units, revenue, or something else?
17. Can a property be reassigned midonboarding? What typically triggers that, and how is continuity with the landlord maintained?
18. Do landlords ever request a specific manager or reject an assigned one? How is that handled?

## 5. Introductory Meeting & Expectation Setting

19. Is the introductory call mandatory, or can it be skipped for straightforward properties? Who decides?
20. What is the output of that meeting — is anything formally documented or signed, or is it informal alignment?
21. What happens if the special conditions the landlord raises conflict with company policy (e.g., a pet policy or payment channel you don't support)? Who has authority to grant an exception?
22. At what point is the management agreement between the company and the landlord actually signed — before documentation review, after the intro meeting, or later? Does the onboarding proceed at risk before signature?

## 6. Photography & Inspection

23. Are the photographer and inspector scheduled simultaneously or sequentially, and are they typically internal staff or external vendors?
24. Are photography and inspection genuinely parallel activities, or does one depend on the other's outcome (e.g., do you wait for a clean inspection before spending on photography)?
25. What are the typical lead times to get a photographer and an inspector on site, and what causes the longest delays?
26. When the inspector finds issues, how are they categorized — is there a distinction between blocking defects and cosmetic items that can be resolved later?
27. Who is responsible for the cost of remediation, and who selects and manages the contractors — the landlord, the property manager, or a company vendor panel?
28. Is there a reinspection, and is there a limit on how many remediation cycles you'll go through before pausing or exiting the property?
29. What happens if the landlord refuses to fix an identified issue? Does that terminate the onboarding, or are there conditional paths forward?
30. Do photos ever need to be redone after repairs, and who notices that?

## 7. Marketing & Pricing

31. Who owns the final rental price — the landlord, the property manager, the financial analyst, or the digital marketing team's pricing model?
32. How is a disagreement between the landlord's desired price and the pricing model resolved, and who escalates it?
33. Is marketing content created from scratch each time, or from templates? Who approves the listing before it goes live — is there a landlord signoff?
34. What is the handoff mechanism between the property manager and the digital marketing team, and how do they know a property is ready for them?
35. How long does marketing production typically take, and is it the same for all property types?

## 8. Activation

36. What exactly happens at "activation" — is it a single system action or a set of steps across multiple systems? Who performs it?
37. Are there any prerequisites checked at activation (e.g., signed management agreement, passed inspection, approved pricing), and is that check automated or manual?
38. Can a property be deactivated or put on hold after going live? What are the common reasons?

## 9. Tenant Screening & Selection

39. When is it the property manager versus a leasing agent who handles screening? What determines that split?
40. What are the actual pass/fail criteria in screening — is there a scorecard for credit, income ratio, employment, and references, or is it a holistic judgment?
41. Who has final approval authority over a tenant, and can a landlord veto or override that choice?
42. In a multipleapplicant situation, how do you decide among them? You mentioned lease length as a factor — what else, and how do you keep that consistent with fair housing obligations?
43. Who is authorized to approve promotional discounts or nonstandard lease terms, and are there limits?
44. What happens when no acceptable applicant appears for an extended period — is there a defined trigger for repricing or remarketing, and who initiates it?
45. What happens if a screened, approved tenant withdraws after selection but before movein? Do you restart, or go to a backup applicant?

## 10. Lease Execution & MoveIn

46. What are the discrete steps between "tenant chosen" and "movein complete," and which of them can happen in parallel?
47. When is the security deposit collected relative to lease signing, and what happens if it isn't paid on time?
48. Is there a movein condition report or key handover step, and who performs it?
49. At what point do you consider the onboarding process formally "complete"? Is it lease signature, movein, first rent payment, or passing the audits?

## 11. Quality Checks, Compliance & Audits

50. You said a compliance officer "might" review the lease and a financial analyst "might" verify pricing — are these mandatory, sampled, or triggered by specific conditions? What are those triggers?
51. Do these reviews happen before the lease is executed or after? If after, what happens when a problem is found on an alreadysigned lease?
52. Are the compliance, financial, and IT checks sequential or parallel, and does any of them have blocking authority?
53. What are the most common findings from each of these three reviews, and how are corrections tracked to closure?
54. Is there any postonboarding review at, say, 30 or 90 days to confirm everything was set up correctly?

## 12. Exceptions & Variants

55. You mentioned special insurance requirements and custom lease clauses. What are the other recurring exception types, and roughly how often does each occur?
56. For custom lease clauses, who drafts and approves them — is legal counsel involved, and what's the turnaround?
57. Is there a defined escalation path when an exception exceeds the property manager's authority? Who are the escalation levels?
58. Are exceptions logged anywhere so that patterns can be spotted, or are they handled case by case in email?
59. Which property types (student housing, luxury, furnished, multiunit, HOAgoverned) deviate most from the standard flow, and how?
60. Have you ever had to unwind or reverse an onboarding partway through? What did that look like?

## 13. Timing, Volume & Performance

61. End to end, what is the typical elapsed time from landlord inquiry to tenant movein — and what does the best case versus worst case look like?
62. Which steps consume the most calendar time, and which consume the most staff effort? Are those the same steps?
63. Where do properties most often get stuck or sit idle, and is anyone monitoring aging cases?
64. What volume of properties do you onboard per month per region, and how seasonal is that?
65. What metrics or SLAs, if any, are tracked on this process today, and who reviews them?

## 14. Roles, Handoffs & Communication

66. Can you list every distinct role that touches an onboarding, including external parties, and say who is accountable overall for getting a property live?
67. At each handoff (coordinator  property manager  marketing  leasing  compliance), how does the receiving party learn it's their turn? Is it a system notification or a person reaching out?
68. You mentioned email, CRM, and project management tools. Which system is the authoritative source of truth for status, and how do you handle information that lives only in email threads?
69. Do landlords have any selfservice visibility into progress, or do they rely on the property manager for updates?
70. Who is responsible for chasing external parties — inspectors, photographers, contractors, previous landlords — when they're slow to respond?

## 15. Improvement & Pain Points

71. If you could change one thing about this process, what would it be and why?
72. Where do you see the most rework or duplicated data entry today?
73. Which steps do you believe add the least value but are hard to remove, and why are they still there?
74. Do different regional offices run this process differently? If so, where do the biggest variations occur?
75. What has changed in this process over the last year or two, and what prompted those changes?
