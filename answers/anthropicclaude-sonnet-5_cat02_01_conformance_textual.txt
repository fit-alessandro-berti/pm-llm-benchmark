# Analysis of Event Log Against Normative Process

## Case-by-Case Evaluation

### **Case 1: Receive  Check  Authorize  Package  Ship  Close**
 **CONFORMS** — This matches the expected sequence exactly for sufficient-inventory scenarios. No violations.

---

### **Case 2: Receive  Check  Package  Authorize  Ship  Close**
 **VIOLATION**

- **Rule violated:** "Authorize payment" should only occur *after* "Check inventory" and *before* packaging.
- **Explanation:** Here, "Package items" occurs at 10:00, *before* "Authorize payment" at 10:30. The normative description explicitly states that payment authorization must happen before items are packaged, since committing resources to packaging before confirming payment validity is illogical (financial risk of preparing goods for an unapproved/invalid payment). This is a clear **ordering violation between Authorize payment and Package items**.

---

### **Case 3: Receive  Check  Authorize  Cancel  Ship  Close**
 **VIOLATION (multiple)**

- **Rule violated #1:** Once "Cancel order" occurs, no fulfillment activities (such as "Ship order") should follow.
- **Explanation:** "Ship order" appears *after* "Cancel order," which directly contradicts the stated principle that shipping should "never occur after a cancellation." This is one of the explicitly called-out violation examples in the description.
- **Rule violated #2 (interpretive):** "Authorize payment" occurring before "Cancel order" is questionable. The normative text ties authorization to the *sufficient inventory* branch. If inventory was insufficient (leading to cancellation), authorizing payment beforehand suggests either (a) a misordering of activities, or (b) the branching logic was not properly followed — payment should not be authorized if the process is ultimately going to cancel due to insufficient stock. This suggests the case incorrectly mixed elements of both branches (fulfillment and cancellation), which is not permitted by the two clearly defined paths in the description.

---

### **Case 4: Receive  Check  Cancel  Close**
 **CONFORMS** — This matches the expected shorter sequence for insufficient-inventory scenarios exactly. No violations.

---

### **Case 5: Receive  Check  Authorize  Ship  Close**
 **VIOLATION**

- **Rule violated:** "Ship order" should only occur *after* "Package items."
- **Explanation:** The activity "Package items" is missing entirely from this case. The process description clearly states that items must be packaged before shipping ("Only after items have been properly packaged is it appropriate to proceed with 'Ship order'"). Skipping packaging and going straight from Authorize payment to Ship order violates this required precedence — it is not merely an ordering issue but a **missing mandatory activity** in the fulfillment path.

## Interpretive Notes

- **Case 2** raises the question posed in the description itself: "whether it's ever acceptable to authorize payment before certain conditions are fully confirmed." Given the explicit statement that packaging must follow authorization, this case is treated as a violation rather than an acceptable variant.

- **Case 3** is the most severe violation, combining two problems: it blends the fulfillment branch (Authorize payment) with the cancellation branch (Cancel order), and then further violates the explicit rule against shipping post-cancellation. This case exemplifies exactly the kind of scenario the description warns against ("shipping after cancellation").

- **Case 5** highlights that even if the *relative order* of remaining activities is technically correct (Authorize before Ship, Ship before Close), the **absence of a mandatory step** (Package items) still constitutes a deviation, since the normative model treats packaging as a required precondition for shipping — not merely a suggested step.
