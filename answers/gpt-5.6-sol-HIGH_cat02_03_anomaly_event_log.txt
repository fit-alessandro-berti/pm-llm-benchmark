A typical controlled P2P sequence is:

**Create requisition  Approve requisition  Issue PO  Receive goods and invoice  Perform two-/three-way match  Release payment  Close case**

The order of **goods receipt** and **invoice receipt** can vary, but payment should generally occur only after the required approvals and matching controls are complete.

| Case | Assessment | Anomalies and concerns |
|---|---|---|
| **1001** | Normal | The requisition is approved before the PO is issued. Goods and invoice are received before matching, and payment is released only after the match. No obvious anomaly. |
| **1002** | Highly anomalous | The **PO is issued before requisition approval**. More seriously, **payment is released before requisition approval, goods receipt, and invoice matching**. The invoice arriving before the goods is not necessarily improper by itself, but it should usually be blocked from payment until receipt and matching. |
| **1003** | Highly anomalous | **Payment is released before the invoice is recorded, before goods are received, and before matching occurs**. Unless this is an explicitly authorized advance-payment arrangement, it represents a major control failure. |
| **1004** | Anomalous | **Goods are received before the PO is issued**. This suggests a retrospective or “after-the-fact” PO, often called maverick buying. The remainder of the sequence—invoice, match, payment—is otherwise orderly. |

### Why these anomalies are problematic

- **Unauthorized purchasing:** Issuing a PO before approval, as in Case 1002, bypasses spending authorization.
- **Payment without validation:** Cases 1002 and 1003 release payment before the invoice/PO/goods receipt match, weakening the standard three-way-match control.
- **Risk of overpayment or fraud:** Payment before confirming the invoice and delivery can lead to paying incorrect, duplicate, fictitious, or undelivered charges.
- **Retrospective procurement:** Receiving goods before a PO, as in Case 1004, means the organization may have committed to a purchase without agreed terms, budget checks, or supplier authorization.
- **Audit and compliance issues:** These sequences can violate procurement policy, approval limits, segregation-of-duties controls, and financial reporting requirements.

The most serious cases are **1002 and 1003**, because payment was made before key controls were completed. **1004** indicates a purchasing-control problem, while **1001** appears compliant with a standard P2P flow. These observations assume ordinary P2P rules; documented exceptions such as approved vendor prepayments could explain some deviations.
